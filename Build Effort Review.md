## The 3 skipped banks

Every one of these was reviewed against primary sources (Companies House filing
history, and — for Revolut — live registry data as of 2026-09-01) before being
confirmed as a genuine, permanent skip, not a time-boxed guess. Full detail and
resolution history is in "Difficulty ranking" below; this is the short version.

1. **VTB Capital plc** — under UK sanctions and in insolvency administration
   since December 2022, with no accounts filed since. Not a data-availability
   problem: the entity is not a going concern.
2. **Bank Of Baroda (UK) Limited** — a permanent FRS 102 §7.1B cash-flow
   exemption (wholly-owned subsidiary), combined with zero quantitative
   Pillar 3 data anywhere in either Annual Report reviewed (purely narrative
   capital sections — no ratios, no figures to build a Pillar-3-only fallback
   from). The bank has also been in a Solvent Wind Down since FY2024.
3. **Revolut Bank UK Ltd** (FRN 981170, Companies House 12871051) — often
   mistaken for the well-known Revolut consumer app, but that app is a
   *different legal entity*: **Revolut Ltd** (company 08804411, incorporated
   2013), an e-money institution and out of scope for this project, which
   only covers PRA-authorised banks. Revolut Bank UK Ltd is a separate,
   much younger entity: incorporated 10 September 2020, granted its UK
   banking licence (with restrictions, the standard "mobilisation" path for
   new banks) in July 2024, and renamed from "Revolut NewCo UK Ltd" to its
   current name only in March 2026. It files full accounts (FY2022 through
   FY2025, most recently 9 May 2026), but FY2023–FY2025 all explicitly claim
   the FRS 101 IAS 7 cash-flow exemption — permanent, since its parent,
   Revolut Group Holdings Ltd, files the consolidated group cash-flow
   statement instead — and FY2022 has no cash-flow statement at all. The only
   Pillar 3 disclosure found anywhere is Revolut Group Holdings Ltd's own
   (group-level, FY24), not one for the standalone UK banking entity. Was
   reassessed once already, at a prior user's request, using a shorter
   FY2022–FY2025 window specifically to rule out the missing FY2021 filing as
   the deciding factor — it wasn't; the double blocker above held regardless.

## Observations

Zopa and LHV (the two fastest among the original builds, both ~26 tool calls) were also the first to use the scaffold/verify scripts — consistent with those cutting boilerplate/verification overhead, though token counts didn't drop correspondingly (research volume, not scaffolding, dominates token spend).

Co-operative Bank (5m 25s) is the single fastest full build, despite a higher tool-call count (43) than Zopa — it needed no OCR and no arithmetic-error investigation, just a large volume of clean source documents from a big institution.

Charity Bank was the longest wall-clock build in the series for most of the project (23m 25s, since surpassed by Bank of Africa UK — see below), ahead of Triodos (18m 7s) and Tandem Bank (18m 34s), despite needing OCR for only 2 of 5 filings. Its length is better explained by needing three separate source types across the five years (Wayback-recovered Pillar 3 docs for FY2021–2023, then Annual-Report-only capital sections for FY2024–2025) than by any single blocker.

Turkish Bank UK, RBS, Access Bank UK, Triodos, Tandem Bank, and Charity Bank all show notably lower token counts (193k–302k) than the bulk of the table (500k–750k typical) despite being among the longest/highest-tool-call builds — this later batch ran longer, higher-tool-call builds at lower token cost, plausibly reflecting more efficient research patterns (Wayback-first searches, reused OCR tooling) rather than genuinely easier source material. RBS in particular required substantial identity-verification research despite its low token figure.

Skip decisions are usually not dramatically faster than builds, with two exceptions: THIS BANK LIMITED resolved in 1m 8s / 10 tool calls (the fastest resolution — build or skip — in the whole series, since the blocker was evident almost immediately), and TD Bank Europe in 3m 31s / 27 tool calls (a clean, quickly-confirmed exemption with no ambiguity to chase down).

For reference, the earlier international-bank screening pass (not a bank build — covered ~8 candidate banks at once) took 3m 57s, 221 tool calls, 442,064 tokens.

Aldermore is the new longest full build after Charity Bank (14m 40s) — its all-5-filings-scanned requirement ties Triodos for the most OCR-dependent build in the series. AIB Group (UK) and Allica both continue the "later batch, lower token count" pattern first noted above despite AIB's genuine research complexity (entity-identity risk plus a newly-discovered exemption-switch pattern) — reinforcing that the pattern reflects more efficient research technique across the series, not easier source material.

WF-002's batch pushed the longest-build title further: Atom Bank (15m 34s) is now the second-longest full build in the series after Charity Bank, with Arbuthnot Latham (14m 38s) and Arab Bank Europe (14m 25s) close behind — all three needed either heavy OCR (Arbuthnot: 3 filings, 139–149 pages each) or a genuinely unresolved data problem (Arab Bank Europe's cash-bridge gap, below) rather than large source volume alone. Arab Bank Europe is the first EUR-reporting workbook in the series, applying the same FX methodology used for the USD banks. Every one of WF-002's 5 banks stayed in the 250k–340k token band — the "later batch, lower token count" pattern now looks like the new normal rather than a one-off.

WF-003 broke the longest-build record outright: Bank of Africa UK ran 25m 30s, beating Charity Bank's 23m 25s by over 2 minutes — driven by a genuine three-way restatement (equity, cash flow, and capital position all restated in the same later Annual Report, corroborated by an auditor's note referencing a regulatory investigation) layered on top of the heaviest OCR load in its batch. Bank of Ceylon UK (16m 0s) also pushed past every earlier bank except Charity Bank/Tandem/Triodos. Bank Of Baroda UK is the slowest self-skip recorded so far (7m 26s, well past THIS BANK LIMITED/Afin Bank's near-instant resolutions) — its blocker required actually reading two full Annual Reports' narrative capital sections cover-to-cover to confirm there was truly nothing quantitative to extract, not just spotting an exemption clause.

WF-004 was the first batch built under an explicit efficiency directive (WebFetch before WebSearch, check `pdf_tools.py scan` before assuming OCR, reuse existing FX rate tables, check later-year comparatives before searching for missing documents). It worked: WebSearch calls per fork dropped to 0, 0, 1, ~1, and 0 — down from routine multi-call use in every earlier batch — with no apparent loss of research quality (both self-caught transcription errors this batch, Bank of China UK's and Bank of Scotland's, were found via `verify_workbook.py`'s reconciliation check working as intended, not sloppier research). Two of the five (BLME, Bank of China UK) needed no OCR and no Wayback fallback at all — the cleanest-sourced builds since Co-operative Bank. The tradeoff: the WebSearch quota is session-wide, not per-fork, so later forks in a batch can still exhaust it even while following the directive (Bank of Scotland, Bank of the Philippine Islands Europe, and Bank Saderat all reported hitting an already-exhausted quota on their first attempt) — worth reserving that session's WebSearch budget for the trickiest bank in a batch rather than assuming it's evenly available.

WF-005 introduced a new failure mode worth tracking: 2 of the 5 forks (Barclays Bank Plc, Brown Shipley) stalled mid-task on a stream issue distinct from hitting the session's usage limit — the notification read "no progress for 600s" rather than "hit your session limit." Both were resumed via `SendMessage` to their `agentId` (telling them exactly what they were doing when they stalled) rather than restarted, which preserved all their completed research. This worked cleanly for both, but inflates their reported duration — excluded from the mean above, see the table note.

WF-006 hit a different, more severe failure mode: the session's usage limit was reached partway through, killing all 5 forks simultaneously with no resumable transcript (unlike WF-005's stream stall). Confirmed nothing had been written to `banks/`/`scripts/` for any of the 5 banks, so all 5 were re-dispatched as fresh forks once the limit reset — none of the wasted research could be salvaged, but no incorrect or partial data was left behind either. All 5 durations in the table above are from the clean second attempt. See `wayfinder/map.md`'s Notes for how to tell this failure mode apart from a stream stall going forward.

WF-006 also surfaced two real bugs in the project's shared tooling, both now fixed: `bank_scaffold.py`'s color picker had a hardcoded single-color fallback once its 20-color palette was exhausted (this project now has 60+ banks, so every scaffold call past the 20th hit the same fallback color — confirmed causing an actual collision), and `bank_workbook.py`'s `add_overview_sheet()` silently produces a broken chart if `ratios=[]` is passed (unlike the explicitly-supported `cash_flow_totals=[]` case) — now documented in the docstring with the established workaround (empty-`{}`-valued entries, first used at Brown Shipley).

## Sanity-check audit (2026-08-27, between WF-004 and WF-005)

At the user's request, ran `python3 scripts/verify_workbook.py` across all 47 workbooks built at that point, then split the raw output across 4 parallel audit forks (~12 banks each) to classify every flagged mismatch — tail-chain/multi-section checker false-positive, trivial rounding, or genuinely unexplained — and specifically confirm several already-known findings (Arab Bank Europe's cash-bridge gap, Bank of Africa UK's three-way restatement, Zenith's FX-rate bug fix) were still correctly reflected in the workbooks.

**Found and fixed:**

### Different "kinds" of problem worth distinguishing

- **Source-document arithmetic errors** (LHV, Zempler, Vanquis) — data-quality catches requiring a documented correction, not blockers or judgment calls.
- **The Zenith/Union Bank of India UK FX-rate bug** — a genuine build-tooling mistake, now documented as a known pitfall in the FX conversion methodology.
- **RBS's entity-identity research** — a correct-entity-identification risk unique to banks that underwent major group restructuring/rebranding, seen only in milder form elsewhere (Clydesdale's PLC→Ltd rename).
- **Tandem Bank's partial exemption** — the cash-flow exemption kicking in mid-window rather than blocking the whole entity, distinct from every prior all-or-nothing exemption case.
- **BNY Mellon's Pillar-3-only treatment** — not really a "problem" at all, but a deliberate scope reduction requested after an initial skip: the first workbook in the series shipped with a structurally incomplete statement (no cash flow, by design, clearly labeled) rather than either full data or a full skip. The pattern is now reusable for United Trust Bank or any other still-skipped exemption bank, if wanted.
- **The cash-flow exemption kicking in mid-window** — first seen at Tandem Bank (exemption elected partway through), now seen a second time in a different shape at AIB Group (UK): a group→solo reporting-basis switch (not a fresh exemption election) that happens to produce the same "some years have cash flow, later years don't" symptom. Worth remembering these are two distinct causes of the same visible pattern, not one recurring bug.
- **Consolidated-vs-solo Pillar 3 basis breaks within a single bank's own history** — ABC International Bank is the first case where the SAME entity's Pillar 3 reporting basis itself changes over the 5-year window (solo FY2021–FY2022, consolidated FY2023–FY2024), as distinct from the more common cash-flow-vs-Pillar-3 basis MISMATCH seen at Vanquis/Tandem (two different statements at two different levels within the same year).
- **Arab Bank Europe's unreconciled cash-bridge gap** — the first genuinely unresolved data problem left in a finished, non-skipped workbook: a €379,777k gap between one year's closing balance and the next year's opening balance that couldn't be traced to a specific cause because the source document that would explain it was unobtainable. Every prior "arithmetic error" case (LHV, Zempler, Vanquis) had an internally-consistent correct figure to fall back on; this one doesn't — it's flagged, not fixed, and is worth remembering as a different failure mode than a data-quality catch.
- **EUR reporting** — Arab Bank Europe is the first EUR-denominated bank in the series, using the same FX conversion methodology built for the USD banks (BoE GBP/EUR spot for stocks, average for flows, programmatic translation plug). No new methodology needed, just a third currency proving the approach generalizes.
- **A restatement corroborated by a regulatory-investigation reference** (Bank of Africa UK) — distinct from every prior restatement case (e.g. BNY Mellon's or Bank of Ceylon's routine cross-vintage restatements) because the source document itself flags an "ongoing regulatory investigation" alongside the prior-year adjustments, rather than a routine methodology or template change. Handled the same way as any restatement (each year's own originally-published figures used, restatement documented), but worth remembering as a case where the underlying cause is genuinely unknown rather than just unstated.
- **A double-blocker skip with no Pillar-3-only fallback** (Bank Of Baroda UK) — distinct from BNY Mellon/ABC International/Bank Mandiri's Pillar-3-only precedent (permanent exemption + genuinely rich Pillar 3 data) and from UBP's double blocker (exemption + identity issue): here the exemption is paired with a second, independent blocker — zero quantitative Pillar 3 data of any kind — so there's no fallback scope reduction available, only a full skip.
- **The build fork catching its own transcription error** (Bank of Beirut UK) — the first time in this series `verify_workbook.py`'s automatic reconciliation check caught a mistake introduced by the build process itself (an omitted dividend line, since the source didn't print an explicit subtotal to check against), rather than a pre-existing error in a bank's own published source document. A reminder the verify step earns its keep on both sides. Recurred twice more in WF-004 (Bank of China UK, Bank of Scotland) — now a proven pattern rather than a one-off.
- **A mid-series functional-currency switch** (Bank of the Philippine Islands Europe) — distinct from every FX-conversion case so far (SMBC, Zenith, UBA UK, Union Bank of India UK, Arab Bank Europe, Bank Saderat, Bank Mandiri Europe), which were all consistently one currency throughout their window. This is the first bank whose OWN reporting currency changed mid-window (GBP→USD during FY2023), requiring the earlier years to stay in the bank's original currency while later years get converted.
- **A live, still-unfolding sanctions situation reflected in the newest year's figures** (Bank Saderat) — distinct from VTB Capital's skip (a settled, years-old insolvency) because the bank remains a going concern and was built, but its FY2025 figures are shaped by sanctions that took effect only weeks before this build. Worth rechecking this specific workbook again once the situation has settled, rather than treating FY2025 as a stable baseline.
- **A fork's own efficiency practice, directly measured** (WF-004) — the first batch where an explicit efficiency directive was given and its effect was measurable in the forks' own reported tool usage (WebSearch calls dropped to 0–1 per fork). Worth repeating this directive on future batches rather than assuming it carries over implicitly.
- **Two entities sharing a near-identical name at the same parent group, deliberately distinguished** — Barclays Bank Plc (WF-005) is the second case in the series after RBS/NatWest Markets of confirming which of two closely-related legal entities is the one actually named on the PRA list, but distinct from RBS's case: both Barclays entities are current and active simultaneously (ring-fenced UK retail vs. non-ring-fenced wholesale/international), not a historic-vs-renamed-successor situation. Required its own filename to avoid colliding with the already-built Barclays Bank UK PLC workbook.
- **A fork stalling mid-task** (WF-005: Barclays Bank Plc, Brown Shipley) — a new failure mode distinct from hitting the session's usage limit (the notification reads "no progress for Ns" rather than "hit your session limit"). Resolved by resuming via `SendMessage` rather than restarting, which preserves completed research but inflates the reported duration — both excluded from the mean in the time/tokens table above.
- **A periodic sanity-check audit catching real, previously-undiscovered errors** (2026-08-27, between WF-004 and WF-005) — the first systematic re-verification pass across every already-built workbook, rather than only checking a bank at build time. Found 2 genuine data errors (Starling's FY2026 total, Chetwood's Company-vs-Group column mixups) that individual build-time verification had missed, plus 2 cosmetic sheet-ordering issues. Confirms the value of repeating this kind of audit periodically rather than treating "verified once at build time" as permanent.
- **Bank of Ireland UK's unbolded subtotal rows, fixed 2026-08-27** — the audit's third finding: three running-subtotal rows in the Cash Flow Statement were tagged `DATA` instead of `TOTAL`, so they weren't bold like every other bank's subtotals and confused the generic checker into treating a 29-row span as one unreconciled block. The underlying figures were always correct (confirmed by hand in the audit) — this was purely a styling/tagging gap, fixed by re-tagging the three rows to `TOTAL` and rebuilding. Checker pass rate improved from 2/4 to 4/6; the 2 remaining flags are the standard expected patterns.
- **A session-limit termination killing multiple forks at once with no resumable transcript** (WF-006) — distinct from a single fork's stream stall (WF-005): here the whole session's usage limit was hit, terminating all 5 forks simultaneously, each with an empty or truncated `result` rather than a genuine last-known-state message. Confirmed nothing had been written to disk for any of the 5 banks before re-dispatching them fresh once the limit reset — the key check that made re-dispatch safe rather than risking silent duplicate/conflicting work.
- **Two reusable-tooling bugs found by ordinary build forks, not a dedicated audit** (WF-006) — `bank_scaffold.py`'s color picker running out of its fixed palette and silently reusing a fallback color (caused a real collision), and `bank_workbook.py`'s `add_overview_sheet()` breaking on an empty `ratios=[]` list. Both were caught incidentally by forks doing normal bank builds, not by the systematic sanity-check audit — a reminder that routine build work surfaces tooling problems too, not just data problems.
- **A mirror-image partial-exemption pattern** (Cater Allen) — every prior "exemption kicks in partway through the window" case (Tandem Bank, AIB Group UK, Atom Bank) blocked the *early-to-mid* years and left later years exempt. Cater Allen is the first case where the exemption is newly adopted only in the *final* year, leaving every earlier year fully disclosed — worth distinguishing from the other three since the practical effect (which years are usable) is opposite.

WF-007 hit a session-limit termination mid-batch (4 of 5 forks killed) and required real recovery diagnosis rather than a clean re-dispatch across the board: one fork's script was a genuine dead end (unfilled scaffold despite its own claim of progress), one was salvageable without any fork work at all (a complete script just needed to actually run), and one produced a false alarm — a workbook that looked catastrophically broken (~2x reconciliation gap) turned out to have entirely correct data, just two unbolded subtotal rows confusing the checker. Coutts & Company and ClearBank's true build effort is therefore not fully captured in the time/tokens table above — see the table's footnotes.

WF-009 hit a second session-limit termination (4 of 5 forks killed again) and produced one of each recovery category in a single batch: EFG Private Bank needed only a rebuild (its fix was already in the script, just unexecuted), FCMB UK was another false alarm (already correct, the checker's usual multi-section blind spot), and FCE Bank + FidBank UK both had real bugs needing dedicated recovery forks — including, at FidBank UK, a third recurrence of the FX opening-balance spot-rate bug first seen at Zenith Bank UK. Across both interrupted batches, a consistent lesson emerged: a cutoff fork's own last message is a hint about what's on disk, never a verdict — check `banks/`/`scripts/` and re-run `verify_workbook.py` before deciding whether a bank needs a fresh dispatch, a plain rebuild, a fix fork, or nothing at all.

**Tooling fixes, 2026-08-28** (at the user's request, fixed now but deliberately not redeployed across already-built banks): `bank_workbook.py`'s `add_overview_sheet()` now properly supports `ratios=[]` (mirrors the existing `cash_flow_totals=[]` support) instead of requiring the empty-`{}`-valued-entries workaround — regression-tested against all 6 banks that exercise these code paths, no changes needed to their scripts. While rebuilding those, found and fixed a **third** instance of the scrambled-sheet-order bug (Brown Shipley, built in WF-005 after the original sanity-check audit had already run) — same fix pattern as Bank Mandiri Europe/Bank Saderat. A full sheet-order check now confirms all 57 built workbooks match the standard order.

- **A cutoff fork's own claimed progress can be wrong** (Charter Court) — a session-limit termination's final message said "building now with full cash flow + not-disclosed Pillar 3," but the script it actually left behind was still the unfilled scaffold template. The lesson generalizes beyond this project: never trust a terminated agent's self-reported state over what's actually on disk.
- **A complete, unexecuted script recovered for free** (Coutts & Company) — the mirror-image case to Charter Court's: a session-limit-interrupted fork had done all the real research and written correct, complete data into its script, but the process was killed before the final `bw.save()` call ran. Trying the script directly (no new fork needed) completed the build in seconds. Worth checking this before assuming any interrupted bank needs full re-dispatch.
- **A ~2x reconciliation gap that wasn't a data error at all** (ClearBank) — the most severe-looking false alarm in the series so far. An interrupted fork's own diagnosis (a tax-line sign error) was itself wrong; the actual cause, found by a dedicated recovery fork, was the same unbolded-subtotal-row bug class fixed at Bank of Ireland UK. A reminder that a large, alarming-looking checker mismatch needs actual investigation before assuming the underlying data is broken — and that a prior fork's own diagnosis of its own problem isn't automatically correct either.
- **A second "double blocker, no fallback" skip** (Citibank UK) — same shape as Bank Of Baroda UK (permanent cash-flow exemption plus zero quantitative capital/liquidity data), now confirmed as a recurring pattern rather than a one-off. Also the second bank in the series (after Bank Of Baroda UK) disclosing an active wind-down of part of its business, which plausibly explains the increasingly minimal disclosure in both cases.
- **Two related entities both mid-crisis-recovery, built cleanly in the same batch** (Credit Suisse UK, Credit Suisse International) — the first pair in this series where both entities' complexity comes from a real, ongoing corporate event (the 2023 UBS rescue) rather than a data-transcription problem: a Part VII business transfer at one, an explicit disclosed controlled wind-down (97.7% asset collapse) at the other. Neither needed a skip — both remain active, separately-reporting entities with real numbers to transcribe, just numbers that tell a dramatic story.
- **A new small-bank regulatory-regime pattern** (Cynergy Bank) — the PRA's Small Domestic Deposit Taker (SDDT) regime, which the bank's own disclosure states "simplifies certain capital requirements," is the first time this project has found an explicit regulatory reason (rather than just "the bank chose not to disclose more") for unusually thin Pillar 3 coverage at an otherwise normal, active UK bank. Worth checking for on other small UK banks going forward — a different category from the FRS 101/102 cash-flow exemption entirely (this affects Pillar 3 disclosure depth, not cash flow statements). Confirmed as a real recurring pattern rather than a one-off just one batch later at DF Capital Bank.
- **A cutoff fork's own self-diagnosis can be wrong, twice over** (FidBank UK) — the interrupted fork blamed a dropped "Loss before tax" line; the actual bug (found by the recovery fork) was three entirely different line items missing their values. A reminder that even a fork's own stated diagnosis of its own problem needs independent verification, not just its claim of having found and fixed something.
- **The FX opening-balance spot-rate bug recurring a third time** (FidBank UK, after Zenith Bank UK and Union Bank of India UK) — now a confirmed pattern rather than two isolated incidents. This should be a standing checklist item on every FX-converted bank build from here on, not something to only check when it's already suspected.
- **A four-way taxonomy of "what a session-limit cutoff actually left behind"**, confirmed across WF-006/007/009: (1) nothing at all, (2) a complete script never executed, (3) a fix applied to the script but never rebuilt, (4) a real bug needing a dedicated fix fork. The same-looking "status: failed, hit your session limit" notification can mean any of these four — always check `banks/`/`scripts/` directly and re-run `verify_workbook.py` rather than assuming which one you're dealing with.

WF-010 ran with no interruptions (all 5 forks completed on their own) — the first fully clean batch since WF-008. One self-skip (GB Bank Limited) and 4 builds.

- **A new self-skip flavor: access-tooling stack, not a legal exemption** (GB Bank Limited) — every prior self-skip was driven by a permanent FRS 101/102 cash-flow exemption (UBP, Bank Of Baroda UK, Citibank UK, ICICI, PNBE, United Trust Bank, TD Bank Europe). GB Bank Limited is the first self-skip driven purely by source-access blockers stacking on one entity (scanned filings + site 403 + an in-session Wayback failure + exhausted WebSearch) with no exemption involved at all — worth a revisit with fresh tool budget rather than treating as permanently unreachable, since none of its blockers are structural in the way a legal exemption is.
- **A parent-consolidated Pillar 3 gap caused by an access blocker, not a genuine non-disclosure** (Goldman Sachs International Bank) — distinct from DB UK Bank/Bank Mandiri Europe's genuinely-thin-Pillar-3 cases: GSIB's own accounts explicitly say the missing 8 of 11 metrics live in its parent's consolidated Pillar 3 document, which this session simply couldn't reach (site 403, Wayback down, WebSearch exhausted) — a documented-but-recoverable gap, not a case of the data not existing.
- **A bank building its own missing-year FX rate from scratch, not just reusing the established table** (FirstBank UK) — every prior FX bank (Zenith, UBA UK, Union Bank of India UK, Access Bank UK, Bank Mandiri Europe, BPI Europe, Bank Sepah International, Credit Suisse International) converted using rates already in the shared table. FirstBank UK is the first case where a source year (FY2021) has no published USD comparative anywhere, requiring an independently-sourced spot/average rate pair to be derived and added, following the same translation-effect-line methodology rather than skipping the year.

WF-011 hit a third session-limit termination (4 of 5 forks killed again, Griffin Bank had already completed) — but this time, unlike WF-007/WF-009, three of the four interrupted banks (Guaranty Trust Bank UK, Gulf International Bank UK, Habib Bank Zurich) had nothing at all on disk and needed full fresh rebuilds, while the fourth (Hampden & Co) had a genuine half-applied edit rather than a false alarm or an unexecuted-but-correct script.

- **A half-applied edit that would have silently dropped real data** (Hampden & Co) — distinct from every prior "half-applied fix" case (FCE Bank's reclassification, FidBank UK's missing lines): here the interrupted fork had *removed* a real, sourced cash-flow line item while chasing a small reconciliation gap, and was cut off before rebuilding — so the workbook on disk was actually complete and plausible-looking, just silently missing a genuine data point that was never restored. A reminder that "the workbook already exists" isn't itself proof nothing is wrong, even when the checker mostly passes.
- **An expected FX pattern broken by an individual bank** (Guaranty Trust Bank UK) — every prior Nigerian-bank-subsidiary build in this project (Zenith Bank UK, UBA UK, FCMB UK, FidBank UK) reports in USD. GTBank UK reports in GBP throughout, confirmed directly rather than assumed from the pattern — worth remembering that a strong prior from similar entities is a starting hypothesis to verify, not a fact to inherit.
- **A sandbox-level PDF fetch truncation, distinct from a source-side blocker** (Gulf International Bank UK) — the first time in this project a document fetch failure traced to this session's own tooling (a 1MB truncation on a Wayback-archived PDF) rather than the source site blocking access or the file being genuinely scanned/image-only. Recovered via `qpdf`/`fitz` xref repair for one year and a same-document prior-year-comparative workaround for the other — worth remembering as a third category of "can't get the PDF" alongside site-blocking and scan-only filings.

WF-012 hit a fourth session-limit termination (3 of 5 forks killed this time — Griffin-Bank-style clean completions for HBL Bank UK and HSBC Innovation Bank before the cutoff). All 3 interrupted banks had nothing on disk and needed full fresh rebuilds; all 3 recovered cleanly with no false alarms or half-applied edits this round.

- **The Wayback Machine outage independently confirmed as real, not session-specific** (Havin Bank) — GB Bank Limited and Goldman Sachs International Bank (WF-010) both reported Wayback as unreachable and it was treated as a possible session/tooling limitation worth revisiting with "fresh budget." Havin Bank's fork hit the same failure and confirmed it was a genuine Internet Archive-side outage (not a per-session restriction) — worth remembering this may still be down on a future revisit of GB Bank Limited/Goldman Sachs International Bank's gaps, not assumed fixed by simply trying again.
- **A missing page inside an otherwise-normal scanned filing** (Hampshire Trust Bank) — distinct from every prior scanned-filing case (which were poor-quality but complete): the FY2025 Companies House scan's own page numbering skips from 63 to 65, genuinely omitting the operating-activities half of the cash flow statement from that specific PDF (confirmed via two independent page-count checks). Recovered by finding the bank's own text-native website copy of the identical report rather than treating the gap as unrecoverable.
- **A Companies House DNS-level outage, distinct from a source site blocking fetches** (HSBC Bank plc) — every prior Companies House access problem in this project was either a genuinely scanned filing or (rarely) a rate-limit; this is the first time Companies House itself was unreachable for an entire session (confirmed via direct `nslookup`, not tool-specific). Recovered by reverse-engineering undocumented query parameters on the bank's own investor-relations site instead — a reminder that a primary-source outage doesn't necessarily mean stopping, if a secondary official source can be found and used as transparently as the primary would have been.
- **A bank's own Annual Report missing its Cash Flow Statement entirely for one year** (HBL Bank UK) — distinct from the cash-flow-exemption cases (Tandem/AIB/Atom/Cater Allen), where the statement is legitimately absent by regulatory election: here FY2021's own AR simply doesn't contain one (confirmed via a full page-by-page review), with no stated exemption reason — recovered from a later year's own comparative column instead, the only place those figures are published, and documented as an unexplained omission rather than a claimed exemption.
