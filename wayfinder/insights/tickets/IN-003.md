## Question

Using IN-001's normalized dataset, run a real statistical clustering of the
145 banks into peer groups, and document the result with rationale — this is
the client's actual ask ("clusterings of banks"), not just a nice-to-have. The
user deliberately left dimension/method selection to this ticket rather than
pre-specifying it, on the grounds that it should be a data-driven call made
once real coverage stats exist (see IN-001's coverage summary).

### What to decide, using IN-001's coverage summary

- **Which dimensions to cluster on.** Prefer metrics that are well-populated
  (real numeric values, not `"Not publicly disclosed"`) across most of the 145
  banks — a dimension only 20 banks have data for will produce a degenerate or
  misleading clustering if forced. Balance-sheet-scale proxies (e.g. Total
  RWAs), capital-adequacy ratios (CET1/Tier 1/Total Capital ratios), and
  liquidity ratios (LCR/NSFR) are the most likely well-populated candidates
  based on `Extra Pillar 3 Disclosures.md`'s cadence findings and
  `Build Effort Review.md`'s pattern notes — confirm against IN-001's actual
  coverage numbers rather than assuming.
- **Which method.** k-means (with a justified k, e.g. via elbow/silhouette) or
  hierarchical clustering are both reasonable defaults; pick based on what the
  actual data shape supports (e.g. hierarchical handles uneven cluster sizes
  and missing-data-driven outliers more gracefully if that's what you find).
  Document the choice and why, don't just default silently.
- **How to handle basis differences.** IN-001's currency/basis flags exist so
  this ticket can decide whether to cluster all 145 banks together with basis
  as a caveat, exclude/separate Group-basis or FX-converted banks, or cluster
  in tiers (e.g. by rough size band first, then within-band). Use judgment;
  document the choice.

### Deliverable

A script or notebook-style artifact under `scripts/` (e.g.
`scripts/cluster_banks.py`) that's re-runnable against a refreshed IN-001
dataset — this map's destination is an *ongoing* capability, so a one-off
Jupyter-style analysis that can't be rerun doesn't satisfy it. Its output:
cluster assignments per bank, the chosen dimensions/method/rationale written
up, and enough structure (e.g. a CSV of bank→cluster, cluster centroids/
summary stats) that IN-005 can turn it into visuals without re-deriving the
clustering itself.

## Progress

Built `scripts/cluster_banks.py`. NumPy is the only third-party dependency
(no sklearn/scipy). The primary output is `research/bank_clusters.csv` (145
bank rows: 113 clustered, 32 flagged "insufficient data"). Each selected
feature carries its source disclosure year in a matching `<dimension>_year`
column. The script also persists `research/bank_clusters_centroids.csv` and
`research/bank_clusters_sweep.csv` for downstream visualisation.

**Dimensions**: all 7 Pillar 3 ratio sheets (CET1 Ratio, Tier 1 Ratio, Total
Capital Ratio, Leverage Ratio, LCR, NSFR, MREL Ratio) — per the user, MREL was
kept in rather than dropped for sparse coverage (9/145 banks have a numeric
value; the rest are genuine non-disclosure, not missing data). Total RWAs and
the absolute capital-amount sheets were excluded: their units aren't
consistent across banks in the extracted dataset (£'000 vs £m vs USD/EUR
conversions), while the 7 ratio sheets are dimensionless percentages and
comparable as-is. Consolidation-basis differences (solo vs. Group) are not
corrected for — a real, documented limitation, not fixed in this pass.

**Row selection per bank/sheet**: some sheets carry a sibling non-percentage
row (e.g. Leverage Ratio's "total exposure measure" amount alongside its "%"
row) — an early run picked whichever row had more populated years,
which pulled in absolute-amount rows for several banks and produced
nonsense (e.g. an "LCR" feature in the hundreds of thousands). Fixed by
preferring any row whose disclosed values are "%"-formatted before falling
back to the most-populated row.

**Value used**: latest disclosed year per bank/dimension (a snapshot, not a
multi-year average) — banks cover different year windows, and this ticket is
about peer grouping, not trend-fitting (IN-004's job).

**Standardization**: robust (median/IQR), not mean/std, then winsorized
(clipped) at ±4 IQR-units. A few known genuine extreme disclosures — Afin
Bank's documented 999999% LCR, and a couple of very high capital ratios at
small/atypical-balance-sheet banks already flagged as "noise, not signal" in
`../../Cross-Bank Trends Analysis.md` — otherwise get isolated as their own
1-2-bank "cluster" at every k tried, crowding out any real peer-group
structure among the other ~110 banks. Winsorizing keeps them recognizably
extreme without letting a single cell's scale dominate every distance
computation.

**Method and k**: k-means (dependency-free numpy implementation — no
sklearn/scipy in this environment), 15 random restarts per k, k swept 2-8,
selected by silhouette score (also numpy, O(n²) — fine at this scale).
**k=2 was chosen** (silhouette 0.66, clearly ahead of k=3's 0.43): a 95-bank
"low CET1/Tier 1 Ratio" cluster (the larger/more established banks, ratios
generally in the teens-to-20s%) and an 18-bank "high CET1/Tier 1 Ratio"
cluster (smaller/specialist banks holding much larger capital buffers,
including Afin Bank's outlier year). Spot-checked against banks with known
profiles (HSBC, Barclays, Vanquis, Atom, Chetwood, Zopa all landed in the
"low" cluster as expected for established/mainstream banks). k=3-6 all had
respectable silhouette scores too (0.4-0.44) and are available in the
script's k-sweep output if IN-005 wants finer visual granularity than the
k=2 split — not chosen here since silhouette scoring, the documented
selection criterion, preferred k=2 outright.

**113/145 banks included in the fit** (>= 4 of 7 dimensions populated);
32 banks below that threshold are listed in the output as "insufficient
data" rather than clustered on mostly-imputed values. Included banks with
missing individual dimensions had those cells median-imputed, flagged
per-cell (`<sheet>_imputed` column) so a downstream consumer can tell a real
value from a filled one.

Auto-generated cluster labels ("low X / high Y") are a starting point for
IN-005's visuals, not final client-facing naming.

**MREL sensitivity**: MREL remains in the primary seven-dimension run as
requested, but only 9/145 banks have a numeric latest MREL value. The script
supports `--exclude-mrel` for a sensitivity run. On the current dataset this
still includes 113 banks and 32 as insufficient, preserves the same 95/18
k=2 split, and raises the k=2 silhouette from 0.6599 to 0.6912. The
sensitivity artifacts are `research/bank_clusters_no_mrel.csv`,
`research/bank_clusters_no_mrel_centroids.csv`, and
`research/bank_clusters_no_mrel_sweep.csv`.

**Reproducibility and interpretation**: this is a latest-observation
snapshot, so the year columns must be considered alongside each value.
Missing dimensions for included banks are median-imputed and explicitly
flagged; banks below the four-dimension threshold are not fitted. Robust
median/IQR scaling and ±4 clipping are deliberate safeguards against known
extreme disclosures, but remain analytical choices to revisit if the dataset
or client question changes. Cluster IDs and labels are not client-facing
classifications.
