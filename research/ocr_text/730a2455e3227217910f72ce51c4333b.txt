The Directors present their Strategic Report for The Bank of London Group Limited (the “Company” or the
“Bank’") for the year ended 31 December 2025. The Company (registered number 12844788) is a private
company limited by shares incorporated and domiciled in the United Kingdom under the Companies Act
2006 and is registered in England and Wales. On 10 March 2025, the Company's registered office changed
to 77 Cornhill, Fourth Floor, London, England EC3V 3QQ.

The Bank's immediate parent is TBOL Holdings UK Limited (“Group”). The Bank's ultimate parent company is
TBOL Holdings Jersey Limited.

1. Mission Statement

Our mission is to create a safe clearing bank for the UK, offering a purpose-built payments, settlement and
clearing platform that is powering the businesses and entrepreneurs of the future. With our technologies,
innovations, and differentiated bank capabilities, we prioritise the preservation of our depositors’ monies
and market stability. All client deposits are heid in full at the Bank of England.

2. Principal Activities

As a UK clearing bank, the principal activities of the Bank are to provide payment and deposit services to
our clients. These focus on:
e Agency, clearing, payment and settlement solutions for regulated and non-regulated domestic and
international companies;
e Deposits-as-a-Service — directly or through deposit aggregators, and;
e Business & Commercial Banking - providing custody, transaction, and treasury banking solutions to
many types of business, domestic and international.

3. Review of Business and Strategy

Our modern, cloud-native technology infrastructure represents a significant departure from traditional
banking platforms, enabling us to offer enhanced efficiency, real-time processing capabilities and improved
resilience. This technological foundation has been crucial in delivering our core services:

e 24/7 Faster Payments processing, with high availability and operational resilience;

e Comprehensive BACS (Bankers’ Automated Clearing System) capability, supporting a broad range of
payment use cases;

e API-first connectivity, enabling efficient client onboarding and integration;
e Reporting and reconciliation tooling, supporting timely operational oversight; and
e Strengthened security controls, aligned to the Bank’s risk management and compliance objectives.

Operations and Innovation

Over the course of 2025, we have continued to develop and enhance our operational capabilities as
follows:

The Group has taken a lease at 77 Cornhill to house our London operations;

We maintained financial crime screening functionality in 2025 and have rolled out an enhanced AML
(anti-money laundering) and sanctions screening system in Q2 2026, to further strengthen our
financial crime control environment.

4. Financial Results for the Year

The Bank recorded a net loss for the year of £23,503k (2024: £23,970k). The net loss for the year
represents costs incurred in setting up infrastructure (non-tech) and hiring the high-calibre workforce
required to continue to develop our capabilities and banking platform. The losses incurred in 2025, and
their impact upon the Bank’s net assets, remain in line with Directors’ expectations, given the development
phase of the business.

The Bank's net interest income increased 51% in the year to £4,141k (2024: £2,748k), with interest income
of £24,290k (2024: £24,027k) primarily representing interest income earned on funds deposited in the
Bank’s account at the Bank of England, and interest expense of £20,149k (2024: £21,279k) being interest
expense payable on customer deposits.

Despite cash and cash equivalents balances falling from £830m to £327m over the course of the year,
primarily driven by deposit aggregators, average cash balances were 18% higher. This combined with more
favourable pricing, contributed to the increase in net interest income.

The Bank of England base rate was cut over the course of 2025 from 4.75% to 3.75%. The Bank’s interest-
earning assets reprice essentially immediately with the base rate and movements in the rate flow through
to interest income with minimal lag. The Bank’s margin is instead a function of the rate paid to depositors
relative to the base rate over the same period.

Administrative expenses for the year were £30,217k (2024: £27,659k), an increase of £2,558k or 9%. The
primary drivers of administrative expenses continue to be employee costs and the management recharge
from Group. The £14,400k (2024: £14,400k) management recharge from Group, for the provision of core
banking technology and related support to the Company, remained fiat on prior year as stipulated by the

9. Community and Environmental, Social and Governance issues

The Bank’s impact on the community, socially and environmentally, is considered part of its governance
and risk framework.

The Board is committed to Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) issues, so that the Bank can
operate responsibly, deliver sustainable outcomes and provide meaningful benefits to its stakeholders.

The Bank's approach to managing financial and non-financial risks related to ESG will evolve progressively
and focuses on the following areas:

*« Policy Development: The Bank’s approach to ESG risks, including climate-related risks, are included in

warranted by the growth of the business.

* Engagement: The Bank will consider whether to engage with third-party providers as part of the
onboarding and ongoing review processes as the business grows.

e Sustainable Operations: Over time, ESG considerations will increasingly be embedded into business
processes, investment considerations, and operational planning to support the Bank's long-term value
creation and responsible growth.

6. Going Concern

In making their assessment of the appropriateness of adopting the going concern basis of accounting, the

Board have prepared profitability, liquidity and regulatory capital forecasts (‘forecasts’) and have undertaken

an assessment, for a period of 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements. As part of

this assessment, various stressed scenarios have also been considered. The key assumptions in producing the

forecasts were:

e the timing and quantum of additional capital injections,

e the timing of the removal of the voluntary arrangement related to new customer onboarding and
regulatory capital requirement changes, and,

¢ growth in revenue.

In addition to the above, when performing the going concern assessment, the Directors have considered that
the Bank is wholly reliant upon financial support from its parent with capital raised by the parent required to be
down streamed to the Bank. Accordingly, for the remainder of this note reference is made to the Group's
ability to raise capital. Since the year end, further capital injections of £35.0m were made into Group and
£31.7m was down streamed to the Bank up to the date of the signing of these financial statements.

While the Board acknowledge the key assumptions and matters noted above, they have considered the
mitigating factors in place, in particular:

1. the commitment and ability of the ultimate parent company shareholders to inject capital into the ultimate
parent company, and the commitment of the ultimate parent company to then down-stream capital to the
Group and then the Group to Bank,

2. the potential growth of its commercial business model, which is still in its infancy, and,

3. the Group’s demonstrable operational capability.

Based on these mitigating factors, the Board have concluded that the Bank will have adequate capital
resources to continue trading for a period of 12 months from the date of approval of these financial
statements, and that the going concern basis of preparation remains appropriate.

The Board recognise that, notwithstanding the commitment and ability of ultimate parent company
shareholders to inject capital into the ultimate parent company, and the commitment of the ultimate parent
company to then down-stream capital to the Group and then the Group to Bank, a material uncertainty over
going concern exists as a result of additional funding requirements and there are significant doubts about
going concern. In addition to the existing material uncertainty surrounding the current funding, the following
factors may necessitate additional financing to meet its financial obligations:

1. Deterioration in Market Conditions ~ A material decline in macroeconomic or sector-specific conditions
could reduce projected revenues, adversely impacting cash flows.

2. Operational and Capital factors ~ The timing of the removal of the voluntary arrangement related to new
customer onboarding and regulatory capital requirement changes.

Should any of these risks materialise, the Group may be required to secure additional funding to discharge its
financial obligations.

These risks result in a material uncertainty related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on
the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern and, therefore, it may be unable to realise its assets and
discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business.

Basis for qualified opinion

Our opinion is qualified in respect of one matter, as was the case in our modified audit opinion in respect
of the financial statements for the year end 31 December 2024.

In September 2022, TBOL Holdings UK Limited (previously Oplyse Holdings Limited — the ‘Parent
company’), together with its subsidiaries, the ‘Group’, implemented a Share Option Plan (the ‘Previous
Plan’) where equity-settled share option awards over shares in TBOL Holdings UK Limited are granted to
employees of the Group, including employees of the Company.

Options issued under the Previous Plan allowed the participants the right to acquire ordinary shares in the
Parent company. As described in note 17, the Company accounts for the Previous Plan as an equity-
settled plan. The fair value of the participants’ services received in exchange for the grant of the options
is recognised as an expense in Administrative Expenses in the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other
Comprehensive Income. A credit is recognised directly in equity in the Share-based payment reserve.

As further explained in note 17, in April 2025, the Board approved the cancellation of the Previous Plan.
The Previous Plan was cancelled in November 2025 after formal confirmations had been issued to
members of the scheme.

The Company also had an arrangement with some of its non-executive directors, where those non-
executive directors could elect to receive a portion of their fee in the form of equity shares of the Parent
(‘the Non-Executive Directors’ Share Plan’).

In prior periods, the limitation of scope relating to share-based payment arrangements extended to both
the Non-Executive Directors’ Share Plan and the Previous Plan. During the current year, sufficient
appropriate audit evidence was obtained in respect of the Non-Executive Directors' Share Plan for current
and prior period balances. Accordingly, the limitation of scope pertains solely to the Previous Plan.

The Directors have estimated the valuation and based the accounting and disclosure of the Previous Plan
on the information they had available, because inadequate historical records have been maintained, as set
out in note 17. As a result of this, we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to
support the valuation, accounting and disclosure of the Previous Plan.

Our application of materiality

We apply the concept of materiality in planning and performing the audit, in evaluating the effect of identified
misstatements on the audit and in forming our audit opinion.

Materiality

The magnitude of an omission or misstatement that, individually or in the aggregate, could reasonably be
expected to influence the economic decisions of the users of the financial statements. Materiality provides a
basis for determining the nature and extent of our audit procedures.

We determined materiality for the Company to be £697k (2024:£664k), which is 3% of equity. We believe that
equity provides us with the appropriate basis of materiality as this aligns closely with the expectations of
primary users of the financial statements being its majority shareholder and the UK regulator to whom capital
adequacy and solvency of the Group is critical. As such we decided to use 3% of equity on the grounds that the
Company is a start-up entity that is in the early stages of executing its business plan, is still loss making and
operates within a highly regulated industry with increased focus on capital adequacy.

During the course of our audit, we reassessed initial materiality as our audit procedures identified a number of
audit differences impacting equity.

Performance materiality

The application of materiality at the individual account or balance level. It is set at an amount to reduce to an
appropriately low level the probability that the aggregate of uncorrected and undetected misstatements
exceeds materiality.

On the basis of our risk assessments, together with our assessment of the Company’s overall control
environment, our judgement was that performance materiality was 50% of our planning materiality, namely
£348k (2024: £332k) as the Company is a start-up and operates within a regulated industry. We have set
performance materiality at this percentage based on a number of considerations, including the number and
amount of identified misstatements, the effectiveness of the control environment and other factors affecting
the entity and its financial reporting.

Reporting threshold
An amount below which identified misstatements are considered as being clearly trivial,

We agreed with the Audit Committee that we would report to them all uncorrected audit differences in excess
of £35k (2024: £33k), which is set at 5% of planning materiality, as well as differences below that threshold
that, in our view, warranted reporting on qualitative grounds. —

1.3. Taxation

Income tax expense comprises current and deferred taxes. Current income taxes are calculated on the
basis of the applicable tax laws of the respective countries and are recognised as expense in the financial
year in which the related taxable income arises.

Liabilities related to current taxes are recognised in the balance sheet as current tax liabilities. Deferred tax
assets and deferred tax liabilities are taken into account using the balance sheet liability method for the
expected future tax consequences of all temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets
and liabilities for financial reporting purposes and the corresponding tax values.

Deferred tax assets are reviewed at each reporting date. In considering their recoverability, the Bank
assesses the likelihood of their being recovered within a reasonably foreseeable timeframe, taking into
account the future expected profit profile and any potential legislative restrictions on use within the
respective Group companies and the tax jurisdictions in which they operate, based on the Bank’s forecast
cashflows. Deferred tax assets arising from temporary differences and from tax loss carry-forwards eligible
for offset are recognised if it is considered probable (more likely than not) that sufficient taxable profits will
be available against which those differences or tax loss carry-forwards can be offset.

Deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities are calculated at tax rates expected to apply in the period in
which the tax assets are expected to be realised, or the tax liabilities settled.

1.4 Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment are tangible assets which the Bank holds for its own use or for rental to
others and which are expected to be used for more than one year. An item of property, plant and
equipment is recognised as an asset when it is probable that future economic benefits associated with the
item will flow to the Bank, and the cost of the item can be measured reliably.

Property, plant and equipment is initially measured at cost, which includes all of the expenditure which is
directly attributable to the acquisition or construction of the asset. Expenditure incurred subsequently for
major services, additions to or replacements of parts of property, plant and equipment are capitalised if it is
probable that future economic benefits associated with the expenditure will flow to the Bank and the cost
can be measured reliably. Day to day servicing costs are included in profit or loss in the year in which they
are incurred.

Depreciation of an asset commences when the asset is available for use as intended by management.
Depreciation is charged to the profit or loss account, so as to write off the asset's carrying amount over its
estimated useful life to its estimated residual value, using a method that best reflects the pattern in which
the asset's economic benefits are consumed by the Bank.

Classification
Receivables and other assets, excluding, when applicable, VAT and prepayments, are classified as
financial assets subsequently measured at amortised cost (note 9).

They have been classified in this manner because their contractual terms give rise, on specified dates
to cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest on the principal outstanding, and the

Recognition and measurement

Receivables and other assets are recognised when the Bank becomes a party to the contractual
provisions of the asset. They are measured at their transaction price where the receivables do not
contain a significant financing component. They are subsequently measured at amortised cost.

The amortised cost is the amount recognised on the receivable initially, minus principal repayments,
plus cumulative amortisation (interest) using the effective interest method (reflecting any difference
between the initial and the maturity amount).

The Bank applies IFRS 9's Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model using the General Approach to its financial
assets. ECL is calculated for each counterparty as the product of the probability of default (PD), the loss given
default (LGD) and the outstanding exposure. Each counterparty is assigned an internal credit rating based on
the riskiness of its business, its payment history and its level of engagement with the Bank. Customers
receive a score of 1 to 9 and this rating determines the PD (where a score of 3 is equivalent to a CCC rating
and 9 is equivalent to a BBB rating), which is derived from published S&P historical default rates, and an LGD
of 45% is applied based on the Basel framework’'s standard rate for unsecured exposures. BBB is considered
the default credit rating with lower scores reducing the credit rating.

This approach consists of three stages, as follows:

Stage 1 is applied to assets that have not had a significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition. For
stage 1, a 12-month ECL is recognised, using 12-month default rates (reflecting potential credit risk events
within 12 months of the reporting date).

Stage 2 is applied to assets that have had a significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition, but that
do not have objective evidence of impairment. The Bank considers a significant increase in credit risk to have
occurred when a balance is more than 30 days past due or where counterparty-specific information indicates
increased risk. For stage 2, a lifetime ECL is recognised, using cumulative default rates over the expected life
of the asset.

Stage 3 is applied when assets are more than 90 days past due or there is other objective evidence of credit
impairment at the reporting date. For stage 3, a lifetime ECL is recognised applying a PD of 100%.

The Bank defines a financial asset as in default when it is 90 days past due or when management

concludes the counterparty is unlikely to pay. The Bank writes off receivables against the related loss
provisions when it concludes there is no reasonable expectation of recovery.

Other assets, including intra-group receivables, are assessed for expected credit loss based on
management's assessment of the counterparty.

16 Leases

The Bank assesses whether a contract is, or contains a lease, at the inception of the contract.

A contract is, or contains, a lease if the contract conveys the right to control the use of an identified
asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.

In order to assess whether a contract is, or contains a lease, management determine whether the
asset under consideration is “identified", which means that the asset is either explicitly or implicitly
specified in the contract and that the supplier does not have a substantial right of substitution
throughout the period of use. Once management has concluded that the contract deals with an
identified asset, the right to control the use thereof is considered. To this end, control over the use of
an identified asset only exists when the Bank has the right to substantially all of the economic benefits
from the use of the asset as well as the right to direct the use of the asset.

Bank as lessee

date, for all lease agreements for which the Bank is a lessee, except for short-term leases of 12

payments as an administrative expense (note 6) on a straight-line basis over the term of the lease
unless another systematic basis is more representative of the time pattern in which economic benefits

Risk management

The Bank uses its Risk Management Framework (RMF) to apply governance and oversight across the Bank.
The RMF has been designed to provide a clear and consistent approach to the management of risks and
controls aligned to risk appetite. The RMF is underpinned by our risk culture and three lines model, aligned to
industry practice and UK Regulatory requirements.

Risk Management Framework

The Bank’s principal risks are laid out below and the order in which they are shown does not imply their
importance. Each risk is equally important in assessing the performance of the Bank in the context of
its overall risk appetite. The principal risks will be reviewed by the Board each year in line with the
Strategic and Annual Planning processes and monitored on an ongoing basis.

The Bank’s approach to identifying and managing risks continued to evolve throughout the period of
mobilisation.

Framework Structure

Our Risk Management Framework provides a structured approach to managing risks across financial,
economic, operational and behavioural domains. This framework is built on the three lines model principle,

Risk Management and Monitoring

Our risk management and monitoring balances our growth ambitions with risk management. We monitor both

Warning indicators which enable proactive risk management.

Risk monitoring occurs through regular reviews by the Executive Risk and Compliance Committee, with
quarterly oversight from the Board Risk Committee, facilitating scrutiny and debate of principal risks.

Fair value

Financial instruments measured at fair value subsequent to initial recognition are grouped into levels 1 to 3
based on the degree to which the fair value is observable:

e Level 1 fair value measurements are those derived from quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets
for identical assets or liabilities.

e Level 2 fair value measurements are those derived from inputs other than quoted prices included
within level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, either directly or indirectly.

« Level 3 fair value measurements are those derived from valuation techniques that include inputs for
the asset or liability that are not based on observable market data (unobservable inputs).

If one or more of the significant inputs are not based on observable market data, the instrument is included in
level 3. The fair values of the Bank’s financial instruments are considered to approximate their carrying values,
due to their short-term and floating rate nature.

these share options. The Group accounts for the Previous Plan as an equity-settled plan. The fair value of the
participant services received in exchange for the grant of the options is recognised as an expense. A credit is
recognised directly in equity against Share-Based Payment Reserve. The total amount to be expensed is
