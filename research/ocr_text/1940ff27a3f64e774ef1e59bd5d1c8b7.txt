The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2024.

Principal Activity
The principal activity of the Bank during the year has been the provision of buy to let mortgages alongside the
raising of retail deposits and servicing of the legacy finance provided for small to medium sized enterprises.

Business Review .

Following completion of the change in control process in 2023 the Bank has invested in both people and
infrastructure as part of its growth plans. This has seen a significant investment in technology to support the bank’s
retail deposit proposition. Additionally, the Bank has continued to invest in both people and technology to support
its buy to let offering with significant growth achieved during 2024. The Bank continues to have a strong capital
ratio with the closing year end position further enhanced by a £17.5 million capital inflow in January 2025. This
capital will support the Bank’s continued growth in line with its plan during 2025.

The investment in the Bank’s infrastructure during 2023 laid the foundations for growth in 2024 with continued
growth planned during 2025.

The Bank’s key financial performance indicators (KPIs) are profitability, liquidity and the Bank's capital to risk
weighted assets ratio. These KPls are shown below:

The loss in 2024 represents the combination of continuing investment in people and infrastructure alongside the
launch and scaling of product offerings. As the growth in the balance sheet progresses during 2025 losses are
expected to reduce in line with plan. The Bank has tested its capability and capacity for growth in 2025 through
delivering material growth across multiple product ranges in 2024.

The Bank has continued to take a conservative approach to its management of liquidity with a Liquidity Coverage
Ratio (LCR) in excess of requlatory requirements.

Interest receivable amounts to £4,436k (2023: £1,118k) and interest payable to £1,997k (2023: £213k). The
increased level of interest receivable in the year is driven by both higher customer receivables as well as the Bank's
liquidity adequacy buffer. Customer interest received has increased due to the new asset classes, with legacy
portfolios reducing due to the amortisation of the asset portfolio and the closure of this asset class to new business
in 2023. The increase in customer deposits in year has reflected increased liquidity needs given the increase in
lending, resulting in higher interest payable.

The management team and staff have been strengthened significantly during 2024 with experienced resources
hired across the business in readiness for continued growth during 2025.

Loans and advances

291 new agreements were originated (2023: 18) amounting to £88.4m (2023: £1.1m). Loans advanced during 2024
were buy to let mortgages. The quality of the Bank's loan book has remained high with very low levels of arrears or
loan losses occurring. ‘

Deposit accounts

Customer deposit accounts have increased from £9.3 million at 31 December 2023 to £106.9m at 31 December
2024. This mirrors the increase in new lending activity. The Bank has continued to hold levels of liquidity above
regulatory requirements.

2024 IT Development
The Bank has continued to invest in and grow its IT development capabilities during 2024 to further enhance its
digital products and services offerings.

Key areas of focus have been the building of proprietary software to support the acquisition and management of
the Bank’s savings customers launched in August 2024. In addition, there has been focus on the development of
the Bank’s Broker portal (due to go live Q1 2025), integrations with lending aggregators to support asset origination
and the migration of the Bank’s websites to a more effective and scalable platform.

Enhancements to the data platforms have taken place throughout 2024, with a new data warehouse being
implemented to support improvements in the collection of data across multiple systems, a more effective data
dictionary and wider reporting of management information across the Bank.

The Bank's core IT infrastructure services continue to be cloud based hosted on the Microsoft Azure platform
leveraging cost effective solutions, enhanced security capabilities, scalability and resilience to support continued
customer and operational growth.

Climate Change

The Bank recognises climate change as a critical issue that has the potential to alter economies, geopolitics, disrupt
business models and markets across all sectors. The Bank wants to play its role in supporting climate resilience
(including through our own operations and supply chain) and a responsible transition to a low carbon economy. A
Climate Risk Framework has been developed to support the climate risk management across the Bank. Climate
risk is considered within the Risk Management Framework and regarded as a cross cutting risk. It is also considered
within the Bank’s Stress Testing Framework.

The Bank will consider how to facilitate the necessary transition in the real economy through customer engagement
and offering products and services to support customers’ transition to a low carbon world.

Given the relatively small size of the Bank’s balance sheet together with its small physical footprint (both in terms
of office space occupied and number of staff) the Bank presently has minimal exposure to climate-related financial
risks. The Bank’s climate-based strategy is stil! evolving; this will involve an analysis of the qualitative and
quantitative climate risk of the balance sheet.

The Bank will continue to develop its climate change strategy during 2025 as it extends its product offering and
scale of lending.

The Bank’s investment into its deposit platform will help to remove an historic reliance on paper based processes
with the move to the use of online documentation significantly reducing the amount of paper and physical
documents which it generates. 2024 has seen a material amortisation of the legacy portfolio as customers are
offered new digital focused products.

Future Developments

The future strategy of the business will see a focus on secured lending activity across both buy to let and owner-
occupied residential mortgages. The Bank will continue to invest in technical infrastructure and the necessary talent
to grow the business. The Bank will continue to offer loans with due regard to its approved credit risk appetite.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties Management

Risk is inherent in every aspect of the Bank’s business and the Bank has a strong culture to ensure that the key
risks to which it is exposed are identified, assessed, controlled, monitored, reported upon and where appropriate
mitigated on a continual basis. The Bank manages risk using the ‘three lines of defence’ model with clear
responsibilities established for all staff in relation to risk management including executive and non-executive
responsibilities documented where applicable under the Senior Managers and Certification Regime.

Board and Executive Committee Structure

The Board has overall accountability and responsibility for the management of risk within the Bank and has
delegated responsibility for day to day risk oversight to the Board Risk Committee. Executive governance ensures
enterprise-wide management of risks and delivery of the Bank’s strategy is in line with the business plan. The Bank
monitors its overall risk profile closely via its governance structure to ensure that the Bank always remains within
Risk Appetite. The Bank’s overarching risk appetite statement defines its fundamental approach to the risks it is
willing to take in pursuing its business plan and drives the qualitative risk appetite statements which are set at
individual principal risk level. These include risks that the Bank actively pursues such as credit through lending and
those risks that occur because of business activities that need to be actively managed and mitigated as appropriate.

During 2024 further enhancements have been made to the Risk Management Framework which covers the key
risk categories and details the approach towards: identification, controls, reporting, appetite, governance etc., of
the risks the Bank faces.

The three lines of defence ‘model is used to support Risk Management along with a range of Board and
Management governance committees, including the Executive Risk Committee, Board Audit Committee and Asset

and Liability Committee.

Strategic and Execution Risk
The Bank maintains a sustainable business plan and delivers the strategic change that meets customer needs and
shareholder objectives.

Key elements to the Bank's strategy during the year focused on the development and launch of new products,
driving growth and moving the Bank to profitability on a strong operational and risk foundation. The Bank closely
monitors and reports its core change programme and product launches via established governance.

Capital Risk

The Bank has no appetite for breaching capital requirements. Its priority is to maintain a capital surplus above total
capital requirements sufficient to absorb any unexpected losses and costs without using regulatory buffers and
ensure that its capital base can support a growing and maturing book across the economic cycle, allowing for
potential downturns, maintaining a capital surplus buffer over minimum regulatory requirements.

Part of Birmingham Bank's capital management activity is the consideration of capital adequacy which is reported
to the Board at every meeting. This review includes an assessment of the Bank's capital for both current and future
expected impacts. The Capital Adequacy at 31 December 2024 was 76.03% (2023: 365%).

The Bank only has Common Equity Tier 1, which is the most robust form of capital and comprises ordinary share
capital, share premium and retained earnings.

Liquidity Risk

The Bank has processes in place to ensure it maintains liquidity resources which are adequate, in terms of both
amount and quality, to ensure there is no significant risk that its liabilities cannot be met as they fall due. This
includes funding risk to ensure that the Bank is always able to access sufficient funding of the stability, tenor and
quality required to meet the Bank’s projected business and cash flow requirements. The Bank will closely manage
its funding risk to sustain itself as a going concern and maintain a low appetite for liquidity risk. The Bank monitors
liquidity daily to ensure it has sufficient liquid assets to meet liabilities as they fall due ensuring that satisfactory
liquidity coverage and loan to deposit ratios are maintained.

Market Risk

The Bank has a limited appetite for Market Risk. Market risk is generally comprised of interest rate risk, currency
risk and other price risk. Birmingham Bank conducts its transactions in sterling and does not deal in derivatives or
commodity markets, except for one basic interest rate hedging contract as detailed in note 17. In the Bank’s case,
market risk is limited to Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book (IRRBB) which is monitored via Key Risk Indicators.
The Bank has a low appetite for maturity and basis mismatches between deposits and loans.

IRRBB is managed through the use of on balance sheet offsets as well as limited use of interest rate derivatives.
The Asset and Liability Committee (ALCO) monitor IRRBB through the impact of both parallel and non-parallel
interest rate shocks on economic value and net interest income.

Credit Risk

Birmingham Bank's primary exposure to credit risk arises through its loans and advances to customers, through its
market position as a specialist buy to let lender. The Bank seeks to lend to Buy to Let landlords who are lower risk
and the risks of the portfolio are monitored using a suite of credit risk appetite metrics, detailed MI to assess binary
and layered risks and to also assess underwriting standards. Stress testing is also employed to ensure that the
Bank maintains sufficient capital.

The Bank closely monitors nonperforming loans, with provisioning requirements and procedures.

Operational! Risk

The Bank looks to maintain a low threshold for unexpected losses, disruption or customer detriment caused by
operational risk events but acknowledges these will occasionally occur, particularly as the Bank delivers the planned
growth within the operating plan. The Bank will ensure that it maintains a strong control environment, supported by
appropriately skilled resources and effectively manage third party suppliers. Allocation of resources should be
proportionate to the Bank's risk profile, balancing cost and effectiveness, in support of the Bank’s business and
change activities. The Bank has a framework in place to ensure operational risks are captured, monitored and
mitigated, with lessons learned from mistakes.

The Bank operates a three lines of defence model with first line ownership of operational risks, review and challenge
by the second line and assurance from the third line. Focus is maintained on key risks, which includes outsourcing,
operational resilience, people, cyber and technology risks. Key risk indicators are in place to escalate issues to
senior management and the Board. Operational risk events are captured, recorded and reviewed ensuring sufficient
controls are in place to address the risks.

Operational Resilience

The Bank maintains a low appetite for operational resilience risk. The Bank seeks to ensure that systems and
operational capabilities are stable and resilient and it has sufficient preventative measures in place to reduce the
risk of a service failure as well as having the capabilities in terms of people, processes and organisational culture,
to adapt and recover if required. This includes having effective business continuity and disaster recovery plans in
place and ensuring rapid and effective communications with the people most affected by a business disruption,
especially its customer base.

Compliance and Conduct Risk

The Bank maintains zero appetite for material losses or breaches from non-compliance; failure to meet regulatory
requirements or delivery against regulatory commitments; and deliberate or negligent behaviours, decision making,
activities or processes which could cause unfair outcomes or harm to our customers and/or undermine market
integrity or competition. Whilst accepting the possibility that minor infrequent risk events may occur the Bank strives
to ensure that it always remains within the law and regulation. Processes are in place to ensure material breaches
would be investigated, identified and reported to the Risk Committee in a timely manner, rule changes are
implemented within the applicable regulatory timelines and staff operate within the Bank’s documented policies and
controls and, where applicable, industry guidelines.

The Bank has taken steps during the year to further develop and enhance compliance and conduct risk capabilities,
policies and procedures and to develop a proportionate Compliance and Conduct Risk Framework, which
incorporates the risk of the Bank, or its customers, being a victim of, or facilitating financial crime in line with the
evolving complexity of the business and plans for future growth.

The Bank is committed to maintaining a proactive relationship with the UK regulators and the Board continues to
ensure regulatory compliance and customer outcomes remain a key Bank focus area.

Corporate Governance

The Bank has a well-established corporate governance structure. The Board supports the principles of good
corporate governance as set out in the UK Corporate Governance Code. Whilst the Bank is exempt from the
provisions of the Code due to it not being a listed entity, and its overall size in terms of employee numbers, it has
reviewed the requirements and will ensure that its governance processes continue to follow best practice.

The Board believes its existing governance processes are appropriate for the current size and structure of the Bank.

Structure of Board and Board Committees

The Board has overall responsibility for the operations of the Bank. There are four independent non-executive
Directors (INEDs). The non-executives were complemented by two executive Directors during 2024: the Chief
Executive Officer and the Chief Financial Officer as well as the addition of a shareholder representative as a further
non-executive Director.

The Board is bound by its byelaws contained in its Memorandum & Articles of Association and has specific
committees appointed by it for the purposes of overseeing Nominations & Remuneration, Audit, and Risk. Each of
these committees has its own terms of reference.

To ensure independence, and reduce the potential for conflict of interest, the sub-committees are each comprised
entirely of INEDs, although individual executive Directors and others attend either as a matter of course or when
requested to provide advice and guidance. An INED chairs each of the Board Committees.

Primary Responsibilities of the Board

While the day-to-day operation of the Bank is delegated to the CEO and by extension to the other members of the
Executive. The Board, appointed by the shareholders to monitor and govern the Bank's operations, is legally
responsible for safeguarding the interests of depositors and shareholders. Although the Board does not manage
the Bank, one of its foremost duties is to recruit and retain suitable management. The Board appraises the Chief
Executive Officer and provides oversight and agreement to the appointment of other senior managers.

In conjunction with the Bank's Executive Committee, the Board is responsible for formulating broad priorities, goals
and strategies for the Bank. The formulation of clear objectives and policies supplies a framework for the Chief
Executive Officer to work within.

The Board both lays out the Bank's goals and monitors the progress against these. One of its main duties in this
capacity is to limit the Bank's exposure to excessive risk of all kinds, including legal, reputational and financial. By
managing risk judiciously, the Board seeks to maintain a balance between enterprise and caution. The Board also
ensures that the Bank complies with all applicable statutes and regulations. This is achieved by monitoring
compliance with regulatory and policy requirements via the Bank’s compliance and audit functions. The audit
operations are not limited to financial matters and encompass the Bank’s structure and operations. The Board is
also responsible for commissioning audits to provide independent assessment and assurance of the Bank.

Responsibilities and Requirements of Independent Non-Executive Directors (INEDs)

The essential role of the INEDs is to provide independent assurance to the Bank’s shareholder that the business is
being conducted in such a manner as to protect the interests of the Bank’s depositors and to comply with the
Principles for Business of the Regulator. This responsibility is discharged via oversight of, and appropriate challenge
to, the Bank’s senior management via the structure of the Bank’s Board and subcommittees. Part of the process
for selection is to ensure the INEDs are familiar with the regulatory principles and practices and to maintain their
knowledge of them.

Committees of the Board

Audit Committee

The Audit Committee’s main responsibility is to help the Board carry out its duties relating to accounting policies,
financial governance, the control framework and financial reporting functions. The Committee reviews the
effectiveness of the Bank's internal controls, approves the internal audit plan and examines internal and external
audit reports. The Committee receives and considers the recommendations of the internal and external audit
function and ensures, via management, that recommendations are implemented where necessary.

The Committee ensures the financial statements give a true and fair view and provide the reader with enough
information to assess the Bank’s performance. Any significant judgements in relation to financial reporting are
reviewed and challenged by the Committee. The Audit Committee appraises the performance of the internal audit
function. The Committee has assessed internal audit resources and is satisfied that these are appropriate to fulfil
their responsibilities. The Committee reviews the Bank’s external audit strategy including the appointment of the
external auditors and approval of the audit fees.

The Audit Committee comprises the independent non-executive Directors. The Audit Committee met six times in
the year. The Committee evaluates its performance and effectiveness annually.

Risk Committee

The Risk Committee’s key responsibilities are the provision of oversight and advice to the Board in relation to
current and potential future risk exposures of the Bank and future risk strategy, including review and challenge of
the Risk Management Framework, determination of risk appetite and to promote a risk awareness culture within
the Bank.

Risks are identified and monitored on an on-going basis and evaluated with appropriate action taken to manage
and control any new risk. The Board has reviewed relevant systems and processes and is satisfied that the Bank
has an appropriate system of internal controls. A monthly risk management framework for reporting is in place that
monitors risk levels to approved risk appetite levels, using status appraisal analysis to alert and appraise regarding
key risks. .

During the year the Board Risk Committee reviewed the Bank’s key risks, emerging risks and management's risk
contro! self-assessment process. In addition, the Risk Committee reviewed the Bank’s Recovery Plan, Internal
Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) and Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process (ILAAP).

The Risk Committee met twelve times in 2024.

Fair Value Measurement
All financial instruments are initially recognised at fair value on the date of initial recognition and, depending on the
classification of the asset or liability, may continue to be held at fair value either through the Statement of
Comprehensive Income or Other Comprehensive Income. The fair value of a financial instrument is the price that

at the measurement date.

When independent prices are not available or if the market for a financial instrument is not active, fair values are
determined by using valuation techniques. These may refer to observable market data, comparison with similar
instruments where market observable prices exist, discounted cash flow analysis, option pricing models and other
valuation techniques commonly used by market participants. However, some of the inputs to the techniques may
be based on unobservable data (e.g. in case of unlisted securities) if there is minimal or no current market data
available, in which case valuation adjustments are done to reflect uncertainties in fair values resulting from lack of
market inputs.

statement.

Impairment losses

Estimation and judgement is used when determining the loss given default and the probability of default in respect
of impairment of loan balances. When accounting standards require an impairment review is undertaken on
intangible assets using estimation and judgement to determine areas such as the value in use.

Effective interest rate (EIR)

Cost arising on the origination of loan assets are amortised over the expected life of the asset on an EIR basis. The
‘effective interest rate’ is the rate that exactly discounts the estimated future cash payments and receipts through
the expected life of the financial asset or liability to the carrying amount of the financial asset or financial liability.
When calculating the EIR the Bank estimates future cash flows considering all contractual terms of the financial
instrument. The calculation of EIR includes those transaction costs and any amounts received which are deemed
to be an integral part of the effective interest rate.

The EIR method of accounting for income recognition requires management to make a number of assumptions, in
particular, management must make a judgement around the estimation of the expected life of the loan asset. In
determining the expected life of the loan asset, management consider anticipated level of prepayments.

The majority of secured lending is originated on 2-or-5-year fixed rate mortgages with interest reverting to a variable
rate after the fixed term ends, and in most cases that variable rate is expressed as a margin over the Bank of
England base. The EIR made! does not predict future changes in base rates. As at the balance sheet date no data
is readily available to the Bank to support a reversion period, and therefore no reversion income is calculated within
the effective interest rate, the Bank’s mortgage book has been written in a higher interest rate environment,
supporting this outlook.

Treatment of IT expenditure
IT costs incurred in relation to software licenses are expensed as incurred rather than capitalized on the basis that
the software is not bespoke to the Bank, is cloud based and not hosted on owned servers.

Provision of a deferred tax asset
A deferred tax asset has not been recognised in the financial statements in respect of losses as management has
concluded that they are unable to accurately forecast when the Bank is considered likely to make profits which
could be utilised against the losses.
