Directors’ report

The Directors present their report and the Financial Statements for the year ended 31 December 2025.

Principal activities

The Bank’s principal activities continue to be Money Market transactions with the provision of short-term placements
to banks and other financial institutions. The Bank also provided to its customers Corporate Lending, Trade Finance
and Foreign Exchange operations. Commercial banking services related to payments were also available for the
Bank’s customers during the year.

Results

In 2025 the Bank achieved a profit on ordinary activities before tax of £346,952 (2024: profit £760,814).
The decrease in profit for 2025 can be explained by a decrease in the Bank of England Base Rate, the increase of
administrative expenses and the decrease in commercial lending transactions throughout 2025.

The Bank’s strategy is to continue to follow its principal activities in order to yield positive results.

All of the income relates to business instigated by the UK office. The Bank has not received any public subsidies
during the year.

Dividend

No dividend has been declared in respect of the retained profit for 2025. A dividend of £1,600,000 was paid in 2025
in relation to retained profits for prior years.

Capital

There was no change to the Bank’s capital during 2025.

Details of the financial instruments are provided in note 22.

Going concern assessment

The Directors have reviewed the Bank’s forecasts, projections and stress tests, taking into account reasonable possible
changes in trading performance, which show that the Company has sufficient financial resources. On the basis of this
review, and after making due enquiries, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Bank has adequate
resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future and for a period of at least 12 months from the
date of approval of the Financial Statements.

Accordingly, they continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the financial statements.

Future developments

The Bank continues to look to diversify its lending portfolio in terms of customer type, geographical location and the
types of goods/products that our customers trade in.

The Bank aims to continue with its strategy to increase its levels of commercial lending, subject to internal limits and
regulatory requirements.

The Bank continues to look for other products that will allow it to diversify its portfolio and increase profitability
without a considerable increment in its risk appetite. The continued levels of profit along with a stable customer base, °
lead the Board to believe that this business strategy remains to be the one the Bank should follow.

Consequently, the Bank intends to follow its existing policies and improve levels of diversification of business, as
follows:

To raise/maintain the Bank’s profit without utilising its balance sheet (e.g. FX transactions, Letters of Credit and
Payments), ensuring a quality service and low fixed costs.

To on-board new customers, especially those based in the UK or other countries outside the EEA, with a particular
emphasis on those looking to invest in Cuba.

To look for new medium-term funding for the Bank.

To increase Trade Finance operations and where possible to decrease the tenor of these operations in order to reduce
credit risk and increase related income.

To preserve Money Market operations.
To increase its levels of commercial lending.

The Bank is always looking to develop new business relationships within the banking community and with any
intermediaries who can support the management of the Bank in the fulfilment of this strategy.

Compliance with all regulations issued by the regulatory authorities both in the UK and Cuba (for the Bank’s
Representative Office) is essential. By being aware of the steps taken by international market participants, as well as
new rules issued, the Bank will be prepared to implement any new measures required.

One of the key objectives of the Bank is to increase its operational resilience, ensuring the continuity of its business
and improving its level of security. There are several IT projects planned to be completed during 2026, including
cybersecurity and IT infrastructure to reduce the Bank’s operational risk and enhance controls associated with these
processes.

The Bank’s Business Continuity Plan (BCP) is under regular review, in order to maintain business as usual for our
customers.

It is the Bank’s intention to help strengthen its operations by fortifying its non-financial resources including taking on
new staff which will help to lessen key staff dependency.

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk that a loss may occur from the failure of another party to perform according to the terms of a
contract.

Credit risk principally arises from lending activities, but can also arise from other on and off-balance sheet activities.
The Bank endeavours to minimise its credit risk exposure in a number of ways: careful consideration of the initial
granting of credit; performing regular, ongoing appraisals of counterparty credit quality; netting of foreign exchange
activities where possible; and prompt review at senior level of bank account reconciliations, to ensure carly
identification of possible settlement risk.

Management of credit risk
The day-to-day oversight of the management of credit risk has been delegated to the General Management Body. This
body is responsible for:

e Formulating and reviewing credit policies including credit assessment, risk grading and reporting along with ensuring
that all relevant procedures are fully documented.
Formulation of risk appetite and appetite risk limits.
Reviewing and assessing new transactions to be recommended for approval.

e Reviewing that all exposures remain compliant with all exposure limits.

Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank encounters difficulty in realising assets or otherwise raising funds to meet

commitments associated with liabilitics or financial obligations.

It is the current practice of the Bank to match client monies placed with asset instruments of a similar tenor. The Bank
measures and manages its cash flow on a daily basis.

Management of liquidity risk

The day-to-day oversight of the management of liquidity risk has been delegated to the General Management Body.
This body is responsible for:

« Formulation of liquidity policies and ensuring that all relevant procedures are fully documented.

* Ensuring that the Bank maintains adequate funding.

« Ensuring that the Bank carries sufficient High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) and Stable Deposits to meet its regulatory

Market risk

Market risk is the risk that the value of a financial instrument will fluctuate because of changes in market rates. Market
risk comprises foreign exchange risk, concentration risk and interest rate risk.

Management of market risk

The Bank takes a very conservative stance in respect of market risk. It does not speculate in exchange rates, preferring
to avoid the risk of exposure by matching its foreign exchange activities. Foreign exchange risk is managed by means of
currency exposure limits placed on intraday and overnight positions, these are monitored by the General Management
Body.

Concentration risk

Concentration risk occurs when a number of counterparties engage in similar activities or operate in the same
geographical region or industry sector.

Management of concentration risk

The majority of the Bank’s exposure is to the UK. Due to its unique relationship, the Bank also has an exposure to
Cuba, however this is to the banking sector where the risk is considered to be low and is mainly offset by cash held as
credit risk mitigation.

Financial crime risk

The main component of financial crime risk faced by the Bank is the risk that the Bank’s products and services may be
used to facilitate financial crime against the Bank, its customers and third parties.

Management of financial crime risk

The Bank has adopted a risk-based approach to the management of financial crime and has the following controls and
procedures in place to help mitigate these risks:

A clearly defined financial crime prevention policy, which is regularly reviewed to ensure that it is fit for purpose, this
is approved by the Board.

Ongoing development, maintenance and reporting of risk appetite measures on financial crime and fraud risk to the
General Management and the Audit and Risk Committee.

Regular assessment of the evolving requirements, to ensure that the Bank meets the requirements of the UK Money
Laundering Regulations.

Regular oversight and review of systems, controls and higher risk customers and transactions.

Provision of training to all relevant staff.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss arising from system failure, human error, fraud and external events. When controls
fail to perform, operational risks can cause damage to reputation, have legal or regulatory implications, or lead to
financial loss. The Bank cannot expect to eliminate all operational risks, but through a control framework and by
monitoring and responding to potential risks, the Bank is able to manage and mitigate the risks. Controls include
effective segregation of duties, access, authorisation and reconciliation procedures, staff education and assessment
processes, including the use of internal audit.

Management of operational risk

In managing its operational risk, the Bank aims to avoid financial loss and damage to the Banks reputation with the
following:

Policies require appropriate segregation of duties including a 4/6 eyes authorisation rule.

Policies regarding reconciliation and monitoring of transaction.

Compliance with regulatory and other legal requirements.

Documented controls and procedures.

Regular assessment of operational risks faced to ensure that controls and procedures are in place to identify and address
the risks.

Reporting requirements regarding operational losses and proposed remedial actions.

Training and professional development.

Regular assessment and review of IT and cyber risks.

De-risking/third party risk
De-risking is where financial institutions or other third parties take a commercial decision that the business of an

account holder carries too high a risk and decides to ask that account holder to close their account(s) with that
institution.

Management of de-risking/third party risk

The Bank continues to search for additional correspondent banking relationships and potential Electronic Money
Institution relationships, which could assist the Bank to meet its payment obligations (incoming and outgoing
payments). In respect to the risk of losing other third parties, the Bank aims to identify potential suppliers in advance to

Financial risk arising from climate

Due to the Banks current business model, business opportunities associated with the climate-related risks and the scope
of support we can offer to our customers are very limited.

The credit risk associated to a direct, physical risk event aligned to climate change is remote. Fixed assets of the Bank
are relatively small in value. In addition, there is a very low probability that some of the Bank’s clients may be subject
to disruption of transportation links as a result of severe weather events, which might delay the transportation of goods.
This could, under extreme circumstances result in delayed settlement of exposures or even more unlikely, possible
credit losses in relation to the Bank’s corporate lending portfolio.

The Bank carries out a risk assessment on an annual basis of our active customers against climate related incidents by
country.

The Bank has assessed the potential impact of climate change risk on its business performing stress tests to quantify the
potential impact. Given the nature of the Bank’s business and that the majority of the exposure is short term, the impact
has been assessed as low.

The Bank formally assesses on an annual basis whether there is any objective evidence that the assets are impaired

22. Financial instruments

The company’s financial instruments comprise cash and balances at central banks, loans and advances to banks, loans
and advances to customers, deposit by banks, customer accounts and the balances duc to parent undertaking.

Risk management

The Bank is very conservative in its approach to risk taking and seeks to engage only in activities with limited risk
exposure. Risks are identified and documented through a risk register system and monitored on a regular basis, this is
then updated to include any new risks as soon as they are identified. This forms part of the Banks Risk Appetite
Statement.

The main risks arising from the Bank’s financial instruments are opcrational risk, liquidity risk, credit risk, market risk
and concentration risk. The General Management of the Bank is charged, by the board, with the responsibility for
reviewing and agrecing policies and procedures for managing cach of these risks and these are summarised below.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss arising from system failure, human error, fraud and external events. When controls
fail to perform, operational risks can cause damage to reputation, have legal or regulatory implications, or lead to
financial loss. The Bank cannot expect to eliminate all operational risks, but through a control framework and by
monitoring and responding to potential risks, the Bank is able to manage the risks. Controls include effective
segregation of duties, access, authorisation and reconciliation procedures, staff education and assessment processes,
including the use of internal audit.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that an entity encounters difficulty in realising assets or otherwise raising funds to meet
commitments associated with liabilities or financial obligations.

It is the current practice of the Bank to match client monies placed with asset instruments of a similar tenor. The Bank

measures and manages its cash flow on a daily basis. As of 31 December 2025, the Bank complies with requirements in
respect of Liquidity Coverage Ratio and Net Stable Funding Ratio laid down by the Prudential Regulation Authority.

Credit risk and credit quality

Credit risk is the risk that a loss may occur from the failure of another party to perform according to the terms of a
contract.

Credit risk principally arises from lending activities, but can also arise from other on and off-balance sheet activities.
The Bank endeavours to minimise its credit risk exposure in a number of ways: careful consideration of the initial
granting of credit; performing regular, ongoing appraisals of counterparty credit quality; netting of foreign exchange
activities where possible; and prompt review at senior level of bank account reconciliations, to ensure early
identification of possible settlement risk.

The Bank derives its credit quality for Banks and other financial institutions from its internal ratings, using its risk
criteria to guide its assessment of credit risk and of potential impairment. There are no facilities with Financial
Institutions in default or impaired in 2025 (2024: nil).

The Bank derives its credit quality for loans and advances to customers from its internal ratings, using its risk criteria to

Interest rate risk

All of the Bank’s lending is at fixed rates. In terms of maturity, value and currency, where possible the loan portfolio is
matched 100%. Where this is not possible the Bank has sufficient capital to cover this. Money market deposits are
placed at the best rates available, however with market wide low yields available on money market funds, the Bank will
sometimes need to place funds with a zero yield. The Bank mitigates this by ensuring that there is sufficient margin in
other transactions while endeavouring to provide competitive rates for its customers.

25. Post balance sheet events

An Executive Order 14380 was issued by the President of the United States of America on 29 January 2026 titled
“Addressing Threats to the United States by the Government of Cuba”. The aim of this order was to blockade the
supply of oil to Cuba by threatening additional tariffs on all exports from other countries to the US in the event that they
attempt to supply oil to Cuba.

On | May 2026, President Trump signed Executive Order 14404 ‘Imposing Sanctions on Those Responsible for
Repression in Cuba and for Threats to United States National Security and Foreign Policy” which marks an
unprecedented expansion of US sanctions on Cuba, when previously, they were almost entirely limited to Cuba as a
country and Cuban persons. The Executive Order steps up the pressure by targeting certain sectors of the Cuban
economy; energy, defence and related material, metals and mining, financial services and security; and the officials of
these entities. It seeks to further isolate the Cuban economy allowing the US to sanction foreign persons engaging in a
variety of economic activities involving the country, similar in scope to those imposed upon Iran and Russia. Foreign
financial institutions and foreign companies engaging in transactions involving Cuba are now potentially at risk of
being sanctioned and exclusion from the lucrative US markets and the US Dollar.

The impact that both executive orders will have on the Cuban economy is expected to impact potential business for the
Bank. In spite of this we are confident that the Bank will be able to reach its forecasted levels.
