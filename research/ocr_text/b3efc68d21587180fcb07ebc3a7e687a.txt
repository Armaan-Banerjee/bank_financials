Principal Activity

Mizuho International ple (“the Company”) is the London based securities underwriting,
broking and trading company within the Mizuho Financial Group, headed by Mizuho
Financial Group, Inc. The Company’s activities include both customer and proprietary
business in debt and equity securities, alternative assets, repurchase activities, money
market loans and deposits, and the provision of custody services In order to meet customer
demand and maintain access to market liquidity the Company arranges and manages issues
of secunties and carries portfolios of securities, together with derivative instruments for
hedging and trading purposes The Company's proprietary activities involve taking positions
aS principal in interest rate, credit and equity products and in alternative assets The
techniques used by the Company to manage the risks inherent in its business are described
In note 30 to these financial statements

The Company has deposit-taking and other permissions relating to its investment and
custody businesses pursuant to the provisions of the Financial Services and Markets Act
2000 and the rules of the Financial Services Authority

The Company has a branch office in Zurich, Switzerland

The Company's activities include transactions undertaken with certain special purpose
entities, which fall to be treated as subsidiary entities as described in note 33 The
consolidation of these entities has had no significant impact on the results for the year, but
has resulted in some reclassifications and expansions of profit and loss and balance sheet
categories

Results for 2008

The performance of the Company and its subsidiaries (“the Group”) has been adversely
affected by the severe disruption of both the US securitisation and wider credit markets
during the year, and the Group has suffered a loss of £2,040 7m for the year ended 31°
March 2008 The Group’s result for the year was chiefly attributable to the performance of
our structured credit businesses, the main contributor being a trading loss of £1,936 7m
recorded by our US Structured Credit business. This result has created significant unutilised
tax losses within the Company, and no deferred tax asset has been recognised due to the
likely timing and level of future profits as described in note 9

US Structured Credit business

During 2007 the Company expanded its Structured Credit business in North America to
engage in the origination, structuring and distribution of asset backed collateralised debt
obligation ("CDO") products. In undertaking this business the Company acquired US asset
backed securities, principally mortgage related, with a view to their subsequent disposal
through securitisation

The market for US mortgage backed secunties and CDO products collapsed during the
second quarter of the financial year The emergence of prospective levels of default on US
mortgage lending which were significantly in excess of previous forecasts, resulted in the
rapid withdrawal of liquidity from these markets and a significant and progressive decline in
market valuations By this time, the Company had executed a small number of securitisation
transactions but had acquired significant further exposures to US mortgage backed
securities

The collapse of market demand prevented the Company from disposing of these assets
through securitisation and resulted in significant revaluation losses throughout the remainder
of the financial year, consistent with the experiences of other market participants These
losses include an additional loss of £267m, which resulted from the inability of a monoline
insurer to fulfil its obligations in respect of credit protection purchased by the Company

In response to the changed market conditions, the Company decided to cease securitisation
business within the US, and during the second half of the financial year has focused on the
management and reduction of exposures acquired through this business Significant
emphasis has been placed upon the valuation of these positions, and market data derived
from the Company’s disposal of some of its positions, has been incorporated within this
process As a result of this valuation work, which has been complicated by the lack of
liquidity and price transparency in these markets, significant write downs have been taken
against these positions Revaluation of residual positions contributed £2,035 1 million of the
total net trading loss of £1,936 7 million recorded for this business, as noted above.

The book value of residual exposures to US asset backed securities is now significantly
reduced from the level held during the first half of the financial year, and sensitivity to
valuation assumptions in respect of these assets Is also much reduced Details of residual
exposures and sensitivity to valuation assumptions are given in note 29 to these financial
statements

Financial position

The scale of losses and withdrawal of liquidity within the US asset backed securities markets
was significantly greater than had been anticipated by the Group's risk management
framework, and the resulting losses borne by the Group necessitated additional capital
contributions from Mizuho Securities Co , Ltd (“Mizuho Securities”) Additional equity share
capital of £2,057 0 million has been received by the Company during the year (further details
are given in note 28 to the accounts)

As a consequence of the ongoing support and commitment provided by Mizuho Securities,
the Group’s financial position remains sound The Group's total equity after retained losses
1s £512 5 million as at 31 March 2008, which compares to total equity of £516 6 million as
at 31 March 2007 As a rasult of the capital contributions received fromm Mizuho Securities,
the Group also reported a strong regulatory capital surplus at the end of the financial year

Other business activities

No new lines of business were commenced by the Group during the year Following the
emergence of significant losses within the US Structured Credit business, the Company
conducted a review of its operations with two principal objectives

~ To rationalise business activities with a view to reducing risk and enhancing
control across all areas of the Group’s business; and

- To restructure operations to an organisational design and size more appropnate
to the ongoing activities of the Group

A revised business plan was developed and implemented based upon this review, with input
from our shareholder The implementation of this plan resulted in reduced activity across
the Group's principal other business lines, in particular within credit and interest rate
structured products, changes tn senior management to reflect the new structure, and the
implementation of a restructuring plan in January 2008 which included the departure of
permanent and contract staff across front and back office areas

Future Prospects

We expect the adverse impact of the so-called “credit crunch” to continue for some time
This introduces a significant degree of uncertainty to the global markets in which the Group
operates Based on our view of the markets and the relatively low carrying values of our
residual positions as noted above, we do not anticipate that any potential future losses will
occur on a similar magnitude to those reported in the current year The Group has continued
to reduce its exposures to US asset backed securities during the 2009 financial year

Our management projections and stress tests based on our residual exposures indicate that
we are sufficiently capitalised to bear further adverse developments within the structured
credit markets

Qur focus for 2009 will be to develop our client facing businesses, particularly in advisory
and fee based areas This will be supported by disciplined proprietary trading activity where
this is complementary to the core client facing business We will continue to encourage
Innovation where this does not bring risk in excess of carefully determined limits Any
activities will be closely coordinated with other Mizuho Financial Group companies and we
expect to build ever closer links where this ts appropnate and permitted by regulation It is
expected that further benefits will accrue once Mizuho Securities completes its merger with
Shinko Securities Co , Ltd, a fellow member of the Mizuho Financial Group

We expect to see some growth in demand for our capital markets products in certain new
markets, particularly in Asia, and we will look to become more globally aligned with Mizuho
Secunties We remain confident that our diversified product offering will enable us to
continue to expand and prosper in these markets in a controlled manner

We will further invest in our infrastructure, in line with business expansion as we remain
focused on strong corporate governance and controls Key aspects of this will include
embedding the learning from the first year of SOx compliance and also responding to the
requirements of the Japanese equivalent of SOx, with which the Mizuho Financial Group will
be required to comply during the 2009 financial year

We will also ensure that the Company prepares for comphance with all relevant new
regulatory requirements including the remaining applicable components of Basel [| The
Board and Management will continue to work together to ensure that we meet these
objectives

As a global financial services business, the Group 1s affected by the factors driving the

operating result may, and are likely to, continue to vary from period to period

limits The Risk Committee meets on a weekly basis It is chaired by the Chief Risk
Officer The Head of Credit Risk, the Head of Market Risk, the Head of Methodology
and Model Validation and representatives of the front and back office also attend the
Risk Committee meetings

relevant regulatory agencies

Accounting Principles, and in accordance with the provisions of the Companies Act 1985
relating to Banking Companies

The consolidated financial statements have been prepared in accordance with applicable
Accounting Standards.

Accounting policies

The accounting policies that are significant in the context of the Company's consolidated
financial statements are described in note 2 The accounting policies adopted by the
Company in the preparation of the financial statements are consistent with those in the
previous year

Basis of measurement

The consolidated financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost
convention, as modified for derivative financial instruments, financial assets and liabilities at
fair value through profit and loss and available for sale assets, all of which are measured at
fair value

Functional and presentation currency

The consolidated financial statements are presented in Sterling which ts the Company's
functional currency Except as indicated, the financial information is presented in Sterling
and has been rounded to the nearest £100,000

Basis of consolidation

The consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company
and its subsidiaries as at and for the year ended 31" March The financial statements of the
subsidiaries that the Company controls are prepared for the same reporting year as the
Company using consistent accounting policies

Subsidiaries, including special purpose companies, are consolidated from the date that
control is transferred to the Company until the date that control ceases

The Company controls a subsidiary, including special purpose companies, when it has the
ability to direct the financial and operating policies of the subsidiary with a view to gaining
economic benefits from its activities and the power to exercise dominant influence over the
subsidiary

Subsidiaries, including special purpose companies, which fall under the control of the
Company are consolidated under the acquisition method of accounting. Under acquisition
accounting, the identifiable assets and liabilities of the entities acquired are included in the
consolidated balance sheet at their fair value at the date of acquisition The cost of
acquisition is measured at the fair value of the consideration given at the date of acquisition.

The excess of the fair value of the subsidiary’s net identifiable assets acquired over the fair
value of the purchase consideration Is recorded as goodwill The excess of the fair value of
the consideration given over the subsidiary’s net identifiable assets acquired over the fair
value of the purchase consideration is recognised immediately in the profit and loss account

2 ACCOUNTING POLICIES

A -— Financial instruments

Trade date accounting 's applied for the purposes of recognising and de-recognising
financial instruments

Recognition

Financial instruments are recognised when the Company becomes party to the contractual
provisions of the relevant financial asset or financial liability

Financial instruments are initially recorded at fair value, being the amount at which an asset
or liability would be exchanged or settled between knowledgeable, willing parties in an arms
length transaction, and in the case of financial assets or liabilittes not subsequently
measured at fair value through profit and loss plus any incremental directly attributable costs
of acquisition or issue, assuming that transactions do not take place under a forced sale or
Iquidation scenario, and that the Company ts a going concern

De-recognition of financial assets
A financial asset is de-recognised when

The contractual rights to receive the cash flows from the asset expire; or

Either the Company has transferred substantially all the nsks and rewards of the financial
asset or the Company has neither transferred nor retained substantially all the risks and
rewards of the financial asset but has transferred control of the financial asset

Where the Company has neither transferred nor retained substantially all of the risks and
rewards of ownership, but contro! has not been transferred, the Company will account for its
continuing involvement in the asset Under this approach, the Company will continue to
recognise the transferred asset to the extent of the continuing involvement in that asset and
recognise an associated liability such that the net carrying value of the transferred asset and
associated liability 1s equal to the fair value of the retained nights and obligations

De-recognition of financial liabilities

Financial liabilities are de-recognised when they have been extinguished as a result of the
cancellation, discharge or expiry of the obligations specified in the contract

Offsetting

Financial assets and liabilities are set off and the net amount presented on the balance
sheet when and only when the Company has a legal right to set off the amounts and intends
either fo settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously

Income and expenses are presented on a net basis only when permitted by the accounting
standards or for gains and losses arising from a group of similar transactions such as the
Company's trading activities

B - Trading financial instruments

Trading securities

Non derivative financial instruments are classified as held for trading if they are acquired for
the purpose of selling in the near term or are part of a portfolio of identified financial
instruments that are managed together and for which there is evidence of a recent actual
pattern of short term profit taking Trading financial instruments include reverse repurchase
and repurchase agreements, trading debt and equity securities and purchased life contracts

Subsequent to initial recognition, trading securities are measured at fair value Gains or
losses realised on de-recognition of trading securities and changes in fair value on trading
securities are recognised in the profit and loss account and reported under net trading
income

Purchased life contracts

A purchased life contract provides the Company with a recetvable due from an insurance
contract, which becomes payable upon the death of the insured party under the life contract
These individuals have no connection with the Company, and accordingly the purchased life
contracts fall to be treated as financial instruments. Purchased life contracts are recorded at
trade date, and are measured at fair value using industry standard approaches for pricing
such transactions These approaches utilise mortality rates based upon general mortality
tables, together with specific adjustments to those rates based upon individual medical
assessment and market observed interest rates Reserves are maintained to ensure that the
modelled valuation of these contracts Is calibrated to the actual transaction price upon trade
date

C — Derivative financial instruments
Trading derivative instruments

Subsequent fo initial recognition, trading derivative instruments are measured at fair value
and disclosed as financtal assets when the fair value is positive and financial liabilities when
the fair value is negative Realised and unrealised gains or losses arising from trading
derivative instruments are recognised in the profit and loss account and reported under net
trading income.

Derivative financial instrument held for risk management purposes

Derivative financial instruments held for risk management purposes comprise swaps and
other derivative financial instruments held to manage the Company's risks

These derivative financial instruments are subsequently measured at fair value and
disclosed as financial assets when the fair value is positive and financial lhabilities when the
fair value is negative Realised and unrealised gains or losses arising from derivative
financial instruments held for risk management purposes are recognised in the profit and
loss account and reported as net trading income

Embedded derivatives

Derivatives embedded within financial instruments that are not accounted for as fair value
financial instruments are separated from their host contracts and accounted for as
derivatives if the economic characteristics of the embedded derivative are not closely related
to those of the host contract and if a separate instrument with the terms of the embedded
derivative meets the definition of a derivative. Embedded derivatives are separated from
their host contracts and are measured at fair value with changes in fair value recognised in
the profit and loss account and reported under net trading losses / income.

Hedge accounting is applied where a designated fair value hedging relationship exists
between a derivative hedging instrument (see accounting policy C - derivative financial

hedge of a financial hability represents a hedge against changes to the fair value of a liability

relationship qualifies for hedge accounting if the following conditions are met

e At inception, formal documentation is prepared to include

e The hedge is assessed for effectiveness on an ongoing basis

The gain or loss from re-measuring the hedging instrument at fair value is recognised in the
profit and loss account The gain or loss on the hedged financial liability attributable to the
hedged risk adjusts the carrying value of the hedged item and Is recognised in the profit and
loss account.

The Company prospectively discontinues hedge accounting for a hedged relationship if

e The hedging instrument expires or ts sold, terminated or exercised,
« The hedge no longer meets the criteria for hedge accounting,
« The Company revokes the designation

Fair values are derived from quoted market prices or valuation techniques as described
below

Active market

Financial instruments that trade within an active market are valued using quoted market
prices or rates available within that market An active market exists where regular prices are
provided by exchanges, dealers, brokers, industry groups or pricing services, and these
prices represent actual market transactions

Financial instruments may be priced from the most advantageous active market to which the
Company has access, and this may result in the recognition of revenue on trade date (‘day
one profit’), to the extent that a fair value instrument has been traded at a favourable price in
comparison to prices available within an active market

Long positions are marked to the bid side, and short positions to the offered side of an active
market Where the Company holds positions which create offsetting nsk positions, then
those offsetting positions are valued at mid market rates, with any resulting net risk positions
being marked to the bid or offered side as appropriate

No active market

Financial instruments that do not trade within an active market are valued using a valuation
technique, which may consist of

« Analysis of current or recent third party transactions in the same instrument, or

« Reference to the value of other instruments, that are substantially the same as the
instrument to be valued, or

« Use of a valuation model

Valuation techniques, including valuation models, meet the following conditions

Consistency with accepted methodologies for pricing financial instruments,

Inclusion of all relevant factors that market participants would consider in setting a price;
Use of market observable date where possible, and

Periodic calibration to actual market transactions where possible

es @e@ 8 @

If, on trade date, a valuation technique results in a different estimate of fair value to the
actual transaction price, then the valuation technique will be cahbrated to the transaction
price, so that no gain or loss is recognised except as described below

Gains or losses will only be recognised subsequently, to the extent that they arse from
changes in a factor (including time) that market participants would consider in setting a price

Far value estimates from valuation techniques may be used on trade date without
calibration to the transaction price, resulting in the recognition of day one gains and losses,
under the following conditions

e Fair value is supported by current market transactions in the same instrument, or
e Fair value is based upon a valuation technique whose variables include only data from
observable markets

Day one gains or losses that do not meet these criteria are deferred and amortised over the
life of the financial instrument to which they relate

M ~ Identification and measurement of impairment

At each balance sheet date, the Company assesses whether there 1s objective evidence that
financial and other assets not carried at fair value through profit and loss are impaired

Financial assets are impaired when objective evidence demonstrates that a loss event has
occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, and that the loss event has an
impact on the future cash flows on the financial asset that can be estimated reliably

Impairment losses on available for sale debt securities are recognised by transferring the
difference between the arnortised acquisition cost and the current fair value out of equity to
profit and loss. When a subsequent event causes the amount of impairment loss on an
available for sale debt secunty to decrease, the impairment loss ts reversed through profit
and loss.

Impairment losses on financial assets carned at amortised cost are measured as the
difference between the carrying amount of the financial assets and the present value of
future cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate Losses are
recognised in the profit and loss account and reflected in an allowance account against
loans and advances Interest on impaired assets continues to be recognised through the
unwinding of the discount When a subsequent event causes the amount of impairment loss
to decrease, the impairment loss is reversed through profit and loss

Other assets are recorded at no more than their recoverable amount Impairment losses are
measured as the difference between the carrying value and the recoverable amount and
recognised through profit and loss

Revenue Is recognised to the extent that tt is probable that the economic benefits will flow to

interest income and expense

Interest income and expense are recognised in profit and loss using the effective interest
rate method The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts the estimated
future cash payments and receipts through the expected life of a financial asset or financial
habthty, or a shorter period where appropriate, to the carrying amount of the financial asset
or financial liability The effective interest rate is established on initial recognition of the
financial asset or financial liability and is not subsequently revised The calculation of the
effective interest rate includes all the fees and points paid or received, transaction costs, and
discounts or premiums that are an integral part of the effective interest rate Transaction
costs are incremental costs that are directly attributable to the acquisition, issue or disposal
of a financial asset or financial liability

Fair value assessment of trading in purchased life contracts uses a valuation model for
which mortality is an input. Mortality is based upon actuarial valuation basic tables (‘VBT’)
which are industry wide published material but do not meet the definition of observable
market data The Company performs periodic calibration of the pricing models and the
mortality curves to prices observed in open trading within the market to obtain comfort that
the valuation models for this business are reflective of traded levels within the market

US Structured Credit transactions

Debt securittes and denvative financial instruments that are held in relation to the Group’s
US Structured Credit business are valued using a variety of different pricing inputs, valuation
assumptions and models, as market liquidity and price transparency for these asset classes
have declined significantly dunng the financial period

Positions held in residential mortgage securities or in credit derivatives that reference such
securites have been valued using a valuation technique This technique utilises evidence
from recent transaction prices in comparable securities, bid prices from market sources, data
from third party price providers, together with valuations derived from a pricing model This
pricing model is reliant on a number of assumptions, which include ultimate delinquency
rates, loss severity and market required discount rates The results derived from these
different data sources are applied in a hierarchical manner, such that greater emphasis 1s
placed on those sources of market data that provide best evidence in relation to the
particular security to be priced

Positions held in CDO securities or in credit derivatives that reference such securities have
also been valued using a valuation technique that utilises evidence from a number of
sources Where relevant, CDO transactions originated by the Group have been priced using
a valuation model, which utilises market data obtained in respect of the underlying reference
pool of those transactions Other CDO transactions have been valued using a valuation
technique that utilises bid prices from market sources and data from third party price
providers which may either relate directly to the CDO being priced, or alternatively may
relate to comparable CDO securities The results derived from these different data sources
have again been applied tn a hierarchical manner, such that greater emphasis Is placed on
those sources of market data that provide best evidence in relation to the particular CDO
security to be priced

Financial investments

B(ii) Trading activity ~ Value at Risk (‘VaR’)

The Company manages the market nsk in its trading portfolios through position and
sensitivity limits, as well as Value-at-Risk (‘VaR’) limits These limits are approved by the
Risk Committee

VaR 1s a technique that produces an estimate of the potential negative change in the market
value of a portfolio over a specified time horizon at a given confidence level

The Company's VaR assumes a time horizon of one day and a confidence level of 99 per
cent

In other words, the Company expects to suffer a one-day loss greater than its VaR only one
day in one hundred The Company uses several VaR models across different segments of
its business
* Full non-linear VaR model which employs Monte-Carlo simulations derived from a
variance/covaniance matnx is used for interest rate, spread, non JPY equity index
and volatility risks,
« JPY equity nsks are calculated using historical simulation models that are based
upon returns derived frorn historical data,
¢ For its Commodity and Foreign Exchange (‘FX’) VaR, the Company uses a linear
variance/covaniance model, and for its equity specific risk it uses a single factor
model

All VaR models based upon Monte-Carlo simulation assume that changes in the risk
parameters have a normal distribution and the parameters of this distribution (1e standard
deviations and correlations) are obtained from Mizuho Financial Group, who calculate them
using exponentially weighted historical data Hence the method s limited by the relevance of
the historical data used

A combination of historical simulation VaR models are used to calculate JPY equity VaR
Linear VaR ts calculated using the Barra model whitch incorporates a single factor CAPM
model and covariances calculated from historical returns Non linear VaR (which
incorporates non finear equity price returns and volatility VAR) 1s calculated using scenario
returns derived directly from historical data The models are limited by the relevance of
market data and relevance of historical observed correlations between different factors for
future market movements

estimated using historical market data The mode! estimates the relationship between

a stock’s residual risk is uncorrelated with the market and with all other stocks The method
's, again, limited by the relevance of the historical data used, and also by the correctness of

The Company’s VaR should be interpreted in light of the limitations of the methodologies
used, which include

¢ Most risk factors are covered, but specifically changes in correlations are excluded This
nsk factor is covered by position and sensitivity limits

* Assumptions made about the nsk factors that apply to specific instruments, or the
relationships between them, may lead to an over- or under-statement of the VaR In
particular, the Company's assumption of perfect correlation between bond spread
movements in the same rating category overstates the correlation between bonds of the
different issuers with that rating

« Changes in risk factors may not be normally distributed In particular, the normal
distribution assumption may underestimate the probability of large market moves

* Historical data may not be a good estimate of the potential changes to risk factors
observed in the future and any modifications to that data may not be adequate.

* VaR based on 99 per cent confidence level does not reflect the potential losses beyond
that percentile

* The VaR presented represents the risk of all portfolios at the close of business Positions
may change substantially during the course of the trading day. All limits (including VaR
limits) and controls apply at ail times

« Zero correlation is explicitly assumed in combining the Monte-Carlo VaR with the VaR
from equity specific risk

VaR should not be viewed as a guarantee of the maximum loss that can be expenenced by
the Company on its portfolio. Nor can the Company be certain that losses will not exceed the
VaR more frequently than once in one hundred trading days

F Operational Risk

Operational risk Is the risk of potential operational losses which anse as a result of the
Company’s business, and include losses attributable to human error, systems failure, fraud
and legal exposure The Company mitigates such risks through the maintenance of a
comprehensive system of internal controls, which incorporate the strict segregation of duties
between front and back office functions The Executive Committee monitors the continued
effectiveness of the control environment by comparison to agreed key performance
indicators Control procedures are reviewed on a regular basis by an independent internal
audit function in addition, the Company maintains and tests contingency facilities to support
operations in the event of disasters

G Legal Risk

Legal risk is the risk that the business activities of the Company have unintended or
unexpected legal consequences and includes risk arising from inadequate documentation,
legal or regulatory incapacity, insufficient authority of a counterparty and uncertainty as to
the validity or enforceability of a contract. The Company identifies and manages legal risk
through effective use of its legal services department, which is independent of the front
office, and external legal advisors
