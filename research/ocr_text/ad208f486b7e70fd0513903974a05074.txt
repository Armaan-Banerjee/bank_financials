Strategic Report

Chief Executive Officer’s Statement

In my statement accompanying the 2022 financial statements, I reported that Melli Bank had managed to end
6 years of losses to return to profitability. I am very pleased to report that in 2023 we have made good progress
in sustaining the improvement in performance and establishing a profit-making trend. This was achieved
despite experiencing a drop of around 10% in the total revenue from €9,221,000 in 2022 to €8,289,000 as a
result of the scarcity of high-margin trade finance transactions. This scarcity meant that projected levels of
business could not be realised in the intended periods, however out efforts have laid the gtoundwork to
strengthen our ability to improve performance on a consistent basis. The pressure on overheads due to the
consumer price inflation started easing in 2023. The average Consumer Price Inflation experienced in the UK.
2023 was around 7.4% against 9.1% in 2022. The staff costs and other administrative expenses benefited from
this trend, with the staff costs decreasing by 3% to €5,443,000 from €5,600,000 in 2022. The non-staff costs
decreased by 21% to €2,052,000 from €2,602,000 in 2022. These cost savings compensated the drop in the
total revenue, with the Bank ending the financial year with a net profit of €630,000, a modest improvement
over the net profit in 2022 of €559,000.

Hand in hand with the business focus, I continued in 2023 to pay particular attention to strengthening the
Bank’s governance and operational resilience through changes at senior executive management level,
including changes in their reporting lines, and putting in place new committees with appropriately focused
terms of reference. The Executive Committee’s membership was expanded to enhance performance and
accountability on all important matters. These actions will continue in 2024 with a thorough update of the
Bank’s succession atrangements. There have been changes in the composition of the Board of Directors,
partly occasioned by the new policy of our parent, Bank Melli Iran, to increase the proportion of non-
executive directors. The Bank will be looking to add new independent non-executive directors to the Board.

I like to put on record my gratitude to the CEO and Deputy CEO for International Affairs of Bank Melli
Iran for the much-valued cooperation and support.

Lam of course indebted to the support of my Board of Directors, and the efforts of my colleagues at Melli
Bank who have continued to rise to the challenge of the more focused and active business strategy we have
developed, and embracing the governance and operational changes we ate implementing together. With the
achievements in 2022 and 2023 behind us and the momentum built, we look forward with confidence to an
improved profitability in 2024.

Business Review

The Bank has continued to organise its resources on the basis of this business model, but has put in place a

due dates.

a) Interest receivable and payable

Interest receivable and payable for all interest-bearing financial instruments measured at amortised cost are
recognised in the income statementusing the effective interest rate method. The effective interest rate is a method
of calculating the amoxtised cost of a financial asset or financial liability and of allocating the interest receivable
or payable over the relevant period using estimated future cash flows.

The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash receipts and payments through
the expected life of a financial instrument ot, where appropriate, a shorter period, to the net carrying amount of
the financial asset or financial liability. When calculating the effective interest rate, the calculation includes all
amounts paid or received by the Bank that are an integral part of the effective interest rate of a financial
instrument, including transaction costs and all other premiums or discounts.

When loans, securities or deposits are impaired, the Bank reduces the carrying amount to its recoverable amount,
being the estimated future cash flow discounted at the original effective interest rate of the instrument.

The Bank’s market risk is not considered to be significant. It does not run a trading book and the only market
tisks it might be exposed to are foreign exchange position risk and interest rate tisk in the banking book. It is

establishes the process for the management of the risks faced by the Bank.

‘The Bank is exposed to credit risk which arises from its commetcial lending, trade finance activities and also

associated credit risk. Credit is driven by principles outlined in the Credit Policy Statement.

Tran has been the Bank’s niche market where it has historically had a large concentration mainly as a result of

(including bank placements) have generally been offered to a select class of high-quality and reputable
customers known to the Bank. To assess risk, the Bank has carefully combined a judgmental approach, taking
account of management quality, industry, and operating environment, with a critical analysis approach
addressing liquidity, leverage, profitability, debt-setvice coverage, and volatility among other factors.

Concentration Risk

Concentration risk arises when a number of the Bank’s counterparties are engaged in similar activities or similar
geographic locations so that they have economic characteristics that would cause their ability to meet their
obligations to be similarly affected by changes in economic or other circumstances, Concentration risk can also
arise from a large exposure to a single counterpasty. Given the Bank’s niche market, a major area of sector
concentration is found in the Iranian banking sector whilst geographic concentration tisk also arises from the
Bank’s exposure to the economy of Iran.

The Bank’s experience of lending to Iranian state-owned banks and businesses is very good, with no losses
recorded from such business duting more than 15 years of Melli Bank’s operation. Furthermore, the Bank has not
recorded any losses from such business whilst operating as a branch of Bank Melli Iran, in total more than 40 years
of expetience without loss. The Iranian state banks have an established track record of meeting all obligations.

The Bank also lends to privately owned Iranian banks. Again, the Bank’s experience has been good with no
recorded losses but the history is shorter and the customers ate inherently less secure. However, given the
regulatory environment and the structure of the Iranian banking sector, the Bank considers that capital losses will
not arise.

The Bank also has certain single name bank concentration due to the restrictions on establishing correspondent
banking relationships.

Jquidity Risk
Liquidity risk is that the Bank, although solvent, does not have available sufficient financial resources to enable
it to meet its obligations as they fall due. The Bank has developed and implemented a liquidity risk management
strategy in accordance with the Bank’s risk tolerance. The Bank considers the:

- composition and maturity of assets and liabilities

- diversity and stability of funding sources

- approach to managing liquidity in different currencies, across borders, and across business lines and legal
entities

- approach to intraday liquidity management

- assumptions on the liquidity and marketability of assets

The strategy takes into account the liquidity needs under normal conditions as well as liquidity implications
under periods of liquidity stress, the nature of which may be institution-specific or market-wide or a
combination of the two.

The Bank sets internal limits, above regulatory requirement, for the Liquidity Coverage Ratio and the Liquidity
Maintenance Ratio. Additionally, the Bank has set an internal requirement to run 2 positive cumulative liquidity
mismatch in the 1 day to 12-month time band.

‘The Bank considers that it has adequate flexibility to address any periods of liquidity stress and the impact of
new business on liquidity is monitored closely.

‘The Assets and Liabilities Committee ensures that liquidity tisk remains within the Bank’s tisk appetite. ‘The
Board Risk Committee oversees and advises the Board on the current liquidity risk exposure.

Operational Risk
Operational risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems or
from external events.

The Bank's focus with regards to operational risk is on Operations, Compliance, Cyberctime and Governance
