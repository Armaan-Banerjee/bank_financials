Forbearance

Forbearance 1s defined as any concessionary arrangement that is made for a penod of three months or more where financial
difficulty is present or imminent Occastonally, some borrowers experience financial difficulties which impact their ability to meet
mortgage or SME finance obligations We seek to identify borrowers who are expenencing financial difficulties as well as contacting
borrowers whose loans have gone into arrears, consulting with them tn order to ascertain the reason for the difficulties, and to
establish the best course of action that can be taken to bring the account up to date In certain circumstances, where the borrower
Is expenencing significant financial distress, we may use forbearance measures to assist the borrower These are considered on a
case-by-case basis and must be in the best interests of the customer The forbearance measures are undertaken in order to
achieve the best outcome for both the customer and the Bank by dealing with financial difficulties and arrears at any early stage

The most widely used methods of forbearance are temporanly reduced monthly payments, loan term extension, deferral of
payment and a temporary or permanent transfer to interest only payments to reduce the borrower's financial pressures Where the
arrangement is temporary, borrowers are expected to resume normal payments within six months Both temporary and permanent
concessions are counted as forborne for 24 months following the end of the concession In all cases, the above definitions are
subject to no further concessions being made and the customers’ compliance with new terms

a) Risk grading methodology

The categorisation of high, medium, low risk ts based on internal grading models The grading models are used to generate a
consistent Bank-wide approach for the grading of customer credit risk exposures for all lending businesses, and provide a relative
internal ranking of risk Drivers for the grade mapping include external credit reference agency risk scores, property valuations and
qualitative factors The relative measure of risk reflects a combined assessment of the probabitity of default by the customer and an
assessment of the expected loss in the event of default

The resulting classification of balances between low, medium and high ts consequently driven by a combination of the probability of
default (‘PD’) and loss given default (‘LGD’) grades as further explained A matnx of fifteen PD and ten LGD grades determine the
category within which each loan is categorised |e those accounts that have a low PD and/or LGD are graded as ‘ow’ Those
graded ‘high’ will be accounts that have either a high PD and/or LGD

> Probability of Default refers to the probability of a customer or counterparty defaulting, which ts typically taken as three
payments past due, within the next 12 months A default probability model predicts this probability by using credit scores
along with financial, behavioural and qualitative inputs

> Key components of the Loss Given Default are the propensity to “cure”, that is for an account to be restored to a
performing status, and the level of security held tn relation to the credit exposure The level of security varies, ranging from
a small number of very short-term unsecured loans in the Asset Finance business, to highly secured loans on residential
property within the Residential Mortgage business The valuation method for assets ts specific to the nature of the
collateral and includes indexation for property valuations

b) Fair value of collateral methodology

For SME Commercial Mortgage agreements, Buy-to-Let and Residential Mortgage, the fair vatue of underlying collateral is
calculated based on the indexed valuation of the property on which the mortgage is secured Where the indexed valuation is
greater than the balance outstanding, the fair value of the collateral is capped to the value of the outstanding balance

For Asset Finance agreements, the estimated fair value of the collateral ts calculated by applying LGDs on a case-by-case basis
The LGD against each loan ts deducted from the balance outstanding to derive a proxy for fair value As the fair value is derived
using LGDs, the fair value calculated includes an element of prudence as the LGD ts based on non-performing loan data

Key considerations in the calculation of the disclosed fair values for those financial assets and habilities carried at amortised cost
include the following

(a) Cash and balances at central banks

These represent amounts with an initial maturity of less than three months and as such their carrying value ts considered a
reasonable approximation of their fair value

(b) Loans and advances to banks

These represent either amounts with an initial matunty of less than three months or longer term variable rate deposits placed with
banks, where adjustments to fair value in respect of the credit nsk of the counterparty are not considered necessary Accordingly
the carrying value of the assets is considered to be not materially different from their fair value

(c) Debt secunties heid to matunty and Amounts due to Aldermore Group undertakings

The fair value of the amounts payable to the Group's securitisation vehicle and the unrated debt securities in the vehicle (the
unrated notes) which the Bank holds as part of the securitisation structure have been estimated with the objective of arnving at fair
values that reflect the prices at the reporting date that would have been determined in an orderly transaction between market
participants under current market conditions

An internal methodology has been applied to determine the estimated fair values using a combination of observable market data
and internal models The most significant inputs are market price data for the externally issued notes of the securitisation vetucle,
market interest rates and an adjustment to the credit spread to reflect the additional credit msk attached to the unrated notes and
estimates of the likely cash flaws to be received on those notes The value of the amounts owed to the securitisation vehicle are
derived from the value of the external and unrated notes of the securitisation vehicle

If reasonable alternative assumptions are applied to those significant inputs which are not observable by reference to external market
data, the fair value of the unrated notes would be in the range from £34 5 million to £32 0 milan (31 December 2014 £33 4 million to
£30 5 million), and the amounts owed to Aldermore Group undertakings would be in the range from £213 6 million to £211 1 million as
at 31 December 2015 (31 December 2014 £303 4 million to £300 5 million)

(da) Other assets and habilties

These represent short term receivables and payables and as such thei carrying value is not considered to be matenally different
from their fair value

{e) Loans and advances to customers

For fixed rate lending products the Bank has estimated the fair value of the fixed rate interest cash flows by discounting those cash
flows by the current appropriate market reference rate used for pricing equivalent products plus the credit spread attnbutable to the
borrower For standard variable rate lending products, and fixed rate products when they revert to the Bank's standard vanable
tate, the interest rate on such products is considered equivalent to a current market product rate and as such the Bank considers.
the discounted future cash flows of these mortgages to be equal to their carrying value The fair value estimations do not
Incorporate adjustments for changes in future credit risk, since loans were granted, however, incurred loss provisions are deducted
from the fair value amounts
