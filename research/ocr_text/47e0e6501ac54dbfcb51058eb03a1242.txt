Trade Finance Activities

Trade finance continues to represent an important pillar of the Bank’s business model. During the year the Bank supported a
range of documentary trade activities, including the advising of letters of credit and related trade services, together with bill
discounting.

Fee income from trade finance activities increased significantly during the year, supported in part by the ceopening of Sri Lanka’s

While Sa Lanka remains an important market for the Bank’s trade finance activities, the Bank also supported selected trade
transactions with counterparties in other Asian markets as part of efforts to broaden and diversify trade finance relationships.

Trade finance activities are managed carefully within the Bank’s risk management framework, taking into account evolving
global trade conditions and applying appropriate credit assessment and monitoring processes.

01 RISK MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK

The Bank maintains a comprehensive risk management framework designed to identify, assess and manage the risks arising from its
business activities. The framework supports the Bank’s strategy while ensuring that risks ace managed within the risk appetite approved
by the Board.

The Bank’s activities expose it to a range of financial and non-financial risks, including credit risk, liquidity risk, market risk, operational
risk and regulatory compliance risk. These risks ace managed through established policies, procedures and internal control systems
designed to ensure prudent risk management and effective oversight.

02 RISK GOVERNANCE

The Board has overall responsibility for the Bank's risk management framework and for determining the Bank's tisk appetite.

The Board Audit and Compliance Committce supports the Board in overseeing the integrity of the Bank's financial reporting, the
effectiveness of its internal control and audit arrangements, and compliance with regulatory and legal requirements. The Committee
oversees the work of the internal and external auditors and monitors the Bank's compliance with applicable laws and regulations.

The Board Risk Committee supports the Board in oversecing the Bank's risk profile and monitoring the effectiveness of the Bank's
risk management and internal control systems. The Committee reviews key tisk indicators, monitors risk exposures against approved
limits and considers emerging risks that may affect the Bank's activities.

Senior management are responsible for the day to day management of risks in accordance with policies and limits approved by the
Board. Risk management responsibilities are embedded across business functions to ensure that risks are identified, assessed and
managed appropriately.

The Bank also maintains supporting governance structures including management committees such as the Asset and Liability
Committee (ALCO), which oversees specific areas including liquidity management and balance sheet risks.

the interests of the Company’s employees
the need to foster the Company’s business relationships with suppliers, customers and other stakeholders

the impact of the Company’s operations on the community and the environment

the desirability of maintaining a ceputation for high standards, of business conduct

Going Concern

The Directors have assessed the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern for a period of not less than twelve months from the
date of approval of these financial statements. The assessment has taken into account the Bank's financial position, capital and liquidity
resources, business plan projections, principal risks and uncertainties and the results of stress testing conducted as part of the Bank's
Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process and Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process.

At 31 December 2025 the Bank maintained capital and liquidity ratios significantly above regulatory minimum requirements and
delivered its thied consecutive profitable year, with profit before tax of £368,000. Total assets stood at £195 million.

The Directors have reviewed the Bank's financial projections for the period through to April 2027. The projections indicate that
capital and liquidity ratios are expected to remain above regulatory minima and the Bank's internal risk appetite thresholds throughout
the assessment period, without any assumption of capital support from the parent. Stress testing conducted under adverse
macroeconomic and idiosyncratic scenarios confirmed that regulatory requirements would be maintained throughout the assessment
period, with management actions available to the Board in the event of material deterioration.

The Bank is a wholly owned subsidiary of Bank of Ceylon, Sri Lanka's largest bank with assets exceeding USD 12 billion. The parent
bank has confirmed its ongoing support for the Bank and, if required, has the financial capacity to provide additional capital or liquidity

11 EXPECTED CREDIT LOSS PROVISIONING

The Bank recognises expected credit loss allowances for financial assets measured at amortised cost in accordance with IFRS 9.
Financial assets subject to the expected credit loss model comprise loans and advances to customers, loans and advances to banks,
cash and cash equivalents, and investments in debt securities,

The expected credit loss model uses a three-stage approach based on changes in credit risk since initial recognition.

Stage 1 - Exposures where there has not been a significant increase in credit risk since initial cecognition. A twelve-month expected
credit loss allowance is recognised.

Stage 2 - Exposures where there has been a significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition, but the exposure is not credit
impaired, Lifetime expected credit losses are recognised.

Stage 3 - Exposures which are credit impaired at the reporting date. Lifetime expected credit losses are recognised.

The Bank determines whether a significant increase in credit tisk has occurred using a combination of quantitative and qualitative
indicators including deterioration in internal credit grade relative to origination, adverse changes in credit score, arrears exceeding

thirty days past due applied as a regulatory backstop, and qualitative indicators such as evidence of financial stress, refinancing
difficulties or covenant breaches.

Credit impaired exposures include those that are ninety days past due or where the Bank considers the borrower unlikely to meet its
obligations in full without recourse to collateral.

Exposures may migrate from Stage 2 back to Stage 1 where credit risk has demonstrably improved and is no longer considered
significantly higher than at initial recognition, subject to a sustained period of satisfactory repayment performance of at least six
months and the absence of adverse qualitative indicators. Equivalent criteria apply for reversal from Stage 3.

ECL Methodology - Inputs, Assumptions and Estimation Techniques
Definition of Default

The Bank considers a financial asset to be in default when one or more of the following conditions are met: the borrower is more
than 90 days past due on any material credit obligation; or the Bank considers the borrower unlikely to meet its credit obligations in
full without recourse to collateral, regardless of the existence of any past due amount. Unlikely to pay indicators include evidence that
the borrower is experiencing significant financial difficulty, the granting of a concession that would not otherwise be considered, the
probability that the borrower will enter bankruptcy or financial reorganisation, and the disappearance of an active market for a financial
asset due to financial difficulties. The definition of default is applied consistently across all portfolios and is consistent with the
definition used for internal credit risk management purposes.

Probability of Default (PD)

Each exposure is assigned an internal credit grade at origination reflecting the borrower's financial strength, repayment capacity and
collateral quality. Grades are reviewed at least annually and more frequently where adverse credit indicators are identified. Base PDs
ace assigned to each internal credit grade by reference to external benchmark default rates consistent with that level of credit risk. PDs
represent the estimated likelihood of default over the relevant horizon, twelve months for Stage 1 exposures and the remaining lifetime
for Stage 2 and Stage 3 exposures. Base PDs are adjusted to reflect current point in time conditions and forward-looking
macroeconomic expectations through the application of scenario specific multipliers as described in the forward-looking information
section above. for Stage 3 exposures assessed individually, PD is set at 100%. PDs are reviewed annually by management and subject
to ongoing validation against observed portfolio performance.

Loss Given Default (LGD)

For property secured exposures, LGD is derived from a collateral recovery model applied on an exposure-by-exposure basis. The
model estimates the net present value of expected recovery proceeds following enforcement, taking into account scenario specific
assumptions for property values, disposal costs and recovery timelines under each of the three macroeconomic scenarios. For well-
collateralised exposures, LGD 1s at or near zero under the base and upside scenarios as expected collateral proceeds cover the
outstanding balance after costs, and the significant majority of probability weighted ECL on property secured exposures therefore
arises from the downside scenario. For treasury and interbank exposures where property collateral is not held, a conservative flat rate
LGD is applied as a prudent assumption in the absence of the Bank's own default and recovery experience on such exposures.

Exposure at Default (EAD)

EAD represents the gross carrying amount of each financial asset at the reporting date, comprising principal balance and accrued
interest.

Collective and Individual Assessment

ECL is calculated ona collective basis for Stage 1 and Stage 2 exposures, grouping financial assets with shared credit risk characteristics.

The single Stage 3 exposure at 31 December 2025 is assessed on an individual basis, with PD and LGD detecmined by reference to
the specific circumstances and collateral position of that exposure.

under each scenario and combined using the assigned probability weights to derive the final allowance. ‘The use of multiple scenarios
reflects the requirement under IFRS 9 that expected credit losses represent a probability weighted estimate that considers a range of
possible outcomes rather than a single most likely outcome.
