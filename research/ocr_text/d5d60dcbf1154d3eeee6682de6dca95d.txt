The Directors are pleased to present the strategic report for Persia International Bank PLC (PIB
or the Bank) for the year ended 31 March 2025.

Introduction

Persia International Bank PLC (“PIB” or “the Bank”), an authorised institution under the
Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, primarily offers commercial banking services to its
customers. Despite the lifting of financial sanctions in 2016, the US's unilateral withdrawal
from the JCPOA in November 2018 and the subsequent re-imposition of OFAC sanctions have
hindered the full resumption of normal banking activities. The current geopolitical situation in
the Middle East and the ongoing conflict between Ukraine and Russia have further added to
the uncertainties

Business Review

Historically, the Bank has provided comprehensive trade finance and credit facilities to its
customers. Trade finance involved receiving and advising letters of credit, adding
confirmations, and providing short-term credit facilities by discounting bills drawn under them.
The Bank also extended credit facilities to corporate and institutional customers, with Iranian
corporate customers forminy a significant portion of its customer base.

In July 2021, the Bank obtained a license from the Central Bank of Italy for its Milan branch
as a third-country bank (TCB) outside the EU. The branch is operational and has implemented
its core banking systems. However, it faces challenges in establishing correspondent banking
relationships due to ongoing US OFAC sanctions.

The Bank's total assets have decreased due to a financial institution drawing on its current
account and an increased ECL provision under IFRS 9. Trade finance, once a crucial part of
the Bank's business, has seen no opportunities recently. The resumption of trade finance
activities is contingent upon resolving correspondent banking relationship issues.

The outlook for 2025-26 remains challenging due to increased economic and geopolitical
uncertainties, ongoing regulatory changes, and difficulties in securing suitable correspondent
banking relationships.

The Bank has enacted a strategic survival plan as it currently faces considerable challenges
stemming from geopolitical uncertainties and a wide range of operational difficulties. In
response to these, the Bank is proactively implementing measures to ensure the uninterrupted
continuity of its business operations. While no further uncertainties are expected at this
juncture, maintaining vigilance and readiness for potential changes remains imperative. The
Bank will continue to strike a balance between growing its balance sheet and prudently
managing the associated risks. The Bank has askilled and motivated team and solid shareholder
support to navigate these uncertainties

The Bank considers the interest income and administrative expenses detailed below to be the
key performance indicators.

Financial Performance during the year 2024-25 and key performance indicators

The Bank's financial performance aligns with the expectations set at the beginning of the year.
It incurred a loss of €550k before the IFRS 9 ECL provision for the year.

The Bank's income primarily comes from interest eamed on money-market placements,
corporate and syndicated loans. The interest income for the year ended 31 March 2025 is
€7,044k, 12% lower than the previous year. The net Fee & Commission income is €61k. The
operating revenue for the year is €6,433k.

The total administrative expenses for the financial year are €6,643k. The Bank has been closely
monitoring its expenses to keep them low by increasing efficiency. The depreciation cost for
the year is €340k. Costs in all categories are lower than the budget but slightly higher than the
previous year due to the rise in the cost of living. The Bank reported an operational loss of
€550k before IFRS 9 expected credit loss provisions. The IFRS 9 expected credit loss (ECL)
provision for the year is €16,719k, an increase due to additional provision on past due accounts.
The loss for the year after IFRS 9 expected credit loss provision is €17,269k.

The total assets of the Bank as of 31 March 2025 were €155,626k. The largest asset class is
loans and advances to banks, including money market placements of €48, 138k.

Risk Assessment and Management

The Bank is currently exposed to a range of nsks, including liquidity risk, credit risk,
operational risk, market risk, interest rate risk, and currency risk. Additionally, the Directors
have identified several other significant risks in the current environment, such as the lack of
GBP and other currencies clearing facilities, regulatory compliance challenges, IT
concentration due to reliance on a limited number of suppliers, succession planning, and
geopolitical risks. The Bank has proactively identified and managed its key risks through a
comprehensive governance framework and robust risk management practices.

Risk Mitigation Measures

The Board and management are confident that they have implemented adequate measures to
mitigate the primary risks identified. The Bank’s overall liquidity position is strong, with major
liabilities settled and the remaining liabilities well-managed. The maturity mismatch between
the Statement of Financial Position assets and liabilities is not considered a significant concern,
as a substantial portion of its short-term funds are held with reputable central banks, including
Banca D’ Italia (the central bank of Italy) and Bank Markazi (the central bank of Iran), as well
as other banks in the EU and Iran.

Liquidity and Financial Stability

The Bank has ensured access to sufficient liquid funds to meet its obligations and operating
costs for the foreseeable future. As a result, the Directors are satisfied that the Bank will remain
a going concern and maintain confidence in its longer-term prospects. The Board’s oversight
and the management’s execution of risk mitigation strategies have positioned the Bank to
navigate the current environment with confidence, ensuring its ongoing stability and long-term
SUCCESS.

Responsibility for the risk management process

The Bank has established a comprehensive framework to manage financial reporting risks
through control activities performed at all levels of the organisation. These activities
encompass guidelines and instructions, approvals, authorisations, verifications, reconciliations,
analytical reviews, and segregation of duties. To further enhance risk oversight, the Board has
established a Risk Committee, comprising two Independent Non-executive Directors & chaired
by an Independent Non-executive Director. This committee is responsible for monitoring,
reviewing, and updating all risks faced by the Bank, both internally and externally.

Internal Controls and Financial Reporting

In terms of internal controls and financial reporting, the Bank systematically obtains both
external and intemal information. Management is regularly provided with relevant information
on the Bank’s activities to ensure timely, current, and accessible data for financial reporting
purposes. This information is disseminated to appropriate functionaries, including the Board,
senior management group, and relevant Board committees.

Board’s Role in Internal Control and Risk Management

The Board holds overall responsibility for the proper arrangement of internal control and risk
management over financial reporting. To support this, the Board has appointed an Audit
Committee, comprising two Independent Non-executive Directors, to undertake more specific
tasks related to the financial reporting process, controls, and monitoring the efficiency of the
Bank's internal control and risk management systems. The Audit Committee regularly reviews
the main features of these systems to ensure their effectiveness.

Monitoring and Reporting of Internal Controls

To ensure the effectiveness of intemal controls pertaining to financial reporting, monitoring

risk management practices.

The Bank has implemented a strong risk management and financial reporting framework,

Risk weighting of assets

The UK and EU have excluded Iran from the list of countries deemed to have equivalent
supervisory and regulatory requirements. As a result, exposure to Iranian entities continues to
be risk-weighted at 150%. This regulatory stance has implications for the Iranian exposures
directly affects the Bank’s risk profile and capital requirements, affect profitability, and limit
business expansion. Given that the Bank’s primary business dealings are with [ranian entities,
this heightened risk weighting will:

1. Increase Capital Requirements: The higher risk weighting necessitates a greater

amount of capital to be held against Iranian exposures, thereby reducing the Bank’s
capacity to extend credit or engage in other business activities.

2. Affect Profitability: The increased capital requirements will likely reduce the Bank’s
return on equity and overall profitability, as more capital is tied up in higher-risk-
weighted assets.

3. Limit Business Expansion: The regulatory measures may constrain the Bank’s ability
to expand its business activities or increase its exposure to Iranian entities, given the
higher capital demands.

To determine whether the credit risk on a financial instrument has increased significantly since

recognition date. This assessment considers both qualitative and quantitative information that

available without undue cost or effort. The Bank's historical experience and expert credit
assessment, including forward-looking information, are key components of this evaluation.

For corporate lending, forward-looking information includes future prospects, including
upside, downside, and base case scenarios of the industries in which the Bank's counterparties
operate. This information is obtained from expert economic reports, financial analysts,
govemmental bodies, relevant think-tanks, and other similar organisations, as well as various
internal and external sources of actual and forecast economic information.
