Developments in Monetary Policy

For the banking community 1973 was a year of contrasts. The velatite nature of the world’s
economic and financial situation, and Britain‘s role within it, necese1.ated con:sdetable adjustments
in Government policy which, as so often before, were reftected in developments in the domestic
financial scene.

Throughout the greater part of the year the Government was committed to an attempt to consolidate
and increase the rate of economic expansion achieved during the second half of 1972. Industry
responded to domestic demand but quickly ran into problems of scarce resources. Interest rates
which had risen sharply at the end of 1972 were maintained during the first quarter, but then fell
away steadily in the following three months, The balance of trade had meanwhile been
deteriorating and increases in world commodity prices were soon reflected in inflationary trends
at home.

Less optimistic views on the health of the U.K. economy became more common in the early summer
and the pound which had floated up against the U.S. dollar, but down against most other currencies
during the first few months of the year, came under severe pressure. The Authorities stepped in
to reverse the trend of interest rates and made a call for Special Deposits. During the autumn, with
a view to restraining money supply expansion, the banks were asked to be more selective in ‘heir
lending activity.

By the final quarter inflationary pressures at home were leading to severe industrial problems and,
even before the influence had been felt of increased prices for Middle East oil supplies, the Balance
of Payments position was deteriorating rapidly. Interest rates, including Bank Base Rates, were
again raised by Government action, to levels over one-haif as high again as the position six months
previously, and a further Special Deposits call —to 6% in all — was made, although two of the
four 4% call instalments were cancelled later, The growing crisis situation necessitated a package
of economic measures in Decemks. and further emphasis was placed on the need for a restraint
on jending to personal customers and to picnerty companies.

Group Activities

Under such rapidly changing financial and economic conditions, | have found ix particularly
heartening to observe the way in which the Banking Group as a whole has adapted to the difficult
circumstances.

The spirit of loyalty and dynamism which characterises the Co-operative Movement in the 70's
already provides the Banking Group with a great development potential. | feel that the applicetion
of Co-operative principles to banking and finance has considerable further appeal.

With this in mind the Bank took a major step during the year by entering into negotiations with a
view to obtaining membership of the Bankers Clearing House. The successful conclusion to these
discussions was made public some weeks ago, and the enormous amount of work involved ir.
preparing for this historic development is already under way.

Wide-ranging expansion plans have been announced to broaden the Bank’s representation in all
appropriate centres of population, while further investigations are taking place into methods of
providing the best possible customer service. During the year negotiations were completed to
acquire premises in a number of towns and cities, and 1974 will see even more activity in this
area. The Bank's branch in Bristol has been relocated in modern premises in the commercial
centre of the City, and Derby branch is being extensively returbished and modernised. Possibly
the most important property acquired for our future expansion was in Cornhill, London, E.C.3,
an office which will in due course house the Bank's International Division, City Office, Money
Department and Co-operative Commercial Bank Limited.

With the transfer of the business of the Banking Department of the former Scottish Co-operative
Society to the Co-operative Bank Limited at 29th June, offices in Glasgow and Edinburgh were
added to our branch network, It is confidently expected that the Co-operative Bank will be as
successful in developing in Scotland as it has been in recent years south of the border.

During 1973 the Bank introduced free banking for personal customers who maintain their accounts
in credit, and in addition it extended its policy of paying interest on personal accounts to caver
all personal account credit balances. Reaction to these measures has been very gratifying and
the number of new accounts opened has already justified our action,

Lending to the personal sector was particularly buoyant during 1973, but the Bank also made
useful e..ansions in its lending te the corporate business sector.

Under the Local Government reorganisation the Bank has succeeded in acquiring the banking
business of many of the major authorities, thus consolidating its already important position in this
sector of the market.

With the entry of the United Kingdom into the Common Market, the Bank became a full member
of the Association of Co-operative Savings and Credit Institutions of the E.E.C. based in Brussels.
This move strengthens the Bank’s informal links with institutions of a similar nature in Europe and
provides an opportunity — which has been gratefully accepted —to make representations on
proposed lagislation by the European Commission.

Co-operative Commercial Bank Limited

‘This subsidiary bank, which represents the merchant banking wing of the Banking Group, has
continued to assist in the development of our representation in the City of London, and business
with other financial institutions in the “ square mile "is increasing.

(b) Investments .

(i) Income from investments includes amortization of premiums and discounts on dated stocks in equal

(c) Losses on advances

The amount charged against operating profit for josses on advances in Co-operative Bank Limited is based on
the average experience of the current and four preceding years. Part of this experience derives from the
banking department of C.W.S. up to the date of transfer to Co-operative Bank Limited [If at any balance
sheet date the provision is not adequate to meet anticipated losses on advances, or is excessive to a material
extent, the appropriate shortfall or excess is charged or credited to profit and loss account.

(d) Depreciation

Depreciation of land is provided in Co-operative Bank Limited at 14% and of buildings at 24% of the original
purchase price. Depreciation of freehold land and buildings in the other group companies is not provided for,
Depreciation of equipment and fittings is provided in equal annual instalments over the estimated working
lives of the assets. The cost and aggregate depreciation on those assets transferred from the C.W.S. at
10th July, 1971, are those relating to the original acquisition of the assets by the C.W.S.

(e) Instalment credit agreements

Credit is taken in the profit and Joss account for finance charges on instalment credit agreements, nat of
dealers’ commission, as instalments are received — for credit sale agreements on an actuarial basis and for
others in equal amounts for each instalment.

if) Deferred Taxation

in the accounting and taxation treatment of certain factors, mainly those referred to above. Provisic: is also
made for potential taxation on surpluses arising from revaluation of properties.

(g) Development land and work in progress

Development Jand and work in progress is valued at the lower of cost, including normal finance charges, and
