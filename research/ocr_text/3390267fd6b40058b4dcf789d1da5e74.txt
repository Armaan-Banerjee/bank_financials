Going Concern

Bank Saderat PLC is pleased that its parent company, Bank Saderat Iran, is continuing its intention
that the Bank will remain as a going concern and will maintain its place in the London market. The
directors are of the view that the US secondary sanctions are mitigated by the steps taken by the
Bank to maintain liquidity and capital adequacy during this time. The financial statements have been
prepared on a going concern basis. In assessing the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern,
the directors have considered the Bank's capital adequacy, liquidity position and financial forecasts
for a period of at least twelve months from the date of approval of the financial statements.
Nonetheless, the continuing uncertainty surrounding correspondent banking relationships and the
broader geopolitical environment represent a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on
the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern. This assessment has included a review of the
Bank’s Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP), Internal Liquidity Adequacy
Assessment Process (ILAAP), and five-year strategic plan.

The directors are satisfied that the Bank has adequate Tier 1 capital to meet its regulatory capital
requirements under the FCA/PRA Rules and that its existing asset base is expected to generate
sufficient income to meet operational expenses and funding obligations for the foreseeable future.

However, the Bank continues to face challenges in securing and maintaining suitable correspondent
banking relationships, primarily as a result of ongoing geopolitical conditions and the continued impact
of a second round of OFAC sanctions imposed on 5 November 2018. These challenges limit the Bank’s
ability to operate certain lines of business and may constrain future growth opportunities.

Despite these uncertainties, the directors have implemented mitigating actions, including diversifying
income sources, enhancing compliance procedures, and strengthening governance over liquidity and
risk management. Based on this, and after making appropriate enquiries, the directors consider that the
Bank will continue to meet its obligations as they fall due and remain in compliance with regulatory
capital and liquidity requirements. Accordingly, they continue to adopt the going concern basis of
accounting in preparing these financial statements.

Risk Management
Risk Management Framework (RMF)

The Risk Management Framework (‘RMF’) of the Bank is to ensure a sustainable business growth and
a proactive approach to manage the risks that arise in the course of doing business. RMF consists of
policies, processes systems and controls, , through which the identified risks are communicated,
monitored and managed.
The overall architecture of RMF is:

e Risk Appetite Statement (‘RAS’)

e Risk identification

e Risk assessment, measurement and mitigation

e Risk monitoring

e Stress testing

e Development of Risk Matrix and Risk Reporting

Bank's ‘Risk Appetite Statement’ describes the process of determining the risk appetite and defines the
type of risk exposure that the Bank can consider and quantifies the amount, which the Bank is prepared
to accept in pursuit of its business objectives. Where the risk management process identifies a risk that
is unacceptable to the Bank and it cannot be mitigated satisfactorily, the risk is avoided if possible. If a
risk cannot be avoided, as it is inherent in the operations of the Bank, the Bank allocates capital to cover
the risk

Responsibility for risk management resides across the Bank including the Board, the Risk Monitoring
Committee and the individual departments. The Bank’s policies and procedures are designed to prevent
risk taking which is beyond the Risk Appetite of the Bank.

Material Risk for the Bank
Credit and counterparty risk

Credit risk is the risk that companies, financial institutions and other counterparties will be unable to
meet their obligations in a timely manner to the Bank, which may result in financial loss. Credit risk
arises principally from the Bank's lending book and from holding investments. The Bank considers only
those counterparties for business transactions, who are credit worthy and have integrity in their
dealings.

Market Risk

Euro is the base currency of operation of the Bank. Market risk arises when open positions in other
currencies are maintained, due to the necessity of the situation and such balances are exposed to
movements in Foreign Exchange (‘FX’) rates, on valuation. Bank Saderat does not maintain a trading
book.

Operational Risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, fraud, people
and systems, or from external events. The Bank has implemented a new core banking system and this
will impact a wide range of activities of the Bank, in the short term. The policies and procedure manual
has to capture the requirements under the new core banking system and the staff has to receive
adequate training in all aspects of the software, which is continuing now.

Liquidity Risk
Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank will encounter difficulty in meeting its obligations in a timely
manner, on its financial liabilities as they become due.

Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book

Interest rate risk is the risk of variability of the fair value of future cash flows arising from financial
instruments due to changes in interest rates and is measured by analysing assets and liabilities into
time bands according to their maturity or next interest repricing date, whichever is the earlier.

Concentration Risk

The Bank's exposure to Iranian business has reduced and will hope to increase in the near future, as
there is little scope to diversify the customer base at this stage.

Foreign Exchange Risk

Foreign exchange risk arises from the change in value, expressed in reporting currency, of assets and
liabilities held in currencies other than the reporting currency, due to fluctuations in spot or forward
exchange rates. The Bank does not take speculative positions in currencies and any net open positions
arise in the ordinary course of business.

The Bank functions in a complex and restricted environment. Business risk arises from inappropriate
responses to the emerging and prevailing issues and the strategy taken by the Bank.

The Audit Committee comprises the non-executive Chairman representing the principal shareholder

Financial Controller, the Compliance Officer, the Internal Auditor and the External Auditor. The Audit

The Audit Committee is responsible for the assessment of the effectiveness of controls that are in place

The Risk Monitoring Committee

The Risk Monitoring Committee comprises the two UK based independent non-executive directors, the
Managing Director and the Chief Risk Officer. It meets twice a year and reports to the Board.

The Risk Monitoring Committee is charged with the responsibility of advising the directors on the nature
and relative scale of the risks confronting the Bank, the appropriateness of the controls intended to
manage those risks and whether the residual risk is within the parameters approved by the Board. The
Risk Monitoring Committee oversees the production of a Risk Register whereby each department
analyses the risks to which it is subject, how it is mitigated and the residual risk is managed. These
departmental risks are then consolidated into a Bank Risk Register. The Risk Monitoring Committee
also ensures that contingency plans are in place to achieve business continuity in the event of serious
disruption to business operations.

Climate Change

The Bank has set out its transitional disclosures according to the recommendations of the Task Force
on Climate Related Disclosures ‘TCFD’ and updates will be made as the quality of climate data and
reporting methodologies continue to evolve. The Bank has adopted the TCFD voluntarily — noting
where, due to current restrictions on business activity, certain recommendations can be built upon in
future periods as and when the Bank can begin to offer a wider range of products.

The risk types which the Bank has identified for more detailed analysis to evaluate the impact on the
‘Bank of potential climate change related financial risks are:

Physical risk — risks which arise from the increasing severity and frequency of climate and
weather-related events, for instance rising sea water levels, floods, heat waves,
hurricanes and storms.

Transition risk — risk which arise from the transition to net zero emissions which will
require structural changes to the economy and the resulting changes in policy, technology
and consumer demand which this entails.

The Board of Directors has direct supervision both over the strategy for climate
change and the management of associated financial risks arising from climate
change. The Risk Management Framework incorporates Climate Risk, and the
Credit Risk process is supplemented with a paralle! assessment of the financial
risks arising from Climate Change risk, Climate Risk is formally reviewed every
six months at the Risk Monitoring Committee which in turn reports to the Board of
Directors. These meetings occurred on the 30" of April and 31% of October 2024.
separately staff training has been implemented to develop organisational
awareness of the impact of climate change. The board considers climate-related
issues when reviewing strategies, major plan and actions, risk management,
annual budgets and business plan and monitors and oversees progress against
goals and targets for addressing climate-related issues.

Risk Management

Climate risk assessment has been incorporated across the overall risk
management process. As Mentioned above the risk management Framework
incorporates Climate Risk, and the Credit Risk process is supplemented with a
parallel assessment of the financial risks arising from Climate Chane Risk.

The bank identifies and assesses Climate-related risk applicable to its corporate
exposures, through a developed set of metrics quantifies the potential financial
risks arising from Climate Change across a range of scenarios considering both
physical and transition risk impact. This enables a scoring matrix to be developed
for each corporate exposure, forming the basis for managing the climate-related
risk going forward.

(b) Basis of preparation
(i) Going Concern

The Financial Statements are prepared on a ‘going concern’ basis as the directors, having taken into
account all relevant information, including annual budgets, considered that the Bank has adequate
Tier 1 capital to sustain it with sufficient capital adequacy, calculated in accordance with the FCA/PRA
Rules, for the foreseeable future; and existing assets will provide a sufficient income stream to cover
expenses far in excess of one year from the date of approval of the Financial Statements. The
directors’ have also assessed and satisfied themselves of the Bank's ability to continue as a going
concern in the context of reasonably foreseeable business and financial risks, economic uncertainties
and liquidity assessments. Although the Bank continues to experience difficulties in securing suitable
correspondent banking relationships following a second round of OFAC sanction in 2018, which
continues to date, directors have put in place systems and procedure to mitigate these and ensure
that the Bank remains a going concern in respect of its liquidity and capital adequacy. The financial
statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. In assessing the Bank’s ability to continue
as a going concern, the directors have considered the Bank's capital adequacy, liquidity position and
financial forecasts for a period of at least twelve months from the date of approval of the financial
statements. Nonetheless, the continued uncertainty surrounding correspondent banking relationships
and the broader geopolitical environment represent a material uncertainty that may cast significant
doubt on the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern. This assessment has included a review of
the Bank's Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP), Internal Liquidity Adequacy
Assessment Process (ILAAP), and five-year strategic plan.

The directors are satisfied that the Bank has adequate Tier 1 capital to meet its regulatory capital
requirements under the FCA/PRA Rules and that its existing asset base is expected to generate
sufficient income to meet operational expenses and funding obligations for the foreseeable future.

However, the Bank continues to face challenges in securing and maintaining suitable correspondent
banking relationships, primarily as a result of ongoing geopolitical conditions and the continued impact
of asecond round of OFAC sanctions imposed on 5 November 2018. These challenges limit the Bank’s
ability to operate certain lines of business and may constrain future growth opportunities.

Despite these uncertainties, the directors have implemented mitigating actions, including diversifying
income sources, enhancing compliance procedures, and strengthening governance over liquidity and

(i) Interest receivable

Interest receivable from financial assets classified as loans and advances is calculated using the
effective interest rate, which is the rate that discounts estimated future cash receipts through the
expected life of the financial asset to that asset's net carrying amount. When a receivable is impaired
recognition of interest income is reduced by calculating it using the original effective interest rate method
on the carrying amount as reduced by any provisions for impairment. The element of contractual income
thereby not recognised in the Income Statement is added to the provision for impairment.

interest income and expense for all interest-bearing instruments are recognised in profit and loss, on
an accrual basis, using the effective interest method. The effective interest method is a method of
calculating the amortised costs of a financial asset or liability and of allocating the interest income or
interest expense. The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts the estimated future cash
payments or receipts over the expected life of instrument or, when appropriate, or shorter period, to the
net carrying amount of the financial asset r financial liability.

The effective interest rate is calculated on initial recognition of the financial asset or liability, estimating
the future cash flows after considering all the contractual terms of the instrument but not the future credit
losses. The calculation includes all amounts paid or received by the bank including fees, expected early
redemptions and related penalties and premiums and discounts that are an integral part of the overall
return as well as direct incremental transaction costs related to the acquisition, issue or disposal of a
financial instrument. For fixed rate financial assets this is the origina! effective interest rate and for
floating rate financial assets this is the current effective interest rate.

(ii) Fee & Commission income ©

Fees earned for services provided are recognised as revenue at the time of the service provided. The
core principle is that revenue is recognised with services rendered under the obligation of the contract

recognises commission income when the amount of revenue can be measured reliably; and it is
probable that the economic benefits associated with the transaction will flow to the company; and the
performance obligation has been fulfilled, typically when the underlying transaction between the
principal and the customer is completed. Commission income is measured at the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding any amounts collected on behalf of third parties. Where

At each balance sheet date non-financial assets not carried at fair value are assessed to determine

If the recoverable amount of the asset (or asset’s cash generating unit) is estimated to be lower than

recognised in the profit and loss account, unless the asset has been revalued when the amount is
recognised in other comprehensive income to the extent of any previously recognised revaluation.
Thereafter any excess is recognised in profit or loss.

If an impairment loss is subsequently reversed, the carrying amount of the asset (or asset's cash
génerating unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but only to the extent
that the revised carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined
(net of depreciation or amortisation) had no impairment loss been recognised in prior periods. A reversal
of an impairment loss is recognised in the profit and loss account.

(k) Financial liabilities

Basic financial liabilities, including trade and other payables, bank loans, loans from fellow group .
companies that are classified as debt, are initially recognised at transaction price, unless the

Debt instruments are subsequently carried at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method.
Fees paid on the establishment of loan facilities are recognised as transaction costs of the loan to the
extent that it is probable that some or all of the facility will be drawn down. in this case, the fee is

Trade payables are obligations to pay for goods or services that have been acquired in the ordinary

due within one year or less. If not, they are presented as non-current liabilities. Trade payables are
recognised initially at transaction price and subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective

Loans to and advances to customers arise when the Bank provides money to a debtor, with no intention
of trading the receivable. They are initially recognised at the present value of cash receivable including

the effective interest rate method. Commercial loans to customers are recognised at costs less any
repayment of capital, and any impairment, plus or minus the cumulative amortisation of the difference

(m) Customer Accounts
Introduction

This accounting policy outlines the recognition, measurement, presentation, and disclosure of customer
accounts in accordance with UK FRS 102.

Recognition and Initial Measurement

Customer accounts, including demand deposits, term deposits, and savings accounts, are recognised
as financial liabilities on the bank’s balance sheet when the customer deposits funds.

- Initially measured at fair value, which is typically the amount deposited by the customer.

- Transaction costs directly attributable to the deposit may be included in the initial measurement unless
classified as fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL).

Subsequent Measurement

Customer accounts are classified as basic financial instruments under Section 110f FRS 102 and are
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate (EIR) method. This method reflects:

- Interest expense accrued on deposits over the term of the account.
- Any premiums, discounts, or transaction costs amortised over the expected life of the deposit.

- Floating-rate deposits that reset periodically to market rates are not subject to amortised cost
adjustments.

Interest Expense Recognition

Interest expenses related to customer deposits are recognised on an accrual basis as finance costs in
the statement of comprehensive income.

- Interest is recognised as an expense when incurred, not when paid.
- Where applicable, interest capitalisation policies must be disclosed.

(n) Impairment of financial assets

Basic financial assets, including trade, commercial loans and advances to banks and customers and
other receivables, cash and bank balances, are initially recognised at transaction price, unless the
arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value
of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Such assets are subsequently carried at
amortised cost using the effective interest method.

At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference between
the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original
effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.

If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was
recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does nat
exceed what the carrying amount would have been had the impairment not previously been recognised.
The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.

Financial assets are derecognised when (a) the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset
expire or are settled or (b) substantially all the risks and rewards of the ownership of the asset are

the practical ability to unilaterally sell the asset to an unrelated third party without imposing additional

If there is objective evidence that an impairment loss on financial assets carried at amortised cost has
been incurred, the amount of the loss is measured as the difference between the asset’s carrying
amount and the present value of estimated future cash flows (excluding future credit losses that have

If, in a subsequent period, the amount of the impairment loss decreases and the decrease can be

allowance account. The reversal shall not result in a carrying amount of the financial asset that exceeds
what the amortised cost would have been, had the impairment not been recognised, at the date the

(t) Provisions and contingencies
Provisions

Provisions are recognised when the bank has a present legal or constructive obligation as a result of
past events, it is probable that an outflow of resources will be required to settle the obligation, and the
amount of the obligation can be estimated reliably.

Where there are a number of similar obligations, the likelihood that an outflow will be required in
settlement is determined by considering the class of obligations as a whole. A provision is recognised
even if the likelihood of an outflow with respect to any one item included in the same class of obligations
might be small.

In particular:

* Restructuring provisions are recognised when the bank has a detailed, formal plan for the restructuring
and has raised a valid expectation in those affected by either starting to implement the plan or
announcing its main features to those affected and therefore has a legal or constructive obligation to
carry out the restructuring.

« Provision is not made for future operating losses.

Provisions are measured at the present value of the expenditures expected to be required to settle the
obligation using a pre-tax rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and
the risks specific to the obligation. The increase in the provision due to passage of time is recognised
as a finance cost.

Contingencies

Contingent liabilities are not recognised, except those acquired in a business combination. Contingent

liabilities arise as a result of past events when (i) it is not probable that there will be an outflow of
resources or that the amount cannot be reliably measured at the reporting

15 Financial risk management

Loans and advances to banks and customers are carried at amortised cost net of impairment provisions
where appropriate.

The activities of the Bank expose it to a variety of risks, the most significant are: market risk, liquidity
risk and credit risk.

Market risk is the potential adverse change in the Bank's net assets or income arising from movements
in interest rates, foreign exchange rates or other market prices. Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank is
unable to meet its obligations as they fall due owing to insufficient financial resources. Credit risk is the
risk of financial loss as counterparties and borrowers are unable to meet their obligations as they fall
due.

15.1. Financial risk management framework

The Board has overall responsibility for the establishment and oversight of the Bank's risk management
framework. Sub-committees of the Board have been established to manage the various risks including
the UK Board, the Audit Committee, the Risk Monitoring Committee, the Assets and Liabilities
Committee and the Credit Committee. The UK board and Risk monitoring committee includes non-
executive directors also. The Audit Committee comprises the three non-executive directors and is
responsible for monitoring compliance with the Bank's risk management policies and procedures, and

for reviewing the adequacy of the risk management framework in relation to the risks faced by the Bank.
The Audit Committee is assisted in these functions by Internal Audit. Internal Audit undertakes both
regular and ad-hoc reviews of risk management controls and procedures, the results of which are
reported to the Audit Committee.

15.3 Credit risk

sector and individual bank or customer. A report of full credit exposures against limits by country is

15.6 The internal assessment criteria for business lending purposes

The Bank has developed a rating system, which is used in conducting reviews of borrowers to assess
their on-going credit standing and to consider whether a loan or advance should be placed on a "watch
list" due to a deteriorating assessment of the borrower. The system is based upon a point scoring
method with points awarded against various criteria from an analysis of financial statements provided
by borrowers. The sum of the points awarded determines the classification of each borrower from A
(the highest rating) to D (the lowest). It is likely that any D rated borrowers would be in default, which
may result in the raising of a provision against the debt. The Bank is careful to detect early on, any
deterioration of the financial conditions of the obligors as well as the providers of different support forms.
Early problem recognition is key to successful risk management. Early warning indicators provide signs
of credit deterioration and include the following:

15.7 Impaired loans and advances

Impaired loans and advances are where the Bank has determined that it will be unable to collect all
principal and interest due according to the contractual terms of the loan agreement through the default
of the borrower and the inability to realise adequate collateral, for whatever reason. When a loan or
advance is deemed to be impaired, the Bank raises a provision for the amount which is determined to
be unrecoverable and reduces the amount of the loan or advance on the Statement of Financial Position
by the amount of the provision. Under FRS 102, the Bank uses the incurred loss model.
