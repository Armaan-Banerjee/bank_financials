Measurement methods: Amortised cost and effective
interest rate

The Company recognises amounts due from or to other
banks, loans and advances to customers, amounts due to
customers and subordinated lending at amortised cost.
Amortised cosi is the value at which the financial asset or
financial liability is measured at initial recognition minus
the principal repayments, plus or minus the cumulative
amortisation using the effective interest method of any
difference between that initial value and the maturity value
and, for financial assets, adjusted for any toss allowance.

The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly ¢iscounts
estimated future cash payments or receipts through the
expected life of the financial asset oc financial liability to
the gross carrying value of a financial asset or to the ©
amortised cost of a financial liability. The catculation does
not consider expected credit loss (“ECL”) and includes

transaction costs, premiums or discounts and fees paid or

received that are integral to the effective interest rate, such
as Origination fees. For credit-impaired financial assets the
Company catculates the credit-adjusted effective interest
rate, which is calculated based on the amortised cost of the
financial asset instead of its gross carrying value and
incorporates the impact of ECL in estimated future cash
flows.

When the Company revises the estimates of future cash
flows, the carrying value of the respective financial asset or
financial liability is adjusted to reflect the new estimated
discount using the original effective interest rate. Any
changes are recognised in profit or loss.

28.1 Market risk measurement methodology

The Company applies lirnits to all market risks. Limits

Major measurement techniques used to measure and
control market risk, are outlined below,

(a) Value at risk

VaR measures the potential loss impact an open risk
positions, under normal market conditions. VaR is
calculated using statistically expected changes in market
parameters for a given holding period at a specified level of
probability. The internal model is based on a variance/co-
variance approach and uses 399% one-tailed confidence
level. The model assumes a 10-day holding period for
purposes of croup internal risk reporting, with a 251-day
observation period for market variables. The VaR modal is
adjusted on an ongoing basis in respense to developments
in the financial markets and to changes in our risk
snanlagement needs. Risk parameters based on the VaR
methodotogy are calculated daily. The use of this approach
docs not prevent losses outside of these limits in the event
of more significant market Movements.

(b) IRRBB Economic value of equity, interest income
sensitivity and maturity gap metrics

The Feonamic Value of Equity (EVE) sensitivity metric refers
to risks on the Company's capital, arising from adverse
movements in interest rates that affect the Company's
balance sheet. Changes in interest rates affect the
underlying value of the Company's assets, liabilities and off-
balance sheet items and hence its economic value, The Net
interest Income (NII) sensitivity metric refers to risks on the
Company's earnings, arising from adverse mnvements in
interest rates that affect the Company's banking book
positions. Changes in interest rates affect the Company's
earnings by altering interest rate-sensitive incarne and
expenses, affecting its net interest incame. These two
metrics are the key measures monitoring interest rate risk.

The Company has implemented an interest rate risk
framework in accordance to its business model and its
interest rate risk exposure, where stress testing is used as
