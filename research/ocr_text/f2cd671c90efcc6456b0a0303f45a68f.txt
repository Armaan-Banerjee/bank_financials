OVERVIEW

The achievement of deposit restrictions being lifted in September 2021 by the Prudential Regulation
Authority (with the consent of the Financial Conduct Authority), after receiving authorisation with
restriction (AwR) in November 2020 was a major milestone towards implementing the Group’s core
strategy to provide UK SMEs with the credit and service they deserve.

During the year, Recognise Bank changed its name from Recognise Financial Services Ltd. to
Recognise Bank Ltd. Since receiving AwR in November 2020, the Bank completed the build out and
testing of the savings infrastructure, met all the regulatory mobilisation conditions to obtain full licence
and commenced lending through four initial lending products from November 2020. These were
Bridging and Working Capital loans, loans to the Professional Practice community and Commercial
Property loans. With deposit restrictions removed in September 2021 after a successful third round of
fundraising, Recognise Bank offers personal savings accounts, with all monies protected by the
Financial Services Compensation Scheme (‘FCSC’). Business savings products are planned for
introduction towards the end of the 2021 calendar year.

During the year, the deep experience within the business development and credit contro] teams was

Recognise Bank is a relationship-led SME lending bank supported by the latest cloud-based

Climate Change

This section details Recognise Bank’s approach to environmental, social and governance (ESG) and
sustainability matters impacting on its business as well as how we impact on the wider environment and
communities in which we operate.

The Board has a key role in overseeing how the Bank’s activities affect the environment and the impact
of climate change on the Bank’s business. In accordance with the requirements of the PRA’s
Supervisory Statement ‘Enhancing banks’ and insurers’ approaches to managing the financial risks
from climate change’, the Board and Exco have allocated responsibility for ESG and sustainability to
the Chief Credit Officer (CCO). The CCO is an SMF18 (Other overall responsibility) and within this
Senior Management Function will be responsible for identifying and managing financial risks from
climate change.

Assisting the CCO, a Responsible Business Group has been established made up from a cross section
of Recognise Bank employees, with the responsibility of implementing the Recognise Bank commitment
towards climate change, the environment and sustainability.

Recognise Bank’s commitment to ESG is being brought to life through the ‘Environmental and
Sustainability Policy’ which incorporates a defined action plan that focusses on 4 key areas.

1. Business

Recognise Bank have an agreed set of ESG metrics focusing on four areas People, Planet, Governance
and Prosperity. Recognise Bank will measure performance against these metrics so that we can then
set goals to improve our environmental and social impact plus focus on sustainability.

2. Products

Recognise Bank will encourage positive ESG and sustainability activity through its lending and deposit
products. By way of example, the Responsible Business Group has started to review product
development with ‘green’ credentials and how we may promote a positive contribution towards
sustainability and the environment. This area will be developed further in the coming year.

Recognise Bank has developed an ESE (Environmental, Social and Ethical) excluded sectors list
focussing on business sectors that negatively impact environmental, social and or ethical values. Next
step is to develop a positive/green credentials sector list to encourage positive ESG and sustainability
activity which will be implemented in the coming year.

3. Physical Risk

Physical risks from climate change arise from a number of factors and relate to specific weather events
(such as heatwaves, floods, wildfires and storms) and longer-term shifts in the climate (such as changes
in precipitation, extreme weather variability, sea level rise, and rising mean temperatures). Recognise
Bank is recording the risk of flooding for properties it lends on through flood risk ratings.

The objective of the Board is to set policies to manage and mitigate risk within acceptable levels whilst

During the year the Bank has continued to develop its risk appetite statements and risk tolerances,
enhance its governance and risk reporting, including monitoring the Board’s top risks, and the

Recognise Bank regards the monitoring and controlling of risks as a fundamental part of the risk

management policies.

The principal risks inherent in the Bank’s business are set out below.

Capital

AS a new and growing bank Recognise Bank raises capital to support its future risk exposures. There
is a risk that Recognise is unable to raise the capital it needs to fund its ongoing activities.

Recognise Bank has a solvent wind down plan, including triggers for consideration or implementation
of the pian. This includes the inability to raise capital to fund itself.

Recognise Bank has a Board approved ICAAP, which includes an assessment of the capital required

basis.

Mobilisation and Operational Risk

Mobilisation refers to the activities to stand the Bank up, including lending and savings platforms to
service customers, and “run the bank” processes.

During 2020/21 a key risk was that the Bank did not execute mobilisation to the satisfaction of the
Board. This risk has diminished as the Bank exited mobilisation and obtained a full banking licence with
deposit restrictions lifted.

Operational risk is the risk that the Bank may be exposed to financial losses from conducting its
business. The exposures to operational risk include a failure of its IT platforms, a failure of a third parties
(including an Outsourcer), and fraud, for example. There are internal controls designed to protect the
Bank from these risks.

Recognise Bank maintains a risk management system that records the risks to which Recognise Bank
is exposed and the key controls that control those risks. These risk and control self-assessments are
subject to regular review by senior management. The risk function reports on the risks recorded in the
system to the Executive Risk Committee (and thereafter the Board Risk Committee), and regularly
reviews individual risk and control self-assessments.

Strategic risk

Strategic risk is the risk that Recognise Bank is not able to achieve its corporate and strategic objectives.
This risk is important given the growth strategy. Principal to this is the ability to raise capital to support
the ongoing growth of the Bank.

The key mitigation is a sustainable business model, which is reviewed for the key assumptions (and
key risks), and the maintenance of a plan for its delivery.

The Board meets at least annually to ensure that the strategy is appropriate for the market and
economy.

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk that borrowers do not meet their obligations to Recognise Bank. There is a well-
defined process for the approval of new lending, which includes assessment of the risks that customers
will not meet their obligations and a review of the affordability of any loan. Lending is overseen by the
Credit Committee which operates under specific delegated powers. Collateral security is obtained for
most ioans, with the majority of new lending being secured on property. As appropriate, loans are
secured over other assets of the company and personal guarantees obtained. Regular reports on the
performance of the lending portfolio are made to the Credit Committee and Board Risk Committee.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that Recognise Bank does not have sufficient financial resources to enable it to
meet its obligations as they fall due, or can only secure such resources at an excessive cost.

The loan to deposit ratio is maintained at a prudent level, and consequently the Bank maintains a high
level of liquidity.

The Board annually approves the Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process (“ILAAP”), wherein
Recognise Bank models stress scenarios and assesses the resultant cash flows in order to evaluate
the Bank’s potential liquidity requirements.

Sufficient liquidity is maintained to meet the outflows in a stressed environment.

Market risk
Recognise Bank has no exposure to foreign exchange, or equity markets.

It is exposed to interest rate risk in two ways. Firstly, if interest rates rise then the cost of liabilities is
likely to rise before assets reprice, and this would result in a lower net interest income (NII risk).
Secondly, if market interest rates rise the value of fixed rate assets will fall by more than fixed rate
liabilities (because typically there are more fixed rate assets and the duration of them is longer value
risk).

Recognise Bank has established limits in relation to both NII risk and market value risks and monitors
these monthly.

Political and Economic Risk

The economic background is currently challenging in the light of the recent COVID 19 pandemic. Risks
can have a negative impact on the wider economy, flowing through to weakening investment and capital
conditions. Such risks could affect the ability to raise future capital for the Bank as it seeks to become
a new entrant as a licenced deposit taker providing SME lending.

The investment from City of London group of £21.9m since year end, has enabled the Bank to exit the
mobilisation period and achieve a full licence which means the Bank is well placed to serve the SME
market.

Competition

Competition from new entrants and incumbent dominant banking institutions could lead to compression
in the market on margins. This would potentially lead to a lowering of management forecasts and a
longer period to reach break-even. This risk is mitigated by exceeding customer expectations by
focusing on customer service and developing products that meet the needs of the Bank’s target SME
market.

Cyber risk

Cyber risk is the risk that Recognise Bank is subject to disruption arising from an interruption to its
platforms, or to the loss of data from its platforms or systems, whether due to malicious actors or
otherwise.

Recognise Bank makes use of industry standard frameworks for its cyber defences, and regularly
reviews its cyber posture using, for example, penetration testing and third party vulnerability reports.
We also review the cyber posture of our critical and important third parties and outsourcers.

The Board and employees are provided with training as part of their induction, and this is refreshed at
least annually. There are regular exercises, such as phishing tests, to maintain and enhance
awareness.

Recognise Bank maintains an Incident Response, which is regularly reviewed and tested at least
annually, and also maintains a business continuity plan including a disaster recovery plan.

Conduct risk

Conduct risk is the risk of selling products to customers which do not meet their needs, failing to deal
with customers’ complaints effectively, not meeting customers’ expectations, and exhibiting behaviours
which do not meet market or regulatory standards.

The key mitigants of conduct risk include;

e A Conduct Risk Policy, which is reviewed at least annually, and which is subject to Board
approval.

e Asuite of training which is taken on induction by all employees, and which is refreshed at least
annually. This includes, for exampie, treating customers fairly, and anti-money laundering.

e There are quality assurance processes to review the outcomes for customers, identify any
improvements that can or should be made, and to report to management.

e There is a compliance monitoring process which reviews the outcomes and the quality
assurance undertaken, and reports to the Board Risk Committee.

Regulatory risk
Regulatory is the risk that Recognise Bank fails to meet regulatory requirements or guidelines that apply
to it. The risk is mitigated through the capital and liquidity (see above) and through compliance

monitoring reviews. Recognise Bank maintains a regulatory tracker of regulatory changes, which is
reported to the Board Risk Committee.

2.18 Identification and measurement of the impairment of financial asset
IFRS 9 is a forward looking ‘expected credit loss’ impairment model and applies to:

— Financial assets measured at amortised cost
— Debt investments measured at fair value through other comprehensive income, and
~ Certain loan commitments and financial guaranteed contracts.

Under the IFRS 9 ‘expected loss’ model, a credit event (or impairment ‘trigger’) is not a prerequisite before
credit losses are recognised. An entity will now always recognise (at a minimum) 12-month expected
credit losses in profit or loss. Lifetime expected losses will be recognised on assets for which there is a
significant increase in credit risk after initial recognition.

IFRS 9 sets out three approaches to impairment:

~ Simplified approach for certain trade receivables, contract assets and lease receivables.
~ Specific approach for purchased or originated credit-impaired financial assets
~ General approach

The simplified approach under IFRS 9 applies to trades receivables, contract assets without significant
financing component where the loss allowance should be measured at initial recognition and throughout
the life of the receivable at an amount equal to lifetime ECL. Property bridging loans in Property & Funding
Solutions (PFS) are individuaily assessed. With this book in wind-down and with a relatively short
maturity, loans under PFS will remain under individual assessment. Any new loans that are booked in
Recognise Bank entity will come under the General approach detailed below.

The individual assessment will also be used when applying IFRS 9 to determine whether it is necessary
to make any provision for lifetime expected credit losses in respect of loans and similar balances between
the Bank and its subsidiary.

Purchased or originated credit-impaired financial assets are treated differently because the asset is credit-
impaired at initial recognition. For these assets, an entity would recognise all changes in lifetime expected
credit losses since initial recognition as a loss allowance with any changes recognised in profit or loss.
Under the requirements, any favourable changes for such assets are an impairment gain even if the
resulting expected cash flows of a financial asset exceed the estimated cash flows on initial recognition.

It is not expected that Recognise Bank will have any purchased or originated credit-impaired assets as
part of its portfolios.

Under the general approach, entities must at each stage of the model recognise a loss allowance for
ECLs against any of the financial instruments subject to impairment accounting. This section will focus
on the general IFRS 9 approach to impairment which follows a three-stage model:

Stage 1 includes financial instruments that have not had a significant increase in credit risk since initial
recognition or that have low credit risk at the reporting date. For these assets, 12-month expected
credit losses (that is, expected losses arising from the risk of default in the next 12 months) are
recognised and interest revenue is calculated on the gross carrying amount of the asset (that is, without
deduction for credit allowance).

Stage 2 includes financial instruments that have had a significant increase in credit risk since initial
recognition (uniess they have low credit risk at the reporting date) but are not credit-impaired. A

significant increase in credit risk as examples (but not restricted to) can include changes in general
economic and/or market conditions or significant changes in the operating results or financial position
of the borrower. Specifically, this can include (but not restricted to) material arrears detected on financial
asset which is greater than 30 days but less than 90 days past due. For these assets, lifetime ECL (that

Stage 3 consists of financial assets that are credit-impaired, which is when one or more events that

Loans and Advances

EIR takes into account the behavioural life of loans which represents the expected duration and profile

Interest Income

For Recognise Bank loan products the interest rate is contractually set at a fixed or variable (floating)

Customers will be able to redeem loans earlier than the contractual loan term subject to payment of an

monitored on a revoiving basis and subject to an annual or more frequent review. The limits are approved

Liquidity Risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank, although solvent, either does not have sufficient financial resources
to enable it to meet its obligations as they fall due or can only secure such resources at excessive cost.
The Bank's approach to managing liquidity is to ensure, as far as possible, that it will always have
sufficient liquidity to meet its liabilities when due, under both normal and stressed conditions, without
incurring unacceptable losses or risking damage to the Bank’s reputation.

The Bank has formal governance structures in place to manage and mitigate liquidity risk on a day-to-
day basis. The Board sets and approves the liquidity risk management strategy.

The PRA requires the Board to ensure that the Bank has adequate leveis of liquidity resources and a
prudent funding profile, and that it comprehensively manages and controls liquidity and funding risks. The
Bank maintains deposits placed in highly liquid unencumbered assets that can be called upon to create
sufficient liquidity to meet liabilities on demand, particularly in a period of liquidity stress.

Valuation of financial instruments

The Bank measures the fair value of an instrument using quoted prices in an active market for that
instrument. A market is regarded as active if quoted prices are readily and regularly available and
represent actual and regularly occurring market transactions. In the Bank this applies to Gilts and
Treasury Bills which are held at fair value and will be determined on a monthly basis by recalculating
the nominal value of each holding against the close-of-business market price for each relevant
instrument.

The bank measures fair value using the following fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the
inputs used in making measurements:

* Level 1: inputs are quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities that the entity
- Can access at the measurement date
* Level 2: inputs are inputs other than quoted market prices included within Level 1 that are
observable for the asset or liability, either directly or indirectly
* Level 3: inputs are unobservable inputs for the asset or liability

29 Events after the reporting date

Buffers.

through a share subscription and a raise of up to £6.9 million through an open offer of new Ordinary
Shares of COLG ("Ordinary Shares"). The proceeds from the subscription together with investment from
COLG will result in capital injection of £12.7m in the third quarter of 2021, which totals £14.2m since year
end and has enabled the Bank to exit mobilisation.

capital of Milton Homes Limited and its subsidiaries to Max Barney Investments Limited for a total
consideration of £9.3 million. The sale, which is classified as a substantial transaction under AIM Rule
12, is conditional on receiving regulatory approval from the FCA for the change in control of Milton Homes
Limited and of its lender. The net proceeds from the sale of Milton Homes are intended to contribute to
the regulatory capital of Recognise Bank.

section 55M(5) of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, the removal of the restriction on carrying
out regulated activity of accepting deposits. The decision was effective from 16 September 2021, at which
point Recognise Bank Limited had obtained a full banking licence.
