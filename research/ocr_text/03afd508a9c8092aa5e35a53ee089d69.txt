Strategic Report

Chief Executive Officer’s Statement

In my previous statement, I reflected on the Bank’s return to sustainable profitability. While 2025 began with
positive momentum, the evolving global political landscape required an immediate and fundamental shift in our
strategic priorities. Our focus moved decisively from revenue expansion to strengthening our operational
framework and ensuring full regulatory compliance.

The financial results for the year reflect the deliberate and necessary actions taken in response to this environment.
We undertook a significant restructuring of our cost base to better align the size and structure of the organisation
with our current mandates. These decisions including a realignment of staffing levels resulted in elevated one-off
costs during the period. However, they represent essential steps to safeguard the Bank’s long-term solvency and
enhance operational efficiency.

Despite the impact of these measures, alongside a challenging geopolitical backdrop, I am pleased to report that
the Bank has achieved its fourth consecutive year of profitability. This outcome underscores the underlying
resilience of our business model.

It is also important to highlight the strength of our financial position, which remains a key pillar of the Bank’s
stability. In particular, our Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR), Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR), and Capital
Adequacy Ratio (CAR) continue to demonstrate robust levels, ensuring that we maintain sufficient high-quality
liquid assets and a stable funding base.

As we move into 2026, our commitment to strong governance and regulatory compliance remains unwavering.
We will continue to work closely with our regulators to navigate the complexities of the current sanctions
environment. I am confident that the decisive and at times difficult, actions taken during the year have positioned
the Bank to maintain its status as a going concern and to continue serving its mandates with integrity, discipline,
and stability.

I would like to place on record my sincere gratitude to the CEO of Bank Melli Iran for his valued cooperation and
support in his capacity as Chairman of our Board. I also extend my thanks to my fellow Board members particularly
our independent Non-Executive Directors for their guidance and oversight. Finally, I would like to recognise the
dedication and professionalism of my colleagues across the Bank, who have embraced a more focused and active
business strategy, as well as the governance and operational changes we are implementing together. Building on
the achievements of 2022, 2023, and 2024, and the momentum we have established, we look ahead with confidence
to continuing our progress and success in the year to come.

Principal Activities

Melli Bank Ple (“the Bank” or “the Company”) is a commercial bank incorporated and registered in England and
Wales under the Companies Act 2006 and domiciled in the United Kingdom. The Bank is authorised by the
Prudential Regulation Authority (“PRA”) and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (“FCA”) and the PRA
under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. The Bank is headquartered in London. It has one branch in
Hong Kong and a representative office in Tehran. The Hong Kong Branch 1s regulated by Hong Kong Monetary
Authority (““HKMA”). The principal activity of the Bank is the provision of retail and commercial banking services.
Following the imposition of UK and EU sanctions in September 2025, the Bank’s operations have been
significantly curtailed. Activities are currently restricted to the orderly management of existing assets and habilities
within a controlled, non-trading environment. The Bank’s registered address is 98a Kensington High Street,
London, W8 48G.

Business Review

Iran remains the Bank’s niche market, and its business model continues to be based on the provision of banking
services to parties involved in trade between Iran and other countries. However, the 2025 financial year was defined
by a significant shift in the Bank’s operating environment. Following the imposition of UK and EU sanctions in
September 2025, which added to existing US sanctions, the Bank’s ability to execute its traditional trade related
model was severely restricted. Despite these challenges, the Bank maintained a governance structure focused on
navigating these operational and regulatory complexities. In 2025, the Bank generated total net income of
€12,286,000 representing a slight decrease of 3.75% compared to the 2024 total of €12,765,000. This resilience in
top-line revenue was largely driven by a 71% increase in fees and commission receivable, totalling €2,317,000 which
offset the decline in interest income from commercial loans and Letter of Credit discounting.

Total staff costs increased by 17% to €8,002,000 (2024: €6,826,000), however this increase was primarily
attributable to the one-off costs associated with the redundancy program initiated in December 2025. Other
administrative expenses rose by 54% to €3,437,000 (2024: €2,239,000). This increase was predominantly driven by
the write-off of historical balances that were deemed unrecoverable. Additionally, the Bank recorded foreign
exchange losses of €638,000 (2024: gain of €255,000).

The Bank recorded no impairment charges in 2025, compared to an impairment of €1,203,000 in 2024. After
accounting for depreciation and amortisation charges, which increased to €666,000 (2024: €365,000) and nil
taxation (2024: nil), the Bank reported a net profit for the year of €181,000 (2024: net profit of €2,356,000).

Going Concern

1. Assessment Basis and Period of Review

The Bank has performed a comprehensive review of the Bank’s ability to continue as a going concern for a period
of at least 12 months from the date of approval of these financial statements. This assessment accounts for the
impact of the Bank’s designation as a sanctioned entity on 29 September 2025 and the subsequent shift in the
Bank’s operational and regulatory environment.

2. Material Uncertainties

The Bank has identified a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt upon the Bank’s ability to continue
as a going concern. This uncertainty relates to the ongoing sanctions regime and the legal "freeze" imposed on the
Bank’s ability to settle certain liabilities. The Bank’s ability to maintain operations 1s currently dependent on General
and Specific Licenses granted by the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI). While the Bank has
successfully secured licenses to settle staff costs, IT infrastructure, and regulatory fees, these are subject to periodic
renewal (next due in October 2026). While legal counsel has advised there is a reasonable expectation of renewal,
the discretionary nature of these government authorisations represents a condition beyond the Bank's direct
control.

6. Restructuring and Profitability

The Bank initiated a restructuring and redundancy programme in late 2025 to align its cost base with its restricted

_ business model. A provision for these costs has been fully recognised in the €11.4 million administrative expenses
for 2025. Despite these one-off costs, the Bank remained profitable in 2025 and is projected to maintain a stable,
albeit reduced, financial performance throughout the going concern period.

7. Conclusion

The Bank acknowledge the inherent complexities of operating under a sanction’s regime and the material
uncertainties created by requirement for periodic licence renewals and current restrictions on deposit repayments.
However, given the Bank’s substantial capital surplus, its high liquidity position relative to its liabilities, and the
established licensing framework for operational costs, the Bank has a reasonable expectation that the Bank has
adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. Accordingly, the Bank continues
to adopt the going concern basis in preparing these financial statements.

1. Strategy and the Duty to Promote Success
During the 2025 financial year, the Board’s primary duty was exercised against a backdrop of significant geopolitical
and operational challenges following the imposition of sanctions by UK and EU on the Bank in September 2025.

While the Bank is a wholly owned subsidiary of Bank Melli Iran, the Directors have acted independently and in
good faith to navigate the legal complexities arising from these sanctions. The Board’s strategic focus shifted during
the final quarter of the year from business growth to capital preservation, strict regulatory compliance, and
protecting the remaining assets of the Bank.

2. Stakeholder Engagement and Impact of Principal Decisions

The Board identifies its key stakeholders as its shareholder, employees, regulators PRA, FCA, HK MA), Office of
Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI), and critical third-party suppliers. The following reflects how the
Board had regard to these interests during the year:

Regulators and Legal Compliance: Decisions made following September 2025 were heavily weighted toward

the FCA, the HMA and appointed a specialist legal firm to manage all license applications with the OFSI.

Employees and the Redundancy Program: The Board recognises that 2025 was a period of considerable
uncertainty for staff. The Board ensured that General Licenses were utilised to prioritise essential staff payroll and

program. The Board weighed the immediate impact on employees against the long-term necessity of aligning the
Bank’s cost base with its restricted operating environment.

Suppliers and Operational Continuity: To maintain operational integrity, the Board has worked closely with
legal a firm to secure the necessary permissions to meet its liabilities. As of the date of this report, we have

regulatory services. The Bank’s legal team remains actively engaged with OFSI to obtain further Specific Licences,
ensuring the Bank maintains professional standards and full compliance with its broader third-party supplier
obligations.

Accounting Policies

The following accounting policies have been applied consistently in dealing with items which are considered
material in relation to the financial statements.

a) faterest receivable and payable

Interest receivable and payable for all interest-bearing financial instruments measured at amortised cost are
recognised in the income statement using the effective interest rate method. The effective interest rate is a method
of calculating the amortised cost of a financial asset or financial liability and of allocating the interest receivable
or payable over the relevant period using estimated future cash flows.

The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash receipts and payments through
the expected life of a financial instrument or, where appropriate, a shorter period, to the net carrying amount of
the financial asset or financial liability. When calculating the effective interest rate, the calculation includes all
amounts paid or received by the Bank that are an integral part of the effective interest rate of a financial
instrument, including transaction costs and all other premiums or discounts.

When loans, securities or deposits are impaired, the Bank reduces the carrying amount to its recoverable amount,
being the estimated future cash flow discounted at the original effective interest rate of the instrument.

b) Fees and commissions - income and expenses

Fees and commissions are recognised in the profit and loss account when the service has been rendered. For
the current reporting period, fee income primarily represents revenue earned on trade finance transactions prior

Loans and advances are basic financial instruments satisfying the conditions set out in FRS 102 sections 11 &

deduction of amounts which are calculated as impairment provisions. Where loans have been acquired at a

Debtors

Imepairment of financial assets

Impairment provisions are made where there is objective evidence of impairment as a result of one or more
events that occurred after the initial recognition of the asset and that loss event (or events) has an impact on the
estimated future cash flows from the asset that can be reliably estimated. Losses expected as a result of potential
future events will not be recognised. Evidence of impairment is considered on both individual and portfolio
bases.

Objective evidence that financial assets ate impaired can include significant financial difficulty of the borrower
or issuer, default or delinquency by a borrower, restructuring of a loan or advance, restructuring of a loan or
advance by the Company on terms that the Bank would not otherwise consider, indications that a borrower or
issuer will enter bankruptcy, the disappearance of an active market for a security, or other observable data relating
to a group of assets such as adverse changes in the payment status of borrowers or issuers, or economic
conditions that correlate with defaults in the group. In addition, for an investment in an equity security, a
significant or prolonged decline in its fair value below its cost is objective evidence of impairment. If there is
objective evidence that an impairment loss on loans and advances has been incurred, the amount of loss ts
measured as the difference between the assets’ carrying amount and the present value of the future cash flows
(excluding future credit losses that have not been incurred). The carrying amount of the assets shall be reduced
either directly or through the use of an allowance account. The amount of the loss shall be recognised in profit
or loss.

Derivatives
A derivative is a financial instrument or other contract with all three of the following characteristics:
- Its value changes in response to the change in foreign exchange rate, specific interest rate, financial
instrument price, commodity price, index of prices or rates, credit rating or credit index, or other variable,
provided that, in the case of a non-financial variable, it is not specific to a party to the contract (Le. the

‘underlying assets’).

- It requires no initial net investment or an initial net investment that is smaller than would be required
for other types of contracts expected to have a similar response to changes in market factors.

-  Itis settled at a future date.
Derivatives, including forward foreign exchange contracts, are recorded at fair value and carried as assets when

their fair value is positive and as liabilities when their fair value is negative. The fair value of such derivatives is
disclosed separately in the financial statements. Changes in fair value of derivatives are reflected in profit or loss.

i) Taxation

Current tax is the amount of income tax payable in respect of the taxable profit for the year or prior years. Tax
is calculated on the basis of tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the period end.
Current tax liabilities are recognised without discounting.

The charge for taxation is based on the profit for the year and takes into account taxation deferred because of
timing differences between the treatment of certain items for taxation and accounting purposes. Deferred tax is
recognised, without discounting, in respect of all timing differences between the treatment of certain items for
taxation and accounting purposes which have arisen but not reversed at the Balance Sheet date. Deferred tax is
charged or credited to profit or loss, except when it relates to items charged or credited directly to equity, in
which case the deferred tax is also dealt with in equity. The notional deferred tax on the actuarial loss under FRS
102 is recognised in Other Comprehensive Income.

Deferred tax is calculated using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted that are
expected to apply when the timing difference reverses. The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at
each Balance Sheet date and reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will
be available to allow all or part of the asset to be recovered. The Bank does not currently recognise deferred tax
(see note 14 for additional information).

Management periodically evaluates the position taken in the tax return with respect to situations in which
applicable tax regulation is subject to interpretation. It establishes provisions, where appropriate, on the basis of
amounts expected to be paid to the tax authorities.

Sanctions and Operational Impact

On 29 September 2025, the Bank was subject to sanctions imposed by the UK Government, with concurrent
restrictive measures announced by the European Union. These actions followed the 28 August 2025 decision
by the UK, France, and Germany to trigger the UN "snapback" mechanism, re-imposing sanctions on Iran
originally lifted under the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action JCPOA).

Current Operational Status

The imposed sanctions prohibit the Bank from engaging in new business activities. All existing transactions
and operational movements now require specific licensing from the Office of Financial Sanctions
Implementation (OFSI) and, where applicable, other relevant authorities. While the Bank’s nsk management
and control frameworks remain in force, the sanctions regime restricts the Bank’s ability to proactively mitigate
certain risks.

environment:

trade finance activities; commercial lending representing a significantly smaller portion of total exposure.
In order to maintain a high-quality asset base, the Bank’s products (including bank placements) have
generally been offered to a select class of high-quality and reputable customer known to the Bank. To

Sanctions limit the Bank’s ability to transfer or place funds, reducing the options available to diversify or
mitigate exposure to specific counterparts. Similarly, the sanctions limit the Bank’s ability to actively manage
and diversity its geographic concentration risk. Despite the sanctions and challenging geo-political environment,
the Bank has not identified a requirement for further provisions. The Bank distinguishes sanctions driven
payment delays from underlying credit issues and remains confident in the recoverability of its assets.

Liquidity Risk: Management:

The Bank continues to monitor the Liquidity Coverage Ration and Net Stable Funding Ratio. It has
strong underlying liquidity however liquidity management is constrained by the requirement for licences
for every transaction. This creates potential operational delays in meeting obligations or moving funds.
The Bank’s robust CET1 capital position provides a substantial buffer that ensures continued solvency.
Furthermore, the CET1 capital provides the bulk of the Bank’s funding ensuring that the Bank is not
dependent on external funding sources.

Market and Foreign Exchange Risk:

The sanctions limit the ability of the Bank to close out foreign exchange positions, as such actions are
classified as new transactions under the sanctions definitions. Market risk remains limited to foreign
exchange position risk and Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book (IRRBB); the Bank does not maintain
a trading book. To mitigate the impact of potential foreign exchange volatility on any unhedged positions
the Bank maintains a robust CET 1 capital position which provides a substantial capital buffer to absorb
any resulting valuation losses without compromising the Banks’s overall solvency. Regarding IRRBB,
the Bank maintains no fixed rate exposure within its long-term maturity buckets. Furthermore, CET 1
is the primary funding source which inherently limits exposure to interest rate shocks.

Operational and Strategic Risk:

Normal operations are constrained by legal and regulatory restrictions. In response, the Bank’s primary
strategic focus has transitioned from commercial expansion to preservation of the existing asset base
and ensuring absolute compliance with the sanctions regime and all other regulatory requirements. To
mitigate disruption to the restricted operations, the Bank maintains comprehensive disaster recovery
protocols, including a fully functional back up site with necessary IT systems and communication
capabilities. However, the ability to maintain operations currently ultimately depends on the renewal of
licences. Whilst legal counsel has advised that there is a reasonable expectation of renewal, the
discretionary nature of these government authorisations represents an external dependency outside the
Bank’s direct control.
