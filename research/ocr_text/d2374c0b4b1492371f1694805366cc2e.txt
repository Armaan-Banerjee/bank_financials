Recovery from the Recession

The past year has seen no let up in the
severity of the current depression, With
domestic production at the lowest level
for over a decade and unemployment
reaching unprecedented heights, one
must seriously ask how much longer the
fabric of the country can sustain these
severe economic and social burdens,
The recent shift in emphasis from
Government policies Jinked directly, and
apparently exclusively, to the growth in
the money supply towards less volatility
in the sterling exchange rate is en-
couraging, but recent forecasts that living
standards are likely to fall further before
thay improve do not help to inspire
confidence that the economy is on a
controlled course.

During the past two years we have
experianced greater attrition of many
British manufacturing sectors than has
previously occurred this century, For
those finns weathering these conditions,
slimming down exercises have created
improved opportunities for the future, if
only a helpful economic climate can now
be created to motivate business men
towards new investment and marketing
initiatives. The measures announced by
the Chancellor of the Exchequer in his
Budget statement this month represent
initial steps on the path required to
generate the necessary motivation. The
banks, for their part, have the funds and
expertise available to help fuel a recovery
as and when it materialises.

service based on co-operative principles,
it is necessary to retain the real value of
the Group’s capital base through retained
earnings. Unfortunately, during 1981,
this was not possible. Although interest
rates remained at exceptionally high levels
during the year and the Bank's Operating
performance was well up to expectations,
the overall earnings were drastically
reduced by the impact of the special
“windfall tax imposed by the Gover--
ment on bank profits. In fact, the tax
was assessed on the level of non-interest
bearing deposits in late 1980 and
efficiency in the collection of deposit
funds on current account was, therefore,

penalised. The impact of the tax on
Co-operative Bank Group profits for the
year has been particularly onerous and
represents, very unfairly we feel, a far
higher proportion of operating profits

Monetary working capital

The monetary working capital adjustment allows for the effect of price changes on the net monetary
working capital by applying the change in Retail Price Index to the average monetary working capital
during the year. For this purpose monetary working capital includes all assets other than fixed assets
less liabilities arising from operating activities,

Depreciation adjustment

The depreciation adjustment is the difference between depreciation based on the current replacement
cost of fixed assets and the depreciation charge in the historical cost accounts, In estimating the value
to the business the following bases have been adopted:

(i) Freehold and leasehold premises have been professionally valued in 1980. These values have
been maintained in 1981,

(ii) Computer hardware and other electronic equipment, together with any related capital expenditure
on software, have been valued at current replacement cost.

(iii) Other fixed assets have been valued at historical cost adjusted to current cost by the use of
relevant indices,

Gearing adjustment

The gearing adjustment reduces the monetary working capital and depreciation adjustment by the
proportion of capital provided other than by shareholders’ funds.

Corresponding amounts

The corresponding amounts for the previous period are shown in values relating to fast year, without
further adjustment,

the historical cost accounts,
