REPORT OF THE DIRECTORS

The directors have pleasure in presenting their report, together with the audited financial statements of SG
Hambros Bank Limited (the “Bank”) for the year ended 31 December 2008.

RESULTS FOR THE YEAR

The profit on ordinary activities for the financial year ended 31 December 2008 after provision for
taxation, as shown in the financial statements, amounted to £24,219,000 compared with a profit of
£3,651,000 for the previous year.

No interim dividend was paid in 2008 (2007: £4,000,000). The directors do not recommend payment of a
final dividend for the year 2008 (2007: £nil).

PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES AND BUSINESS REVIEW

The Bank and its subsidiaries form the private banking arm of the Société Générale Group (“SG Group”)
in the UK, the Channel Islands, Gibraltar, Canada and the Bahamas. The Bank offers its international on-
shore and offshore clientele an integrated wealth management service, including the provision of banking
products and trust structures as well as investment management advice.

The Bank continued to grow organically in the UK in 2008, with average staff headcount increased to 187
(2007: 158) whilst the number of client relationship managers increased to 69 (2007: 41). The Bank
expanded its regional presence during 2008 and plans to consolidate this expansion in 2009. Assets under
management increased by 14%, before market movements, to over £ 2.8 billion. Margin on assets under
management reduced to 119 basis points, reflecting weakening of business profits in the latter part of the
year.

On 29" February 2008, the Bank purchased the Canadian Wealth Management group, a client advisory
business based in Calgary, Alberta, consisting of 23 staff and £ 335 million of client assets under
management.

On 5" of September 2008, the Bank provided £ 28 million additional capital to its subsidiary SG Hambros
Bank Gibraltar Ltd to strengthen the subsidiary balance sheet and provide capital to finance the
acquisition of the ABN AMRO Gibraltar private banking business, a business consisting of 24 staff and
approximately £ 652 million of client assets under management.

On the 1* of January 2009 SG Hambros Bank & Trust (Bahamas) Ltd a 100% subsidiary of the Bank,
sold its private banking business to SG Private Banking Bahamas Limited, a Société Genérale company,
for $24,6 million. SG Hambros Bank & Trust (Bahamas) Ltd continues to operate in the Bahamas as Trust
and Mutual fund administration business.

The credit markets were unstable in 2008 and remain highly unsettled in 2009; as the Bank is a deposit
taking entity with a small credit book, the impact has been favourable to the Bank’s interest margin
performance, the treasurer continues to monitor the markets carefully for any impact on the Bank’s future
profitability. The market for the Bank’s holdings of debt securities has also been impacted by the credit
conditions, but no permanent impairment has been experienced in the Bank’s portfolio of securities and
no issues regarding redemptions and interest payments have been experienced. The Bank continues to
manage holdings within authorised limits and regular impairment reviews are undertaken.

LIQUIDITY & GOING CONCERN

The Bank’s financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis as the Directors believe there are
no material uncertainties that lead to significant doubt upon the entity’s ability to continue as a going
concern. In particular liquidity management remains a key part of the Bank’s monitoring processes and
the Bank’s tiquidity risk control and mitigation is detailed in the accompanying notes to the financial
statements. The Bank maintains sufficient liquidity to exceed regulatory liquidity test limits and maintains
additional liquidity such that under stress tested scenarios, these minimum required levels will continue to
be exceeded. In event of reduced liquidity then mitigation strategies are clearly defined including, but not
limited to, SG Group funding

The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Bank are detailed in the notes of the accounts.

the Bank will continue in business,

with reasonable accuracy at any time the financial position of the Bank and to enable them to ensure that
the financial statements comply with the Companies Act 1985.

The directors have a general responsibility for taking reasonable steps to safeguard the assets of the Bank

STATEMENT OF ACCOUNTING POLICIES
For the year ended 31 December 2008

ACCOUNTING CONVENTION

The financial statements are prepared on the historical cost basis of accounting. They have been drawn up
in accordance with applicable accounting standards in the United Kingdom. The Bank’s financial
statements are prepared in accordance with the British Banking Association’s Statements Of
Recommended Practices covering Advances, Securities, Derivatives, Contingent Liabilities and
Commitments and Segmental Reporting, and in accordance with Schedule 9 of the Companies Act 1985,

GOING CONCERN

The Bank’s financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis as the Directors believe there are
ho material uncertainties that lead to significant doubt upon the entity’s ability to continue as a going
concern.

GROUP FINANCIAL STATEMENTS

8G Hambros Bank Limited (the “Bank”) is exempt by virtue of Section 228 of the Companies Act 1985,
from the requirement to prepare group financial) statements and deliver them to Registrar of Companies.
These financial statements present information about the Bank as an individual undertaking and not about
its group. The Bank is included in the consolidated financial statements of Société Genérale SA (“SG”)
which is incorporated in France.

NET INTEREST INCOME

Net interest income comprises income from banking activities, namely interest receivable on loans,
advances, deposits placed and investment in debt securities less interest payable on deposits taken. Interest
arising from the internal funding of dealing activities is included within interest receivable. Revenue is
recognised as interest accrues using the effective interest method.

DIVIDENDS
Revenue is recognised when the Bank’s right to receive payment is established.

FEES AND COMMISSIONS RECEIVABLE

Fees and commissions are recognised on an accruals basis. Arrangement fees are booked as deferred
income on the balance sheet and recognised in the profit and loss account over the period of the
arrangement.

FOREIGN CURRENCIES

Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies have been translated into sterling at the
rates of exchange ruling at the balance sheet date. Net income in foreign currencies is translated into
sterling and recorded at the rates of exchange ruling at the end of each day.

Foreign currency equity investments hedged by foreign currency borrowings or foreign exchange
contracts are retranslated into sterling at the rates of exchange ruling at the balance sheet date and the
exchange differences arising taken directly to reserves, The exchange difference arising on related foreign
currency borrowings or foreign exchange contracts is also offset, as a reserve movement, against this
exchange difference.

DEALING PROFITS
This comprises profits and losses from foreign exchange activities and from dealing in financial
instruments, including interest and dividends receivable, less associated funding costs.

PENSION COSTS

The Bank operates a defined contribution pension scheme and contributes to a Société Générale Group
(“Group”) defined benefit pension scheme. Contibutions are charged to the profit and loss account as
they become payable in accordance with the rules of the scheme.

PROVISIONS FOR LIABILITIES
A provision is recognised when the group has a legal or constructive obligation as a result of a past event
and it is probable than an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation.

PROVISIONS FOR BAD AND DOUBTFUL DEBTS

Specific provisions are made against loans and advances when, as a result of a detailed appraisal of the
loans and advances portfolio, it is considered that recovery is doubtful. Specific provisions are deducted
from loans and advances. Provisions made during the year (less amounts released and recoveries of
amounts written off in previous years) are charged against profits. When the collection of interest is in
significant doubt it is credited to a suspense account. Where there is no longer any realistic prospect of
recovery the outstanding debt is written off.

DEFERRED TAX

Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the
balance sheet date where transactions or events have occurred at that date that will result in an obligation
to pay more, or right to pay less tax, with the exception of deferred tax assets which are recognised only to
the extent that the directors consider that it is more likely than not that there will be suitable taxable profits
from which the future reversal of the underlying timing differences can be deducted.

Deferred tax is measured on an undiscounted basis at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the periods
in which timing differences reverse, based on tax rates and laws enacted or substantively enacted at the
balance sheet date,

DEBT SECURITIES AND EQUITY SHARES

Securities and shares intended for use on a continuing basis in the Bank’s activities are classified as debt
securities, Such shares and securities are carried at amortised cost less provisions for any permanent
diminution in value. Realised profits and losses are calculated by reference to book value. The cost of
dated investment securities is adjusted for the amortisation of premiums or discounts on an average yield
to expiry basis. Realised profits and losses on such securities are calculated by reference to amortised
cost,

SHARES IN GROUP UNDERTAKINGS
The carrying value of the Bank’s investments in subsidiary undertakings is stated at historical cost less
any provision for impairment,

PARTICIPATING INTERESTS

Participating interests are investments in companies, not being group undertakings, whose directors
include representatives of the Bank. The Bank intends to hold these investments long term with the
purpose of securing a contribution to the Bank’s profits. Such interests are stated at cost less any provision
for impairment.

TANGIBLE FIXED ASSETS
Tangible fixed assets are written off on the straight-line basis over their estimated useful lives as follows:

Leasehold properties 3 years
Computer equipment 3-5 years
Furniture, fixtures and fittings 10 years

The carrying values of tangible fixed assets are reviewed for impairment when events or changes in
circumstances indicate the carrying value may not be recoverable.

DERIVATIVE CONTRACTS AND OTHER OFF-BALANCE SHEET INSTRUMENTS

Derivative contracts and off-balance sheet instruments, consisting of interest rate swaps, foreign exchange
forwards and options are held by the Bank. Interest rate swaps and certain foreign exchange forwards are
held for the purpose of hedging the Bank's interest rate risk and currency risk respectively and are shown
at cost and the profits and losses are recognised in the income as interest accrues. Foreign exchange
forwards and options are also held for customer facilitation and hedged either by reciprocal derivative
contracts or managed within the Bank’s spot foreign exchange positions, as part of the overall currency
risk management, These derivatives are accounted for at cost and the profits and losses are recognised in
the income as interest accrues.

CASH FLOW STATEMENT

Under Financial Reporting Standard | (Revised) the Bank is exempt from the requirement to prepare a
cash flow statement. This is on the grounds that for the year ended 31 December 2008 more than 90% of
the voting rights were controlled by SG and a consolidated cash flow statement, including the cash flows
of the Bank for this year, is included in SG’s financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2008
which are publicly available.

RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS

Under Financial Reporting Standard 8, the Bank is exempt from the requirement to disclose related party
transactions within the SG Group on the grounds that for the year ended 31 December 2008 more than
90% of the voting rights were controlled by SG whose financial statements are publicly available.

GOODWILL

Goodwill is amortised on a straight line basis over 15 years with a review for impairment carried out
annually.

Defined Benefit Scheme

The Bank participates in a multi-employer scheme, the SG UK Defined Benefit Scheme (“the Scheme”). This
Scheme is not open to new members. The Scheme was created with effect from 1 January 2004 following the
merger of the Société Générale Group Retirement and Death Benefit Scheme for United Kingdom Staff (the
London Branch Scheme) and the Société Générale Strauss Turnbull Securities Limited Pension Scheme (the
SGST Scheme) with the SG Hambros Scheme. To effect this merger, the assets and liabilities of the SGST and
London Branch schemes were transferred into the SG Hambros Scheme. In addition, members of the 5G
Hambros Scheme received benefit improvements and the financial effects of these benefit improvements are
reflected in the disclosure below.

The Scheme is a "multi-employer scheme”, in accordance with paragraphs 8-12 of the FRS 17. The Directors
based on the advice of the Scheme Actuary consider that, in accordance with paragraph 9(b) of the Standard,
the Bank is unable to identify on a consistent and reasonable basis its share of the underlying assets and
liabilities of the Scheme. Therefore, for the purposes of FRS 17, the Bank accounts for its pension cost arising
under the Scheme as if it were a defined contribution scheme.

The total defined benefit pension cost and contribution for the Bank was £689,342 in 2008 (2007: £607,082
previously £736,134). A full actuarial valuation of the SG Hambros scheme was last carried out as at 31"

REVIEW OF THE BANK’S RISK PROFILE

The Bank’s financial instruments comprise deposits, money market assets and liabilities, some cash and liquid
resources, and various other items that arise directly from its operations. In the ordinary course of business
customer deposits are deployed in the money market. The Bank’s share capital is invested in government
bonds with maturities up to 10 years. The Bank in addition enters into derivatives transactions (principally
interest rate swaps and forward foreign currency contracts). The purpose of such transactions is to manage the
interest rate and currency risks arising from the Bank's operations and other financial instruments.

The main risks arising from the Bank’s financial instruments are credit risk, liquidity risk, interest rate risk,
foreign currency risk and operational risk. Risk policies are reviewed and approved by the Asset and Liability
Committee (ALCO), as and when necessary. The Board has delegated the approval of the risk based policies to
the ALCO. Adherence with these policies is monitored by the Board.

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk that a customer or counterparty be unable or unwilling to meet a commitment that it has
entered into with the Bank. The Bank manages its counterparty credit risk, based on defined limits, to optimise
the use of credit availability and to avoid excessive risk concentration. Credit risk is reported to the ALCO.
Reports to ALCO include information on large credit exposures, asset concentration and country exposures,
and provisioning levels.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank will encounter difficulty in realising assets or otherwise raising funds to
meet commitments. The Bank monitors expected cash inflow and outflow for the following year. Its policy
throughout the year has been at all times to maintain sufficient high quality liquid assets to cover the expected
net cash outflow and to comfortably exceed the limits for the 0 to 8 and 0 to 30 day periods set by the
Financial Services Authority. In addition the Bank models a number of liquidity stress scenarios when in a
hypothetical period of restricted liquidity, the effect of the pre defined liquidity mitigation strategies of the
Bank are modelled and ongoing compliance with the regulatory limits is confirmed. The mitigation strategies
include access to SG Group funding from credit arrangements.

The customer deposit base represents a stable source of funding due to the number and diversity of depositors.
Liquidity is managed through Treasury loans and deposits with the money markets.

At 31 December 2008, none of the Bank’s deposits and money market liabilities was due to mature in more
than one year. The Bank maintains agreed lines of credit with SG Group companies to ensure that in the event
of issues of refinancing in the money markets, the Bank’s deposits and money market liabilities can be
replaced in orderly and timely fashion with group counterparties.

Interest rate risk

The Bank deals in currencies at both fixed and floating rates and uses interest rate swaps to generate the
desired interest profile and to manage the Bank's exposure to interest rate fluctuation. The Bank's policy is to
enhance the return on financial instruments by controlled mismatching of the dates on which interest
receivable on assets and interest payable on liabilities are next reset to market rates or if earlier, the dates on
which the instruments mature. The Group Head of Risk Management receives regular reports on interest rate
risk exposures. Levels of risk are maintained within defined limits and are monitored daily. The ALCO
considers all exposures on a monthly basis.

The Bank's policy to invest free capital is to purchase government securities of varying maturities up to ten
years. At 3! December 2008 £111m (2007: £126m) of the Bank’s free capital was invested in UK government
securities with a weighted average duration of 3.57 years (2007: 4.12 years).

Interest rate swaps may be used to hedge the interest rate exposure.

Foreign currency risk

Foreign currency risk is the risk that the value of a financial instrument will fluctuate because of changes in
foreign exchange rates. The Bank’s foreign exchange exposure arises from providing services to customers
and from its investment in overseas subsidiary and associated undertakings. In the case of subsidiary
undertakings, the Bank’s policy is to hedge against these structural foreign exchange risks with currency
borrowings, forward currency transactions or currency swaps. The Bank's Dollar investment in SG Hambros
Bank & Trust (Bahamas) Ltd is hedged by an equivalent amount of approximately $51m of Customer
Deposits. The Group Head of Risk Management receives regular reports on foreign currency exposures.
Levels of risk are maintained within defined limits and are monitored daily. The ALCO considers all
exposures on a monthly basis.

The table in note 27 shows the Bank’s exposure to major currencies as at 31 December 2008.
Operational risk

Procedures and controls to manage operational risk are codified in manuals. Breaches are reported to
management and all losses exceeding a minimum level are recorded by the Group Risk Department. Losses or
control failures above a certain level, or of a repetitive or serious nature, are reported to the Board of Directors
who have executive responsibility for risk.
