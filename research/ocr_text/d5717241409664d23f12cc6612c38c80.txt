The Directors present their Strategic Report for The Bank of London Group Limited ("the Company” or “the
Bank”) for the year ended 31 December 2023. The Company (registered number 12844788) is a private
company limited by shares incorporated and domiciled in the United Kingdom under the Companies Act 2006
and is registered in England and Wales. On 10 March 2025, the Company’s registered office changed to 77
Cornhill, Fourth Floor, London, England EC3V 3QQ.

The Bank’s immediate parent is Oplyse Holdings Limited, formerly known as The Bank of London Group
Holdings Limited (together with its subsidiaries, “the Group’). Feliesskap Group & Holdings Corporation
Limited, formerly known as TBOL Group & Holdings Corporation Limited, which was incorporated in Jersey on
1 November 2023, became the ultimate parent company of the Bank on 2 May 2024.

1. Mission Statement

Our mission is to create a safe clearing bank for the UK, offering a purpose-built payments, settlement and
clearing platform that is powering the businesses and entrepreneurs of the future. With our technologies,
innovations, and differentiated bank capabilities, we prioritise the preservation of our depositors’ monies and
market stability. All client deposits are held in full at the Bank of England, which means that we are less
exposed to the risk of traditional ‘bank runs’. 100% of our clients’ deposits are liquid, unencumbered and
immediately available upon demand.

2. Principal Activities

As the UK’s sixth clearing bank, the principal activities of the Bank are to provide payment and deposit
services to our clients. These focus on:
« Agency, clearing, payment and settlement solutions for regulated and non-regulated domestic and
international companies;
« Banking-as-a-Service - enabling companies to embed regulated banking products into their own
branded offerings, powered by our bank licence and platform;
* Deposits-as-a-Service ~ directly or through deposit aggregators; and
e Business & Commercial Banking - providing custody, transaction, and treasury banking solutions to
many types of business, domestic and international.

banking platforms, enabling us to offer enhanced efficiency, real-time processing capabilities and improved
resilience. This technological foundation has been crucial in delivering our core services:

4. Financial Results for the Year

The Bank recorded a net loss for the year of £12,414k (2022 restated: £12,823k). The net loss for the year
represents costs incurred in setting up infrastructure (non-tech) and hiring the high-calibre workforce
required to develop the capabilities and banking platform, to progress to exiting mobilisation and provide
clearing, payment and other banking services.

The Bank earned net interest income in the year of £1,194k (2022: £226k), with interest income of £3,502k
(2022: £227k) primarily representing interest income earned on funds deposited in the Bank's account at the
Bank of England, and interest expense of £2,308k (2022: £1k) being interest expense payable on customer
deposits.

Expenses were £13,230k (2022 restated: £12,575k), up marginally year on year. However, there was an
increase in employment costs due to an increase of 34 in average headcount and a resulting increase in
computer, software and licence expenses. This was offset by a reduction in professional and other expenses.

The Bank's net assets as at 37 December 2023 increased to £26,686k (2022 restated: £18,109k), with the
net loss for the year being offset by an increase in shareholders’ capital during the year.

Qualified opinion

We have audited the financial statements of The Bank of London Group Limited (the ‘Company’ for the year
ended 31 December 2023 which comprise the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income,
the Statement of Financial Position, the Statement of Changes in Equity, the Statement of Cash Flows and
the related notes 1 to 20, including material accounting policy information. The financial reporting framework
that has been applied in their preparation is applicable law and UK adopted international accounting
standards.

In our opinion, except for the possible effects of the matter described in the basis for qualified opinion
section of our report, the financial statements:

e give a true and fair view of the Company’s affairs as at 31 December 2023 and of its loss for the year
then ended;

e have been properly prepared in accordance with UK adopted international accounting standards; and
e have been prepared in accordance with the requirements of the Companies Act 2006.

Basis for qualified opinion

In December 2022, the parent company, Oplyse Holdings Limited (“the Group"), implemented a Share Option
Plan where equity-based awards over shares in Oplyse Holdings Limited, are granted to employees of the
Group, including employees of the Company.

Options issued under the Share Option Plan allow the participants the right to acquire ordinary shares in the
parent company. As described in note 17, the Group accounts for the Share Option Plan as an equity-settled
plan. The fair value of the participant's services received in exchange for the grant of the options is
recognised as an expense in Administrative Expenses in the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other
Comprehensive Income. A credit is recognised directly in equity in the Share-Based Payment Reserve.

The Directors have estimated the valuation, accounting and disclosure of the Share Option Plan because
inadequate historical records have been maintained, as set out in note 17. As a result of this, we were unable
to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to support the valuation, accounting and disclosure of the
Share Option Plan. In respect of the year ended 31 December 2023 this impacted the Share-Based Payments
Reserve of £1,205k (2022: £714k) in the Statement of Financial Position and £469k (2022: £706k) of share-
based payment charges included within Administrative Expenses. In addition, we could not determine
whether any adjustment to the Share-Based Payments Reserve (£714k) and Retained Earnings (£14,145k)
as at 1 January 2023 was necessary.

Our application of materiality

We apply the concept of materiality in planning and performing the audit, in evaluating the effect of identified
misstatements on the audit and in forming our audit opinion.

Materiality

The magnitude of an omission or misstatement that, individually or in the aggregate, could reasonably be
expected to influence the economic decisions of the users of the financial statements, Materiality provides
@ basis for determining the nature and extent of our audit procedures.

We determined materiality for the Company to be £801k, which is 3% of equity. We believe that equity
provides us with the appropriate basis of materiality as this aligns closely with the expectations of primary
users of the financial statements being its majority shareholder and the UK regulator to whom capital
adequacy and solvency of the group is critical. As such we decided to use 3% of equity (lower end of the
range) on the grounds that the Company is a start-up entity that is in the early stages of executing its
business plan, is still loss making and operates within a highly regulated industry with increased focus on
capital adequacy.

During the course of our audit, we reassessed initial materiality as our audit procedures identified a number
of audit differences which resulted in us performing a reassessment of original assessment at planning.

Performance materiality s
The application of materiality at the individual account or balance level. It is set at an amount to reduce to
an appropriately low level the probability that the aggregate of uncorrected and undetected misstatements
exceeds materiality.

On the basis of our risk assessments, together with our assessment of the Company's overall control
environment, our judgement was that performance materiality was 50% of our planning materiality, namely
£400k as the Company is a start-up and operates within regulated industries. We have set performance
materiality at this percentage based on a number of considerations, including the number and amount of
identified misstatements, the effectiveness of the control environment and other factors affecting the entity
and its financial reporting.

Reporting threshold
An amount below which identified misstatements are considered as being clearly trivial.

We agreed with the Audit Committee that we would report to them all uncorrected audit differences in
excess of £40k, which is set at 5% of planning materiality, as well as differences below that threshold that,
in our view, warranted reporting on qualitative grounds.

We evaluate any uncorrected misstatements against both the quantitative measures of materiality discussed
above and in light of other relevant qualitative considerations in forming our opinion.

Going concern

The Bank financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis. As discussed in section 6 of the
Directors’ Report on page 13, and in note 1.2 below, the Board recognise that, notwithstanding the commitment

3. Regulatory Investigation Risks - Unfavourable or punitive outcomes from the ongoing

its assets and discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business.

1.4 Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment are tangible assets which the Bank holds for its own use or for rental to others

recognised as an asset when it is probable that future economic benefits associated with the item will flow
to the Bank, and the cost of the item can be measured reliably.

Property, plant and equipment is initially measured at cost, which includes all of the expenditure which is
directly attributable to the acquisition or construction of the asset. Expenditure incurred subsequently for
major services, additions to or replacements of parts of property, plant and equipment are capitalised if it is

can be measured reliably. Day to day servicing costs are included in profit or loss in the year in which they
are incurred.

Depreciation of an asset commences when the asset is available for use as intended by management.
Depreciation is charged to the profit or loss account, so as to write off the asset's carrying amount over its
estimated useful life to its estimated residual value, using a method that best refiects the pattern in which

Classification

Recognition and measurement

Receivables and other assets are recognised when the Bank becomes a party to the contractual

The Bank applies IFRS 9’s Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model using the General Approach to its financial

of default (PD) and loss given default (LGD), using historical trends and forward-looking data.

This approach consists of three stages, as follows:

e Stage 1 is applied to assets that have a low credit risk at initial recognition, or those have not had a
significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition. For stage 1 ECL, the 12-month ECL is used
{reflecting potential credit risk events that might occur within 12 months of the reporting date).

e Stage 2 is applied to assets that have incurred a significant increase in credit risk since initial
recognition, but that do not have objective evidence of impairment. For stage 2 ECL, these lifetime
ECLs are used (reflecting expected lifetime credit losses that result from all possible credit risk events
over the expected life of the asset).

e Stage 3 is used when financial assets are non-performing or have objective evidence of credit
impairment at the reporting date. For stage 3 ECL, lifetime ECLs are used.

1.7. Share-based payments

The Group maintains a number of share-based payment plans for employees and certain Directors of
the Bank. When such awards are made to employees, the fair value of these payments at grant date
serves as the basis for calculating the personne! expenses. Share-based payments that are not subject
to any further conditions are expensed immediately at grant date. Share-based payments that are
subject to the completion of a service period are expensed over the respective service period.

Conditions that determine whether the counterparty receive the share-based payment are separated
into vesting conditions and non-vesting conditions. Vesting conditions are all conditions that determine
whether the entity receives the services that entitle the counterparty to the share-based payment and
may be differentiated further between service or performance conditions.

Performance conditions are either market conditions or non-market performance conditions. All other
conditions are considered non-vesting conditions.

The modified grant-date method requires entities to distinguish between the above-mentioned vesting
and non-vesting conditions.

Market conditions and non-vesting conditions are reflected as an adjustment (discount) to the initial
estimate of fair value at grant date of the instrument to be received and there is no true up for
differences between estimated and actual vesting due to market conditions.

The impact of service conditions and non-market performance conditions on vesting is estimated at
grant date, but it is not reflected in the grant-date fair value itself. Instead, the accounting for the share-
based payment is based on the number of equity instruments for which the service and non-market
performance conditions are expected to be met. Subsequently, these estimates are trued up for
differences between the number of instruments expected to vest and the actual number of instruments
vested.

Share-based payment plans that are settled in own equity instruments (shares or options) result in an
offsetting credit in equity and are not remeasured for subsequent changes in the fair value of the
underlying equity instruments.

Share-based payment plans that are settled by the payment of cash are recognised as a liability and
are adjusted through the Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income for changes in
fair value of the underlying equity instruments until final settlement.

Employee Share Option Pian

Employees and certain Directors of the Company participate in an equity-settled share-based remuneration
scheme which is operated by the Company’s parent company, Oplyse Holdings Limited. All employees are
eligible to participate, with the only vesting conditions being that the individual remains an employee of the
Company over the vesting period, which is typically 3 years, or if an ‘exit event’ occurs.

An exit event includes, but is not limited to a share sale, an asset sale and an Initial Public Offering. No options
may be transferred, assigned or charged, and any purported transfer, assignment or charge shall cause the
option to lapse immediately. Option holders who leave the Group and who are considered to be good leavers
may keep the vested portion of their respective awards, but it will not usually become exercisable early.

There are no cash settlement alternatives. The Group does not have a past practice of cash settlement for
these share options. The Group accounts for the Option Plan as an equity-settled plan. The fair value of the
Participant services received in exchange for the grant of the options is recognised as an expense. A credit is
recognised directly in equity against Share-Based Payment Reserve. The total amount to be expensed is
determined by reference to the fair value of the options granted, calculated on a Black Scholes basis.

Options issued under the Option Plan allow Participants the right to acquire Ordinary Shares of £0.10 each in
