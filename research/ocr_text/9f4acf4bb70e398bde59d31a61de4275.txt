Strategic report

The directors are pleased to present their Strategic Report of JN Bank UK Ltd (“the Bank”) for the
year ended 31 March 2023.

Key highlights

The Bank has had a mixed year. The business development in data management, IT infrastructure,
systems and controls has progressed. The Bank's customers have however had to contend with the
end of the pandemic, rising interest rates, the most significant increase in cost of living For more
than a generation and a background of economic uncertainty with recession always threating. As
we enter the new Financial year the outlook is more positive with interest rates Falling, a few green
shoots of economic growth and the possibility of recession and unemployment reducing.

The book grew in the year from £57.3m to a peak of £80.0m in November 2022. New originations
were progressing with the developments in automatic acceptance and pre-population of
applications being launched. The lending criteria continued to develop as the book and the analysis
of its performance developed. A number of new distribution partners were established.

As our customer started to feel the impacts of the cost-of-living squeeze, performance of the
portfolio deteriorated and in managing the portfolio to the risk appetite, volumes were
progressively reduced as lending conditions were tightened. In December 2022 a decision was
made to pause lending altogether to rebuild underwriting operating models to account for the
change in economic conditions.

The Bank grew its net interest income significantly to £6.8m (2022: £3.1m) however losses
increased to £12.7m (2022: £6.5m) due to the impacts of higher credit losses and pausing lending.

Business model

The business model of the Bank is to provide unsecured personal lending across the retail market
in segments which may be typically underserved by the larger banks. We differentiate ourselves
with straightforward products, and a personalised approach to lending, that offer us a competitive
advantage. In order to do this, the Bank employs a high level of data analysis and risk management
expertise. Lending is originated both directly and through partners and is Funded by offering
simple, competitively priced online savings products directly to personal customers.

Our model is underpinned by a commitment to the disciplines of risk management, regulatory
compliance and governance. Risk appetite and culture are set by the Board to provide the
appropriate tone From the top.

Our values

We exist to work For our customers and their communities. We understand the importance of
treating everyone with compassion and respect, that everyone has a story, and that is why helping
our customers Find a way to achieve their goals is at our heart.

Our approach is straightforward - Financial products that are fair, transparent and simple. Our
background, being part of The Jamaica National Group, gives us a unique focus as part of a mutual
organisation. We celebrate the differences of all our customers and the Bank’s ethos, as part of a
mutual organisation, is built on inclusion and care for all. We care about our customers, their
individual needs, and the environment in which we live.

Business review

To mitigate the impact of the pandemic the Bank implemented additional restrictions to moderate
lending volumes and reduce credit. New lending in the First half of the Financial year was strong
however further deterioration in the UK economy exposed weaknesses in the business’ credit
assumptions. This led to higher than planned credit risks. The volumes of loans written has been
controlled through credit risk criteria, pricing and proactive management. During the year in order
to Further manage credit risk new lending was reviewed with substantially all lending paused in
December 2022. The break in lending has allowed processes, systems and credit models to be
reviewed and lending recommenced on 10° July 2023.

As aresult, new loans lent to customers decreased to £44m (2022: £63m) in the year. The portfolio
balance before provisions at the yearend was £68m (2022: £60m). As a result of reduced loan book
growth and longer term customer deposits the overall level of deposits have increased to £99m
(2022: £70m).

Customer service remains at the heart of the business. The pause in lending is enabling the
development of our customer journey and strengthened our relationship with our customers. The
Bank was shortlisted for the Best Personal Lender, Treating Customers Fairly and Best British Bank
awards at the British Bank Awards in 2023. These awards are voted for by consumers and reflect
the great level of engagement we have with our customers, and we are proud of our achievements.

Enhancements to the collection Functionality have been implemented during the year. The
allowance for expected credit losses (ECLs) has increased to 18.2% of gross loans to customers
(2022: 4.5%).

Revenue has increased in the year £6.8m (2022: £3.1m) however the increased credit losses have
increased the loss For the financial year to £12.7m (2022: £6.5m loss).

Principal risks and uncertainties

Financial institutions operate in a competitive and highly regulated environment and are subject to
economic and other uncertainties. The management of risk is therefore central to the continuing
success of the Bank.

The Board is responsible For determining the Bank's risk management framework and system of
internal controls, which are designed to enable the Bank to achieve its objectives within a managed
risk profile, not to eliminate all risk. To do this, the Bank bases its risk management framework on
the ‘three lines of defence’ model. The ‘first line’ of defence is the checks and controls utilised by
operational staff directly engaged in the management of the risks in their area of activity. The
‘second line’ is the assurance provided by the Bank's Risk Management function. The ‘third line’ is
the independent internal audit Function which undertakes reviews, the reports of which are
regularly provided to management and the Board Audit and Board Risk Committees by an external
provider.

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of suffering Financial loss should borrowers or
counterparties default on their contractual obligations to the Bank. These
risks are overseen by the Board Risk Committee and managed by policies and
procedures operated by the three lines of defence as set out above, along
with the Executive Credit Committee.

Credit risk on consumer lending is mainly mitigated by way of automated and
manual credit assessment, affordability checks and case-by-case review.

Credit risk on treasury instruments and Financial institutions is limited
through a Financial risk management policy to manage exposures, and as at
31 March 2023 the exposures are predominantly to the UK government.

Liquidity and
Funding risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank is unable to settle obligations with
immediacy and maintain public and stakeholder confidence. Funding risk is
the risk that the Bank is unable to realise assets or otherwise raise Funds on
economic terms, and/or within reasonable timescales. The Bank mitigates
these risks by holding an adequate portion of its assets in the Form of cash
balances and high-quality liquid assets, and by managing the maturity profile
of its Funding portfolio. The Bank manages its liquid assets to self-imposed
limits, in addition to regulatory metrics, that ensure access to sufficient
liquidity given a range of possible adverse scenarios.

Operational
risk

Operational risk is the risk of losses resulting From inadequate or failed
internal processes, people and systems or from external events, such as third-
party supplier Failure, cyber-attacks or Fraud. The Bank has a low tolerance
for operational losses and seeks to operate in such a way that these losses
are mitigated. The Bank recognises that some operational risk events are
inevitable and therefore ensures that significant risk events are logged,
investigated and subject to root-cause analysis.

Concentration
risk

Concentration risk arises From exposure to groups of connected
counterparties, or from counterparties in the same economic sector,
geographic region or From the same activity or commodity. As a provider of
unsecured personal loans and savings accounts in the UK consumer markets,
the Bank is exposed to concentration risk. However, this risk is actively
monitored, and limits are imposed on individual credit exposures and Funding
sources to mitigate this risk.

As a Board of Directors we consider, both collectively and individually, that we have acted in the
way we consider, in good faith, would be most likely to promote the success of the Bank for the
benefit of its stakeholders as a whole in the decisions taken during the year ended 31 March 2023.

JN Bank UK Ltd aims to be a Bank in which people want to invest, From which people want to
procure services, with which people want to partner and for which people want to work. This
requires the Board, Executive Committee, senior managers and other employees to maintain an
approach to strategic, Financial and operational decision-making that is values-based and
sustainable in approach, and therefore aligned to the requirements and expectations of Section
172.

The Following are how the Directors have had regards to the matters set out in sections 172 (a)-(F)
when discharging their section 172 duties and the effect of that on certain of the decisions taken
by them.

a. Likely consequences of any decision in the long term

As a Board of Directors, we have taken decisions for the long term, our aim is always to uphold high
standards of conduct and to behave responsibly to ensure that management also operate the
business in a responsible manner. We understand that our business can only grow and prosper over
the long term if we understand and respect the views and needs of our key stakeholders: our
regulators, customers, colleagues and the communities in which we operate, as well as our
suppliers, the environment and the shareholder to whom we are accountable. This is reflected in

Our governance is designed to ensure that we take into account the views of all our stakeholders,

our decisions public. Examples during the year include:

A decision was made to reduce and eventually pause lending to ensure that the business processes
were aligned with risk appetite. This decision was made by the Board and senior management.
Investors, regulators, and staff were kept informed at all stages. The longer terms plans were
regularly reviewed with the Board and investors who made the decision to keep supporting the
Bank's growth in the longer term.

A decision was made to transfer some intangible assets to the Ultimate Parent Company (The

advantages to the Bank in demonstrating an ability to sell loans. The sale would provide additional
asset management fees for the Bank and demonstrate that systems and processes were in place
enhancing prudential risk management. The sale of loans would also enable the Group to enhance
the management of market risk in businesses that have GB pound liabilities. This in turn would
develop a stronger group and hence greater longer term stability For the Bank.

Going Concern

Notwithstanding a loss for the year of £12.7m (2022: £6.5m loss), the pausing of lending in
December 2002 and resumption of lending on 10% July 2023, the Financial statements have been
prepared on the going concern basis, which the directors consider to be appropriate For the
Following reasons.

The directors have taken into account the Bank’s business plan and related Forecasts For the next
5 years up to 31 March 2028. These Forecasts indicate that, taking account of severe but plausible
downside scenarios the Bank will have sufficient resources to continue in business For 12 months
from the date of approval of these Financial statements.

The directors have modelled a severe but plausible downside scenario using macro-economic
assumptions that they consider to be at least as severe as those in the Bank of England's 2022
published stress scenario. These assumptions result in a significant increase in projected credit
losses in the severe but plausible scenario. This is driven by the uncertainty over the impact of
inflationary pressures in the economy.

The downside scenario indicates that the Bank is dependent on JN Financial Group Limited
providing additional Financial support during that period in order to continue with its business
plans. JN Financial Group Limited has nevertheless indicated its intention to continue to make
available funds as are planned by the Bank for the period covered by the Forecasts. In addition, the
directors have considered the capital strength of JN Financial Group Limited in their assessment of
the Group’s ability to provide the additional Financial support.

The financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis as the directors are
satisfied that the Bank will be able to operate with adequate levels of both liquidity and capital for

d.1 Measurement of ECLs

ECLs are an unbiased probability-weighted estimate of the present value of credit losses, taking
account of forward-looking information. ECLs are measured as the difference between contractual
cash flows and expected cash Flows arising from an asset, discounted at the asset's effective
interest rate.

When measuring ECLs, the Bank assesses the probability of default (“PD”), the expected exposure
at the time of default, exposure at default (EAD), and the loss that is expected to arise in the event
of default, loss given default (LGD). The maximum period considered is the maximum contractual
period over which the Bank is exposed to credit risk. The Bank typically assesses ECLs on an
individual asset basis.

IFRS 9 requires an impairment loss allowance to be recognised at an amount equal to either 12-
month ECLs or lifetime ECLs. Lifetime ECLs are the ECLs that result from all possible default events
over the expected life of a Financial instrument. 12-month ECLs are the ECLs that result from
default events that are possible within the 12 months after the reporting date.

The Bank measures impairment loss allowances at an amount equal to lifetime ECLs, except for the
Following which are measured as 12-month ECLs:

e treasury assets that are determined to have low credit risk at the reporting date. The Bank
considers a treasury asset to have low credit risk when its credit rating is equivalent to the
globally understood definition of ‘investment grade’; and

e other Financial assets on which credit risk has not increased significantly since their initial
recognition and which were not considered to be credit impaired at the time of initial
recognition.

Lifetime ECLs are required where the credit risk on a Financial asset has increased significantly since
initial recognition (except for investment grade treasury assets, as noted above), or where the
assets were credit impaired at the time of initial recognition.

h.1 Effective interest rate

Interest income and expense are recognised in profit or loss using the effective interest method.
The ‘effective interest rate’ is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash payments or
receipts through the expected life of the financial asset or Financial liability to the gross carrying
amount of the Financial asset or financial liability.

When calculating the effective interest rate For Financial instruments the Bank estimates Future
cash Flows considering all contractual terms of the Financial instrument, but not ECLs.

The calculation of the effective interest rate includes transaction costs and Fees paid that are an
integral part of the effective interest rate. Transaction costs include incremental costs that are
directly attributable to the acquisition or issue of a Financial asset or financial liability.

A key source of uncertainty is the rate of early settlement of loans (prepayments). A higher rate of
prepayments can change the estimated life of the loan and hence the EIR. Prepayments are
currently not easy to predict and there is insufficient observable data to apply a portfolio treatment
For prepayments. The Bank therefore apportions costs over the contractual tife of customer loans.
Any EIR balance resulting From capitalised costs is written off on settlement of loan ahead of the
contractual date.

j.3 Impairment of non-financial assets

For impairment testing, assets are grouped together into the smallest group of assets that
generates cash inflows from continuing use that is largely independent of the cash inflows of other
assets or Cash Generating Unit(s) (CGUs). Due to the simple nature of the bank the whole business
is treated as CGU.

The recoverable amount of an asset or CGU is the greater of its value in use and its Fair value less
costs to sell. Value in use is based on the estimated Future cash Flows, discounted to their present
value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of
money and the risks specific to the asset or CGU.

Animpairment loss is recognised if the carrying amount of an asset or CGU exceeds its recoverable
amount.

k) Employee benefits

k.1 Defined-contribution plans

A defined-contribution plan is a post-employment benefit plan under which the Bank pays fixed
contributions into a separate entity and will have no legal or constructive obligation to pay further
amounts. Obligations for contributions to defined-contribution pension plans are recognised as an
expense in profit or loss in the periods during which services are rendered by employees.

k.2 Short-term benefits

Short-term employee benefit obligations are measured on an undiscounted basis and are expensed
as the related service is provided. A liability is recognised for the amount expected to be paid under
short-term cash bonus or profit-sharing plans if the Bank has a present legal or constructive
obligation to pay this amount as a result of past service provided by the employee and the
obligation can be estimated reliably.

\) Provisions and contingencies

A provision is recognised in the statement of financial position when the Bank has a present legal
or constructive obligation as a result of a past event, that can be reliably measured, and it is
probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation.

Provisions are determined by discounting the expected Future cash Flows at a pre-tax rate that
reflects risks specific to the liability.

When it is not probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required, or the amount cannot
be estimated reliably, the obligation is disclosed as a contingent liability unless the probability of
outflow of economic benefits is remote. Contingent assets are not recognised in the Financial
statements but are disclosed when an inflow of economic benefits is probable.

n) Taxation

Tax on the profit or loss For the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the
income statement to the extent that it relates to items recognised directly in the statement of
profit and loss. Income tax relating to items recognised directly in equity or other comprehensive
income is recognised in equity or other comprehensive income respectively and not in the
Statement of Profit or Loss

Current tax is the expected tax payable or receivable on the taxable income or loss For the year,
using tax rates enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date, and any adjustment to tax
payable in respect of previous years.

Deferred tax is provided on temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and
liabilities For Financial reporting purposes and the amounts used For taxation purposes. The amount
of deferred tax provided is based on the expected manner of realisation or settlement of the
carrying amount.of assets and liabilities, using tax rates enacted or substantively enacted at the
reporting date.

The recognition of a deferred tax asset requires management to make assumptions concerning
Future taxable profits against which deferred tax assets can be recovered.

Deferred tax assets are recognised only when it is probable that taxable profits will be available
against which the deferred tax asset can be utilised. The Bank has considered the recoverability of
deferred tax assets taking into account the following:

e the availability of sufficient taxable temporary differences;

e the probability that the entity will have sufficient taxable profits in the foreseeable Future,
in the same period as the reversal of the deductible temporary difference or in the periods
into which a tax loss can be carried back or Forward; and

e the availability of tax planning opportunities that allow the recovery of deferred tax assets.

Valuation techniques

Fair values are determined using the Following Fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of
the inputs in measuring Fair value:

Level 1: Quoted prices For similar instruments

Level 2: Directly observable market inputs other than Level 1 inputs
Level 3: Inputs not based on observable market data

(i) Cash and cash equivalents

The fair values of cash and cash equivalents, which are carried at amortised cost are not considered
to be materially different From their book values. This is considered is reached due to their short-
term nature and the use of highly rated counterparties. As these are cash balances, they are
considered to be directly observable and considered to be level 2 measurements.

(ii) Loans to customers

To assess the likely Fair value of the Bank's loan assets in the absence of a liquid market, the
directors have considered the estimated cash Flows expected to arise From the Bank's investments
in its loans to customers based cn a mixture of market-based inputs, such as rates and pricing and
non-market based inputs such as redemption rates. Given the mixture of observable and non-
observable inputs these are considered to be level 3 measurements.

(iii) Investment Securities

The fair value of investment securities is disclosed by reference to market prices. Investment

29 Risk Management

The main areas of Financial risk that the business is exposed to are:
a. Credit risk;
b. Concentration credit risk;
c. Liquidity risk;
d. Market risk and;

a) Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of Financial loss to the Bank if a customer or counterparty to a financial
instrument Fails to meet its contractual obligations. The Bank recognises impairment loss
allowances For ECLs calculated as per IFRS 9 requirements at the statement of financial position
date.

Exposure to credit risk

Credit risk exposure is the amount of loss that the Bank would suffer if all counterparties to which
the Bank is exposed were to default at once. This is represented substantially by the gross carrying
amount of Financial assets, without taking account of the value of any collateral held.

There are no off-balance-sheet assets and the maximum exposure to credit risk is represented by
the amount of Financial assets in the statements of financial position.

Credit risk measurement
Loans and advances

The estimation of credit exposure For risk management purposes is complex and requires the use
of models, as the exposure varies with changes in market conditions, expected cash Flows and the
passage of time. The assessment of credit risk of a portfolio of assets involves estimations as to
the likelihood of defaults occurring, of the associated loss ratios and of default correlations
between counterparties. The Bank measures credit risk using Probability of Default (PD), Exposure
at Default (EAD) and Loss Given Default (LGD).

Credit risk grading

The Bank uses internal credit risk gradings that reflect its assessment of the credit-worthiness of
individual counterparties at origination date. Borrower and loan specific information collected at
the time of application is fed into this rating model. This is supplemented with external data such
as credit bureau scoring information on individual borrowers. In addition, the model enables expert
judgement from management to be fed into the final internal credit rating For each exposure. This
allows for considerations which may not be captured as part of the other data inputs into the
model.

The credit grades are calibrated such that the risk of default increases exponentially at each higher

Paucity of data

The credit models use historic data to make assumptions on credit performance. The
Bank has only been trading for 3 years and has limited historical data covering
performance of the loan book. The credit model therefore has a lower predictive ability
than a more mature model.

The Covid pandemic disrupted some of the relationships between macroeconomic
drivers and Macroeconomic Factors that drive the ECL models, in particular, the link
between economic activity and unemployment. Unemployment is a key driver of credit,
and this was heavy supported by government actions that are unlikely to reoccur.

The Ukrainian war delivered a sudden increase in inflation which has not been seen for
50 years. The ECL models used CPI and this is highly correlated, however the lack of
historic data of the portfolio in changing CPI increases the potential for variability in the
modelled outcomes.

The Bank finds that while the ECL models have significant correction to historical data
the volatile economics drivers of the last Few years may lead to the model becoming less
predictive in more stable times. In these circumstances there is a higher risk of default
than the models predict. The Bank therefore has used judgment to increase the
provision For expected credit losses in the year. This is determined by adding an
additional 45bps provision for all loans and a further 30bps on loans that the credit
models have not calculated any indications provision for credit risk. ie stage 1 loans.

Interest rate risk is the potential adverse impact on the Bank's future cash flows From changes in interest
rates and arises from the differing interest rate risk characteristics of the Bank’s assets and liabilities. In

a reduction in interest income or an increase in interest expense relative to variable rate interest flows.

e) Capital risk and management

The Bank has operated in line with its capital risk appetite as set by the Board and above its
regulatory requirements throughout the period ended 31 March 2023.

Capital is held to protect depositors, by ensuring that the Bank has sufficient capital resources to
repay liabilities even in the face of unexpected losses. Day-to-day capital management is delegated
to the Chief Financial Officer and overseen by the risk function, ALCO, Risk Committee: and
ultimately the Board. The Bank's objective in managing its capital is to maintain appropriate levels
of capital to support its business strategy and to meet regulatory requirements at all times. The
Bank manages its capital in accordance with Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and Capital
Requirements Directive (together referred to as CRD IV). The framework is enforced by the PRA.
The PRA sets and monitors the Bank's capital requirements.

The PRA's approach to the measurement of capital adequacy is primarily based on monitoring the
relationship of the capital resources requirement to available capital resources. The regulator sets
Total Capital Requirement (TCR) For each bank in excess of the minimum resources requirement of
8% of risk weighted assets. A key input to the TCR setting process is the Bank's Internal Capital
Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP).

The Bank's policy is to maintain a strong capital base to maintain stakeholder and market
confidence and to sustain the Future development of the business. The Bank monitors current and
Forecast levels of capital against the capital risk appetite approved by the Board and reports the
capital position to ALCO, Risk Committee and the Board on a regular basis. The capital Forecast
forms an integral component of the annual budgeting process and is updated in line with changes
to the Bank's business plan.
