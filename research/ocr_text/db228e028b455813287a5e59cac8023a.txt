Chairman's Statement

| am happy to present my second Annual Financial Report of Persia International Bank (“PIB”). The
Year 2014 -15 continued to be a challenging period for PIB as the Bank has to continue to function in an
environment of constraints amidst great expectations of easing of the sanctions.

Notwithstanding the uncertain business environment and the reduced resources available to the Bank,
PIB was able to post a net profit of € 518k on a total asset base of € 225m.

The sanctions regime imposed by the UN, EU and USA has impacted the Iranian Banks severely,
especially those entities registered in Europe. Correspondent banking relationships with other
international banks were a casualty as Iranian banks were denied many essential banking services like
clearing and intermediation of money transfers, owing to the sanctions regime. It is the strength of PIB
that it has remained resilient during this period and has met all its regulatory and legal requirements and
has learned to function within the constraints. This is due to the inherent strength of PIB and its
management structure that, despite all the sanctions and the consequent operational restrictions, the
Bank has been able to function and maintain itself as a going concern.

The sanctions regime is expected to be removed due to the positive developments in the understanding
between Iran and the western countries on the nuclear issue. However it is discouraging to learn about
the rejection by the EU Council of the well considered judgements of the EU General Court annulling
some of the EU notifications in the past imposing sanctions on PIB, Bank Mellat and Bank Tejarat. The
EU council has appealed against the court judgements in respect of Bank Mellat and has relisted PIB
and Bank Tejarat.

The global macroeconomic scene in the past year was characterised by low oil prices and historically
low interest rates. The continuing tow oil price in the world market has severely impacted many
countries where oil receipts form significant proportions of their GDPs. Markets may continue to be
impacted by the political crises around the world and the general economic scenario.

It is therefore a significant achievement for the Bank to show a profit before tax of € 613K for the
financial year 2014-15, despite the long term designation of the Bank. The balance sheet size of the
Bank is € 225 million reflecting, a gradual reduction over the previous five years, from over € 1 billion,
following repayment of most of the loans and the return of customer deposits. The major portion of
customer deposits have been repaid under HMT licenses and the remaining portion of unpaid deposits
to customers and banks can be dealt with easily in due course. As disclosed in Note 25 of the financial
statements, the number of overdue loan accounts has reduced significantly. The remaining overdue
loan accounts are sufficiently collateralised and the loan book of the Bank remains sound. The loan
impairment provision for this year is only € 527K, which was offset by recoveries of €289k on loans
previously written off. The liquidity position of the Bank remains very strong.

The regulatory changes have become very significant during the year 2014 and will continue to be
challenging in the years to come. PIB has successfully moved into the new regulatory reporting regime,
submitting the returns under COREP. In Dubai PIB has also conformed to the new DFSA returns regime
which is being implemented from Q1 2015.

As in the last four years, no dividend is proposed for this year 2014-15.

The post sanctions period is of much interest now, and the PIB management has a comprehensive
reactivation plan in place in order to protect our customer relationships and restore our business. The
Bank will continue to engage with its customers, past, present and future to sustain a business
relationship which has been interrupted for the time being only. PIB is fully committed to conform and
comply with the legal and regulatory requirements of the UK Government and its regulators. The Bank
has retained a small team of core staff, and values their skills which will stand it in good stead when we
resume normal business in the future. We confidently look forward to the dawning of a new era.

Strategic Report

Introduction

Persia International Bank is an authorised institution under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. Under normal
operating conditions the principal activity of the Bank is the provision of commercial banking services to its customers.
However, on 27 July 2010 the EU imposed Financial Sanctions on the Bank, freezing all its financial assets and
restricted its functions. The Bank was subjected to a licensing regime, requiring Her Majesty's Treasury (HMT)
authorisations to initiate all transactions and no new business can be undertaken. The Bank can continue to receive
loan repayments in respect of prior contracts into a designated frozen account. Whilst there is no allegation that the
Bank has violated or breached any UK or EU laws or regulations, the sanctions were imposed for the ostensible reason
that Persia International Bank was 60% owned by Bank Mellat (Iran). The Bank appealed to the EU General Court
against the sanctions and succeeded in its appeal, and the Court's judgement on 06 September 2013 annulled the
European Union sanctions notification. Though this judgement was never challenged by the EU Council, the sanctions
were re-imposed afresh on 15 November 2013 on the premise that PIB is owned by two designated entities, namely
Bank Mellat and Bank Tejarat which had themselves been listed previously.

Business Review

Prior to the EU sanctions, the Bank offered a comprehensive service in trade finance and credit facilities to its
corporate customers. Under trade finance, the Bank issued letters of credit, added confirmation and provided short
term credit facilities by discounting the bills drawn under them. It extended credit facilities to its corporate customers
either on bilateral terms or as part of a syndicate. Iranian corporate customers constituted a major portion of the
customer base of the Bank. The Bank had been performing well since its inception until 2010. A strong customer base
was built up with a robust trade finance and corporate credit portfolio.

The first international branch was opened in August 2005 at Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC), Dubai which
further expanded its trade finance capabilities. However, the Dubai branch operation was suspended by the Dubai
regulator (DFSA) in March 2010 and the branch has remained inactive throughout the last five financial years.

The balance sheet total of the Bank is at € 225,013k which is marginally higher than in the previous year owing mainly
to exchange rate movements. The balance sheet size is reducing in line with repayments in the loans portfolio and
customer deposits. Trade finance activities ceased as of July 2011.

Financial Performance during the year 2014-15

The interest income for the year to 31 March 2015 has declined by 9.65% from €5,691k in the previous year to
€5,142k in the current period. Profit after tax has increased from €491k to €518k during the same period. The increase
in profit is mainly due to writing back of provisions following recoveries in respect of impaired investment account, the
final dividend received on account of a loan account fully written off and also due to reversal of the impairment of the
Dubai property. The Bank's income is presently derived mainly from interest income on the Nostro balance with Bank
Markazi, Central Bank of Iran, and on loans arranged prior to the EU sanctions, and from the amortisation of fee
income.

Our fee & commission income is €38k and fee & commission paid out is €15k compared to €64k and €13k respectively
in the previous year. The FX loss on revaluation of open positions in foreign currency assets is €56k compared to
€145k loss in the previous year.

The total administrative expenses for the financial year are €4,220k compared to €4,460k during the previous year. The
impairment charge for the year net of recoveries is €238k in the current year and €167k in the previous year.
Depreciation cost has increased from €17k to €96k due to recognising depreciation of the new fit out of our Dubai
branch. During this year the Dubai property was re-valued resulting in a reversal of a previous impairment of €1,040k.

We used to provide the whole range of trade finance services which included documentary credits, advising and

confirmation of LCs and discounting of Trade Bills drawn on banks, which generated significant discounts and fee
income. However this activity ended sometime in July 2011 due to EU sanctions.

Risk Assessment and Management

The directors have identified, compliance risk, concentration risk, credit risk, succession risk and IT risk as the major
risks faced by the Bank in the present situation. The board and the management believe that they have taken adequate
measures to mitigate these primary risks. Steps have been initiated to diversify payment arrangements and advance
payments have been made to secure essential services and supplies, withdrawal of which could severely hamper our
routine functions. The Bank's liquidity position is strong with major liabilities settled and the remaining liabilities
manageable. The mismatch in the maturities of the balance sheet assets and liabilities is not a cause of concern.
Though a significant portion of its short term funds are kept with Bank Markazi in Iran, the Bank has access to sufficient
liquid funds within the EU to meet its obligations and operating costs for the foreseeable future. The Directors are
satisfied that the Bank will remain a going concern for the foreseeable future and they remain confident regarding its
longer term prospects.

Financial reporting risks are managed through control activities performed at all levels of the organisation. These
activities include guidelines and instructions, approvals, authorisations, verifications, reconciliations, analytical reviews,
and segregation of duties. The Bank has also established a Risk Committee headed by a non-executive director. Risk
committee (see Note 25 for details) monitors, reviews and updates all other risks within the Bank. in terms of internal
control and financial reporting information, external and internal information is obtained systematically, and the Bank's
management is provided with relevant information on the Bank’s activities. Timely, current and accessible information
relevant for financial reporting purposes is provided to the appropriate functionaries, such as the Board, the senior
management group and relevant committees.

The Board has the overall responsibility for the proper arrangement of internal contro! and risk management over
financial reporting. The Board has appointed the Audit Committee (members are two independent non-executive
directors) to undertake more specific tasks in relation to financial reporting process contro! and monitoring the efficiency
of the Bank's internal control. The Audit Committee also reviews regularly the main features of the internal control and
risk management systems pertaining to the financial reporting process.

To ensure the effectiveness of internal control pertaining to financial reporting, monitoring activities are conducted at all
levels of the organization. Monitoring is performed through ongoing follow-up activities, separate evaluations, or a
combination of the two. Separate internal audit assignments may be initiated by the Board or management. The scope
and frequency of separate evaluations depend primarily on the assessment of risks and the effectiveness of ongoing
monitoring procedures. Internal control deficiencies are reported to the management and serious matters of
deficiencies to the Board.

There were no frauds or serious matters reported during the past year. The Board regularly reviews the financial
reports, including comparison of actual figures with prior periods and budgets, other forecasts and monthly cash flow
estimates. The Bank has a full set of policies and procedure manuals. These cover all of the Bank's activities and are
reviewed and revised annually by the Board and the executive directors respectively. Management accounts are
prepared monthly and these form the basis for the annual accounts.

Future Developments

Although the Bank is unable to function normally in the present situation, as far as possible we maintain operational
readiness and capability to work towards medium term objectives. We have a reactivation plan in place; we have
identified our future staffing requirements and, once we are permitted to return to normality, we will commence a
recruiting process. We appreciate that this will take time, but we are ready for this challenge. The Bank is very
conscious of its environment, and to this end ensures that it fully complies with all regulatory and legal requirements of
the UK Government and both the Prudential Regulation Authority and Financial Conduct Authority. The prospects for
lifting of the EU/UN and OFAC sanctions have increased with the statement of P5 +1 countries and Iran on 2 April
2015. The final agreement is expected to be signed by 30 June 2015. Once the sanctions regime is lifted, the Bank
intends to diversify its money market placements within and outside Iran to maximise returns and to develop its
business. A comprehensive review of the IT system is being undertaken to identify the right core banking software for
the Bank. PIB has a comprehensive action plan in place to reactivate the business once the sanctions are lifted.

Financial statements

The financial statements are prepared in Euros (€), as this is the currency in which the Bank conducts its
principal activities.

Principal activities

The principal activities of Persia international Bank are international banking and related services within the
scope of permission granted to the Bank under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000.

Matters included within the Strategic Report

Details of business review, future developments, indication of financial risk management objectives and policies,
and the Bank’s exposure to market risk, credit risk and liquidity risk have all been included in the Strategic
Report.

Business review and future developments

The Bank is incorporated and domiciled in the UK. The financial statements have been prepared under
International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) as adopted by the European Union. The Bank is required by
the Companies Act 2006 to set out in this report a fair view of the business of the Bank during the financial year
ended 31 March 2015 and of the position of the Bank at the end of the year and a description of the principal
risks and uncertainties facing the Bank. The business review and an analysis of future developments are given
in the Strategic Report.

Going Concern

The Directors have assessed the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern and are satisfied that the Bank
has the resources to continue in business for at least the next 12 months and for the foreseeable future. Further,
the directors are not aware of any material uncertainties which may cast significant doubt upon the Bank's ability
to continue as a going concern. The Bank's liquidity position is strong with major liabilities settled and the
remaining liabilities can be easily managed. Although as a result of the sanctions, the funds with the Central
Bank of Iran cannot be easily accessed within the EU, the funds within the EU itself along with our cash
holdings are available to PIB to meet its operational requirements, which require only local authorising bodies
and HM Treasury license. The total funds available within EU and London are €22,804k and the average
administrative expenses for one year are €5,300k. The Bank can meet its operational expenses for the
foreseeable future within the funds available in EU and London.

The directors derive confidence from the fact that the Bank, under the present management, has survived the
challenges of the last five years and are satisfied with the present position of the Bank in terms of its capital
adequacy and liquidity. It is for these reasons that the financial statements continued to be prepared on a going
concern basis.

Share capital
The total issued share capital is Euro 100 million. There were no changes in the year.
Results and Dividends

The results for the year are set out on page 12. The profit attributable to shareholders for the financial year
amounted to €518k, a 0.490% return on average shareholders’ equity (2013/14: €491k, 0.491%). This has been
transferred to reserves. Liquidity is very strong, the Tier 1 Capital ratio is also very robust and net loan
impairments are oniy 0.48% of the total loans for this year. Given the sanctions and the resulting significant
changes to the Bank's operations, the only other key performance indicator relevant to the Bank's results is
compliance, which is addressed in the Strategic Report. Owing to the restrictions imposed by sanctions; the
Bank's results are no longer under the direct control of the directors.

No dividend was paid during the period (2013/14: nil). The directors do not recommend payment of a dividend
for the year ended 31 March 2015.

General Information

The Bank came under the purview of EU sanctions on 27 July 2010. The immediate impact was the freezing of
all the financial assets of the Bank and restrictions in conducting business. The follow up measures during the
succeeding years further restricted business and maintaining business relationships with other banks, which
severely restricted the normal operations of the Bank. Service providers like SWIFT, Reuters and software
companies also declined to have business relationship with the Bank. The Bank is under the licensing regime
and all transactions have to be approved by HMT either by a general license or specific license.

The Bank can continue to receive loan repayments, into designated frozen accounts. There is no allegation that
the Bank has broken any UK or EU laws or regulations. The sanctions are imposed on Persia International Bank
for the ostensible reason that it is 60% owned by Bank Mellat (Iran). During the period from the date of imposing
sanctions until now, HMT has issued a number of 'General Licenses' and ‘Specific Licences’ to enable the Bank
to function and meet its obligations to staff, customers and regulators. These licenses inter alia permit the Bank
to receive funds under prior contracts, in terms of the contractual agreement. Such funds will be received only in
designated frozen accounts.

Since 27 July 2010 HMT has granted a number of licenses to the Bank. These allow, inter alia, the following:

« Payments due to the Bank under contracts, agreements or obligations concluded before 27 July 2010 to
be paid into a frozen account.
Payments to be made to staff, suppliers and depositors holding accounts with the Bank.
A third party may present to the Bank, claims under a letter of credit with supporting documents
evidencing the transactions and an interest in the settlement or financial resources or any other
instrument of export financing, provided the transaction related to a contract and the documents in
question are entered into before 27 July 2010,

»* Specific licenses are also issued at the request of the Bank to release securities taken as collaterals for
a given loan. The Bank expects this arrangement to continue in respect of repayments of contractual
obligations and routine payments.

Operating segments

An operating segment is a component of the Bank that engages in business activities from which it may earn
revenues and incur expenses. Its operating results are reviewed regularly by the Bank's Management Committee
(which comprises the CEO, CFO and COO and it meets at least once a month) and the Asset Liability Committee
(see Note 25 for details). Decisions about resource allocation are made and the performance can be assessed
using the available discrete financial information. The Chief Executive Officer is the chief operating decision
maker of the Bank.

Interest income and expense

Interest income and expense on financial assets and liabilities measured at amortised cost is recognised in the
statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method, which allocates the income or
expense over the relevant period. The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future
cash flows to the carrying value on initial recognition, through the expected life of the financial asset or liability. In
respect of impaired loans where it is probable that interest on the loan will not flow to the entity, interest income is
not recognised in the statement of comprehensive income.

Fee and commission income

Fees receivable which represent a return for services provided are credited to income when the related service is
performed. Fees receivable, which represent a return for risk borne and are in the nature of interest, are taken to
the statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method over the expected life of the
transaction to which they relate.

Provisions
A provision is recognised if, as a result of a past event, the Bank has a present legal or constructive obligation
that can be estimated reliably, and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the

obligation. Provisions are determined by discounting the expected future cash flows at a pre-tax rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money, and where appropriate, the risks specific to the liability.

Financial assets:

Financial assets are classified into the following categories:

e Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss: a financial asset is classified as such if it is held
principally for the purpose of selling in the short term. In the current and prior year, the Bank did not
hold any financial assets at fair value through profit or toss.

e loans and receivables: are non-derivative financial assets with fixed or determinable payments not
quoted in an active market; and

* available for sale: are financial assets not classified in one of the above categories.

Loans and receivables are initially recognised at fair value, plus directly attributable transaction costs, and
subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method.

Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss are initially and subsequently measured at fair value with
changes in fair value taken to the statement of comprehensive income.

Available for sale investments are non-derivative investments that are designated as available for sale or are not
classified as another category of financial assets. They are carried at fair value. Available for sale financial
assets are initially and subsequently measured at fair value, with changes in fair value recognised directly in
equity until disposal, when the cumulative gain or loss is taken to profit or loss and recognised in the statement of
comprehensive income. Foreign exchange gains and losses, interest income under the effective interest rate
method and impairment losses arising on available-for-sale securities are taken to profit or loss and recognised in
the statement of comprehensive income.

Financial liabilities and equity

Financial liabilities and equity instruments are initially recognised at fair value; this occurs when the Bank
becomes a party to the contractual provisions of the corresponding instruments. The Bank derecognises financial
liabilities when its contractual obligations are discharged or cancelled or expire. Financial liabilities and equity
instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity
instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the Bank after deducting all of its
liabilities. Financial liabilities comprise amounts due to other banks, customer accounts and subordinated debt
liabilities. They are measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Equity instruments issued by
the Bank are recorded at the proceeds received, net of direct issue costs.

Impairment of financial assets

A financial asset is impaired and impairment losses are incurred if there is objective evidence of impairment, as a
result of events that occurred after initial recognition of the financial asset. The amount of the loss is measured,
for financial assets held at amortised cost and as available-for-sale, as the difference between the carrying
amount of the asset and the value of the revised estimated future cash flows, discounted at the asset's original
effective interest rate. Financial assets are written off only when it is reasonably certain that any future cash flows
are irrecoverable.

Given the sanctions, the Bank has not entered into any new loans since 27 July 2010 and an assessment as to
the recoverability of each exposure in the portfolio is performed at the balance sheet date or earlier if a definite
impairment has occurred.

Operating lease rentals

Rentals payable under operating leases are accounted for on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

Valuation of financial instruments

The Bank measures fair values using a fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in
making the measurements.

Level 1: Quoted market price (unadjusted) in an active market for an identical instrument is taken as the fair
value.

Level 2: Valuation techniques based on observable inputs, either directly (ie. as prices) or indirectly (i.e. derived
from prices). This category includes instruments valued using: quoted market prices in active markets for similar
instruments; quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered less than active: or
other valuation techniques where all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.

Level 3: Under this category the Bank's assumptions about the market and market conditions are used in pricing
the asset and based on the best information available in the circumstances and not merely based on observable
market data (unobservable inputs).

Property plant and equipment

Tangible fixed assets are stated at cost less provision for depreciation and any impairment in value. Depreciation
of equipment, fixtures and fittings is provided on a straight-line basis over estimated useful lives - 3 years.

Freehold property is depreciated over 50 years from the date it is available for use.
Impairment of property, plant and equipment

The carrying amount of the Bank's property, plant and equipment are reviewed at each reporting date to
determine whether there is any indication of impairment. If any such indication exists, then the asset's
recoverable amount is estimated. The recoverable amount of an asset is the greater of its value in use and its fair
value less costs to sell. In assessing value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present
value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects current market assessment of the time value of money and the
risks specific to the asset. Impairment loss is recognised, if the carrying amount of an asset exceeds its estimated
recoverable amount. Impairment losses are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income. Reversals of
impairment losses are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income to the extent that the increased
carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined had no impairment loss
been recognised for the asset in the prior years.

Taxation
(a) Current income tax

Income tax payable (receivable) is calculated.on the basis of the applicable tax-law in the respective jurisdiction
and is recognised as an expense (income) for. the period except to the extent that current tax related to items that
are charged or credited in other comprehensive income or directly to equity. In these circumstances, current tax
is charged or credited to other comprehensive income or to equity. .

(b) Deferred income tax

Deferred income tax is normally provided in full, using the liability method, on temporary differences arising
between the tax bases of assets and liabilities and their carrying amounts in the financial statements. Deferred
income tax is determined using tax rates (and laws) that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the date
of the statement of financial position and are expected to apply when the related deferred income tax asset is
realised or the deferred income tax liability is settled. The tax effects of carry-forwards of unused losses or
unused tax credits are recognised as an asset when it is probable that future taxable profits will be available
against which these losses can be utilised.

Pensions and other post-retirement benefits

The Bank operates a defined contribution scheme, charging contributions to profit or loss within the statement of
comprehensive income as they become payable in accordance with the rules of the scheme.

Foreign currencies

Transactions in foreign currencies are recorded at the rate of exchange at the date of the transaction. A
monetary item is a right to receive or an obligation (contractual or constructive) to deliver a fixed or determinable
number of units of currency. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies at the reporting
date are reported at the rates of exchange prevailing at that date. Translation differences on monetary items are
recognised in profit or loss within the statement of comprehensive income. The average rate for £/€ applied
during the year was 0.7819 (2013/14; 0.8421).

4, Capital Adequacy:

PIB has adopted Standardised Approach (SA) to assess its Credit Risk and the Basic Indicator

The PRA has set the Individual Capital Guidance (ICG) for the Bank and the Capital Planning Buffer

capital that the Bank is required to hold so it is available to absorb losses or to cover increasing

control. Based on the stressed capital plan, the PRA has identified a fixed sum as a CPB that PIB
should hold to absorb losses, should adverse circumstances materialise that are outside the normal

PIB produces the ICAAP document annually incorporating the ICG and CPB as outlined above.
ICAAP has documented the additional capital requirements over and above the ICG and CPB
requirements.

PIB also produces an Individual Liquidity Adequacy Assessment (ILAA) document every year to
apprise the Board of the Bank's on-going assessment and quantification of liquidity risks and how
those risks are mitigated and how much current and future liquidity is required in relation to the

5. Risk Management:
5.1 Credit Risk:

PIB is exposed to credit risk in its on and off balance sheet loan assets. The risk is mitigated by
setting counterparty limits, industry sector limits and country exposure limits for counterparties within
its overall architecture of credit risk management. The relevant policy and procedure guidelines are
documented in the ‘Credit and Concentration Risk’ policy and ‘Credit and Concentration Risk’
procedures manual. Day-to-day management of credit is the responsibility of the credit department.
The ALCO reviews the policies and is responsible for the overall management of credit risk. PIB has a
conservative approach to loan exposures and secures most of the loans _ with collateral, which always
exceed the related exposure by a significant margin. PIB follows Standardised Approach (SA) to
assess the credit risk. PIB will make use of third party ratings if it is required and such ratings of the
counterparties are available. The credit portfolio of the Bank is simple, in that we do not have any
complicated exposures to derivatives or such other complex credit instruments. Syndicated and
bilateral loans to corporate customers and banks constitute the loan assets of the Bank.

PIB was active in Trade Finance business with its niche client base focused in Iran. Trade Finance is
a short term self liquidating business, which generates fee income. The credit risk is relatively low.
However, the Trade Finance business has ceased to exist due to imposition of EU sanctions. The
Bank will resume its Trade Finance activities once the sanctions regime is removed. Under Trade
Finance, PIB acts as the advising and / or negotiating bank on letters of credit that have been issued
to finance exports to Iran. This is a credit-risk free service, and requires no capital support. Credit
risk is zero. PIB also acts as a payment Bank on instructions received from shareholder banks. This is
a risk free transaction as funds will be paid out only when applicable funds are available in the
sharehoider banks. However it poses an operational and reputation risk for the Bank. Capital will be
allocated for operational and reputational risks.

Discounting:

PIB generally extends lines of credit to a list of counterparty banks for maintaining Nostro balances,
Money Market and overnight placements. Bank lines are also utilised for discounting the L/Cs of the
respective banks. Such discounting are for a maximum period of one year. A large proportion of this
portfolio is made up of the discounting of LC’s issued by Iranian banks including the two shareholder
banks, Bank Mellat and Bank Tejarat. The bank usually maintains a significant credit portfolio of such
discounting facilities. This is bank exposure risk and capital allocated at 100% risk weighting.

« Guarantees:
PIB acts as a guarantor to the importers and exporters of goods and services by providing guarantees
that the beneficiary will fulfil the contract and ship the goods. The guarantees issued are always
secured, often being counter guaranteed by the beneficiaries’ bankers, usually International Banks, or
by a deposit pledged for an amount equal to the value of the guarantee. This is virtually credit risk free
as guarantees are issued against 100% security, if in the same currency or 100% + approved margin
if in some other currency. Contingency risk capital will be allocated.

6. Market risk

The Market Risk exposure for PIB arises due to open positions in currencies, primarily in AEDs and to
a small extent in other currencies. All financial instruments are held in the banking book and are
marked to the market. The gains or losses are immediately recognised in the Profit and Loss account.

The fixed asset in Dubai is valued in AEDs, the currency of purchase. However, in accordance with
International Accounting Standards IAS 21, the value of the property is determined as on the date of
transaction or valuation and therefore the values are not re-translated once again on the accounting
dates. This leaves only a small proportion of open positions in financial assets, the loss or gain of
which is recognised in the P&L account. PIB values its open positions on a monthly basis on a fair
value basis and adjusts for any gain or loss through the profit & loss account. The value of the open
positions related to financial assets is considered for the Risk Weighted Exposure (RWE).

As at 31 March 2015 the FX spot position risk: € 4,128,000
7. Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of losses arising from deficiencies or failures in internal processes, human
factor or from external systems and events. This definition includes legal risks, but does not include
strategic or reputational risks. In most financial institutions, these risks relate to a wide range of
potential failures in a firm's activity: fraud, human errors and damage to operational capability of
computer systems, loss of information, theft, and fire (i.e. events that are not directly related to credit
and market risks).

Other deficient operational control procedures may relate to account opening procedures, the input of
customer details to computer systems, regulations and procedures for transferring information to the
customer, balance verification procedures in customers’ accounts, loan extension procedures, non-
maintenance of separation between different functions, etc.

The bank maintains a comprehensive business continuity/disaster recovery plan. This incorporates an
off-site location capable of continuing the Bank’s minimum operations for a short period. Disaster
recovery procedures are in place and are reviewed and tested once a year. All data in the server are
backed up on a daily basis and stored off-site. The recovery plan includes the Dubai branch
operation. The Bank has outsourced its Internal Audit function to a professional firm, which reports to
the Audit Committee on the adequacy of the internal control systems and monitoring aspects.

The bank currently has one branch in Dubai, DIFC opened in August 2005 and a representative office
in Tehran opened in 2008. The branch ledgers are maintained on the Head office computer system
with full back-up. The relevant Head Office departments have access to the branch ledgers and Head
Office is able to monitor the business continuously and regularly. The branch has limited credit
extension limits which relate to short term (up to one year) trade finance discounting. Their credit

approval process follows the London credit policy and procedures. All non-trade finance credit
applications are recommended to London for approval, where they are subjected to the normal credit
assessment procedures. The credit functions are centralised in London and hence credit risk is not

The Bank's committees’ responsibilities have been extended to incorporate the branch business and

Calculation of Risk Weighted Exposure (RWE) for Operational Risk for the whole Bank:

We follow the Basic Indicator Approach for calculating RWE for capital adequacy purposes. The
Operational Risk Requirement (ORR) is 15% of three years’ average of Net Interest Income (NII) +
Commission Income + Other income. Then ORR is multiplied by 12.5 to arrive at the Risk Weighted
Exposure, which as at 31 March 2015 was € 11,501,000.

interest rate risk can arise in the banking book but the bank’s policy is to reduce it by using floating

9. Remuneration

The Board takes overall responsibility for the remuneration of all the staff in the Bank, on which it is advised by
the Remuneration Committee and the Executive management. The only exception to this is the remuneration of
the Board itself, including that of the Executive and Non-Executive Directors, which is determined by the
Chairman, but complies with the PRA’s and /or FCA’s Remuneration Code.

The Principles

The Board’s general policy is to offer remuneration, which will be sufficient to ensure that the Bank is able to
attract and retain staff, who are well qualified to perform the functions of each post, in such a manner as will,
when aggregated with the rest of the Bank’s employment policies, achieve the Bank's policy objectives
efficiently. To this end, the Bank’s policy is, so far as is reasonably possible, to pay market rates of remuneration
for all employment posts.

Salary

Salary makes up much the largest part of staff remuneration, and the level applied to each post reflects the
responsibility, which it carries, and also the skill required of the holder, as indicated by the market. Other factors
taken into account in determining appropriate remuneration at the level of the individual include performance in
the post, commitment, attendance and, to a minor extent, length of service, in so far as it reflects valuable
experience.

The Bank takes the “pulse” of the London market by reference to data from recruitment agencies and other
informal sources of remuneration paid by a range of institutions in the City of London. For the overseas offices,
respected local market surveys are used. Salaries are paid in cash monthly in arrears, subject to deduction of tax
and National Insurance contributions. They are fixed (normally for one year in advance), but are reviewed
annually, following formal appraisals, It is the Bank’s policy, where possible, to maintain the purchasing power of
salaries, by making an annual adjustment to reflect upward movements in the Retail Prices Index. The exception
to this procedure is the salaries paid to Directors, which are reviewed every two years.

The salaries of the Non-executive British Directors are reviewed as and when necessary by the Chairman,
also by reference to the practice of other relevant banks in the City.

The remuneration of the expatriate Iranian Executive Directors comprises seven elements, (i) basic salary, (ii)
housing allowance, (ili) car allowance, (iv) a benefit in kind in the form of private health insurance cover, (v)
annual profit-related bonus, (vi) an annual allowance towards travel cost for the directors and their families to
return home, and (vii) a lump sum is also payable on retirement from the Bank. All seven elements are subject to
deduction of UK Income Tax and Employees’ National Insurance contributions, but these charges are borne by
the Bank, not the Directors. The result is that the Directors receive a net sum each month. All the Directors’
salaries are reviewed every other year and adjusted, if deemed appropriate, by the Chairman, with advice, where
needed, from the Remuneration Committee.

Variable remuneration:

It has become the Bank's practice to pay part of employees’ remuneration by way of profit related variable
remuneration. However, the award of any such variable remuneration is entirely at the discretion of the Board.
The level of profit-related variable remuneration depends upon the annual profits of the Bank as a whole, and it is
determined by the Board, following the recommendation of the Remuneration Committee.

Pension

The Bank has arranged for its staff a non-contributory Group Personal Pension Plan This is a defined contribution
pension scheme, and the Bank makes contributions at rates of 7 - 20% of basic salary, depending upon the age
of the member.
