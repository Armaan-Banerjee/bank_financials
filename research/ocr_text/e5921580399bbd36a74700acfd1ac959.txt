Chairman’s Statement

This is my first Annual Financial Report of Persia International Bank PLC (“PIB”) since | became Chairman
of the Bank on 7 July 2015. The Financial Year 2015-16 is a very significant one for the Bank, as it
emerged from the constraints of a prolonged sanctions regime to re-join the international banking
community.

However, more than 5 years of sanctions and continuing denial of access to both the UK and international
payments systems have taken their toll, and for the first time in the history of the Bank, a loss of €498k
was posted for the financial year 2015-16. The business environment is still uncertain and many obstacles
to the Iranian banks resuming normal operations are yet to be resolved.

The sanctions regime imposed on Iran by the UN, EU and USA, related to the development of Iran's
nuclear capacity, was lifted on 16 January 2016. All the de-listed Iranian banks had anticipated the
resumption of normal banking activities with international banking institutions within a reasonable period
of time. However, this was not to be the case. Some US sanctions on Iran remain in force, and most
western international banks are nervous of being held to account by US Federal and State regulators for
breach of any of those sanctions. International banks are therefore unwilling to trade with, or enter into
correspondent relationships with, Iranian banks. Certain issues, notably compliance and legal, relating to
dealings with Iran and Iranian institutions remain, and urgent and concerted efforts are being made by all
interested parties to resolve them. Encouragingly, agreement has been reached for PIB shortly to jain
the Target 2 payments system through Deutsche Bundesbank.

The new enhanced risk weighting of exposures to Iran and Iranian entities, which requires the allocation
of increased amounts of capital to Iranian risks, is likely to be a dampener on the volume growth of our
business. This, coupled with the current low interest regime, which is anticipated to persist for some time
yet, is expected to have an adverse effect on the future profitability of the Bank.

The global macroeconomic scene in the past year was characterised by low oil prices and historically low
interest rates, which have even fallen into negative territory in certain countries. The continuing low oil
price in the world market has had a severe impact on many countries where oil receipts form significant
proportions of their GDPs, although oil revenues account for only some 15% of Iran’s GDP and some 30%
of Iran's government expenditure. Markets continue to be affected by political crises around the world and
the generally poor economic outlook.

It is therefore a significant achievement for the Bank to have reported profits after tax in four out of the
five previous years. Its balance sheet size have fallen to €222million reflecting a gradual reduction over
the previous six years, from over €‘billion, following the repayment of most loans and the return of
customer deposits. As disclosed in Note 20 of the financial statements, the number of overdue loan
accounts has been reduced significantly. The net loan impairment charge for this year is only €251k. The
remaining overdue loan accounts are sufficiently collateralised to need no provision, and the rest of the
loan book remains sound. The liquidity position of the Bank remains very strong.

Regulatory changes have become very significant during the year 2015-16 and will continue to be
challenging in the years to come. PIB has successfully moved into the new regulatory reporting regime,
submitting returns under COREP. In Dubai PIB has also conformed to the new DFSA returns regime.

As in the last five years, no dividend is proposed for this year 2015-16.

The PIB management and the staff are focused on preparing the Bank for the early resumption of trading,
even as they await resolution of the issues surrounding correspondent banking relationships. New
personnel are being recruited, to complement the small team of core staff, who have been retained since
the imposition of sanctions in 2010. The Bank intends to engage with its customers, past, present and
future, in order to sustain business relationships which have been interrupted during the sanctions regime.
PIB is fully committed to conforming and complying with the legal and regulatory requirements of the UK
Government and its regulators. We confidently took forward to a new period of business growth and
challenges.

| take this opportunity to thank the Board members and the staff of P!B for their continued dedication and
loyalty to the institution.

Strategic Report

The directors present their strategic report for Persia International Bank ple for the year ended 31 March 2016.

Introduction

Persia International Bank (“PIB”) is an authorised institution under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. Under
normal operating conditions the principal activity of the Bank is the provision of commercial banking services to its
customers. The EU/UK financial sanctions imposed on the Bank on 27 July 2010 were lifted on 16 January 2016.
However, normal banking activities have not yet been resumed owing to continuing difficulties in arranging UK clearing
facilities and in establishing correspondent relationships with international banks. Nevertheless, the Bank has recently,
in June 2016, joined the Target 2 payments system through the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Business Review

Historically, the Bank offered a comprehensive service in trade finance and credit facilities to its corporate customers.
Under trade finance, the Bank received and advised letters of credit, added confirmation and provided short term credit
facilities by discounting the bills drawn under them. PIB extended credit facilities to its corporate customers either on
bilateral terms or as part of a syndicate. Iranian corporate customers constituted a major portion of the customer base of
the Bank. The Bank had been performing well since its inception meeting all its short and medium term goals until 2010.
A strong customer base was built up with a robust trade finance and corporate credit portfolio.

The first international branch was opened in August 2005 at Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC), Dubai which
further expanded the Bank's trade finance capabilities. However, the Dubai branch operation was suspended by the
Dubai regulator (DFSA) in March 2010 and the branch has remained inactive throughout the last five financial years.

The balance sheet total of the Bank is €222,384k, which is lower than in the previous year owing mainly to scheduled
repayments of customer loans, and the return of customer deposits. Trade finance activities ceased as of July 2011
and is expected to be resumed once the issues surrounding correspondent banking relationships are resolved.

Financial Performance during the year 2015-16

The interest income as at 31 March 2016 increased by 7.74% from €5, 142k in the previous year to €5,540k in the current
period. The Bank has reported a pre-tax loss, of €707k (after tax loss of €498k), for the first time since its inception in
2002. The reason for the loss is that there has been no new business in the past six years (and the major portion of loan
accounts have been closed). The Bank's income is presently derived mainty from interest earned on the Nostro balance
with Bank Markazi, Central Bank of Iran, on loans arranged prior to the EU sanctions, and from the amortisation of fee

income.

Fee & commission income receivable was €31k and fee & commission payable was €21k. Other income (which was
constituted of foreign exchange income) was €214k.

The total administrative expenses for the financial year were €5,568k compared to €4,220k during the previous year. The
net impairment charge for the year under review for loans and advances is €251k and for available for sale financial
assets impairment is €416k. Depreciation cost was €191k during the year under review compared to €96k during the
previous year. The increase in depreciation provision is due to a full year’s depreciation of our Dubai property which is

Page 3
being written down over a period of 50 years and also due to depreciation on computer hardware and software purchased
during the year.

The Bank used to provide a range of trade finance services which included documentary credits, advising and
confirmation of LCs and discounting of Trade Bills drawn on banks, which generated significant discounts and fee income.
These activities will be resumed during the year 2016-17 once correspondent banking relationships are established.

The total assets of the Bank are €222,384k, The single largest asset is the Nostro balance with Bank Markazi which is
€148, 187k. Loans and advances to customers have decreased from €50,022k to €44,775k due to scheduled repayments.
The loan portfolio will continue to wind down until normal banking activities resume and new Joans are made. Loans and
advances to banks have increased from €10,336k to €14,732k due to receipt of one, loan repayment into our nostro
accounts.

Risk Assessment and Management

The Bank is exposed to liquidity risk, credit risk, operational risk, market risk, interest rate risk and currency risk (see
Note 20 for further details on the management of these risks). The Directors have also identified the lack of GBP clearing
facilities and foreign currency correspondent relationships, compliance, concentration, IT, political and succession risks
as the major risks faced by the Bank in its present situation.

The board and the management believe that they have taken adequate measures to mitigate these primary risks. Steps
have been initiated to diversify as far as possible GBP payment arrangements and advance payments have been made
to secure essential services and supplies, until participation in the local GBP clearing system is established. The Bank's
overall liquidity position is strong with major liabilities settled and the remaining liabilities manageable. The mismatch in
the maturities of the balance sheet assets and liabilities is not a cause for concern. Though a significant portion of its
short term funds are kept with Bank Markazi in Iran, the Bank has access to sufficient liquid funds within the EU to meet
its obligations and operating costs for the foreseeable future. The Directors are satisfied that the Bank will remain a going
concern for the foreseeable future and they remain confident regarding its longer term prospects. Pillar 3 disclosures are
made separately and published on our web site. ;

The Bank Is in the process of recruiting a Chief Risk Officer to strengthen the Bank’s risk management process.

Financial reporting risks are managed through control activities performed at all levels of the organisation. These activities
include guidelines and instructions, approvals, authorisations, verifications, reconciliations, analytical reviews, and
segregation of duties. The Board has also established a Risk Committee chaired by an independent non-executive
director. This committee (see Note 20 for details) monitors, reviews and updates all risks faced by the Bank from within
and without. In terms of internal controls and financial reporting information, external and internal information is obtained
systematically, and the Bank's management is provided with relevant information on the Bank's activities. Timely, current
and accessible information relevant for financial reporting purposes is provided to the appropriate functionaries, such as
the Board, the senior management group and the relevant Board committees.

The Board has the overall responsibility for the proper arrangement of internal control and risk management over financial
reporting. The Board has appointed the Audit Committee (members are two independent non-executive directors) to
undertake more specific tasks in relation to the financial reporting process control and monitoring the efficiency of the
Bank's internal control. The Audit Committee also reviews regularly the main features of the internal control and risk
management systems pertaining to the financial reporting process.

To ensure the effectiveness of internal control pertaining to financial reporting, monitoring activities are conducted at all
levels of the organization. Monitoring is performed through ongoing follow-up activities, separate evaluations, or a
combination of the two. Separate internal audit assignments may be initiated by the Board or management. The scope
and frequency of separate evaluations depend primarily on the assessment of risks and the effectiveness of ongoing

There were no frauds or serious lapses reported during the past year. The Board regularly reviews the financial reports,

The Bank has a full set of policy and procedures manuals. These cover all of the Bank's activities and are reviewed and

Risk weighting of assets:

The EU notification on 12 December 2014 on the equivalence of the supervisory and regulatory requirements of non-EU
countries has excluded Iran from the list of those countries and therefore exposure to Iran will be risk weighted at 150%.
Given the position that our business activities are focused on Iran and Iranian corporate and financial institutions, this
new risk weighting measures will impact the business level that the Bank can achieve and the future profitability of PIB.
The Business Plan of the Bank has set a target business level of €375m for the forthcoming financial year 2016-17.

Reputation

The Bank is authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority and it is
structured in accordance with the principles of good governance. This includes employing two independent non-executive
directors, one of whom chairs the Audit and the other Chairs the Risk Committee and Remuneration Committee, and
operating management level committees to ensure ‘four eyes’ are involved in all major policy and operational decisions.
We also take seriously all aspects of conduct risk and fully subscribe to the principles of ‘treating custorners fairly’ to
ensure that the trust they put in us as bankers is well founded.

Employees

We aim to maintain a highly skilled work force to meet the challenges and opportunities of an active financial services
company. To achieve this we will:

e Provide opportunities for our staff to develop their skills and capabilities through training and other means so that
they can progress within the organisation,

e Supplement the skills of existing staff by recruiting suitably qualified new staff as appropriate,

e Value diversity and promote a sensible work life balance,

e Responsibly and appropriately reward contributions to long term success,

e Seek to retain staff through ‘family friendly’ policies dealing with all staff in sensitive and pragmatic manner.

Future Developments

The Bank awaits further developments in respect of correspondent banking arrangements, however the Bank maintains
operational readiness and capability to work towards medium term objectives. Following the lifting of the sanctions the
reactivation plan has become operational, and new staff have been recruited to complement the existing staff. The Bank
is very conscious of its operating environment, and to this end ensures that it fully complies with all regulatory and legal
requirements of the UK Government and both the Prudential Regulation Authority and Financial Conduct Authority. Once

correspondent banking relationships have been established, the Bank intends to diversify its money market placements

and software operating systems is now under way, with a view to replacing the present IT system with something modern
and appropriate to its needs within the next 12 months.

Operating segments

An operating segment is a component of the Bank that engages in business activities from which it may earn
revenues and Incur expenses. Its operating results are reviewed regularly by the Bank's Management Committee
(which comprises the CEO, CFO and COO and it meets at least once a month) and the Asset Liability Committee
(see Note 28 for details). Decisions about resource allocation are made and the performance can be assessed
using the available discreet financial information. The Chief Executive Officer is the chief operating decision maker
of the Bank.

Interest Income and expense

Interest Income and expense on financial assets and liabilities measured at amortised cost is recognised in the
statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method, which allocates the income or
expense over the relevant period. The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash
flows to the carrying value on initial recognition, through the expected life of the financial asset or liability. In respect
of impaired loans where it is probable that interest on the loan will not flow to the entity, interest income is not
recognised in the statement of comprehensive income.

Fee and commission Income

Fees receivables which represent a return for services provided are credited to income when the related service is
performed. Fees receivable, which represent a return for risk borne and are in the nature of interest, are taken to
the statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method over the expected life of the
transaction to which they relate.

Provisions

A provision is recognised if, as a result of a past event, the Bank has a present legal or constructive obligation that
can be estimated reliably, and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the
obligation. Provisions are determined by discounting the expected future cash flows at a pre-tax rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money, and where appropriate, the risks specific to the liability.

Financial assets:

Financial assets are classified into the following categories:

e financial assets at fair value through profit or loss: a financial asset is classified as such if it is held
principally for the purpose of selling in the short term. In the current and prior year, the Bank did not
hold any financial assets at fair value through profit or loss;

e loans and receivables: are non-derivative financial assets with fixed or determinable payments not
quoted in an active market; and

¢ available for sale: are financial assets not classified in one of the above categories.

Loans and receivables are initially recognised at fair value, plus directly attributable transaction costs, and
subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method.

Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss are initially and subsequently measured at fair value with
changes in fair value taken to the statement of comprehensive income.

Available for sale investments are non-derivative investments that are designated as available for sale or are not

classified as another category of financial assets. They are carried at fair value. Available for sale financial assets
are initially and subsequently measured at fair value, with changes in fair value recognised directly in equity untit

method and impairment losses arising on available-for-sale securities are taken to profit or loss and recognised in
the statement of comprehensive income.

A non-derivative financial asset may be reclassified from the available-for-sale category to the loans and

Financial liabilities and equity

Financial liabilities and equity instruments are initially recognised at fair value; this occurs when the Bank becomes
a party to the contractual provisions of the corresponding instruments. The Bank derecognises financial liabilities
when its contractual obligations are discharged or cancelled or expire. Financial liabilities and equity instruments
are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is
any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the Bank after deducting all of its liabilities. Financial
liabilities comprise amounts due to other banks, customer accounts and subordinated debt liabilities. They are
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Equity instruments issued by the Bank are
recorded at the proceeds received, net of direct issue costs.

Impairment of financial assets

A financial asset is impaired and impairment losses are incurred if there is objective evidence of impairment, as a
result of events that occurred after initial recognition of the financial asset. The amount of the loss is measured, for
financial assets held at amortised cost and as available-for-sale, as the difference between the carrying amount of
the asset and the value of the revised estimated future cash flows, discounted at the asset's original effective
interest rate. Financial assets are written off only when it is reasonably certain that any future cash flows are
irrecoverable.

Operating lease rentals
Rentals payable under operating leases are accounted for on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

Valuation of financial Instruments
The Bank measures fair values using a fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in

making the measurements.

Level 1: Quoted market price (unadjusted) in an active market for an identical instrument is taken as the fair value.
Level 2: Valuation techniques based on observable inputs, either directly (i.e. as prices) or indirectly (i.e. derived
from prices). This category includes instruments valued using: quoted market prices in active markets for similar
instruments; quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered less than active; or
other valuation techniques where all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.
Level 3: Under this category Bank's assumptions about the market and market conditions are used in pricing the
asset and based on the best information available in the circumstances and not merely based on observable market
data (unobservable inputs).

Property plant and equipment
Tangible fixed assets are stated at cost less provision for depreciation and any impairment in value. Depreciation
of equipment, fixtures and fittings is provided on a straight-line basis over estimated useful lives' - 3 years.

Freehold property is depreciated over a period of 50 years.

Impairment of property, plant and equipment

The carrying amount of the Bank's property, plant and equipment are reviewed at each reporting date to determine
whether there is any indication of impairment. If any such indication exists, then the asset's recoverable amount is
estimated. The recoverable amount of an asset is the greater of its value in use and its fair value less costs to sell.
In assessing value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax
discount rate that reflects current market assessment of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset.
Impairment loss is recognised, if the carrying amount of an asset exceeds its estimated recoverable amount.
Impairment losses are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

(a) Current income tax

Income tax payable (receivable) is calculated on the basis of the applicable tax law in the respective jurisdiction

Deferred income tax is normally provided in full, using the liability method, on temporary differences arising between

The Bank operates a defined contribution scheme, charging contributions to profit or loss within the statement of

Foreign currencies

Transactions in foreign currencies are recorded at the rate of exchange at the date of the transaction. A monetary

units of currency. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies at the reporting date are
reported at the rates of exchange prevailing at that date. Translation differences on monetary items are recognised

Market risk

Non-trading market risk

The Bank does not trade either interest rate or exchange rate risk. Interest rate and currency risk arise in the Bank's banking book through holding of
financial instruments, including loans and deposits. Exposure to movements in interest rates arises from mismatches bet interest rate sensitive
assets and liabilities. The risk is monitored and managed on a daily basis by the Bank's Treasury function. Details of interest rate risk exposure are
presented in note 21. The Bank similarly does not take (trading) open positions in foreign currencies and so is exposed to very little foreign exchange
risk, see note 22.

Interest rate risk

The Bank does not have interest rate gaps in excess of one year. Non-interest bearing assets and liabilities are included with capital funds which
are also undated. The potential effect of a 1% ads t in interest rates across all currencies and all dates at 31 March 2016 would
increase this year's loss by €116k (2014: €51k). This calculation is determined by closing all interest rate gap through comp ting market deals
at rates 1% above or below the existing gap rates.

The exposure to currency risk is measured and monitored daily. The small remaining open positions resulting from banking operating
transactions are closed out in the market as soon as it is practical todo so. The effect of an adverse 5% movement in the rates of
exchange between the short and long currencies would not be significant to affect the Bank's results.
