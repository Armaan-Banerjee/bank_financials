Basis of consolidation and accounting date

(i) The accounts of the group are made up for the 52 weeks to 11th January, 1986. Since the group accounting date is
virtually coterminous with the calendar year 1985 the financial year’s figures are headed 1985 and the corresponding
figures for the previous year are headed 1984. The Accounts have been prepared under the historical cost convention and
in accordance with Sections 258 and 259 of the Companies Act 1985 and Schedule 9 to that Act.

(ii) Associated companies
In the consolidated balance sheet associated compaities are shown at cost, including advances, and the group’s share of
reserves from the date of acquisition, less amounts written off.

ii) Preminm on acquisition of shares

The premium paid on the acquisition of shares in subsidiaries, being the excess of the maount paid over the net tangible
assets ut the date of acquisition, has been written off against reserves.

Tavestments

4) Investment Income
Income from investments includes amortization of premiums and discounts on dated stocks, value for which is taken ins
equal annual instalments from the date of acquisition to maturity.

Gi) Sale of Investments
Realised profits and losses on sales of investments are taken to profit and loss account in the year in which they arise,

Losses on Advances

‘The amount charged against operating profit for losses on advances comprises specific provision against identifiable losses
and a general provision to cover latent but unidentifiable losses due to doubtful debts. Both provisions are based ona yearend
appraisal of advances, Advances are shown on the balance sheet after deducting these provisions,

Depreciation
Depreciation is provided on astraight line basis at the following rates which: are estimated to write down the assets to realisable
values at the end of their useful lives;
Freehold and long leasehold inildings 2:5 per cent per annum
Short leasehold buildings life of lease
Equipment 10 to 20 per cent per annum
Vehicles 25 per cent per annum.
Leasing

Leased equipment is valued at original cost less depreciation, which is calculated to write off that cost over the primary period
of the lease. Depreciation for the period represents the full amount of leasing payments due in the period, les the amounts
credited to the profit and loss account.

Tngome from assets leased to customers is credited to the profit and loss account in proportion to the funds invested and
includes amounts in respect of both regional development grants and tax credits which arise from falling corporation tax rates
grossed up at the average rate of corporation tax applicable to the period,

Assets acquired under finance leases are capitalr.d, based on the purchase price of the assets, Depreciation is provided on the
same basis as for owned assets. The interest clement of the lease payment is charged to profit and loss account over the primary
period of the lease, The capital value of the lease is included in the balance sheet ag a liability reduced by the capital element of
the lease payments,

tn the case of back is back leases, obligations of the Group under finance leases are now included in Current, Deposit and Other
accounts, and rentals receivable from lessees are included in Assets Leased to Customers, both net of future finance charges.
Previously, such amounts were not included in the balance sheet. To reflect this change the 1984 figures have been restated.

Operating lease rentals we debited to profit and loss account as they accrue,

Hire Purchase and instalment credit agreements

Credit is taken in the profit and loss account for finance charges on hire purchase and instalment credit dyteements, ner of
dealers’ commission, using the ‘Rule of 78,’ with the exception of agreements having a duration of five years and over where
finance charges are allocated on an actuarial basis.

Deferred taxation

Provision i) rade for taxation on timing differences between profits stated in *he accounts and profits computed for taxation
purposes rere ihere is a reasonable probability that such taxation will become payable in the foreseeable future.

Exchange rates
Balances in foreign currencies are expressed in sterling atthe rate ruling at the balance sheet date, All exchange differences are
salt with in the profit and loss account.

Money at call and short notice
Previously, market placings under one year were included under liquid assets. This policy has now been amended to include
market placings under 30 days. To reflect this change in accounti ig policy, the 1984 figures have been restated.

(a) Monetary working capital — . ;
The monetary working capital adjustment allows for the effect of price changes on the net monctary working capital by
applying the change in Retail Price Index to the average monetary working capital during the year. Por this purpose
monetary working capital includes all assets other than fixed assets less liabilities arising from operating activities.
(b) Depreciation adjustment ;
‘The depreciation adjustment is the difference between depreciation based on the current replacement cost of fixed assets
and the depreciation charge in the historical cost accounts, In ¢stimating the value to the business the following bases
have been adopted:
(i) Freehold and leaschold premises were professionally valued in 1981. ‘These values have been adjusted to current
cost in 1985 by the use cf relevant indices.
(i) Computer hardware and other electronic equipment, together with any related capital expenditure on software,
have been valued at current replacement cost.
(ii) Other fixed assets have heen valued at historical cost adjusted to current cost by the use of relevant indives,
(c) Gearing adjustment ; . ;
The gearing adjustment rcduces the monetary working capital and depreciation adjustment by the proportion of capital
provided other than by sharcholders’ funds,
(d) Corresponding amounts .
