Pillar 3 Disclosure

Financial institutions within the scope of Basel I! are required to disclose information about their risk exposures and the
risk assessment processes they have used. These disclosures are in addition to those required in the Annual Report and
Financial Statements and form Pillar 3 of the Basel Itt framework.

Basel Ill is implemented in the European Union (EU) via the Capital Requirements Directive (CRD) which consists of three
Pillars.

Pillar 1 sets out the minimum capital requirements that banks are required to meet for credit, market and operational
risk.

Under Pillar 2, banks and their supervisors at the PRA assess whether additional capital is required to cover risks not
covered in Pillar 1.

Pillar 3 complements the minimum capital requirements (Pillar 1) and the supervisory review process (Pillar 2) with the
aim of developing a set of disclosure requirements which enable market participants to assess information on the risks

that a bank is subject to and its capital and risk management procedures.

Detailed information relating to Pillar 3, which has not been audited, is available on the Bank's website at www.saderat-
ple.com

Business review

The Bank's income is derived principally from its interbank placements, under existing business relationships, rather than
from commercial activities. However, now the Bank has permission to resume normal business this balance will gradually
change as commercial activity restarts.

As shown on the Statement of Financial Position total assets have increased from €224.7m at 31st December 2018 to
€225.5m at 31st December 2019 and shareholders’ funds have increased from €190.7m to €191.1m due to retained
profits.

Corporate governance statement

The Board is ultimately responsible for the Bank’s system of internal controls and it discharges its duties in this area by
ensuring management implements effective systems of risk identification, assessment and mitigation. These risk
management systems are designed to manage, rather than eliminate, the risk of failure to achieve business objectives
and cannot provide absolute assurance against material misstatement or loss. Management is responsible for establishing
and maintaining adequate internal controls over financial reporting. Internal controls over financial reporting are designed
to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements
for external reporting purposes. A strategic planning and budgeting process is in place. One of the directors'
responsibilities is regular monitoring of performance against plan on a monthly basis and discussing reasons for variances.
The Executive Board receives relevant briefings from the Assets and Liabilities Committee (ALCO) and the Credit
Committee. The Board receives relevant briefings from the Risk Monitoring Committee, Audit Committee and the
Remuneration and Personnel Policy Committee.

The Risk Monitoring Committee's role is to identify and discuss the nature of the risks encountered and ensure that
management recognises the full range of risks to which the Bank is exposed, and their relative scale, stress tested under
various scenarios, in order that controls may be designed and resources deployed to manage those risks effectively, and
that the residual risk is contained within parameters approved by the Board. The Audit Committee inter alia oversees the
control environment, ensuring that risks have been adequately recognised, that controls in place are appropriate to
manage those risks, and particularly that the controls are operating effectively.

ALCO is responsible for monitoring the management of the Bank’s financial resources to ascertain that revenues are
maximised within the Bank’s policy guidelines and the applicable regulatory framework. ALCO meets on a monthly basis.

Principal risks, uncertainties and risk management

Financial sanctions and the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action

Financial sanctions were imposed upon the Bank on 27th July 2010 by the Council of the European Union. These sanctions
were removed on 22nd October 2016 and Bank Saderat PLC is no longer subject to an asset freeze and requires no licences
from HM Treasury in the United Kingdom to deal with its funds and economic resources.

Although the sanctions were removed, the Bank was still prohibited from undertaking new business and commercial
lending until receiving confirmation from the Regulators that the Bank was able to resume normal business, which was
received on 13" September 2018. During 2018 the USA withdrew

from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action and re-imposed sanctions on tran, which is affecting the speed and nature
of the Bank’s return to normal business.

Liquidity and Market risk

The bank operations expose it to liquidity risk, interest rate risk, credit risk and foreign exchange risk. The quantitative
and qualitative disclosures regarding the Bank's management of these risks are described in detail in note 15 of the
Financial Statements.

Operational Risk
The bank also faces operational risk, including the risk of non-compliance with regulatory and legal requirements, is

managed by strict adherence to detailed and comprehensive procedures together with high-level managerial oversight of
the Bank's operations.

Future developments

The Bank's capital base at 31st December 2019 was €191.1m (2018: €190.7m) which consists of tier 1 capital. Tier 1 capital
is the total of the Bank's ordinary share capital, general banking reserve and retained earnings. The Bank has always
complied with its regulatory capital requirement.

The Bank's plan is to gradually reactivate certain elements of its pre-sanction business activities. Initially the Bank will
focus on participation in commercial lending facilities and syndicated project finance. However, the ability and timing of
the Bank’s participation in these opportunities is impacted by the hardening of the US position on sanctions against Iran
and constraints (see below) on our ability to conduct normal business.

The Bank is going to achieve its targets by its strong capital position, risk awareness, caution and prudent behaviour.

Constraints which are most challenging for the Bank are the availability of: UK clearing system, European clearing,
correspondent banking services and SWIFT. In order to overcome some of these limitations the Bank has conducted
studies into the feasibility of opening a branch in the EU to enable access to an EU clearing system and to deposit funds
that qualify as high quality liquid assets. However, given that Brexit has occurred and the passporting rules for opening a
branch will not last beyond the end of 2020, the bank has decided to study and prepare the necessary steps in order to
apply for a third-country licence in 2021.

The directors have considered the impact of Covid-19 and have concluded that it is not going to have a significant impact

on the Bank. This is discussed in more detail in note 24 to the accounts.

(e) Impairment of financial assets carried at amortised cost

If there is objective evidence that an impairment loss on financial assets carried at amortised cost has been incurred,
the amount of the loss is measured as the difference between the asset's carrying amount and the present value of
estimated future cash flows (excluding future credit losses that have not been incurred) discounted at the financial

Factors considered in determining whether or not a financial asset is impaired include the adequacy and ease of

If, in a subsequent period, the amount of the impairment loss decreases and the decrease can be related objectively
to an event occurring after the impairment was recognised (such as an improvement in the debtor's credit rating), the
previously recognised impairment loss is reversed by adjusting the allowance account. The reversal shall not result in
a carrying amount of the financial asset that exceeds what the amortised cost would have been, had the impairment
not been recognised, at the date the impairment is reversed. The amount of the reversal is recognised in the Income
Statement.

Loans and advances to banks and customers are carried at amortised cost net of impairment provisions where

The activities of the Bank expose it to a variety of risks, the most significant being: market risk, liquidity risk and credit
risk,

Market risk is the potential adverse change in the Bank's net assets or income arising from movements in interest
rates, foreign exchange rates or other market prices. Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank is unable to meet its
obligations as they fall due owing to insufficient financial resources. Credit risk is the risk of financial loss as
counterparties and borrowers are unable to meet their obligations as they fall due.

Financial risk management framework

The Board has overall responsibility for the establishment and oversight of the Bank's risk management framework.
Sub-committees of the Board have been established to manage the various risks including the UK Board, the Audit
Committee, the Risk Monitoring Committee, the Assets and Liabilities Committee and the Credit Committee. The UK
Board and the Risk Monitoring Committee have both executive and non-executive members; the Audit Committee has
only non-executive members; and the Assets and Liabilities Committee and the Credit Committee have only executive
members. These committees report regularly to the Board.

The Audit Committee is responsible for monitoring compliance with the Bank's risk management policies and
procedures, and for reviewing the adequacy of the risk management framework in relation to the risks faced by the
Bank. The Audit Committee is assisted in these functions by Internal Audit. Internal Audit undertakes both regular and
ad-hoc reviews of risk management controls and procedures, the results of which are reported to the Audit
Committee.

Financial risks to which the Bank is subject

The internal assessment criteria for business lending purposes

The Bank has developed a rating system which is used in conducting reviews of borrowers to assess their on-going
credit standing and to consider whether a loan or advance should be placed on a “watch list" due to a deteriorating
assessment of the borrower. The system is based upon a points scoring method with points awarded against various
criteria from an analysis of financial statements provided by borrowers, The sum of the points awarded determines
the classification of each borrower from A (the highest rating) to D (the lowest). It is likely that any D rated borrowers
would be in default, which may result in the raising of a provision against the debt.

The Bank is careful to detect early on, any deterioration of the financial conditions of the obligors as well as the
providers of different support forms, Early problem recognition is key to successful risk management. Early warning
indicators provide signs of credit deterioration and include the following:

- Deterioration in financial ratios, covenants, breaches and waiver requests.
- Macroeconomic environment deteriorates.

~ Industry comes under pressure.

-  Dectine in share / bond / CDS prices.

- Rating agency downgrades and changes to negative outlook.

- Rapid changes in technology and product life cycles.

- Adverse comments in the media.

A default shall be considered to have occurred when an obligor is unlikely to pay its credit obligations or is past due
on any material credit obligation.

A period of 90 days past due is the regulatory definition and should be used as a benchmark. However consideration
must be given to the circumstances of each obligor and a past due period of less than 90 days may indicate payment
is unlikely. Once a debt is 90 days past due it will be referred monthly to the UK Board or Board meeting for review.

Capital management

The Bank seeks to maximise its capital base within the requirement to provide its shareholder with a return by way of
dividend that compensates the shareholder for the cost of funding its investment in the share capital of the Bank at a
level that is commensurate with.its peers in the market and adequately reflects the risk to the shareholder. The Bank's
regulator, the Prudential Regulation Authority also sets and monitors, a minimum capital requirement based upon a
prescribed ratio of capital to total risk exposure.

The Bank's capital for regulatory purposes is analysed into two tiers:

Tier 1 capital which includes ordinary share capital, retained earnings and the general banking reserve and Tier 2
capital which includes qualifying subordinated debt.

24. Going Concern and Covid-19

The Directors have carefully considered the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the Bank’s operations, particularly
the ability of staff to perform key functions and the Bank’s continued ability to meet its financial and regulatory
obligations satisfactorily. This consideration includes the effectiveness of measures, such as remote access, to assist
in mitigating the impact. The Directors have concluded that, based on such assessments and having regard to the
resources available to the Bank, there are no material uncertainties and that the going concern basis can continue to
be adopted in preparing these annual report and accounts.
