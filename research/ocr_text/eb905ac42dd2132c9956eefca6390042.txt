For the year ended 31 March 2022

The Directors are pleased to present their strategic report for the Persia International Bank PLC
for the year ended 31 March 2022.

Introduction

Persia International Bank PLC (“P{B” or “the Bank”) is an authorised institution under the
Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. The Bank's principal activity is the provision of
commercial banking services to its customers. Although financial sanctions imposed in 2010 were
lifted on 16 January 2016. However, in November 2018 the US decision to unilaterally withdraw
from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (SCPOA) agreement and subsequent re-imposition
of OFAC sanctions means the normal banking activities have not yet been fully resumed owing to
continuing difficulties in arranging UK clearing facilities and in establishing correspondent
relationships with international banks.

Business Review

Historically, the Bank has offered a comprehensive service in trade finance and credit facilities to
its customers. Under trade finance, the Bank used to receive and advise letters of credit. add
confirmation and provide short term credit facilities by discounting the bills drawn under them.
PIB also extended credit facilities to its corporate and institutional customers either on bilateral
terms or as part of syndication. Iranian corporate customers constituted a significant portion of
the Bank’s customer base built up with a robust trade finance and corporate credit portfolio.

On 23 July 2021 the Banks received licence from the Central bank of Italy for its Milan branch as
a branch of third country (TCB) outside EU. On 30 October 2021 PIB Milan was granted access
to TARGET2 through the central of Italy, La Banca d'Italia (Bd'l) to process trade receipts, trade
payments and also to hold high quality liquid asset (HQLA) and excess funds. The branch ts in the
process of setting up its core banking systems and to be operationally ready. It is expected fo be
tully operational for business this year and this will enable the Bank to expand its business mainly
in trade finance and credit facilities.

The Statement of Financial Position total assets of the Bank have reduced slightly due to the
decrease in placement by shareholder banks. Before the sanctions Trade Finance had been an
important portfolio in PIB’s business lines. Although it has secured a €19.8 forfaiting business
during the year however, in recent years the Bank did not have much opportunities in this line of
business. Further resumption of trade finance activities is possible only when the issues
surrounding correspondent banking relationships are resolved. The Bank will consider all viable
options in the future.

and political uncertainties, ongoing regulatory changes and impact of Covid-19 pandemic as well

OFAC sanctions

Following the decision of the US Government to unilaterally withdraw from the JCPOA, the
OFAC sanctions were imposed in November 2018. These sanctions prohibit people from the US
engaying in any trade, financial and other dealings with Iran. This has prevented the Bank from

* engaging in business with the US based or linked counterparties and has made dealings with other
countries also more difficult. The management is of the view that the impact of OFAC sanctions
although visible but will not hamper the present business pattern of the Bank. [ts primary business
dealings are in euro and exposures to Iranian entities. The non-availability of SWIFT impacted
business communications and transfer of funds. This has been managed through book transfer
mechanism with the involvement of fraternal banks in the EU.

Risk weighting of assets

The EU notification on the equivalence of the supervisory and regulatory requirements of non-EU
countries has excluded Iran from the list of those countries, and therefore exposure to Iran will
continue to be risk weighted at 150%. Given that our business activities are mainly focused on
{ranian corporate and financial institutions, these risk weighting measures will impact the business
level that the Bank can achieve and therefore its profitability.

2.10.1 Significant increase in credit risk

The bank monitors all financial assets, issued loan commitments and financial guarantee
contracts that are subject to the impairment requirements to assess whether there has been a
significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition. {f there has been a significant
increase in credit risk the Bank will measure the loss allowance based on lifetime rather than
12-months ECL.

The Bank's accounting policy is not to use the practical expedient that financial assets with
‘low’ credit risk at the reporting date are deemed not to have had a significant increase in credit
risk. As a result, the bank monitors all financial assets, issued loan commitments and financial
guarantee contracts that are subject to impairment for significant increase in credit risk.

In assessing whether the credit risk ona financial instrument has increased significantly since
initial recognition, the bank compares the risk of default occurring on the financial instrument
on the reporting date based on the remaining maturity of the instrument with the risk of a default
occurring that was anticipated for the remaining maturity at the current reporting date when the
financial instrument was first recognised. In making this assessment. the Bank consider both
qualitative and quantitative information that is reasonable and supportable, including historical
experience and forward-looking information that is available without undue cost or effort,
based on the Bank's historical experience and expert credit assessment including forward-
looking information.

For corporate lending forward-looking information includes the future prospects of the
industries in which the Bank's counterparties operate, obtained from expert economic expert
reports, financial analysts, governmental bodies, relevant think-tanks and other similar
organisations, as well as consideration of various internal and external sources of actual and
forecast economic information.

The Bank allocates its counterparties to a relevant internal credit risk grade depending on their
credit quality, The quantitative information is a primary indicator of the significant increase in
credit risk and is based on the change in lifetime PD. The PDs used are forward-looking and
the Bank uses the same methodologies and the data used to measure the loss allowance for
ECL. The qualitative factors that indicate significant increase in the credit risk are reflected in
PD models on a timely basis. However, the Bank still considers separately some qualitative
