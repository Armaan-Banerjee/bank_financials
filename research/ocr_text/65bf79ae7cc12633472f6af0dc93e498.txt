The Directors present their Strategic Report for The Bank of London Group Limited ("the Company” or “the
Bank”) for the year ended 31 December 2024. The Company (registered number 12844788) is a private
company limited by shares incorporated and domiciled in the United Kingdom under the Companies Act
2006 and is registered in England and Wales. On 10 March 2025, the Company's registered office changed
to 77 Cornhill, Fourth Floor, London, England EC3V 3QqQ.

The Bank's immediate parent is Oplyse Holdings Limited (“Oplyse”, “Group”). The Bank’s ultimate parent
company is Fellesskap Group & Holdings Corporation Limited.

1. Mission Statement

Our mission is to create a safe clearing bank for the UK, offering a purpose-built payments, settlement and
clearing platform that is powering the businesses and entrepreneurs of the future. With our technologies,
innovations, and differentiated bank capabilities, we prioritise the preservation of our depositors’ monies
and market stability. All client deposits are held in full at the Bank of England, which means that we are less
exposed to the risk of traditional ‘bank runs’. 100% of our clients’ deposits are liquid, unencumbered and
immediately available upon demand.

2. Principal Activities

As the Uk’s sixth clearing bank, the principal activities of the Bank are to provide payment and deposit
services to our clients. These focus on:
e Agency, clearing, payment and settlement solutions for regulated and non-regulated domestic and
international companies;
e Embedded Banking - enabling companies to embed regulated banking products into their own
branded offerings, powered by our bank licence and platform;
* Deposits-as-a-Service ~ directly or through deposit aggregators, and;
e Business & Commercial Banking - providing custody, transaction, and treasury banking solutions to
many types of business, domestic and international.

4. Financial Results for the Year

The Bank recorded a net loss for the year of £23,970k (2023: £12,414k). The net loss for the year
represents costs incurred in setting up infrastructure (non-tech) and hiring the high-calibre workforce
required to continue to develop our capabilities and banking platform.

The Bank earned net interest income in the year of £2,748k (2023: £1,194k), with interest income of
£24,027k (2023: £3,502k) primarily representing interest income earned on funds deposited in the Bank’s
account at the Bank of England, and interest expense of £21,279k (2023: £2,308k) being interest expense
payable on customer deposits.

Administrative expenses for the year were £27,659k (2023: £13,230k), an increase of £14,429k. This
increase was driven primarily by the introduction of a £14,400k management recharge from Oplyse, for the
provision of core banking technology and related support to the Company.

The Bank’s net assets as at 31 December 2024 decreased to £22,143k (2023: £26,686k), with the net loss
for the year being offset by an increase in shareholders’ capital during the year.

While the Board acknowledge the key assumptions and matters noted above, they have considered the
mitigating factors in place, in particular:

1. the commitment of the ultimate parent company shareholders to inject capital into the ultimate parent

and then the Group to Bank,

2. the potential growth of its commercial business model, which is still in its infancy, and,

3. the Group’s demonstrable scalable operational capability.

resources to continue trading for a period of twelve months from the date of approval of these financial

statements, and that the going concern basis of preparation remains appropriate.

2. Operational and Capital factors — The timing of the removal of optional operational measures and

financial obligations.

Basis for qualified opinion

Our opinion is qualified in respect of one matter, as was the case in our modified audit opinion in respect of
the financial statements for the year end 31 December 2023.

In December 2022, the parent company, Oplyse Holdings Limited (“the Group”), implemented a Share
Option Plan where equity-based awards over shares in Oplyse Holdings Limited, are granted to employees
of the Group, including employees of the Company.

Options issued under the Share Option Plan allow the participants the right to acquire ordinary shares in
the parent company. As described in note 17, the Group accounts for the Share Option Plan as an equity-
settled plan. The fair value of the participants’ services received in exchange for the grant of the options is
recognised as an expense in Administrative Expenses in the Statement of Profit or Loss and Other
Comprehensive Income. A credit is recognised directly in equity in the Share-Based Payment Reserve.

As further explained in note 17, in April 2025, the Board approved the cancellation of the Share Option Plan.
The plan was cancelled in November 2025 after forma! confirmations had been issued to members of the
scheme.

The Directors have estimated the valuation and based the accounting and disclosure of the Share Option
Plan on the information they had available, because inadequate historical records have been maintained,
as set out in note 17. As a result of this, we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to
support the valuation, accounting and disclosure of the Share Option Plan. In respect of the year ended 31
December 2024 this impacted the Share-Based Payments Reserve of £1,632k (2023: £1,205k) in the
Statement of Financial Position and £404k (2023: £469k) of share-based payment charges included within
Administrative Expenses. In addition, in respect of 1 January 2024, we could not determine whether any
adjustment to the Share-Based Payments Reserve (£1,205k) (1 January 2023: £714k) and Retained
Earnings (£26,559k) (1 January 2023: (£14,145k)) was necessary.

Consequently, we were unable to determine whether any adjustment to these financial statement line
items was necessary. Our opinion on the current period’s financial statements is also modified because of
the possible effect of this matter on the comparability of the current period's figures and the
corresponding figures.

In addition, were any adjustment to the share-based payments charges or Share-Based Payments Reserve
be required, the Strategic Report and Directors’ Report would also need to be amended.

The valuation, accounting and disclosure of share-based payments has been determined to be a key audit
matter for this year too due to subjective inputs into managements calculations.

Material uncertainties relating to going concern

We draw attention to the Basis of Preparation in note 1 in the financial statements, which indicates that the
ability of the Company to continue as a going concern is subject to material uncertainties The Company is
loss making and has experienced a number of adverse events requiring significant attention as set out in

1. Whether the Company will be able to raise required additional equity capital;

2. Whether the Company has the ability to execute its business plans, which includes a number of

Our application of materiality

We apply the concept of materiality in planning and performing the audit, in evaluating the effect of
identified misstatements on the audit and in forming our audit opinion.

Materiality

The magnitude of an omission or misstatement that, individually or in the aggregate, could reasonably be
expected to influence the economic decisions of the users of the financial statements. Materiality provides
a basis for determining the nature and extent of our audit procedures.

We determined materiality for the Company to be £664k, which is 3% of equity. We believe that equity
provides us with the appropriate basis of materiality as this aligns closely with the expectations of primary
users of the financial statements being its majority shareholder and the UK regulator to whom capital
adequacy and solvency of the group is critical. As such we decided to use 3% of equity (lower end of the
range) on the grounds that the Company is a start-up entity that is in the early stages of executing its
business plan, is still loss making and operates within a highly regulated industry with increased focus on
capital adequacy.

During the course of our audit, we reassessed initial materiality as our audit procedures identified a
number of audit differences which resulted in us performing a reassessment of original assessment at
planning.

Performance materiality

The application of materiality at the individual account or balance level. It is set at an arnount to reduce to
an appropriately low level the probability that the aggregate of uncorrected and undetected misstatements
exceeds materiality.

On the basis of our risk assessments, together with our assessment of the Company's overall control
environment, our judgement was that performance materiality was 50% of our planning materiality, namely
£332k as the Company is a start-up and operates within regulated industries. We have set performance
materiality at this percentage based on a number of considerations, including the number and amount of
identified misstatements, the effectiveness of the control environment and other factors affecting the
entity and its financial reporting.

Reporting threshold
An amount below which identified misstatements are considered as being clearly trivial.

We agreed with the Audit Committee that we would report to them all uncorrected audit differences in
excess of £33k, which is set at 5% of planning materiality, as well as differences below that threshold that,
in our view, warranted reporting on qualitative grounds.

We evaluate any uncorrected misstatements against both the quantitative measures of materiality
discussed above and in light of other relevant qualitative considerations in forming our opinion.

1.3. Taxation

Income tax expense comprises current and deferred taxes. Current income taxes are calculated on the
basis of the applicable tax laws of the respective countries and are recognised as expense in the financial
year in which the related taxable income arises.

Liabilities related to current taxes are recognised in the balance sheet as current tax liabilities. Deferred tax
assets and deferred tax liabilities are taken into account using the balance sheet liability method for the
expected future tax consequences of all temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets
and liabilities for financial reporting purposes and the corresponding tax values.

Deferred tax assets are reviewed at each reporting date. In considering their recoverability, the Bank
assesses the likelihood of their being recovered within a reasonably foreseeable timeframe, taking into
account the future expected profit profile and any potential legislative restrictions on use within the
respective Group companies and the tax jurisdictions in which they operate, based on the Bank's forecast
cashflows. Deferred tax assets arising from temporary differences and from tax loss carry-forwards eligible
for offset are recognised if it is considered probable (more likely than not) that sufficient taxable profits will
be available against which those differences or tax loss carry-forwards can be offset.

Deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities are calculated at tax rates expected to apply in the period in
which the tax assets are expected to be realised, or the tax liabilities settled.

Recognition and measurement

Receivables and other assets are recognised when the Bank becomes a party to the contractual
provisions of the asset. They are measured at their transaction price where the receivables do not
contain a significant financing component. They are subsequently measured at amortised cost.

The amortised cost is the amount recognised on the receivable initially, minus principal repayments,
plus cumulative amortisation (interest) using the effective interest method (reflecting any difference
between the initial and the maturity amount).

The Bank applies IFRS 9’s Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model using the General Approach to its financial
assets. Under the General Approach, IFRS 9’s ECL model uses a dual measurement approach that requires
recognition of either 12-month ECLs or lifetime ECLs. ECLs are calculated based on estimates of
probability of default (PD) and loss given default (LGD), using historical trends and forward-looking data.
This approach consists of three stages, as follows:

e Stage 1is applied to assets that have a low credit risk at initial recognition, or those have not had a
significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition. For stage 1 ECL, the 12-month ECL is used
(reflecting potential credit risk events that might occur within 12 months of the reporting date).

e Stage 2 is applied to assets that have incurred a significant increase in credit risk since initial
recognition, but that do not have objective evidence of impairment. For stage 2 ECL, these lifetime
ECLs are used (reflecting expected lifetime credit losses that result from all possible credit risk
events over the expected life of the asset).

e Stage 3 is used when financial assets are non-performing or have objective evidence of credit
impairment at the reporting date. For stage 3 ECL, lifetime ECLs are used.

The Bank defines a financial asset as in default when it is 90 days past due. The Bank writes off
receivables against the related loss provisions when it concludes there is no reasonable expectation of
recovery.

Other assets, including intra-group receivables, are assessed for expected credit loss based on
management's assessment of the counterparty.

1.7 Share-based payments

The Group maintains a number of share-based payment plans for employees and certain Directors of
the Bank. When such awards are made to employees, the fair value of these payments at grant date
serves as the basis for calculating the personnel expenses. Share-based payments that are not
subject to any further conditions are expensed immediately at grant date. Share-based payments that
are subject to the completion of a service period are expensed over the respective service period.

- Conditions that determine whether the counterparty receive the share-based payment are separated
into vesting conditions and non-vesting conditions. Vesting conditions are all conditions that
determine whether the entity receives the services that entitle the counterparty to the share-based
payment and may be differentiated further between service or performance conditions.

Performance conditions are either market conditions or non-market performance conditions. All other
conditions are considered non-vesting conditions.

The modified grant-date method requires entities to distinguish between the above-mentioned
vesting and non-vesting conditions.

Market conditions and non-vesting conditions are reflected as an adjustment (discount) to the initial
estimate of fair value at grant date of the instrument to be received and there is no true up for
differences between estimated and actual vesting due to market conditions.

The impact of service conditions and non-market performance conditions on vesting is estimated at
grant date, but it is not reflected in the grant-date fair value itself. instead, the accounting for the
share-based payment is based on the number of equity instruments for which the service and non-
market performance conditions are expected to be met. Subsequently, these estimates are trued up
for differences between the number of instruments expected to vest and the actual number of
instruments vested.

Share-based payment plans that are settled in own equity instruments (shares or options) result in an
offsetting credit in equity and are not remeasured for subsequent changes in the fair value of the
underlying equity instruments.

Risk management

The Bank uses its Risk Management Framework (RMF) to apply governance and oversight across the Bank.
The RMF has been designed to provide a clear and consistent approach to the management of risks and

controls aligned to risk appetite. The RMF is underpinned by our risk culture and three lines model, aligned to

industry practice and UK Regulatory requirements.

The Bank’s principal risks are laid out below and the order in which they are shown does not imply their
importance. Each risk is equally important in assessing the performance of the Bank in the context of
its overall risk appetite. The principal risks will be reviewed by the Board each year in line with the
Strategic and Annual Planning processes and monitored on an ongoing basis.

The Bank’s approach to identifying and managing risks continued to evolve throughout the period of
mobilisation.

The Bank faces various financial risks in its operations, principally marginal market risk (specifically interest

Framework Structure

Our Risk Management Framework provides a structured approach to managing risks across financial,
economic, operational and behavioural domains. This framework is built on the three lines model principle,
emphasising frontline risk ownership with second-line challenge and third-line assurance, defining
organisational responsibilities, processes and monitoring requirements.

Risk Management and Monitoring

Our risk management and monitoring balances our growth ambitions with risk management. We monitor both
qualitative and quantitative metrics to assess risk exposure, supported by Key Risk indicators and Early
Warning indicators which enable proactive risk management.

Risk monitoring occurs through regular reviews by the Executive Risk and Compliance Committee, with
quarterly oversight from the Board Risk Committee, facilitating scrutiny and debate of principal risks.

Capital Management Strategy

Maintaining appropriate capital levels is crucial for meeting regulatory requirements and supporting the Bank’s
business objectives. Key capital risks include potential defaults on receivables, unexpected operational losses
or challenges in capital raising.

The Bank's capital management approach includes:

e Board-set capital thresholds exceeding regulatory minimums;

e Periodic Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) reviews, submitted to the PRA;

e Implementation of Standardised and Basic Indicator approaches for credit and operational risk
assessment; and

e Endeavouring to comply with regulatory capital requirements.

Interest rate risk

Interest rate risk refers to the potential financial loss arising from un-hedged or mismatched asset and liability
positions that are sensitive to interest rate fluctuations. The Bank’s deposits are primarily at variable interest
rates, with £729 million (2023: £191 million) held in instant access accounts and £78 million (2023: £21 million)
in notice accounts with a 14-day repricing period. As a result, changes in interest rates have a minimal impact
on net interest income.

The majority of the Bank's assets are held in the Bank of England cash reserve (see note 8) and are subject to
fluctuations in the Base Rate. A 200 basis point increase/decrease in the Bank of England base rate would
have had a £103,000 negative/positive (2023: £27,000) net annualised impact on 2024 performance.

Fair value

Financial instruments measured at fair value subsequent to initial recognition are grouped into levels 1 to 3
based on the degree to which the fair value is observable:

e Level 1 fair value measurements are those derived from quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets
for identical] assets or liabilities.

« Level 2 fair value measurements are those derived from inputs other than quoted prices included
within level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, either directly or indirectly.

« Level 3 fair value measurements are those derived from valuation techniques that include inputs for
the asset or liability that are not based on observable market data (unobservable inputs).

If one or more of the significant inputs are not based on observable market data, the instrument is included in
level 3.

The fair values of the Bank's financial instruments are considered to approximate their carrying values, due
to their short-term and floating rate nature.

17. Share-based payments

Employee Share Option Plan

Employees and certain Directors of the Company participate in an equity-settled share-based remuneration
scheme which is operated by the Company's parent company, Oplyse Holdings Limited. All employees are
eligible to participate, with the only vesting conditions being that the individual remains an employee of the
Company over the vesting period, which is typically 3 years, or if an ‘exit event’ occurs.

An exit event includes, but is not limited to a share sale, an asset sale and an Initial Public Offering. No options
may be transferred, assigned or charged, and any purported transfer, assignment or charge shall cause the
option to lapse immediately. Option holders who leave the Group and who are considered to be good leavers
may keep the vested portion of their respective awards, but it will not usually become exercisable early.

There are no cash settlement alternatives. The Group does not have a past practice of cash settlement for
these share options. The Group accounts for the Option Plan as an equity-settled plan. The fair value of the
Participant services received in exchange for the grant of the options is recognised as an expense. A credit is
recognised directly in equity against Share-Based Payment Reserve. The total amount to be expensed is
determined by reference to the fair value of the options granted, calculated on a Black Scholes basis.

Options issued under the Option Plan allow Participants the right to acquire Ordinary Shares of £0.10 each in
the Parent in the future, at a set price, at exercise prices of £225.59 or £395.45, over a vesting period of an
