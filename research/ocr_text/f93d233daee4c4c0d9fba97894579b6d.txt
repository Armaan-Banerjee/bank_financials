Directors’ Report

The Directors present their report and the audited financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2011
Principal activities

QIB (UK) pic (the ‘Company’ or the ‘Bank’), was incorporated with the intention of developing and offering
Shar’a compliant financial products and solutions in the UK and Europe and to attract investment into these
markets from the Gulf and Middle East The Company received authorisation from the Financial Services
Authority (FSA) on 29 January 2008 after which date it commenced operations as a Shan'a compliant
investment bank in line with the objectives stated above

Financial results

The financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2011 are shown on pages 11 to 46 The profit for
the year Is £91,338 (2010 loss of £1,207,238)

Business review

2011 was a year of continuing development for the Bank The focus in the previous years, since authorisation in
2008, was on building the operational and business infrastructure required to support the Bank’s objectives
These years saw steady financial progression year on year as these foundations were put in place

Although 2011 stilt brought general market uncertainties driven by global financial events, the Bank was able to
build further upon the work previously completed, as reflected by the continued positive trend in the financial
results

The Bank’s main business lines remain as Asset Management, Corporate Finance, Real Estate and Treasury

Asset Management

The Asset Management division continued to expand its product offering with the aim of providing a wide range
of Shar''a compliant investment solutions to clients including high net worth individuals, sovereign wealth funds
and institutional investors

It was a positive year for the Global Sukuk Plus Fund managed by the Bank The fund continued to perform well
with the annual return of 4 9% in the year exceeding benchmark The total return since the fund's inception in
March 2009 has been 19 7% This positive track record has made the fund more attractive to new investors and
there has been solid growth in the total fund size, which was £1119 million as at 31 December 2011 (2010
£50 3 million)

Two new funds were launched during the year, The Islamic Financial Institution Fund, although incorporated in
September 2010, was fully launched in early 2011 The objective of this fund is to invest in the equity of listed
Shara complant financia! institutions Good performance was seen in the year with an annualised return of
71% In late 2011, the Qatar Opportunites Fund was also launched This fund will invest in equities exposed
directly to the period of forecast growth in Qatar

Additional structured note products were launched during the year, offenng investors the opportunity to obtain
returns linked to the performance of different stock markets over a fixed period, whilst also offering capital
protection

Dunng the year a decision was taken that the Liquidity Fund, which had been launched in May 2010, would be
placed on hold at the current time The fund had offered investors daily liquidity and the reassurance of an
AAfISI+ S&P credit rating Due to the current market rates of return, it was judged that the fund could not build
optimum scale at this tme There was no investor disadvantage and all investments were retumed to investors
upon matunty as planned with the target returns being paid in full The re-opening of the fund will be reviewed as
market conditions change and once it is judged that optimum benefits of scale can be obtained for investors

Fair value hierarchy

IFRS 7 specifies a hierarchy of valuation techniques based on whether the inputs to those valuation
techniques are observable or unobservable Observable inputs reflect market data obtained from independent
sources, unobservable inputs reflect the Company's market assumptions These two types of inputs have
created the following fair value hierarchy

* Level 1 — Quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical assets or liabilities This level includes
listed equity securities on exchanges

* Level 2 — Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable for the asset or ltability,
either directly (that is, as prices) or indirectly (that ts, derived from pnces)

+ Level 3 — Inputs for the asset or habulity that are not based on observable market data (unobservable inputs)
This level includes equity investments with significant unobservable components

This hierarchy requires the use of observable market data when available The Company considers retevant
and observable market prices in tts valuations where possible
