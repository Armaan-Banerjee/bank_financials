Managing Director’s Statement

Although there are currently no UN, EU or UK sanctions against the Bank the United States’
unilateral withdrawal from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) in 2018 and the
consequential re-imposition of Office of Foreign Asset Control (OFAC) sanctions against
numerous Iranian: entities, including the Bank, in particular the extra-territorial nature of those
sanctions continues to severely restrict Iranian trade and banking business in Europe and the UK.
Access to the SWIFT network was suspended for the majority of Iran’s State-owned banks:
including the Bank in November 2018. The conflict between Russia and Ukraine, and the ongoing
events in the Middle East, whilst not directly affecting the Bank, has stalled the negotiations that
were taking place between the original parties to the JCPOA and the US.

The above factors and the current political situation in the US and the UK are unlikely to have a
positive impact on the Bank’s operating environment and so the Bank continued to take a cautious. -
approach to asset recognition and business opportunities during the year. Recognising the PRA
focus on strong risk controls, financial and operational resilience the Bank’s three-year business
plan remains similarly cautious. ,

Since the permission to resume business given in 2016, political events have resulted in little of
the expected business developing but the Bank has been successful in staff retention and further
recruitment where necessary, and in 2023-2024 refreshed its IT infrastructure to future proof
operational functionality. These achievements have enabled the Bank to deliver on the
shareholder's stated requirement that the Board and Management manage the Bank to meet all
PRA threshold conditions and ensure the Bank's staff and systems can meet any current
operational needs as well as future business development once the political situation improves.

The Bank has continued to maintain strong capital and liquidity positions, utilising excess liquidity
to achieve good returns. The Bank's High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) have been supported by
the purchase of state issued Eurobonds and UK Gilts, under a Board-approved mandate. The
Bank’s key capital and liquidity ratios remain comfortably above regulatory minimum levels.

The success of the Bank’s remote working processes and infrastructure have led to the
implementation of an ongoing hybrid working from home programme providing staff with flexible
arrangements and no reduction in operational efficiency or of the ability of the Bank to function
normally. This policy has proved effective and been well received by staff. Management will
continue to monitor business levels and Key Performance Indicators to ensure any need to
change this policy is implemented in good time and with full staff consultation.

The Bank’s results for the year ended 31 March 2024 are set out in the Annual Report
commencing on page 27. Whilst a general higher interest rate environment and good cost control :
have enabled the Bank to maintain profitability, core business volumes remain low and a cautious
provisioning approach has resulted in reduced net profitability. Bank Sepah, Iran and the Bank's
Board continue to see a positive future for the Bank once the current political situation improves.
The ongoing support of the Shareholder and the Board’s continued stewardship have enabled
the Bank, the management team and staff to have another successful year in the face of major
challenges. | would like to thank our Chairman, fan Coleman, who has led the Board with acumen .
and clarity. Collectively the Board, with directors old and new, have provided challenge and
guidance to the Executive whilst maintaining good governance. | give my personal thanks to my
fellow directors ‘and to all members of staff for their continued efforts and support during the past
year. .

Strategic Report

Business strategy

The majority of the Bank's credit exposure is to Iranian financial institutions in Europe and Iran.
The ongoing US action and the UK’s departure from the EU have all heavily restricted the Bank’s
ability to pursue its Euro business strategy. No direct access to the UK payment systems
continues to prevent Sterling business.

Currently the difficulties around the flow of funds and/or the perfection of security to support
bilateral or syndicated lending persist. The loss of SWIFT access has continued to restrict regular
trade finance business.

Despite this, the Bank's long-term strategy remains resolute: future growth of Trade Finance
business alongside good quality lending. The lack of opportunities to expand the lending book
however has resulted in money market placements to maintain profitability at plan levels.

To support its business strategy, in the years immediately following the implementation of the
JCPOA, the Bank recruited quality staff and upgraded its core IT systems. In 2023/2024 it
refreshed core IT hardware and software to cope with technology upgrade and to be ready to
accommodate future business volume increases. Active engagement with trade associations,
advisory services and: government information services support: efforts to: ensure all staff are
equipped with the skills to meet their roles. In line with Board and regulatory focus, governance
continues to be reviewed and enhanced embedding Financial and Operational Resilience as well
as Climate Change awareness in everything we do, with a suite of policies and procedures which
fit together and are regularly reviewed.

Financial and Operational Resilience

In line with PRA focus and despite the restrictions the Bank faces, Financial and Operational
Resilience are core to how the Bank is managed and operates. A strict Credit Policy with all
exposures reviewed by Credit and/or Risk Committees, and exposures predominantly to
regulated Financial Institutions. The Bank maintains liquidity and capital ratios well above
regulatory requirements and has demonstrated its financial resilience in its ILAAP, ICAAP and
Recovery Plan stress test results.

Operational Resilience poses significant issues to the Bank as many third-party suppliers are
unable to service the Bank due to their banker's or their own compliance policy as a result of US
Sanctions. Despite this the Bank has implemented technology upgrades, is able to support all
staff working remotely and has a fully remote disaster recovery provision. The Bank has identified
its.Important Business Services (IBS), set impact tolerances, mapped vulnerabilities, undertaken
scenario tests and assessed their impact, in good time for the March 2025 PRA deadiine.

ESG and Climate Change

The Bank has implemented an Environmental, Social, Governance and Climate Change Strategy
taking account of physical and transitional climate risks and embedding these in its Risk
Framework and credit processes. A:formal Climate Change assessment is incorporated within:
every credit application including positive scoring for those which are environmentally sustainable
or provide a net positive outcome. The Bank is an equal opportunities employer, does not tolerate
harassment or prejudice with applicable policies in place and has a strong governance structure,
with tone led from the top.

The Bank recognises that the economy of its home market, Iran, has traditionally been dominated
‘by fossil fuel and :mineral ‘resource ‘sectors ‘but ‘its government -has ‘recently looked ‘to ‘support
renewables and diversification. Although the Bank has provided financing for traditional sectors
in the past, there is currently limited involvement in these areas. In time, the Bank’s focus will be
on supporting sustainable projects and facilitating the transition of these traditional sectors into
more environmentally-friendly and socially-responsible practices. This aligns with both the Iranian
‘Government and the Shareholder’s strategy, and should in time lead to good business
opportunities.

Capital Adequacy

The Bank has adopted the PRA’s implementation of the EU’s Capital Requirements Directive
(CRD V). The CRD V framework consists of three Pillars.

Pillar 1 determines the minimum. capital requirements of banks to cover credit, market and-
operational risks.

Pillar 2 can be divided into two major components: (i) institutions are expected to establish sound,,.
effective and complete strategies and processes to assess and maintain, on an ongoing basis,
the amounts, types and distribution of internal capital commensurate to their risk profiles, an
‘Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP), as well as robust governance and
internal control arrangements and (ii) a supervisory review and evaluation process (SREP). The
key purpose of the SREP is to ensure that institutions have adequate arrangements, strategies,
processes and mechanisms as well as capital and liquidity to ensure a sound. management.and.
coverage of their risks, to which they are or might be exposed, including those revealed by stress
testing and risks that the institution. may pose-to the. financial-system.

As part of the CRD V requirements an ICAAP has been approved by the Board which identifies a
number of other risks faced by the Bank which attract capital under the Pillar 2 rules. The total
capital requirement: of the Bank is determined: as: the sum of the Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 capital:
requirements.

Pillar 3 of the CRD V requires all banks to publish certain details of their risk exposures, capital
adequacy and risk management procedures over and above that provided in the Annual Report
and Financial Statements. This information, which has not been audited, has been published on
the Bank's website www. banksepah.co.uk.

The Bank's capital has been well in excess of both the combined Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 and the
PRA's requirements throughout the year. At.31 March 2024, the Bank's total capital requirement
amounted to €39.1m (2023: €43.8m) which means that, with Tier 1 capital of €171.4m (20237
€171.2m), the Bank had a capital surplus of €132.1m (2023: €126.3m) at the year-end or 337.6%
(2023: 288.6%). These capital figures exclude the current year profit. .

Liquidity

The Bank is subject to the requirements set out in the PRA’s. Rulebook. Individual Liquidity
Guidance (ILG) is set by the PRA through a Liquidity Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process
(LSREP) which, in turn, is guided by the Bank’s own Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment
Process (ILAAP).

The Treasury Department ensures that the Bank’s balance sheet remains robust and maintains
liquidity in accordance with both internal and regulatory requirements. The Bank continues to
maintain sufficient liquidity to meet all known and likely demands which could be made upon it by
its customers and ensures that such liquidity is available on a day-to-day basis.
Strategic Report (continued)

The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) requires a bank to maintain unencumbered High Quality
Liquid Assets ("HQLA”) that are equal to or in excess of 100% of its total net cash outflow
assumptions over a 30-day period. The Bank maintained a healthy liquidity position during the
year which was significantly in excess of the regulatory minimum. The LCR showed a steady
increase during the year due primarily to the purchase of additional sovereign bonds which
increased HQLA. The LCR at the year-end was 2877%.

Cash, cash equivalents and deposits placed with banks comprise ‘90% of the Bank's total assets
as at 31 March 2024 (2023: 82%).

These are monitored by the Risk Committee and implemented through the Bank’s Credit
Committee which has a limited delegated authority. AN exposure limits are presented to Credit
Committee with applications and reviews including full credit analysis, input from Compliance and
Risk, with Climate change risks assessed.

All limits are reviewed at least annually with Board approval for those above the Credit

Risk management

The Board of Directors is responsible for setting the Bank’s overail risk appetite and documenting
this within a Risk Appetite Statement (“RAS”). A Risk Framework document which sets out the
risk management structure, its roles and operational systems is also approved by the Board.

The Bank operates a “three lines of defence” methodology. Individual departments within the
Bank are the first line of defence.and the senior managers in the department are the risk-owners.
There is appropriate segregation of activities. between departments. consistent with the scale of
the Bank's business. Each department is subject

to relevant policies and have their own departmental procedures. Policies are approved. by the.
Board and procedures are approved by Executive Management Committee (EMC).

‘The second line of defence, comprising the separate Risk and Compliance departments,
provide independent oversight of the first line of defence. In accordance with the Risk
Framework the first and second lines are responsible for proposing changes. to the policies and
procedures to mitigate risk and for identifying. emerging risks.

The Risk department provides particular oversight of credit, liquidity, market and operational risk.

Compliance provides oversight in respect-of regulatory risk and risk reporting. This second line of
defence delivers regular standing and. ad hoc reports to the Risk Committee which review. these
and provides recommendations to the Board. These reports include the Risk Register and the
latest Monthly Risk Report. The latter report details the principal risks facing the Bank and records
month-end figures. against the Early Warning Indicators and the-minimum level of risk appetite:

The Bank’s principal and emerging risks are reviewed by the EMC and the Risk Committee on a
regular basis, alongside associated financial, operational and compliance controls. These
documents, along with Operational Risk Events and Loss notification reports, and their associated
remedial plans and mitigative actions, are formally reviewed by the Risk Committee on a quarterly
basis.

The third line of defence consist of the internal audit function which provides independent |
assurance and Challenge, reporting to the Audit Committee. The internal audit function has been

auditors.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal inherent risks that the Bank faces are credit, market, operational, concentration and
liquidity risks. Information on these principal risks is provided in note 25. The Directors have also
identified the lack of access to GBP clearing facilities, loss of key personnel, lack of diversity of
IT service providers and the geo-political uncertainties as giving rise to further major risks to which
the bank is exposed.

The Bank is committed to maintaining robust and effective governance to ensure the efficient and -
prudent running of its operation. The responsibility for the risk management framework is
discharged and administered through a number of delegated committees, both Board and
Executive. in addition, the Managing Director has executive responsibility for the overall.
operational risk management of the Bank.

Day-to-day risk oversight is delegated to key control departments, namely, Risk Management,
Compliance, Operations and the Finance department ensuring that there is arobust management
structure with clear segregation of duties and responsibilities, v where Possible, in keeping with the
scale and complexity of the Bank.

The Board has ultimate responsibility for the setting of the Bank's strategy, its risk appetite and
on-going management of the Bank's risk exposure. This is communicated and embedded in the
Bank's culture through a suite of policies and procedures. The Bank's key risk principles are set
out in its Risk Framework. ;

The Board and the management have taken appropriate measures to mitigate its key risks as far
as possible and have established other means to facilitate its GBP payments until such time it
can re-join the GBP clearing system. =

Further to the above risks, the Bank has also considered the potential risk and impact of the
prominent global crises on its operations such as ESG and climate change which have been
covered at the start of this report.

Going concern basis of accounting

The Directors have reviewed the current and potential future business activities and financial
position of the Bank, including an assessment of capital and liquidity requirements for the
foreseeable future in line with its business plan.

This review covers the going concern up to 31 July 2025. The going concern assessment is
prepared by Executive Management, reviewed and approved by the Board.

The planned business activities are very cautious and takes into account the current geopolitical
issues and restrictions the Bank operates within.

As at 31 March 2024, the Bank held eligible capital of €171m (excluding current year profit) and
had a capital surplus of €132m (2023 €126m). !n line with previous years, the 2024 profits will be -
retained to further strengthen the capital position.

The Bank’s latest ICAAP and ILAAP documents stress tests which were updated during the
financial year and demonstrate its resilience given its significant capital surplus and liquidity
position. The banks ended the year with very strong capital and liquidity ratios.

Based on this review, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Bank has adequate
resources to continue in operational existence for at least up to 31 July 2025. Therefore, the
financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis. .

(b) Interest income and expense

Interest income and expense for all interest-bearing instruments are recognised in profit or loss, on an accruals
basis, using the effective interest method. The effective interest method is a method of calculating the amortised
- cost of a financial asset or liability and of allocating the interest income or interest expense. The effective interest
rate is the rate that exactly discounts the estimated future cash payments or receipts over the expected life of.
instrument or, when appropriate, a shorter period, to the net carrying amount of the financial asset or financial
liability.

The effective interest rate is calculated on initial recognition of the financial asset or liability, estimating the future
cash flows after considering ail the contractual terms of the instrument but not future credit losses. The
calculation includes all amounts paid or received by the Bank including fees, expected early redemptions and
related penalties and premiums and discounts that are an integral part of the overall return as well as direct
incremental transaction costs related to the acquisition, issue.or.disposal of.a financial instrument. -For fixed rate
financial assets this is the original effective interest rate and for floating rate financial assets this is the current
effective interest rate.

(c) Fees and commission income

Fees and commissions are generally recognised at the point in time when the performance obligation is satisfied.
Loan participation fees are amortised over the term of the loan through inclusion in the effective interest rate.
Commissions arising from trade finance related business such as advising and payments under letters of credit
are recognised at the transaction date.

Business Model Assessment

An assessment of the bank's business model for managing financial assets is fundamental to the classification

Solely Payments of Principal and Interest Test

impairment Basis

IFRS 9 requires a forward-looking expected credit loss methodology. All financial assets not held at FVPL and
any undrawn commitments are assessed for an allowance for expected credit loss.

The expected credit loss ("ECL") is calculated as the probability of default ("PD”) applied to a forecast exposure
at default (“EAD”) multiplied by the Loss Given Default (“LGD”), which is the inverse of the anticipated recovery
fate, to calculate the expected credit exposure (“ECE”). The ECE is then discounted by the effective interest
rate (“EIR”) to calculate the ECL.

Expected Credit Loss Methodology .
For a financial instrument ECL is calculated as the Expected Credit Exposure (‘ECE’) discounted by EIR.

The ECE is calculated for the following exposures:
e Financings and debt instruments not held at FVPL

Amounts due from financial institutions and customers

Cash and balances with banks

Other assets representing amounts held in advance of payments to creditors

Off-balance sheet credit exposures such as undrawn committed facilities, letters of credit and
guarantees.

Financings subject to the calculation of ECE are allocated into three stages.

Stage1: This is the stage allocated to the exposure when recorded. At the time of recording an ECE is calculated
based on a one-year probability of default. This continues until derecognised or the exposure moves to stage 2
or 3.

: Stage 2: Where a credit exposure has experienced a significant increase in credit risk since origination the Bank
~ allocates the exposure to Stage 2. The PDs and LGDs are recalculated over the contractual maturity of the
‘instrument or if overdue but not impaired on an expected maturity.

“Stage 3: Credit exposures considered impaired are transferred to stage 3. The PD for these exposures is fixed
‘at 100%. The LGD is calculated on an individual basis as the weighted average of different scenarios in
accordance with individual scenarios. Impairment Josses and releases are considered separately from the
Expected Credit Loss calculation for stage 1 and stage 2 exposures.

Write-offs

Financial Assets are written off when the Bank determines that the borrower does not have assets or sources
of income that could generate sufficient cash flows to repay the amounts subject to the write-off. A write-off
constitutes a derecognition event.

Interest income basis

Interest is calculated on the gross value of the interest-bearing instruments for stage 1 and stage 2 financial
assets. Per IFRS 9, for credit-impaired financial assets, the Bank applies the effective interest rate of the
amortised cost of the financial asset net of impairment in subsequent reporting periods.

Definitions- ECL Calculation Components
PD: The estimate of the likelihood of a payment default over a given time horizon. PDs are allocated to ratings .
in accordance with Board approved Expected Credit Loss Policy. Individual exposures are allocated to
exposures by Credit Committee at the time of annual review or approval and are reviewed at the year end.

EAD: The outstanding exposure at default prior to any adjustment for collateral held. Committed facilities are
assumed to be fully drawn. Uncommitted facilities or exposures on demand are calculated on actual balance.
Bonds and other securities are calculated on the amount repayable on maturity.

(j) Corporation Tax

Corporation tax on the profit or loss for the year comprises current tax and deferred tax. Corporation tax is
recognised in profit or loss except to the extent that it relates to items recognised directly in other comprehensive
income in which case it is recognised in other comprehensive income.

Current tax is the tax expected to be payable on the taxable profit for the year, calculated using rates applicable
at the Statement of Financial Position date, and any adjustment to tax payable in respect of previous years.

Deferred tax is accounted for using the balance sheet liability method in respect of temporary differences arising
from differences between the carrying amount of assets and liabilities in the Financial Statements and the
corresponding tax basis used in the computation of taxable profit.

In principle, deferred tax liabilities are recognised for all taxable temporary differences and deferred tax assets
are recognised to the extent that it is probable that taxable profits will be available against which deductible
temporary differences can be utilised.

Deferred tax is calculated at the rates that are expected to apply when the asset or liability is settled. Deferred
tax is charged or credited in the Income Statement, except when it relates to items credited or charged directly
‘to other comprehensive income, in which case the deferred tax is also dealt with in other comprehensive income.

‘The directors believe that the judgements made in determining the Bank’s tax liabilities are reasonable and
appropriate; however actual experience may differ and materially affect future tax charges.
