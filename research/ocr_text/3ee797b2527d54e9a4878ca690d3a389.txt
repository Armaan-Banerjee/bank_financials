Results and financial highlights

The Company's total funds under management and administration decreased 10.3% to £46.4 billion at 31 December 2022 (2021: £51.7
billion) impacted by economic uncertainty and worldwide volatility in the markets. Despite market conditions, the Company continued
to attract net organic inflows during the year. As a result of lower average funds under management and administration operating
income decreased to £351.1 million from £352.9 million in 2021. Operating costs increased £24.2 million or 8.7% to £302.0 million
(2021: £277.7 million) resulting in a profit before tax of £49.2 million (2020: £75.2 million).

Treasury assets

As a licensed deposit taker, the Company holds most of Rathboues Group Pic and fellow subsidiaries’ surplus liquidity on its balance
sheet together with clients’ cash. Cash in client portfolios as held on a banking basis, excluding balances due to fellow Group
undertakings, of £2.5 billion (2021: £2.3 billion), represented 5.4% of total funds under management and administration at 31
December 2022 compared to 4.4% at the end of 2021. Cash held in designated client money accounts was £5.7 million (2021: £7.9
million).

During the year, the share of treasury assets held with the Bank of England reduced to £1.4 billion from £1.5 billion at 31 December
2021. Client balances increased year on year, peaking in September before falling as portfolio investment increased.

The treasury department of the Company, reporting through the banking committee to the Baard, operates in accordance with
procedures set out in a board-approved treasury mamual and monitors exposure to market, credit and liquidity risk as described in note
29 to the financial statements. The treasury department invests in a range of securities issued by a relatively large number of
counterparties, These counterparties must be single ‘A’-rated or higher by Fitch at the time of investment and are regularly reviewed
by the banking committee.

Loans to clients

Loans are provided as a service to investment management clients who have short-to-medium term cash requirements. Such loans are
nonnally made on a fully secured basis against portfolios held in the Company's nominee name, requiring two times cover, and are
usually advanced for five years (see note 14 to the financial statements). In addition, charges may be taken on property held by the
client to meet security cover requirements. The Company's ability to provide such loans is a valuable additianal service, for example,
for clients that require bridging finance when buying or selling their homes.

Loans advanced decreased to £160.0 million at the end of 2022 (2021: £168.0 million) as clients demand for bridging finance
decreased in favour of drawing down fram investment portfolios.

Intanpible assets

Intangible assets arise principally from acquired growth in funds under management and administration and are categorised as
goodwall and client relationships. Intangible assets reported on the balance sheet also include purchased and developed software. At 31
December 2022, the total carrying value of intangible assets arising from acquired growth was £158.1 million (2021: £170.4 million).
During the year, purchased and acquired client relationship intangible assets of £1.0 million were capitalised (2021: £8.6 million). No
goodwill was acquired in the year (2021: none).

Client relationship intangibles are amortised over the estimated life of the client relationship, generally a period of 10 to 15 years, but
no longer than 15 years. When client relationships are lost, any related intangible asset is derecognised in the year. The total
amortisation charge for client relationships in 2022, including the impact of any lost relationships, was £13.3 million (2021: £13.5
million).

Goodwill, which arises fram business combinations, is not amortised but is subject to a test for impairment at Ieast annually. No
goodwill was found to be impaired during 2022 or 2021.

Further details on the Company’s intangible assets are provided in note 20 to the financial statements.

Impairment of goodwill and intangible asscts

At each balance sheet date, the Company reviews the carrying amounts of its intangible assets to determine whether there is any
indication that those assets have suffered an impairment Joss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is

The recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value-in-use. In assessing value-in-use, the estimated future
cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value
of money.

Goodwill is tested for impainment at least annually. For the purposes of impairment testing, goodwil] is allocated to the Company’s
groups of cash generating units (“CGUs”). The carrying amount of each graup of CGUs is compared to its value-in-use, calculated
using a discaunted cash flow method. If the recoverable amount of the group of CGUs is less than the carrying amount of the group of
units, the impairment Joss is allocated first to reduce the carrying amount of the goodwill allocated to that group of units and then ta
the other assets of the group of units pro-rata on the basis of the carrying amount of each asset in the group of units.

Client relationship intangibles are tested for impairment by comparing the fair value of funds under management and administration
for each individually acquired client relationship (or, for clicut relationships acquired with a business combination, each acquired
portfolio of clients) with their associated amortised valuc. An example of evidence of impairment would be lost client relationships. In
detennining whether a client relationship is Jost, the Company considers factors such as the level of funds withdrawn and the existence

Provisions and contingent liabilities

Contingent liabilities are possible obligations that depend on the outcome of uncertain future events or those present obligations where
