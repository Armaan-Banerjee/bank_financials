Strategic report

The directors are pleased to present their Strategic Report of JN Bank UK Ltd (“the Bank”) For the
year ended 31 March 2022.

Key highlights

The Bank has progressively grown in size and maturity in the year. We have continued to invest in
cloud-based digital technology, data and people to create an efficient and scalable model. This has
allowed us to steadily expand our business and to deliver an excellent customer experience while
successfully navigating the disruption and uncertainty created by the COVID-19 pandemic
("pandemic"). This has enabled the Bank to growits net interest income significantly to £3m (2021:
£0.1m) as the business gradually scales up to become profitable, projected in 2023.

Business model

The business model of the Bank is to provide unsecured personal lending across the retail market
in segments which may be typically underserved by the larger banks. We differentiate ourselves
with straightforward products, and a personalised approach to lending, that offer us a competitive
advantage. In order to do this, the Bank employs a high level of data analysis and risk management
expertise. Lending is originated both directly and through partners and is Funded by offering
simple, competitively priced online savings products directly to personal customers.

Our model is underpinned by a commitment to the disciplines of risk management, regulatory
compliance and governance. Risk appetite and culture are set by the Board to provide the
appropriate tone From the top.

Our values

We exist to work for our customers and their communities. We understand the importance of
treating everyone with compassion and respect, that everyone has a story, and that is why helping
our customers find a way to achieve their goals is at our heart.

Our approach is straightforward - Financial products that are Fair, transparent and simple. Our
background, being part of The Jamaica National Group, gives us a unique focus as part of a mutual
organisation. We celebrate the differences of all our customers and the Bank's ethos, as part of a
mutual organisation, is built on inclusion and care for all. We care about our customers, their
individual needs, and the environment in which we live.

Business review

To mitigate the impact of the pandemic the Bank implemented additional restrictions to moderate
lending volumes and to reduce credit risk during the pandemic, and more recently in the Face of
the deterioration of economic forecasts in 2022. The volumes of loans written has been controlled
through credit risk criteria, pricing and proactive management. Despite the challenges of the
pandemic, the Bank has delivered controlled growth within its risk appetite. As a result, gross
lending increased to £63m in the year. The portfolio balance before provisions at the year end was
£60m (2021: £9m). To Fund lending growth, customer deposits have increased to £70m (2021:
£32m).

We have improved our customer journey and strengthened our relationship with our customers
resulting in a Trustpilot score of 4.9 stars and culminated in the award of ‘Best Personal Loan
Provider’ at the British Bank Awards (BBA) 2022. The Bank was also shortlisted For the ‘Customer
Service Champion’ award. These awards are voted for by consumers and reflect the great level of
engagement we have our customers, and we are proud of our achievements.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Financial institutions operate in a competitive and highly regulated environment and are subject to
economic and other uncertainties. The management of risk is therefore central to the continuing
success of the Bank.

The Board is responsible for determining the Bank's risk management Framework and system of
internal controls, which are designed to enable the Bank to achieve its objectives within a managed
risk profile, not to eliminate all risk. To do this, the Bank bases its risk management framework on
the ‘three lines of defence’ model. The ‘first line’ of defence is the checks and controls utilised by
operational staff directly engaged in the management of the risks in their area of activity. The
‘second line’ is the assurance provided by the Bank’s Risk Management Function. The ‘third line’ is
the independent internal audit reports regularly provided to management and the Board Audit and
Board Risk Committees by an external provider.

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank is unable to settle obligations with
immediacy and maintain public and stakeholder confidence. Funding risk is
the risk that the Bank is unable to realise assets or otherwise raise funds on
economic terms, and/or within reasonable timescales. The Bank mitigates
these risks by holding an adequate portion of its assets in the Form of cash
balances and high-quality liquid assets, and by managing the maturity profile
of its Funding portfolio. The Bank manages its liquid assets to self-imposed
limits, in addition to regulatory metrics, that ensure access to sufficient
liquidity given a range of possible adverse scenarios.

Operational
risk

Operational risk is the risk of losses resulting From inadequate or Failed
internal processes, people and systems or from external events, such as third
party supplier Failure, cyber-attacks or fraud. The Bank has a low tolerance
for operational losses and seeks to operate in such a way that these losses
are mitigated. The Bank recognises that some operational risk events are
inevitable and therefore ensures that significant risk events are logged,
investigated and subject to root-cause analysis.

Banking is a highly regulated market and can be affected by frequent changes
and updates in legislation and regulation. The Prudential Regulation
Authority (PRA) and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) are the primary
bodies which regulate the banking industry in the UK. The Bank operates
within a defined set of Frameworks and policies which outline the regulatory

implemented internal processes to capture any Future legislative and
regulatory change well in advance of its implementation date, thus ensuring
that the Bank is able to continue to adequately meet all regulatory
requirements.

Climate
change

As the worldwide focus on climate change intensifies, both the physical risks
and the transitional risks associated with climate change continue to grow.

Physical risks can relate to specific weather events, such as storms and
flooding, or to longer-term shifts in the climate, such as rising sea levels.
Transitional risks may arise From the adjustment towards a ‘low carbon’
economy, such as loss of employment in ‘high carbon’ industries. The Board
reviews climate change risks on a regular basis.

The Board has considered the financial risks From the climate crisis.

The Bank would appear to be relatively isolated from the impacts of climate
change.

e The Bank has only one office and one branch, and lending is not
secured on physical assets, there are no material exposures to
physical climate crisis risks, either now or in the foreseeable Future.

« it has no exposure to property or businesses that could see changing
fortunes resulting From legislation or economic impacts their business
models.

However, it is possible that the wider impact on the economy and
unemployment could be material. Any impact on the Bank would therefore
be market-wide rather than idiosyncratic. The transition to a lower carbon
economy may have a more significant long-term impact, and the risk forms
part of the Bank's risk Framework. The Bank has undertaken stress testing by
reviewing the possible impact of climate change on the UK economy and
hence the Bank.

The results show that although the impact (higher unemployment and lower
gross domestic product (GDP)) would have a material impact on the Bank, its
viability would not be impacted.

Going Concern

Notwithstanding a loss For the year of £6.5m (2021: £6.3m loss), the financial statements have been
prepared on the going concern basis, which the directors consider to be appropriate for the
Following reasons.

The directors have taken into account the Bank's business plan and related forecasts For the next
S years up to 31 March 2027. These forecasts indicate that, taking account of severe but plausible
downside scenarios, and a downside scenario where no new capital funding is obtained, the Bank
will have sufficient resources to continue in business for the foreseeable Future including a period
of at least 12 months From the date of approval of these Financial statements.

The directors have modelled a severe but plausible downside scenario using macro-economic
assumptions that they consider to be at least as severe as those in the Bank of England's 2021
published stress scenario. These assumptions result in a significant increase in projected credit
losses in the severe but plausible scenario. This is driven by the uncertainty over the impact of the
pandemic and inflationary pressures in the economy.

The downside scenario, with no new capital, indicates that the Bank is not dependent on JN
Financial Group Limited providing additional Financial support during that period in order to
continue in operational existence. JN Financial Group Limited has nevertheless indicated its
intention to continue to make available funds as are planned by the Bank For the period covered by
the Forecasts. In addition, the directors have considered the capital strength of JN Financial Group
Limited in their assessment of the Group's ability to provide the additional Financial support.

The financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis as the directors are
satisfied that the Bank will be able to operate with adequate levels of both liquidity and capital For
at least a period of 12 months From the date of approval of these Financial statements. tn making
this assessment, the directors have considered a wide range of information relating to present and
future conditions, including the Bank's statement of Financial position, Future projections of
profitability, cash Flows, capital resources, and the longer-term strategy of the business.

d) Financial assets - Impairment

The Bank recognises impairment loss allowances For Expected Credit Losses (“ECLs”) on the
Following Financial assets:
e Loans and advances to customers; and

d.1 Measurement of ECLs

ECLs are an unbiased probability-weighted estimate of the present value of credit losses, taking
account of Forward-looking information. ECLs are measured as the difference between contractual
cash Flows and expected cash Flows arising from an asset, discounted at the asset’s effective
interest rate.

When measuring ECLs, the Bank assesses the probability of default (“PD”), the expected exposure
at the time of default, exposure at default (EAD), and the loss that is expected to arise in the event
of default, loss given default (LGD). The maximum period considered is the maximum contractual
period over which the Bank is exposed to credit risk. The Bank typically assesses ECLs on an
individual asset basis.

IFRS 9 requires an impairment loss allowance to be recognised at an amount equal to either 12-
month ECLs or lifetime ECLs. Lifetime ECLs are the ECLs that result from all possible default events
over the expected life of a financial instrument. 12-month ECLs are the ECLs that result From
default events that are possible within the 12 months after the reporting date.

The Bank measures impairment loss allowances at an amount equal to lifetime ECLs, except for the
Following which are measured as 12-month ECLs:

e treasury assets that are determined to have low credit risk at the reporting date. The Bank
considers a treasury asset to have low credit risk when its credit rating is equivalent to the
globally understood definition of ‘investment grade’; and

e other Financial assets on which credit risk has not increased significantly since their initial
recognition and which were not considered to be credit impaired at the time of initial
recognition.

Lifetime ECLs are required where the credit risk on a Financial asset has increased significantly since
initial recognition (except for investment grade treasury assets, as noted above}, or where the
assets were credit impaired at the time of initial recognition.

d.2 Forward-looking information

The Bank incorporates forward-looking information into both its assessment of whether the credit
risk of a Financial asset has increased significantly since initial recognition and its measurement of
ECLs. The time period over which the Bank assesses forward-looking information is the maximum
period over which the Bank is exposed to credit risk. The Bank considers a range of representative
scenarios For the possible Future direction of key economic variables. The scenarios are derived by
reference to external information where this is publicly available and appropriate, together with
internally generated views. A probability-weighting, based on management judgement, is assigned
to each scenario.

The Bank's ‘base case’ scenario represents a view of the most likely outcome and is aligned with
information used by the Bank for other purposes such as strategic planning and budgeting. The
selection of alternative scenarios is intended to model the non-linear impact of economic factors
on ECLs For the Bank’s Financial assets.

For unsecured loan impairment, the Bank typically considers a minimum of three scenarios; this
includes the base case scenario, together with an upside scenario (more optimistic than the base
case) anda downside scenario (more pessimistic than the base case). For treasury asset impairment,
the Bank typically considers the base case scenario and a downside scenario.

d.6 Write-offs

h.1 Effective interest rate

Interest income and expense are recognised in profit or loss using the effective interest method.
The ‘effective interest rate’ is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash payments or
receipts through the expected life of the financial asset or Financial liability to the gross carrying
amount of the Financial asset or Financial liability.

When calculating the effective interest rate For Financial instruments the Bank estimates future
cash Flows considering all contractual terms of the financial instrument, but not ECLs.

The calculation of the effective interest rate includes transaction costs and Fees paid that are an
integral part of the effective interest rate. Transaction costs include incremental costs that are
directly attributable to the acquisition or issue of a financial asset or Financial liability.

A key source of uncertainty is the rate of early settlement of loans (prepayments). A higher rate of
prepayments can change the estimated life of the loan and hence the EIR. Prepayments are
currently not easy to predict and there is insufficient observable data to apply a portfolio treatment
for prepayments. The Bank therefore apportions costs over the contractual life of customer loans.
Any EIR balance resulting from capitalised costs is written off on settlement of loan ahead of the
contractual date.

The Bank has capitalised £586k (2021: £70k) of transaction costs which are incremental and directly
attributable to the issue of customer loans, such as Fees and commissions paid to aggregators over
the expected life of the customer loan. The Bank expensed £182k (2021: £5k) of transaction costs
attributable to the effective interest rate treatment during the year ended 31 March 2022.

Amendments to IFRS 16 Leases: COVID-19-Related Rent Concessions Beyond 30 June 2021 (Issued

March 2021)
The amendment provides an extension to the current beneficial relief (May 2020
amendment) permitted to be applied by lessees that have been subject to COVID-19-
related rent concessions. The amendment extends the period allowed For rent concessions
from 30 June 2021 to 30 June 2022. The relief provides a practical expedient that permits
entities to account For a change in the lease payments resulting from COVID-19-related rent
concessions as if the change was not a lease modification with the impact of the rent
concession, therefore, being recognised in profit or loss For the period and not over the
residual term of the lease. The amendment was UK-adopted in May 2021.

Effective from date of announcement by the IFRS Interpretations committee in April 2021

IFRS Interpretations Committee (IFRIC) agenda decision: Accounting For configuration or
customisation costs within a Software as a Service (SaaS) arrangement
° The IFRIC agenda decision discusses how to account for configuration or customisation

costs associated with software purchased From a supplier in a Software as a Service (SaaS)
arrangement (i.e. a cloud-based computing arrangement). An intangible asset should not be
recognised where an entity does not control the software being configured or customised,
and those configuration or customisation activities do not create a resource controlled by
the entity that is separate From the software. Where an intangible asset is not recognised
for the costs of configuration or customisation of the application software, then the costs
shouid be recognised as an expense in line with the nature of the contract and based on
who performs the services. The costs may therefore be expensed immediately or
recognised as a prepayment and expensed over the term of the contract.

The amendments do not have a material effect on the Bank’s Financial statements.

effect on the Bank’s Financial statements.

reasonable due to their short term nature and use of highly rated counterparties.

(ii) The Fair value of investment securities is disclosed by reference to market prices. Investment
securities, being government debt securities, are financial instruments issued by sovereign
governments and include both long-term bonds and short-term bills with fixed or Floating rate
interest payments. These instruments are generally highly liquid and traded in active markets
resulting in a Level 1 classification. When active market prices are not available, the Bank uses
discounted cash flow models with observable market inputs of similar instruments and bond prices
to estimate Future index levels and extrapolating yields outside the range of active market trading, in
which instances the Bank classifies those securities as Level 2. The Bank does not have Level 3
government securities where valuation inputs would be unobservable.

(iii) The Fair value represents the discounted amount of estimated Future cash flows after allowing
For expected impairment provisions and discounted at current market rates.

a. Credit risk
Credit risk is the risk of Financial loss to the Bank if a customer or counterparty to a financial
instrument fails to meet its contractual obligations. The Bank recognises impairment loss
allowances For ECLs calculated as per IFRS 9 requirements at the statement of financial position
date.
Exposure to credit risk

Credit risk exposure is the amount of loss that the Bank would suffer if all counterparties to which
the Bank is exposed were to default at once. This is represented substantially by the gross carrying
amount of financial assets, without taking account of the value of any collateral held.

There are no off-balance-sheet assets and the maximum exposure to credit risk is represented by the
amount of financial assets in the statements of Financial position.

Credit risk measurement
Loans and advances

The estimation of credit exposure For risk management purposes is complex and requires the use of
models, as the exposure varies with changes in market conditions, expected cash Flows and the
passage of time, The assessment of credit risk of a portfolio of assets involves estimations as to the
likelihood of defaults occurring, of the associated loss ratios and of default correlations between
counterparties. The Bank measures credit risk using Probability of Default (PD), Exposure at Defautt
(EAD) and Loss Given Default (LGD).

Credit risk grading

The Bank uses internal credit risk gradings that reflect its assessment of the credit-worthiness of
individual counterparties at origination date. Borrower and loan specific information collected at
the time of application is fed into this rating model. This is supplemented with external data such as
credit bureau scoring information on individual borrowers. In addition, the model enables expert
judgement from management to be fed into the Final internal credit rating for each exposure. This
allows For considerations which may not be captured as part of the other data inputs into the madel.

The credit grades are calibrated such that the risk of default increases exponentially at each higher
risk grade.

e. Capital risk and management

mn

The Bank has operated in line with its capital risk appetite as set by the Board and above its
regulatory requirements throughout the period ended 31 March 2022.

Capital is held to protect depositors, by ensuring that the Bank has sufficient capital
resources to repay liabilities even in the face of unexpected losses. Day-to-day capital
management is delegated to the Chief Financial Officer and overseen by the risk Function,
ALCO, Risk Committee and ultimately the Board. The Bank's objective in managing its capital
is to maintain appropriate levels of capital to support its business strategy and to meet
regulatory requirements at all times. The Bank manages its capital in accordance with Capital
Requirements Regulation (CRR) and Capital Requirements Directive (together referred to as
CRD IV). The Framework is enforced by the PRA. The PRA sets and monitors the Bank's capital
requirements.

The PRA's approach to the measurement of capital adequacy is primarily based on monitoring
the relationship oF the capital resources requirement to available capital resources. The
regulator sets Total Capital Requirement (TCR) for each bank in excess of the minimum
resources requirement of 8% of risk weighted assets. A key input to the TCR setting process
is the Bank’s Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP).

The Bank's policy is to maintain a strong capital base to maintain stakeholder and market
confidence and to sustain the future development of the business. The Bank monitors
current and forecast levels of capital against the capital risk appetite approved by the Board
and reports the capital position to ALCO, Risk Committee and the Board on a regular basis.
The capital forecast Forms an integral component of the annual budgeting process and is
updated in line with changes to the Bank's business plan.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss arising From inadequate or failed internal processes, people
and systems or From external events. These could arise from information technology,
information security, project outsourcing, tax, legal, fraud and compliance risks.

The Bank has established an Operational Risk management framework, underpinned by clear
policies which are geared to ensuring the Bank operates with a low risk appetite for operational
risk Failures.

The First line of defence is responsible for the management of operational risk in accordance
with stated policies, governance, agreed processes and controls. The second line of defence
provides oversight and challenge supplemented by a programme of regular assurance testing.
The third line of defence (via internal audit) provides an independent review of key operational
risk controls and Frameworks via an agreed programme of audits.

The responsibility of monitoring and reporting of the overall operational risk profile is
undertaken through the Risk Management & Conduct Committee, the Executive Committee and
Board Risk Committee.
