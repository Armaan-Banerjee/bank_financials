Chairman’s Statement

! was appointed Chairman of the Board on 12 July 2016 following the resignation, for personal reasons of
Mr.A.Rastegar, who had been appointed Chairman in July 2015. | would also like to thank our shareholders Bank
Mellat and Bank Tejarat for their continued support in the difficult times.

The year 2016 — 2017 was a year of challenges and opportunities for Persia International Bank (PIB). The nuclear
sanctions imposed on Iran by the UN, EU and USA were lifted on the 16'" January 2016. It was hoped that this
would lead to a normalisation of relationships between Iran and the rest of the world but this has not yet been
achieved in full. Some US sanctions still remain in place and the new President of the USA appears not to agree
with the establishment of any financial relationship with Iran.

Many western banks have signed Deferred Prosecution Agreements with US regulators and are therefore unwilling
to trade with or enter into correspondent relationships with Iranian or lranian-owned banks. Although PIB has
established direct membership of Euro clearing services (Target2) through Deutsch Bundesbank since early June
2016, in the UK PIB has still been unable to establish sterling clearing arrangements with any major UK bank and
this is a disappointment to us and a constraint on business development. Moreover, our Dubai branch has not yet
been reactivated because we do not have any reliable correspondent relationship, which is one of the main
concerns of local regulators. It is hoped that there will be progress on these issues in the coming months. We have
recruited staff as required to complement our core team of staff, as well as installing a new core banking system
which we intend to have fully operational by 30 June 2017.

Due to the lifting of sanctions and a more business-oriented environment, GDP growth in Iran was 8.3% for the
year ended March 2017 and it is forecast to increase marginally in the next year. On the production side, growth is
mainly driven by higher hydrocarbon production. On the expenditure side, consumption, investment and exports
are expected to be the main drivers. The repatriation to Iran of part of the frozen US assets has been of benefit
and the Iran current account position is expected to turn into a surplus in 2017, primarily driven by rising oil exports.

As we cannot fully reactivate due to lack of sterling clearing services, our main aim during the last 12 months was
to keep costs under control to the best of our ability, whilst at the same time ensuring we are ready to take
advantage of increased business activity, as it arises. This of course has had an impact on our balance sheet and
on our profit and loss account for the year ending 3ist March 2017. As at the year-end our loss was €1,648K as
compared to a loss of €498K in 2015/6. Balance sheet size has been reduced to a figure of €197,743K. As disclosed
in note 19 of the financial statements, there is no change in the number of overdue loans. Impairment reversals
for the year are €1,353k and where other loan repayments are overdue, sufficient collateral is available to protect
the Bank. This position is closely monitored.

As in the past, no dividend is proposed for 2016-2017.

As well as holding our equity share capital of €100m, our shareholders also hold in the same ratio subordinated
loan notes of €46.5m which is eligible as Tier 2 capital.

The Bank’s liquidity remains very strong.

Strategic Report

The Directors present their strategic report for Persia intemational Bank plc for the year ended 31 March 2017.

Introduction

Persia International Bank (“PIB”) is an authorised institution under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. Under
normal operating conditions the principal activity of the Bank is the provision of commercial banking services to its
customers. The EU/UK financial sanctions imposed on the Bank on 27 July 2010 were lifted on 16 January 2016.
However, normal banking activities have not yet been resumed owing to continuing difficulties in arranging UK clearing
facilities and in establishing correspondent relationships with international banks. Nevertheless, the Bank has recently,
in June 2016, joined the Target 2 payments system through the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Business Review

Historically, the Bank offered a comprehensive service in trade finance and credit facilities to its corporate customers.
Under trade finance, the Bank used to receive and advise letters of credit, add confirmation and provide short term credit
facilities by discounting the bills drawn under them. PIB also extended credit facilities to its corporate customers either
on bilateral terms or as part of a syndicate. Iranian corporate customers constituted a major portion of the customer base
of the Bank, The Bank had been performing well since its inception, meeting all its short and medium term goals until
2010. A strong customer base was built up with a robust trade finance and corporate credit portfolio.

The first overseas branch was opened in August 2005 at Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC), Dubai, which
further expanded the Bank’s trade finance capabilities. However, the Dubai branch operation was suspended by the
Dubai regulator (DFSA) in March 2010 and the branch has remained inactive throughout the last seven financial years.

The balance sheet total of the Bank is €197,743k, which is lower than in the previous year owing mainly to scheduled
repayments of customer loans, and the return of customer and bank deposits. Trade finance activities ceased as of July
2011, but they are now poised for full resumption, once the issues surrounding correspondent banking relationships are

resolved,

In the year 2017-18, the Bank intends to increase its money market placements with Iranian banks, and also to extend
trade finance re-financing facilities to selected Iranian banks, as part of an exercise to diversify its business. Money
market placements with banks in Iran offer attractive returns. Those from re-financing facilities are less good, but they

bring other business and cement relationships.

Financial Performance during the year 2016-17

The interest income as at 31 March 2017 decreased by 23.38% from €5,540k in the previous year to €4,245k in the
current period. The Bank has reported a loss €1,648k for the second year since its inception in 2002. The reason for the
loss is that there has been no new major business in the past seven years (and most of the loans have been repaid). The
Bank’s income is presently derived mainly from interest earned on money-market placements and on loans arranged
prior to the EU sanctions, and from amortised fee income. Bank Markazi, the Central bank of Iran, has stopped paying

interest on the nostro balances maintained with them since October 2017.

Fee & commission income receivable was €20k and fee & commission payable was €156k. The latter was entirely due
to commission paid on a transfer of euro funds, caused by the Bank’s lack of access to normal GBP and euro
correspondent banking relationships. Other income (which mostly consisted of foreign exchange income) was €212k.

The total administrative expenses for the financial year were €7,035k compared to €5,568k during the previous year. The

was €254k, compared to €191k during the previous year. The increase in depreciation provision is due to depreciation
on computer hardware and software purchased during the year.

The Bank used to provide a range of trade finance services which included documentary credits, advising and
confirmation of LCs and discounting of Trade Bills drawn on banks, which generated significant discounts and fee income.
These activities will increase during the year 2017-18, once full correspondent banking relationships are established.

The total assets of the Bank are €197,743k. The biggest asset class is money market placements, the single largest
asset being the €81,066k nostro balance with Bank Markazi. Corporate loan repayments have caused a fall of €16,459k
in loans and advances to customers and the loan portfolio will continue to wind down until normal banking activities
resume and new loans are made.

Risk Assessment and Management

The Bank is exposed to liquidity risk, credit risk, operational risk, market risk, interest rate risk and currency risk (see
Note 20 for further details on the management of these risks). The Directors have also identified the lack of GBP and
euro clearing facilities, regulatory compliance, IT, political and succession risks as the major risks faced by the Bank in
its present situation. Membership of Target 2 has to some extent facilitated euro payments within the euro zone. During
the year 2017-18 the use of Target 2 to make euro payments will be increased as much as possible.

The Board and the management believe that they have taken adequate measures to mitigate these primary risks. Steps
have been initiated to diversify as far as possible GBP payment arrangements and advance payments have been made

_ to secure essential services and supplies, until participation in the local GBP clearing system is established. The Bank's
overall liquidity position is strong with major liabilities settled and the remaining liabilities manageable. The mismatch in
the maturities of the balance sheet assets and liabilities is not a cause for concern. Though a significant portion of its
short term funds are kept with Bank Markazi in Iran, the Bank has access to sufficient liquid funds within the EU to meet
its obligations and operating costs for the foreseeable future. The Directors are satisfied that the Bank will remain a going
concern for the foreseeable future and they remain confident regarding its longer term prospects. Pillar 3 disclosures are
made separately and published on our web site.

The Bank has recruited a Chief Risk Officer to strengthen the risk management process.

Financial reporting risks are managed through control activities performed at all levels of the organisation. These activities
include guidelines and instructions, approvals, authorisations, verifications, reconciliations, analytical reviews, and
segregation of duties. The Board has also established a Risk Committee chaired by an Independent Non-executive
Director. This committee (see Note 19 for details) monitors, reviews and updates all risks faced by the Bank internally
and externally. In terms of internal controls and financial reporting, external and internal information is obtained
systematically, and the Bank's management is provided with relevant information on the Bank's activities. Timely, current
and accessible information relevant for financial reporting purposes is provided to the appropriate functionaries, such as
the Board, the senior management group and the relevant Board committees.

The Board has the overall responsibility for the proper arrangement of internal control and risk management over financial
reporting. The Board has appointed the Audit Committee (members are two Independent Non-executive Directors) to
undertake more specific tasks in relation to the financial reporting process control, and monitoring the efficiency of the
Bank's internal controls. The Audit Committee also reviews regularly the main features of the intemal control and risk

management systems pertaining to the financial reporting process.

Risk weighting of assets

The EU notification on 12 December 2014 on the equivalence of the supervisory and regulatory requirements of non-EU
countries has excluded Iran from the list of those countries, and therefore exposure to Iran will continue to be risk
weighted at 150%. Given that our business activities are focused on Iran and Iranian corporate and financial institutions,
this risk weighting measures will impact the business level that the Bank can achieve and its future profitability.

Reputation

The Bank is authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority and it is
structured in accordance with the principles of good governance. This includes employing two Independent Non-
executive Directors, one of whom chairs the Audit and the other the Risk Committee and Remuneration Committee, and
oversight of other management level committees to ensure ‘four eyes’ are involved in all major policy and operational
decisions. We also take seriously all aspects of conduct risk and fully subscribe to the principles of ‘treating customers
fairly’ to ensure that the trust they put in us as bankers is well founded.

Employees

We aim to maintain a highly skilled work force to meet the challenges and opportunities of an active financial services

company. To achieve this we will:

they can progress within the organisation,

e Supplement the skills of existing staff by recruiting suitably qualified new staff as appropriate,

e Value diversity and promote a sensible work life balance,

e Responsibly and appropriately reward contributions to long term success,

« Seek to retain staff through ‘family friendly’ policies dealing with all staff in a sensitive and pragmatic manner.

The Bank also complies with all the requirements under the Senior Managers & Certification Regime (SMCR)
Future Developments
The Bank awaits further developments in respect of correspondent banking arrangements. Meanwhile, it maintains

operational readiness and the capability to work towards its medium term objectives. Following the lifting of the sanctions

As soon as we have established more international correspondent banking arrangements, the Bank intends to diversify

Directors’ Report

The Directors present their report and the audited financial statements of Persia International Bank Plc (the
“Bank") for the year ended 31 March 2017.

Financial statements

The financial statements are prepared in Euros (€), as this is the currency in which the Bank conducts its principal
activities.

Principal activities

The principal activities of the Bank are international banking and related services within the scope of permission
granted to the Bank under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000.

Matters included within the Strategic Report

Details of business review, future developments, indication of financial risk management objectives and policies,
and the Bank’s exposure to market risk, credit risk and liquidity risk have all been included in the Strategic
Report.

Business review and future developments

The Bank is incorporated and domiciled in the UK. The financial statements have been prepared under
International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) as adopted by the European Union. The Bank is required by
the Companies Act 2006 to set out in this report a fair view of the business of the Bank during the financial year
ended 31 March 2017 and of the position of the Bank at the end of the year and a description of the principal risks
and uncertainties facing the Bank. The business review and an analysis of future developments are given in the
Strategic Report.

Going Concern

The Directors have assessed the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern and are satisfied that the Bank
has the necessary available resources to continue in business for at least the next 12 months and for the
foreseeable future thereafter. Further, the directors are not aware of any material uncertainties which may cast
significant doubt upon the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern. The Bank’s liquidity position is strong
with major liabilities settled, and the remaining liabilities can be easily managed. The total funds available within
the Eurozone and London are €34,552k and the average administrative expenses for one year are about €7,000k.
The Bank can meet its operational expenses for the foreseeable future with the funds available in the Eurozone
and London.

The Directors derive confidence from the fact that the Bank, under the present management, has survived the
challenges of the last seven years, and they are satisfied with the present position of the Bank in terms of its
capital adequacy and liquidity. It is for these reasons that the financial statements continue to be prepared on a
going concern basis.

Share capital

The total issued share capital is Euro 100 million. There were no changes in the year.

management have been defined in the Bank's policies and procedures which are approved and updated annually

effectiveness of ongoing monitoring procedures. Internal control deficiencies are reported to the management and
serious deficiencies to the Board. The internal audit function is outsourced to an external professional

organisation.

overseas locations.

flow forecasts. The Bank has a full set of policy and procedures manuals. These cover all the Bank's activities
and are reviewed and revised annually by the Board and the Executive Directors respectively. Management

Operating segments

An operating segment is a component of the Bank that engages in business activities from which it may earn
revenues and incur expenses. Its operating results are reviewed regularly by the Bank's Management Committee
(which comprises the CEO, CFO and COO and it meets at least once a month) and the Asset Liability Committee
(see Note 28 for details). Decisions about resource allocation are made and the performance can be assessed
using the available discreet financial information. The Chief Executive Officer is the chief operating decision maker
of the Bank.

Interest income and expense

Interest income and expense on financial assets and liabilities measured at amortised cost is recognised in the
statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method, which allocates the income or
expense over the relevant period. The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash
flows to the carrying vaiue on initial recognition, through the expected life of the financial asset or liability. In respect
of impaired loans where it is probable that interest on the loan will not flow to the entity, interest income is not
recognised in the statement of comprehensive income.

Fee and commission income

Fees receivables which represent a return for services provided are credited to income when the related service is
performed. Fees receivable, which represent a return for risk borne and are in the nature of interest, are taken to
the statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method over the expected life of the
transaction to which they relate.

Provisions

A provision is recognised if, as a result of a past event, the Bank has a present legal or constructive obligation that
can be estimated reliably, and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the
obligation. Provisions are determined by discounting the expected future cash flows at a pre-tax rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money, and where appropriate, the risks specific to the liability.

Financial assets:
Financial assets are classified into the following categories:

- e financial assets at fair value through profit or loss: a financial asset is classified as such if it is held
principally for the purpose of selling in the short term. In the current and prior year, the Bank did not
hold any financial assets at fair value through profit or loss;

e loans and receivables: are non-derivative financial assets with fixed or determinable payments not
quoted in an active market; and
e available for sale: are financial assets not classified in one of the above categories.

Loans and receivables are initially recognised at fair value, plus directly attributable transaction costs, and
subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method.

Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss are initially and subsequently measured at fair value with
changes in fair value taken to the statement of comprehensive income.

Available for sale investments are non-derivative investments that are designated as available for sale or are not
classified as another category of financial assets. They are carried at fair value. Available for sale financial assets
are initially and subsequently measured at fair value, with changes in fair value recognised directly in equity until
disposal, when the cumulative gain or loss is taken to profit or loss and recognised in the statement of
comprehensive income. Foreign exchange gains and losses, interest income under the effective interest rate
method and impairment losses arising on available-for-sale securities are taken to profit or loss and recognised in
the statement of comprehensive income.

A non-derivative financial asset may be reclassified from the available-for-sale category to the loans and
receivables category if it otherwise would have met the definition of loans and receivables and if the Bank has the
intention and ability to hold that financial asset for the foreseeable future or until maturity,

Financial liabilities and equity

Financial liabilities and equity instruments are initially recognised at fair value; this occurs when the Bank becomes
a party to the contractual provisions of the corresponding instruments. The Bank derecognises financial liabilities
when its contractual obligations are discharged or cancelled or expire. Financial liabilities and equity instruments
are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is
any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the Bank after deducting all of its liabilities. Financial
liabilities comprise amounts due to other banks, customer accounts and subordinated debt liabilities. They are
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Equity instruments issued by the Bank are
recorded at the proceeds received, net of direct issue costs.

Impairment of financial assets

A financial asset is impaired and impairment losses are incurred if there is objective evidence of impairment, as a
result of events that occurred after initial recognition of the financial asset. The amount of the loss is measured, for
financial assets held at amortised cost and as available-for-sale, as the difference between the carrying amount of
the asset and the value of the revised estimated future cash flows, discounted at the asset's original effective
interest rate. Financial assets are written off only when it is reasonably certain that any future cash flows are
irrecoverable.

Operating lease rentals
Rentals payable under operating leases are accounted for on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

Valuation of financial instruments
The Bank measures fair values using a fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in

making the measurements.

Level 1: Quoted market price (unadjusted) in an active market for an identical instrument is taken as the fair value.
Level 2: Valuation techniques based on observable inputs, either directly (i.e. as prices) or indirectly (i.e. derived
from prices). This category includes instruments valued using: quoted market prices in active markets for similar
instruments; quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered less than active; or
other valuation techniques where all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.
Level 3: Under this category Bank's assumptions about the market and market conditions are used in pricing the
asset and based on the best information available in the circumstances and not merely based on observable market
data (unobservable inputs).

Property plant and equipment
Tangible fixed assets are stated at cost less provision for depreciation and any impairment in value. Depreciation
of equipment, fixtures and fittings is provided on a straight-line basis over estimated useful lives’ - 3 years.

Freehold property is depreciated over a period of 50 years.

Impairment of property, plant and equipment

The carrying amount of the Bank's property, plant and equipment are reviewed at each reporting date to determine
whether there is any indication of impairment. If any such indication exists, then the asset's recoverable amount is
estimated. The recoverable amount of an asset is the greater of its value in use and its fair value less costs to sell.
In assessing value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax
discount rate that reflects current market assessment of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset.
Impairment loss is recognised, if the carrying amount of an asset exceeds its estimated recoverable amount.
Impairment losses are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Taxation
(a) Current income tax

Income tax payable (receivable) is calculated on the basis of the applicable tax law in the respective jurisdiction
and is recognised as an expense (income) for the period except to the extent that current tax related to items that
are charged or credited in other comprehensive income or directly to equity. In these circumstances, current tax is
charged or credited to other comprehensive income or to equity.

(b) Deferred income tax

Deferred income tax is normally provided in full, using the liability method, on temporary differences arising between
the tax bases of assets and liabilities and their carrying amounts in the financial statements. Deferred income tax
is determined using tax rates (and laws) that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the date of the
statement of financial position and are expected to apply when the related deferred income tax asset is realised or
the deferred income tax liability is settled. The tax effects of carry-forwards of unused losses or unused tax credits
are recognised as an asset when it is probable that future taxable profits will be available against which these
losses can be utilised.

Pensions and other post-retirement benefits
The Bank operates a defined contribution scheme, charging contributions to profit or loss within the statement of
comprehensive income as they become payable in accordance with the rules of the scheme.

Foreign currencies

Transactions in foreign currencies are recorded at the rate of exchange at the date of the transaction. A monetary
item is a right to receive or an obligation (contractual or constructive) to deliver a fixed or determinable number of
units of currency. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies at the reporting date are
reported at the rates of exchange prevailing at that date. Transiation differences on monetary items are recognised
in profit or loss within the statement of comprehensive income. Non-monetary assets and liabilities that are
measured at historical cost in a foreign currency are translated using the spot exchange rate as at the date of
recognition.

RISK MANAGEMENT
The following note on Risk Management states the Bank's policy in normal circumstances.
In transacting its normal business the Bank is exposed to the following principal risks: liquidity, credit, operational and market risk.

It is the responsibility of the Risk Committee ("RC"), a Board Committee, to review and to monitor the Bank's risk appetite and profile, risk management
policies and procedures, and its risk management in practice, and to make recommendations to the Board on these matters. The RC comprises 6
members, an Independent Non-executive Director as Chairman, the CEO, CFO, COO and one other Independent Non-executive Director and the Chief
Risk Officer (CRO). The RC meets at least four times a year, and from time to time as circumstances may require.

Overall responsibility for monitoring and managing the balance sheet risk is delegated by the Board to its Asset and Liability Committee ("ALCO"). It
concerns itself with the structure of the balance sheet, in particular its capital adequacy, liquidity, funding requirements, and tax capacity and planning,
and also with credit, operational and market risks. The Committee is responsible for ensuring that the Bank does not exceed its agreed risk Appetite,
and to that end it receives regular reports of the Bank's exposures.

Membership of the ALCO comprises the CEO as Chairman, the CFO, COO, one Independent Non-executive Director, the Financial Controller,
Operations Manager, Treasury Manager, the Chief Risk Officer and Credit Manager as Secretary. ALCO is required to meet once a month and is
chaired by the CEO. It can meet more regularly if required, eg. to consider the impact of significant changes In market situations. The monthly meeting
follows a fixed agenda but it also reviews additional material as necessary, eg. qualitative risks such as reputation or political risk. The agenda includes
reports on the four prime risks above, the bank’s monthly management accounts and comparison to budget, the regulatory (PRA) requirements and
new business, if any. The directors are required to review the ALCO and Risk Committee minutes at the quarterly board meetings.

Liquidity risk

Being adequately liquid at all times is one of the most significant risks for the Bank. However, due to continuing impediments in establishing
correspondent banking relationships and participating in clearing arrangements, the Bank is unable to function normally and expand its business
activities. This has resulted in the Bank holding net liquid assets of €84m in cash and central bank balances and also about € 32m in nostro balances

unable to meet its financial obligations in a timely manner as and when they fall due. Liquidity is monitored on a maturity mismatch basis and various
stress scenarios are normally tested regularly. The Bank is holding €15.9m as on 31 March 2017, as part of its High Quality Liquid Assets to manage
the liquidity risk. The Bank has no access to the wholesale money markets and no bank will intermediate money transfers.

Management of liquidity risk

The Bank's liquidity is managed by its treasury function. The Treasurer must ensure that the Bank can meet its obligations to
its customers and also fully comply with the PRA's requirements. The CFO (executive director) who has full knowledge of

Credit risk

Credit risk arises where the possibility exists of a counterparty defaulting on its obligations. Credit risk exposures are required to be

€ 4000k and recommends those above € 4000k for board approval. The credit exposure reports and credit committee

The PRA also sets and monitors a minimum capital requirement based upon the ratio of capital to total risk weighted exposures.
