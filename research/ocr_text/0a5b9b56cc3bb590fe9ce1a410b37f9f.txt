Pillar 3 Disclosure
Financial institutions within the scope of Basel III are required to disclose information about their risk
exposures and the risk assessment processes they have used. These disclosures are in addition to

those required in the Annual Report and Financial Statements for the year 2025 and form Pillar 3 of the
Basel Itl framework.

Basel I! is implemented in the United Kingdom under the PRA rulebook.

Pillar 1 sets out the minimum capital requirements that banks are required to meet for credit, market
and operational risks.

Under Pillar 2, banks and their supervisors at the PRA assess whether additional capital is required to
cover risks not covered in Pillar 1.

Pillar 3 complements the minimum capital requirements (Pillar 1) and the supervisory review process
(Pillar 2) with the aim of developing a set of disclosure requirements which enable market participants

to assess information on the risks that a bank is subject to and its capital and risk management
procedures.

Detailed information relating to Pillar 3, which has not been audited, is available on the Bank's website
at www.saderat-ple.com

Going Concern

The war that began on 28 February 2026, following military strikes on Iran and the subsequent
regional escalation, has significantly impacted the business environment and financial markets. As a
result, the Bank has experienced a decline in interest income and disruptions to its normal business
operations, affecting repayment of its commercial loans, however without any effect on its going
concer. In response to these conditions, management has implemented cost-control measures and
downsized operating expenses to align with the reduced level of interest income and to maintain
financial stability during this period of uncertainty. Due to the sanctions and management of
operational costs, the bank had rolled over some of its placements prior to the sanction, while some
not rolled over, remained at zero interest, an impact of the sanction, will decrease its income earning
assets while following the same strong and strict policies to total adherence to all regulatory matters.
The imposition of UK government sanctions in September 2025, the Bank's activities are significantly
restricted and are currently limited to managing existing assets and liabilities within a controlled, non-
trading environment till the removal of sanctions.

Despite the impact on interest income and business operations, the Bank’s ability to continue as a
going concern remains unaffected due to its high liquidity. The Bank continues to receive committed
financial and operational support from its parent company, which has confirmed its intention to
provide the necessary resources to enable the Bank to meet its obligations as they fall due and
continue its operations for the foreseeable future.

Bank Saderat PLC is pleased that its parent company, Bank Saderat Iran, is continuing its intention
that the Bank will remain as a going concern and will maintain its place in the London market. The
directors are of the view that the US secondary sanctions are mitigated by the steps taken by the
Bank to maintain liquidity and capital adequacy during this time. The financial statements have been
prepared on a going concern basis. In assessing the Bank’s ability to continue as a going concern,
the directors have considered the Bank's capital adequacy, liquidity position and finangial forecasts
for a period of at least twelve months from the date of approval of the financial statements.
Nonetheless, the continuing uncertainty surrounding correspondent banking relationships and the
broader geopolitical environment represent a material uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on
the Bank’s ability to continue as a going concern. This assessment has included a review of the
Bank’s Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP), Internal Liquidity Adequacy
Assessment Process (ILAAP), and five-year strategic plan.

Material Risk for the Bank
Credit and counterparty risk

Credit risk is the risk that companies, financial institutions and other counterparties will be unable to
meet their obligations in a timely manner to the Bank, which may result in financial loss. Credit risk
arises principally from the Bank's lending book and from holding investments. The Bank considers only
those counterparties for business transactions, who are credit worthy and have integrity in their
dealings. The Directors have assessed the recoverability of the Bank's placement and are satisfied that
there is no significant credit risk associated with the Bank's placements with other banks. Based on the
creditworthiness of the counterparties and the historical performance. Accordingly, no material credit
losses are anticipated in respect of these balances.

Market Risk

Euro is the base currency of operation of the Bank. Market risk arises when open positions in other
currencies are maintained, due to the necessity of the situation and such balances are exposed to
movements in Foreign Exchange (‘FX’) rates, on valuation. Bank Saderat does not maintain a trading
book. .

Operational Risk

Operationai risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, fraud, people
and systems, or from external events. The Bank has implemented a new core banking system and this
will impact a wide range of activities of the Bank, in the short term. The policies and procedure manual
has to capture the requirements under the new core banking system and the staff has to receive
adequate training in all aspects of the software, which is continuing now.

Liquidity Risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank will encounter difficulty in meeting its obligations in a timely
manner, on its financial liabilities as they become due. The UK has imposed certain sanctions that
restrict some of the Bank’s business activities. However, the relevant authorities have granted a number
of licences that permit the continuation of specific business activities and facilitate access to the
resources necessary for the Bank to operate. Based on the creditworthiness of the Bank’s
counterparties and their historical repayment performance, the directors are satisfied that there is no
significant liquidity risk associated with the Bank’s balances.

Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book

Interest rate risk is the risk of variability of the fair value of future cash flows arising from financial
instruments due to changes in interest rates and is measured by analysing assets and liabilities into
time bands according to their maturity or next interest repricing date, whichever is the earlier.

Foreign Exchange Risk

Foreign exchange risk arises from the change in value, expressed in reporting currency, of assets and
liabilities held in currencies other than the reporting currency, due to fluctuations in spot or forward
exchange rates. The Bank does not take speculative positions in currencies and any net open positions
arise in the ordinary course of business.

The Bank functions in a complex and restricted environment. Business risk arises from inappropriate
responses to the emerging and prevailing issues and the strategy taken by the Bank. The UK has

The Risk Monitoring Committee

The Risk Monitoring Committee comprises the two UK based independent non-executive directors, the
Managing Director and the Chief Risk Officer. It meets twice a year and reports to the Board.

The Risk Monitoring Committee is charged with the responsibility of advising the directors on the nature
and relative scale of the risks confronting the Bank, the appropriateness of the controls intended to
manage those risks and whether the residual risk is within the parameters approved by the Board. The
Risk Monitoring Committee oversees the production of a Risk Register whereby each department
analyses the risks to which it is subject, how it is mitigated and the residual risk is managed. These
departmental risks are then consolidated into a Bank Risk Register. The Risk Monitoring Committee
also ensures that contingency plans are in place to achieve business continuity in the event of serious
disruption to business operations.

Governance

The Board of Directors has direct supervision both over the strategy for climate
change and the management of associated financial risks arising from climate
change. The Risk Management Framework incorporates Climate Risk, and the
Credit Risk process is supplemented with a parallel assessment of the financial
risks arising from Climate Change risk. Climate Risk is formally reviewed every
six months at the Risk Monitoring Committee which in turn reports to the Board of
Directors. These meetings occurred on the 30° of April and 31° of October 2025.
Separately staff training has been implemented to develop organisational
awareness of the impact of climate change. The board considers climate-related
issues when reviewing strategies, major plan and actions, risk management,
annual budgets and business plan and monitors and oversees progress against
goals and targets for addressing climate-related issues.

Risk Management

Climate risk assessment has been incorporated across the overall risk
management process. As mentioned above the Risk Management Framework
incorporates Climate Risk, and the Credit Risk process is supplemented with a
parallel assessment of the financial risks arising from Climate Change Risk.

The Bank identifies and assesses Climate-related risk applicable to its corporate
exposures, through a developed set of metrics quantifies the potential financial
risks arising from Climate Change across a range of scenarios considering both
physical and transition risk impact. This enables a scoring matrix to be developed
for each corporate exposure, forming the basis for managing the climate-related
risk going forward.

This scoring approach is also applied to underwriting risk forming part of the credit
risk assessment process. The bank will continue to develop this as clients
familiarise themselves with these new procedures and as our understanding of
underlying risks improves, technical capabilities evolve, and methodologies
develop and become more standardised.

Strategy

Metrics to evaluate climate change risks and across a range of stressed scenarios
have also been developed. The Bank recognises the importance of Climate
Change and is committed to manage both physical and transitional financial risks
due to climate change by controlling its portfolio risk and focussing strategically on
assets that will have a positive impact on climate change. The small size of the
loan book and the restrictions on business activity limit the Bank's ability to
implement this yet in a significant way, but the Bank is optimistic that its business

(n)} Impairment of financial assets

Basic financial assets, including trade, commercial loans and advances to banks and customers and

At the end of each reporting period financial assets measured at amortised cost are assessed for
objective evidence of impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference between
the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original
effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss.

if there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was
recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not
exceed what the carrying amount would have been had the impairment not previously been recognised.
The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.

Financial assets are derecognised when (a) the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset
expire or are settled or (b) substantially all the risks and rewards of the ownership of the asset are
transferred to another party or (c) contro! of the asset has been transferred to another party who has
the practical ability to unilaterally sell the asset to an unrelated third party without imposing additional
restrictions.

been incurred, the amount of the loss is measured as the difference between the asset's carrying
amount and the present value of estimated future cash flows (excluding future credit losses that have

Josses of the obligor and Jack of working capital evidenced by an analysis of published financial

Provisions

Provisions are recognised when the bank has a present legal or constructive obligation as a result of
past events, it is probable that an outflow of resources will be required to settle the obligation, and the
amount of the obligation can be estimated reliably.

Where there are a number of similar obligations, the likelihood that an outflow will be required in
settlement is determined by considering the class of obligations as a whole. A provision is recognised
even if the likelihood of an outflow with respect to any one item included in the same class of obligations
might be small.

15 Financial risk management

Loans and advances to banks and customers are carried at amortised cost net of impairment provisions

The activities of the Bank expose it to a variety of risks, the most significant are: market risk, liquidity
risk and credit risk.

Market risk is the potential adverse change in the Bank's net assets or income arising from movements
in interest rates, foreign exchange rates or other market prices. Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank is
unable to meet its obligations as they fall due owing to insufficient financial resources. Credit risk is the
risk of financial loss as counterparties and borrowers are unable to meet their obligations as they fall
due.

15.1 Financial risk management framework

The Board has overall responsibility for the establishment and oversight of the Bank's risk management
framework, Sub-committees of the Board have been established to manage the various risks including
the UK Board, the Audit Committee, the Risk Monitoring Committee, the Assets and Liabilities
Committee and the Credit Committee. The UK board and Risk monitoring committee includes non-
executive directors also. The Audit Committee comprises the three non-executive directors and is
responsible for monitoring compliance with the Bank's risk management policies and procedures, and
for reviewing the adequacy of the risk management framework in relation to the risks faced by the Bank.
The Audit Committee is assisted in these functions by Internal Audit. Internal Audit undertakes both
regular and ad-hoc reviews of risk management controls and procedures, the results of which are
reported to the Audit Committee.

15.5 The internal assessment criteria for business lending purposes

The Bank has developed a rating system, which is used in conducting reviews of borrowers to assess
their on-going credit standing and to consider whether a loan or advance should be placed on a “watch
list” due to a deteriorating assessment of the borrower. The system is based upon a point scoring
method with points awarded against various criteria from an analysis of financial statements provided
by borrowers. The sum of the points awarded determines the classification of each borrower from A
(the highest rating) to D (the lowest). It is likely that any D rated borrowers would be in default, which
may result in the raising of a provision against the debt. The Bank is careful to detect early on, any
deterioration of the financial conditions of the obligors as well as the providers of different support forms.
Early problem recognition is key to successful risk management. Early warning indicators provide signs
of credit deterioration and include the following:

- Deterioration in financial ratios, covenants, breaches and waiver requests.
- Macroeconomic environment deteriorates.

- Industry comes under financial, economic and political pressure.

- Decline in share / bond / CDS prices.

- Rating agency downgrades and changes to negative outlook.

- Adverse comments in the media.

A default shall be considered to have occurred when an obligor is unlikely to pay its credit obligations
or is past due on any material credit obligation. A period of 90 days past due is the regulatory definition
and should be used as a benchmark. However, consideration must be given to the circumstances of
each obligor and a past due period of less than 90 days may indicate payment is unlikely. Once a debt
is 90 days past due it will be referred monthly to the UK Board or to the full board for review.

15.6 Impaired loans and advances

Impaired loans and advances are where the Bank has determined that it will be unable to collect all
principal and interest due according to the contractual terms of the loan agreement through the default
of the borrower and the inability to realise adequate collateral, for whatever reason. When a loan or
advance is deemed to be impaired, the Bank raises a provision for the amount which is determined to
be unrecoverable and reduces the amount of the loan or advance on the Statement of Financial Position
by the amount of the provision. Under FRS 102, the Bank uses the incurred loss model.
