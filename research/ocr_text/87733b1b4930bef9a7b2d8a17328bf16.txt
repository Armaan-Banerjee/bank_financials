Strategic report

The directors are pleased to present their first Strategic Report of JN Bank UK Ltd (“the Bank”) for the 16-
month period from 19 December 2018 to 31 March 2020.

Key highlights

JN Financial Group Limited, the Bank’s controlling party, has shown its support of the Bank’s growth plans
by injecting £25.4m of capital in the current financial period to successfully obtain a banking license and
establish the Bank.

Of this, the Bank has invested more than £10m in the last 16 months developing its technology platform
and infrastructure to enable the future official launch of a digital retail bank, including the community-
focussed branch in Brixton which is to be opened.

Business model

The Bank is a wholly owned subsidiary of JN Financial Group Limited (“the parent company”), which is
wholly owned by The Jamaica National Group Limited (“ultimate parent company”). Its main vision is to
create long-term value for customers and to conduct business guided by the principle of mutuality. The
Jamaica National Group Limited was founded more than 140 years ago as a mutual building society and
remains a mutual organisation dedicated to serving its members. The Jamaica National Group Limited has
been operating in the UK for over 30 years to support its customers living in the UK. The Bank was formed
in December 2018 and was successfully granted a banking license in December 2019 by the UK regulatory
authorities. ,

Once its launch has been completed, the business model of the Bank is to provide unsecured personal
lending into selected market segments which are typically underserved by the larger UK banks, by utilising
contemporary underwriting systems and techniques to achieve good outcomes for customers and
sustainable returns. Lending is funded by offering simple, competitively priced online savings products.

Our model is underpinned by a commitment to disciplines of risk management, regulatory compliance
and governance. Risk appetite and culture are set by the Board to provide the appropriate tone from the
top. ,

Business review

The Bank made a loss during the investment phase of its creation and infrastructure development. The.
Bank is well capitalised with a strong liquidity position and is ready to start business after completing a
pilot phase with a limited number of customers in order to test, refine and improve its new systems and
processes.

Future developments

in view of a deterioration in the economic outlook in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic, the Bank has
adopted a cautious approach to launching in the UK. Changes have been made to mitigate credit risk in
the current environment and additional verification checks have been introduced. Given the economic
backdrop, the initial three to six-month pilot period will focus on the acquisition of borrowers, so that a
thorough assessment can be made of the system and processes around lending.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Financial Institutions operate in a competitive environment and are subject to economic uncertainties.
The management of risk is therefore central to the continuing success of the Bank.

The Board is responsible for determining the Bank’s risk management framework and system of internal
controls, which are designed to enable the Bank to achieve its objectives within a managed risk profile,
not to eliminate all risk. To do this, the Bank bases its risk management framework on the ‘three lines of
defence’ model. The ‘first line’ of defence is the checks and controls utilised by operational staff directly
engaged in the management of the risks in their area of activity. The ‘second line’ is the assurance
provided by the independent review processes deployed by the Bank’s Risk Management function. The
‘third line’ is the independent internal audit reports regularly provided te management and the Board
Audit and Board Risk Committees by an external provider. During the period, the Bank has established its
risk management framework, while preparing for the faunch of its service offerings to customers and
managing the risk of that launch. The following table summarises the principal risks relevant to the Bank
once its launch has been completed and the strategies in place to manage them.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of losses resulting from inadequate or failed
internal processes, people and systems or from external events, such as
cyber-attacks or fraud. The Bank has a low tolerance for operational
losses and seeks to operate in such a way that these losses are mitigated.
The Bank recognises that some operational risk events are inevitable and
therefore ensures that any significant risk events are logged,
investigated and subject to root-cause analysis.

Concentration risk

Concentration risk arises from exposure to groups of connected
counterparties, or from counterparties in the same economic sector,
geographic region or from the same activity or commodity. As a provider
of unsecured personal loans and savings accounts in the UK consumer
markets, the Bank is exposed to concentration risk. However, this risk is
actively monitored, and limits are imposed on individual credit
exposures and funding sources to mitigate this risk.

Legislative and
regulatory risks

Banking is a highly regulated market and can be affected by frequent
changes and updates in legislation and regulation. The Prudential
Regulation Authority (PRA) and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)
are the primary bodies which regulate the banking industry in the UK.
The Bank operates within a defined set of frameworks and policies,
which outline the regulatory requirements and associated monitoring
practices. In addition, the Bank has implemented internal processes to
capture any future legislative and regulatory change well in advance of
its implementation date, thus ensuring that the Bank is able to continue
to adequately meet all regulatory requirements.

Economic and political
environment

The Bank is unlikely to be directly affected by the UK leaving the EU as it
operates entirely in the UK. However, as part of its ICAAP and ILAAP, the
Bank has considered several stress scenarios that could be triggered by
a severe macro-economic downturn and has evaluated the impact on
the Bank's capital and liquidity position. The scenarios consider the
possible impact on the Bank of changes in key economic variables such

Results for the year

The Bank reported a loss of £6.8m for the period. This reflects the investment expenditure and operating
costs for the bank prior to its expected formal public launch in the fourth quarter of 2020.

Proposed Dividend

The directors do not recommend the payment of a dividend.

Political Donations

The Bank did not make any political donations or incur any political expenditure during the period.

Research and Development Activities

The Bank does not undertake formal research and development activities. However, new products and
services are developed in each of the business lines in the ordinary course of business in accordance with
the Bank’s product and pricing governance framework. Under this framework, new products, campaigns
and business initiatives are reviewed and approved by the Bank’s executive committees, most notably the
Assets and Liabilities Committee (ALCO) and the Propositions and Product Committee. In addition to new
products and services, the Bank invests in internally generated intangible assets including computer
systems.

Subsequent events

The Bank continues to monitor developments arising from the spread of the COVID-19 virus around the
world which was declared a pandemic by the World Health Organisation on the 11 March 2020. As a
consequence, the economic outlook is uncertain with the longer-term outcome dependent on the severity
and length of the coronavirus pandemic and the mitigating impact of Government and other measures in
the UK and across the world. While the outlook is now subject to significant uncertainty, the Bank is
currently in a strong position to deal with a likely significant economic downturn. However, the longevity
of the COVID-19 pandemic and related containment measures could have a material adverse effect on
the success of the Bank’s public launch and future prospects as well as on the Bank’s potential customers,
employees and suppliers.

Subsequent to the year end, the Bank commenced employee restructuring activities resulting in a charge
to the income statement of £108k and 4 employees being made redundant.

Potential litigation

There is a potential claim of approximately £90k which has been brought against the Bank in respect of
damages for an alleged breach of contract for office accommodation. It is the opinion of the Bank’s legal
counsel that it is unlikely this claim would be successful, should it be pursued.

Going Concern

The financial statements are prepared on the going concern basis as the directors are satisfied that the
Bank has adequate resources to continue in business for a minimum period of 12 months from the date
of signing the financial statements (the going concern period).

In making this assessment, the directors have considered a wide range of information relating to present
and future conditions including the current state of the statement of financial position, future projections
of profitability, cash flows, capital resources and the longer-term strategy of the business. The Bank’s
capital and liquidity plans, including stress tests, have been reviewed by the directors. The Bank’s forecasts
and projections, including a range of stressed scenarios, show that it will be able to operate with adequate
levels of both liquidity and capital throughout the going concern period.

The directors have also assessed the financial implications of the risks associated with COVID-19, including
the expected effect of management actions taken in response to the pandemic. While the COVID-19
impacts are not yet fully known and will depend on many factors including the duration or re-imposition

of the enforced closure of certain businesses and social facilities, the social distancing measures and the
success and continuity of the Government and Bank of England measures put in place to support the
economy and businesses, the directors have factored in potential increases in credit risk and lower and
slower assumptions of the expected growth in both lending and deposit taking into their forecasts . Having
considered a severe but plausible downside scenario incorporating significant increases in credit risk and
significantly slower growth assumptions, the directors concluded that both capital and liquidity forecasts
remained within present regulatory requirements, including use of capital buffers, throughout the going
concern period.

The directors believe that the Bank has sufficient resources to continue its activities and has sufficient
capital to enable it to continue to meet its regulatory capital requirements as set out by the PRA,
throughout the going concern period.

Measurement of ECLs

ECLs are an unbiased probability-weighted estimate of the present value of credit losses, taking account
of forward-looking information. ECLs are measured as the difference between contractual cash flaws and
- expected cash flows arising from an asset, discounted at the asset’s effective interest rate.

When measuring ECLs, the Bank assesses the probability of default, the expected exposure at the time of
default, and the loss that is expected to arise in the event of default. The maximum period considered is

the maximum contractual period over which the Bank is exposed to credit risk. The Bank typically assesses
ECLs on an individual asset basis.

IFRS 9 requires an impairment loss allowance to be recognised at an amount equal to either 12-month
ECLs or lifetime ECLs. Lifetime ECLs are the ECLs that result from all possible default events over the
expected life of a financial instrument. 12-month ECLs are the ECLs that result from default events that
are possible within the 12 months after the reporting date.

The Bank measures impairment loss allowances at an amount equal to lifetime ECLs, except for the
following which are measured as 12-month ECLs:

© treasury assets that are determined to have low credit risk at the reporting date. The Bank

* other financial assets on which credit risk has not increased significantly since their initial
recognition and which were not considered to be credit impaired at the time of initial recognition.

Lifetime ECLs are required where the credit risk on a financial asset has increased significantly since initial
recognition (except for investment grade treasury assets, as noted above), or where the assets were credit
impaired at the time of initial recognition.

Forward-looking information

The Bank incorporates forward-looking information into both its assessment of whether the credit risk of
a financial asset has increased significantly since initial recognition and its measurement of ECLs. The time
period over which the Bank assesses forward-looking information is the maximum period over which the
Bank is exposed to credit risk. The Bank determines a range of representative scenarios for the possible
future direction of key economic variables. The scenarios are derived by reference to external information
where this is publicly available and appropriate, together with internally generated views. A probability-
weighting, based on management judgement, is assigned to each scenario.

The Bank’s ‘base case’ scenario represents a view of the most likely outcome and is aligned with
information used by the Bank for other purposes such as strategic planning and budgeting. The selection
of alternative scenarios is intended to model the non-linear impact of economic factors on ECLs for the
Bank’s financial assets.

For unsecured loan impairment, the Bank typically considers a minimum of three scenarios; this includes
the base case scenario, together with an upside scenario (more optimistic than the base case) and a
downside scenario (more pessimistic than the base case). For treasury asset impairment, the Bank
typically considers the base case scenario and a downside scenario.

Other intangible assets that are acquired by the Bank are stated at cost less accumulated amortisation
and less accumulated impairment losses.

Amortisation

Amortisation is charged to the income statement on the straight-line basis over the estimated useful lives
of intangible assets unless such lives are indefinite. Intangible assets with an indefinite useful life,
intangible assets not yet available for use and goodwill are systematically tested for impairment at each
reporting date. Other intangible assets are amortised from the date they are available for use.

Purchased software and costs directly associated with the internal development of computer software

are capitalised as intangible assets where the software is an identifiable asset controlled by the Bank

which will generate future economic benefits and where costs can be reliably measured.

Website development costs are capitalised as intangible assets where costs can be reliably measured. The
website is an identifiable asset controlled by the Bank which will generate future economic benefits.

Purchased software and computer software/website development costs are amortised on the straight-
line basis over the estimated useful life of between three and seven years.

r) Taxation

Tax on the profit or loss for the year comprises current and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the income

recognised in equity.

Current tax is the expected tax payable or receivable on the taxable income or loss for the year, using tax
rates enacted or substantively enacted at the reporting date, and any adjustment to tax payable in respect
of previous years.

Deferred tax is provided on temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities for
financial reporting purposes and the amounts used for taxation purposes. The following temporary
differences are not provided for: the initial recognition of goodwill; the initial recognition of assets or
liabilities that affect neither accounting nor taxable profit other than in a business combination, and
differences relating to investments in subsidiaries to the extent that they will probably not reverse in the
foreseeable future. The amount of deferred tax provided is based on the expected manner of realisation or
settlement of the carrying amount of assets and liabilities, using tax rates enacted or substantively enacted
at the reporting date.

A deferred tax asset is recognisedonly to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profits-will-be
available against which the temporary difference can be utilised.

Capital Management

The Bank has operated in line with its capital risk appetite as set by the Board and above its
regulatory requirements throughout the period ended 31 March 2020.

Capital is held to protect depositors, by ensuring that the Bank has sufficient capital resources to
repay liabilities even in the face of unexpected losses. Day-to-day capital management is
delegated to the Chief Financial Officer and overseen by the risk function, ALCO, Risk Committee
and ultimately the Board. The Bank's objective in managing its capital is to maintain appropriate
levels of capital to support its business strategy and to meet regulatory requirements at all times.
“The Bank manages its capital in accordance with Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and
Capital Requirements Directive (together referred to as CRD IV). The framework is enforced by
the PRA. The PRA sets and monitors the Bank's capital requirements.

The PRA's approach to the measurement of capital adequacy is primarily based on monitoring the
relationship of the capital resources requirement to available capital resources. The regulator sets
Total Capital Requirement (TCR) for each bank in excess of the minimum resources requirement of
8% of risk weighted assets. A key input to the TCR setting process is the Bank's Internal
Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP).

The Bank's policy is to maintain a strong capital base to maintain stakeholder and market
confidence and to sustain the future development of the business. The Bank monitors current and
forecast levels of capital against the capital risk appetite. approved by the Board and reports the
capital position to ALCO, Risk Committee and the Board on a regular basis. The capital forecast
forms an integral component of the annual budgeting process and is updated in line with changes to
the Bank's business pian.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss arising from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and
systems or from external events. These could arise from information technology, information
security, project outsourcing, tax, legal, fraud and compliance risks.

The Bank has established an Operational Risk management framework, underpinned by clear
policies which are geared to ensuring the Bank operates within a low risk appetite for operational
risk failures.

- The first line of defence is responsible for the management of operational risk in accordance with
stated policies, governance, agreed processes and controls. The second line of defence provides
oversight and challenge supplemented by a programme of regular assurance testing. The third line
of defence (via internal audit) provides an independent review of key operational risk controls and
frameworks via an agreed programme of audits.

The responsibility of monitoring and reporting of the overall operational risk profile is undertaken

through the Risk Management & Conduct Committee, the Executive Committee and Board Risk
Committee.
