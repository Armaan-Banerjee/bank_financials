Chairman’s Statement —
For the year ended 31 March 2019

The financial year - ending March 31 2019, like the preceding year continued to be a
challenging one for the Bank. There were many headwinds for the business outside the sphere
of influence and control of the Board and Management. Much of our existing business is,
naturally, derived from and based in Iran, both from customers based in Iran and.customers
trading with Iran, and this will remain the case for the foreseeable future.

There is growing political pressures on Iran, particularly with the announcement of the United
States of America administration to not abide by the JCPOA agreement, and lack of clearing
facilities in London also continues to be a severe restriction. The Board is also mindful of the
impact of Brexit on its ability to hold its high quality liquid assets with the German Central
Bank and management are planning for viable alternatives.

These challenges have hampered PIB’s activities and its ability to operate. During the year, the
Board decided to close its Dubai branch and Tehran representative office.

2019, was a year of significant change in the personnel and service providers to the bank. Mr
Meskarian, the CEO of the bank announced his retirement effective as end of March 2019 after
17 years with the Bank. The Board and | are grateful for his leadership and dedication to PIB
over this period and wish him well in his well-earned retirement. The Board is extremely
pleased that Mr Nasrollahi has agreed to take over as the CEO. Mr Nasrollahi’s knowledge and
experience of PIB will be a great asset in achieving a smooth transition to this new role. We
bid farewell, to Pat Quinn, the Chair of the Risk Committee and Independent Non-Executive
Director and welcomed David Blackmore as his successor. We also welcomed Grant Thornton
and MHA MacIntyre Hudson as Internal and External Auditors of the bank respectively in

Whilst, the Bank’s liquidity and capital base remain strong to support the existing operation;

unclear.in the short term.

The Board and I are nonetheless hopeful that, normality will return in the medium term.
Therefore, the Board is focused on ensuring that the infrastructure and operation of the Bank

Strategic Report
For the year ended 31 March 2019

The Directors present their strategic report for Persia International Bank PLC for the year ended
31 March 2019.

Introduction

Persia International Bank PLC (‘“PIB” or “the Bank”) is an authorised institution under the
Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. The Bank’s principal activity is the provision of
commercial banking services to its customers. Although financial sanctions imposed in 2010
were lifted on 16 January 2016. The USA’s decision to unilaterally withdraw from the JCPOA
Treaty and subsequent re-imposition of OFAC sanctions means the normal banking activities
have not yet been fully resumed owing to continuing difficulties in arranging UK clearing
facilities and in establishing correspondent relationships with international banks. In June
2016, the Bank joined the Target 2 payments and clearing system through the Deutsche
Bundesbank.

Business Review

Historically, the Bank has offered a comprehensive services in trade finance and credit facilities
to its customers. Under trade finance, the Bank used to receive and advise letters of credit, add
confirmation and provide short term credit facilities by discounting the bills drawn under them.
PIB also extended credit facilities to its corporate customers either on bilateral terms or as part
of a syndication. Iranian corporate customers constituted a major portion of the Bank’s
customer base. The Bank had been performing well since its inception, meeting all its short
and medium term goals until] 2010. A strong customer base was built up with a robust trade
finance and corporate credit portfolio.

The first overseas branch was opened in August 2005 at Dubai International Financial Centre
(DIFC), which further expanded -the.Bank’s trade. finance. capabilities.. However, the Dubai
branch operation was suspended by the Dubai regulator (DFSA) in March 2010 and the branch
was inactive since. Currently, Dubai branch is in the process of closure.

The balance sheet total assets of the Bank is lower than in the previous year because of
scheduled repayments of existing loans, return of customer and banks deposits. Before the
sanctions Trade Finance had historically been an important portfolio in PIB’s business lines.
In recent years the Bank did not have this opportunity and saw a downward trend in its trade
finance activities. Further resumption of trade finance activities 1s possible only when the issues
surrounding correspondent banking relationships are resolved. Although there was no trade
finance business at the end of March 2019 the Bank will however consider all viable options
in future years.

In the year 2019-20, the Bank will reduce its money market placements with Iranian banks and
extend trade finance re-financing facilities to selected Iranian banks, as part of an exercise to
diversify its business. Money market placements with banks in Iran offer some returns, and
wil continue until credit expansion takes place. Returns from re-financing facilities are
significantly less but undertaken with the expectations that they will facilitate new businesses
in course of time and establish relationships with banks and corporate customers in Iran.

Financial Performance during the year 2018-19 and key performance indicators

The interest income for the year ending 31 March 2019 decreased by 43.5% to €1,524k
(FY2018: €2,696k). The Bank has reported a Loss of €5,839k. The main reasons for the loss is
lack of new business in recent years and scheduled repayments of earlier booked loans. The
Bank's income is presently derived mainly from interest earned on money-market placements
and loans arranged prior to the sanctions. .There is a very small fee income from limited trade
finance activities and payment services.

Fee & commission income receivable was €122k (FY2018: €54k) and fee & commission
payable was €216k (FY2018: €219k). The latter was entirely due to commission paid on a _
transfer of euro funds, caused by the Bank’s lack of access to normal GBP and euro
correspondent banking relationships. Other income (which mostly consisted of foreign
exchange income) was €150k (FY2018: €245k).

The total administrative expenses for the financial year were €7,131k as compared to €7,914k
during the previous year. The Bank has been closely monitoring and control its expenses in
order to achieve efficiencies. Depreciation cost was €462k (FY2018: €376k). This is due to
depreciation on computer hardware and software.

The total assets of the Bank as at 31 March 2019 were €166,729k (FY2018: €184,318k). The
biggest asset class is loans and advances to the banks which include money market placements
€34,000k (FY2018: €75,676k). The single largest asset being the €27,000k Money Market
placement with one of the shareholder banks. Money Market placements are kept for varying
periods of maturity. The management envisage replacing money market deposits with
corporate loans when the opportunity arises for new lending.

The Bank consider the interest income and administrative expenses detailed above to be the
key performance indicators.

Risk Assessment and Management

The Bank is exposed to liquidity risk, credit risk, operational risk, market risk, interest rate risk
and currency risk (see Note 28 for further details on the management of these risks). The
Directors have also identified the lack of GBP and other currencies clearing facilities,
regulatory compliance, IT concentration due to reliance on few suppliers, geo-political and
succession risks as the major risks faced by the Bank in its present situation. Direct membership
of Target 2 has to some extent facilitated euro payments within the euro zone’but this may be
discontinued following Brexit.

The Board and the management believe that they have taken adequate measures to mitigate
these primary risks. Steps have been initiated to diversify as far as possible GBP payment
arrangements and advance payments have been made to secure essential services and supplies
until participation in the local GBP clearing system is established. The Bank's overall liquidity
position is strong with major liabilities settled and the remaining liabilities manageable. The
mismatch in the maturities of the balance sheet assets and liabilities is not a cause for concern,

Brexit

Following Brexit, the direct membership of Target 2 system to transfer funds within euro and
also access to Deutsche Bundesbank to maintain High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) for LCR
purposes may not be available. However, Management of the Bank is confident of using the
services of EU based banks to transfer funds within the euro zone.

Maintenance of HQLA is essential to conform to the requirements of Liquidity Coverage Ratio
(LCR). At present part of the funds with Bundesbank under Target 2 are earmarked as HQLA.
PIB management envisages opening a branch in Italy to access ECB & Target 2 and HQLA
could be held, as well as enabling the Bank potentially to other business from southern Europe.

Funds denominated in euro to corporate customers in Iran will be made available through one
of our Shareholder Banks and Bank Markazi. Money Market placements with banks in Iran
will be intermediated by Shareholder Banks and Bank Markazi. Similarly repayments from
corporate customers and banks in Iran will be through intermediation of Shareholder Banks
and Bank Markazi.

Some purchases and supplies can also be paid in euro with an arrangement with the parties
concerned and the correspondent banks in euro zone.

OFAC sanctions

Following the decision of the US Government to unilaterally withdraw from the JCPOA, the
OFAC sanctions were reimposed on PIB. The management is of the view that the impact of
OFAC sanctions-will be visible ‘but may not->hamper the present business pattern of the Bank.
Businesswise the primary dealings are in euro and exposures are to Iranian entities. The non-
availability of SWIFT impacted business communications and transfer of funds. This has been
managed through book transfer mechanism with the involvement of fraternal banks in the EU.
As regards repayment of the loan instalments and interest, the management has been advised
by the Central Bank of Iran, that such transactions will be given priority for availability of
Foreign Exchange.

Risk weighting of assets

The EU notification on 12 December 2014 on the equivalence of the supervisory and regulatory
requirements of non-EU countries has excluded Iran from the list of those countries, and
therefore exposure to Iran will continue to be risk weighted at 150%. Given that our business
activities are focused on Iran and Iranian corporate and financial institutions, these risk
weighting measures will impact the business level that the Bank can achieve and therefore its
future profitability.

Reputation

The Bank is authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority and regulated by the Financial
Conduct Authority and is structured in accordance with the principles of good governance.

This includes employing two Independent Non-Executive Directors, one of whom chairs the
Board & the Audit Committee and the other chairs the Risk Committee and Remuneration

eyes’ are involved in all major policy and operational decisions. We also take seriously all
aspects of conduct risk and fully subscribe to the principles of ‘treating customers fairly’ to
ensure that the trust customers put in us as bankers is well founded.

Future Developments

The Bank hopes for further developments in respect of correspondent banking arrangements.
Meanwhile, it maintains operational readiness and the capability to work towards its medium
term objectives. Following the lifting of the sanctions in January 2016 the reactivation plan

_ become operational and new staff were recruited to complement the existing staff. However,
the reimposition OFAC sanctions following the unilateral withdrawal of the United States of
America (USA) from the JCPOA had a setback on our expectations of resuming normal
banking business operations. The Bank managed the full force of the EU, UN and OFAC
sanctions in the past and is now poised to manage the aftermath of the withdrawal of USA from
the JCPOA.

- For financial guarantee contract, the ECL is the difference between the expected
payments to reimburse the holder of the guaranteed debt instrument less any amount
that the Bank expect to receive from the holder, the debtor or any other party.

2.10.3 Significant increase in credit risk

The bank monitors all financial assets, issued loan commitments and financial guarantee

guarantee contracts that are subject to impairment for significant increase in credit risk.

In assessing whether the credit risk on a financial instrument has increased significantly since
initial recognition, the bank compares the risk of default occurring on the financial instrument
on the reporting date based on the remaining maturity of the instrument with the risk of a default
occurring that was anticipated for the remaining maturity at the current reporting date when the
financial instrument was first recognised. In making this assessment, the Bank consider both
qualitative and quantitative information that is reasonable and supportable, including historical
experience and forward-looking information that is available without undue cost or effort,
based on the Bank’s historical experience and expert credit assessment including forward-
looking information.

For corporate lending forward-looking information includes the future prospects of the
industries in which the Bank’s counterparties operate, obtained from expert economic expert
reports, financial analysts, governmental bodies, relevant think-tanks and other similar
organisations, as wel] as consideration of various internal and external sources of actual and
forecast economic.information.

The Bank allocates its counterparties to a relevant internal credit risk grade depending on their
credit quality. The quantitative information is a primary indicator of the significant increase in
credit risk and is based on the change in lifetime PD.

The PDs used are forward-looking and the Bank uses the same methodologies and the data
used to measure the loss allowance for ECL.

The qualitative factors that indicate significant increase in the credit risk are reflected in PD
models on a timely basis. However the Bank still considers separately some qualitative factors
to assess if credit risk has increased significantly. For corporate lending there is particular focus
on assets that are included on a ‘watch list’? given an exposure on a watch list once there is a
concern that the creditworthiness of the specific counterparty has deteriorated.

If the .difference. in:present value is greater than 10% the Bank deems. the-arrangement: is

2.27 Provisions

Provisions are recognised when the Bank has a present legal or constructive obligation as a

carrying amount is the present value of those cash flows.

Where some or all of the economic benefits required to settle a provision are expected to be
recovered from a third party, a receivable is recognised as an asset if it is virtually certain that
reimbursement will be received and the amount of the receivable can be measured reliably.

@) Interest and similar income and expense

For all financial instruments measured at amortised cost, interest income or expense is
recognised using the effective interest rate (“EIR”). The effective interest rate is the rate that
exactly discounts estimated future cash payments or receipts through the expected life of the
financial instrument or a shorter period, where appropriate, to the net carrying amount of the

costs that are directly attributable to, and an integral part of, the instrument, but not future credit
losses.

The carrying amount of the financial asset or financial liability is adjusted if the Bank revises

original effective interest rate and the change in carrying amount is recorded as interest and
similar income for financial assets and interest and similar expense for financial liabilities.

28. Risk Management
The following note on Risk Management states the Bank's policy in normal circumstances.

In transacting its normal] business the Bank is exposed to the following principal nsks: liquidity,
credit, operational and market risk

It is the responsibility of the Risk Committee ("RC"), a Board Committee, to review and to
monitor the Bank's risk appetite and profile, risk management policies and procedures, and its
risk management in practice, and to make recommendations to the Board on these matters. The
RC comprises 5 members, an Independent Non-executive Director as Chairman, the CEO,
CFO, COO and one other Independent Non-executive Director and the Chief Risk Officer
(CRO). The RC meets at least four times a year, and from time to time as circumstances may
require.

Overall responsibility for monitoring and managing the balance sheet risk is delegated by the
Board to the Asset and Liability Committee ("ALCO"). It concerns itself with the structure of
the balance sheet, in particular its capital adequacy, liquidity, funding requirements, and tax
capacity and planning, and also with credit, operational and market risks. The Committee is
responsible for ensuring that the Bank does not exceed its agreed risk appetite, and to that end
it receives regular reports of the Bank's exposures.

Membership of the ALCO comprises the CEO as Chairman, the CFO, COO, one Independent
Non-executive Director, the Financial Controller, Operations Manager, Treasury Manager, the
Chief Risk Officer and Credit Manager as Secretary. ALCO is required to meet once a month
and is chaired by the CEO. It can meet more regularly if required, e.g. to consider the impact
of significant changes in market situations. The monthly meeting follows a fixed agenda but it
also reviews additional material as necessary, e.g. qualitative "risks such as reputation or
. political risk. The agenda includes reports on the four prime risks above, the bank’s monthly
management accounts and comparison to budget, the regulatory (PRA) requirements and new
business, if any. The directors are required to review the ALCO and Risk Committee minutes
at the quarterly Board meetings.

28.1 Liquidity risk

Being adequately liquid at all times is one of the most significant risks for the Bank. However,
due to continuing impediments in establishing correspondent banking relationships and
participating in clearing arrangements, the Bank is unable to function normally and expand its
business activities. This has resulted in the Bank holding net liquid assets of €70.1m in cash
and central bank balances including balances with Bundesbank and also about €5.6m in nostro
balances with banks primarily in the euro zone, posing no significant liquidity risk in the near
future. Liquidity risk, in normal circumstances occurs when the bank is unable to meet its
financial obligations in a timely manner as and when they fall due. Liquidity is monitored on
a maturity mismatch basis and various stress scenarios are normally tested regularly. The Bank

can approve those up to € 4,000k and recommends those above € 4,000k for Board approval.
The credit exposure reports and credit committee minutes are discussed at the ALCO meetings.
The Bank normally takes legally enforceable security for facilities which it grants; this may be

37. Effects of changes in accounting policies

In the current year, the Bank has applied IFRS9 Financial Instruments (as revised in July 2017)
and the related consequential amendments to other IFRS Standards that are effective for an
annual period that begin on or after 1 January 2018, which resulted in change in accounting
policies and adjustments to the amounts previously recognised in the financial statements. The
Bank did not early adopt any of IFRS9 in previous periods.

As permitted by the transitional provisions of IFRS9. The Bank elected not to restate
comparative figures. Any adjustments to the carrying amounts of financial assets and liabilities
at the date of transition were recognised in the opening retained earnings and reserves of the
current period. The Bank does not apply hedge accounting.
