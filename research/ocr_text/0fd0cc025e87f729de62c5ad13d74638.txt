Introduction

Redwood Bank opened for business in August 2017, just four months after securing its initial banking

licence, with a view to offering a real alternative for small and medium sized organisations (SME’s) looking to
make sure that their properties and cash work harder for them. The Bank offers secured SME mortgages for
business owners, as well as for experienced commercial and residential property investors. it also offers
competitive business deposit accounts. Its goal is to offer British businesses simple, transparent loans and
savings accounts backed up with tailored service, efficient systems and with the knowledge that money is being
invested back into British business. to

The principal activities of the Bank therefore comprise secured property lending to businesses, along with
business deposits.

Fs

Business review
Performance during the first few months of operations has exceeded targets and the position at the year-end of

31 December 2017 provides a strong platform from which to enter 2018.

Despite making a loss for the year, as was expected, the performance exceeded expectations. It resulted in a
net increase in Shareholders funds of £9.7m due to new shares issued during the year.

The total number of employees increased significantly during the year, following the granting of the banking
licence, the occupation of the business premises in Letchworth and Warrington, and the commencement of
trading. At the end of 2017 there were a total of 36 staff and directors.

Lending and deposits both ended the year ahead of budget and customer feedback has been very positive.
Redwood has been at the top of the Business Deposit Best Buy tables, with our 95 and 35 day notice accounts
having garnered a great deal of attention. This has had a positive impact on our liquidity position. We have also
concentrated, successfully, on the quick and efficient processing of both deposits and customer loan requests.

The credit approved loan pipeline was strong as at 31 December 2017 and continues to grow due to a stable
demand, which we expect will continue to meet our growth appetite and strategic plan.

Principal risks and uncertainties

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of suffering financial loss should borrowers or counterparties default on their contractual
obligations to the Bank. These risks are managed by the Board Risk Committee and are mainly mitigated by
way of security in the form of property.

Credit risk on Treasury instruments is controlled and limited through a counterparty risk policy to manage
exposures.
Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank will encounter difficulty in meeting obligations associated with financial
liabilities. The Bank mitigates this risk by managing cash generation through deposit products, and supplements
this with careful management of cash balances, investments in Treasury Bills and UK Government Gilts.

Competitive risks

Ensuring healthy competition and fair customer outcomes in the UK banking market has been a sharp focus for
both the regulator and the Competition and Markets Authority.

In the aftermath of the banking crisis, small and medium sized businesses suffered as many banks tightened
lending requirements. This provided an opportunity for the Bank to enter into the market and deliver on the
needs of such businesses.

While there is a degree of competition from both existing mainstream and other new challenger banks,
Redwood Bank is able to take advantage of opportunities in the market and ensure future growth, while
concentrating upon its heartland regions and operating within its risk appetite.

Legislative and regulatory risks

Banking is a highly regulated market and can be affected by frequent changes and updates in legislation. The
Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) are the primary bodies which
regulate the banking industry in the UK. The main prudential risks faced by the Bank are in respect of capital
and liquidity adequacy, mitigated by the Bank having implemented strong controls in respect of these matters,
including reviewing all new regulatory changes and carrying out careful and prudent planning. Regarding
customer conduct risk, this is mitigated by ensuring that customer outcomes and treating customers fairly are
considered in everything the Bank does.

Customer behavioural risk

Customers are increasingly demanding online or digital servicing and greater flexibility in lending and savings
products. Time is also of the essence, as customers expect more frequent communication throughout the
lending application process as well as quicker approval decisions. The Bank employs experienced, specialist
underwriters, enabling the processing of customer applications quickly.

Redwood Bank launched its e-banking system during November 2017, just three months after trading
commenced, allowing savings customers the freedom to access their account information online at any time.

By employing specialist underwriting expertise, Redwood Bank maintains agility and speed in operation,
allowing the Bank to process customer applications quickly, ensuring customer satisfaction while adhering to
our risk appetite. .

Economic and political environment

The UK’s decision to leave the European Union and the triggering of Article 50 could potentially have an
adverse impact on the market. Although the full impact is yet to be seen, the risk has been appropriately
identified and taken into account appropriately within our strategic plan.

Changes to the UK tax regime and revisions to regulatory standards are being monitored by the Board’s Risk
Committee to ensure continued current and future compliance.

Financial key performance indicators
The Bank made a loss before tax for the year ended 31 December 2017 of £3,341,382 (2016: £377,105).

Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital as at 31 December 2017 was £9.1m and exceeded the regulatory
minimum set by the PRA.

The average lending rate for the year was 6.44% and the average interest rate paid on deposits was 1.26%.

The Bank's liquidity coverage ratio at 31 December 2017 greatly exceeded the regulatory minimum set by the
PRA and at that point the Bank had a total value of high quality liquid assets of £15m.

Other key performance indicators

All staff completed general conduct training and have all passed the requisite tests.

Customer feedback has been monitored since the launch in August 2017 and no formal complaints have been
received.

Future developments and prospects

Redwood Bank will continue to build on the strong foundations that have been put in place, by continuing to
build a strong business development function, supported by experienced operations, finance and management

teams.

The products provided will be designed to meet the financial needs of customers and allow them more time to
focus on their businesses.

This is just the beginning for Redwood Bank, and we are committed to deliver simple and transparent financial
products in a timely fashion to British businesses.

Through continued investment in our strategy and people, we aim to deliver the highest levels of customer
service for local and national businesses and their owners.

Interest income and expense are recognised in comprehensive income using the effective interest

payments and receipts through the expected life of the financial asset or financial liability (or, where
appropriate, a shorter period) to the carrying amount of the financial asset or financial liability. When
calculating the effective interest rate, the Bank estimates future cash flows considering all
contractual terms of the financial instrument, but not future credit losses.

The calculation of the effective interest rate includes transaction costs and fees paid or received that
are an integral part of the effective interest rate. Transaction costs include incremental costs that are
directly attributable to the acquisition or issue of a financial asset or financial liability.
Interest income and expense presented in comprehensive income include:

- interest on financial assets and financial liabilities measured at amortised cost calculated on and

effective interest basis; and
- interest on available-for-sale investment securities calculated on an effective interest rate basis.

‘Fair value’ is the amount for which an asset could be exchanged, a liability settled, or an equity

When available, the Bank measures the fair value of an instrument using the quoted price in an
active market for that instrument. A market is regarded as active if transactions for the asset or
liability take place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide pricing information on an ongoing
basis. ;

If there is no quoted price in an active market, then the Bank uses valuation techniques that
maximise the use of relevant observable inputs and minimise the use of unobservable inputs. The
chosen valuation technique incorporates all of the factors that market participants would take into
account in pricing a transaction.

transaction price — i.e. the fair value of the consideration given or received. If the Bank determines

Effective interest rate

Interest earned from loans and advances is measured under the Effective Interest Rate Method (EIRM).
Management must therefore use judgement to estimate the expected life of each instrument and hence
the expected cash flows relating to it. Management reviews the expected lives on a segmental basis,
whereby products of a similar nature are grouped into cohorts that exhibit homogenous behavioural
attributes.

The key assumptions applied by management in the EIR methodology are regarding the behavioural life
of the assets and the quantum of future early redemption fee income. The expected life behaviours are
subjected to changes in internal and external factors and may result in adjustments to the carrying value
of loans which must be recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Management has limited historical experience of the Bank's customer behaviours due to the relative
immaturity of the portfolios and therefore models expected behaviour based on market trends and
experience. The actual behaviour of the portfolios is compared to the modelled behaviour on a quarterly
basis and the modelled behaviours are adjusted if the modelled behaviour materially deviates from actual
behaviour, with adjustments recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Impairment of financial assets

group of financial assets is impaired. A financial asset or a group of financial assets is impaired and
impairment losses are incurred if, and only if, there is objective evidence of impairment as a result of one
or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the asset (a ‘loss event’) and that loss event
(or events) has an impact on the estimated future cash flows of the financial asset or group of financial
assets that can be reliably estimated.

Capital risk management re

The Bank's objective in managing it’s capital is to maintain appropriate levels of capital to support its
business strategy and meet regulatory requirements.

The Bank’s capital resources are comprised of 100% CET1 regulatory capital. Its total capital ratio sits at
59.78% (unaudited) and its regulatory capital requirements for CET1, Tier 1 and total capital are
comfortably met.

The Bank manages its capital under Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and Capital Requirements
Directive (together referred to as CRD1V). The framework is enforced by the Prudential Regulatory
Authority (PRA). The PRA sets and monitors the Bank's capital requirements.

The PRA's approach to the measurement of capital adequacy is primarily based on monitoring the
relationship of the capital resources requirement to available capital resources. The regulator sets
Individual Capital Guidance (ICG) for each bank in excess of the minimum resources requirement of 8%.
A key input to the ICG setting process is the Bank's Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process
“ (ICAP). -

The Bank's policy is to maintain a strong capital base to maintain investor and market confidence and to
sustain the future development of the business. ‘

The Bank has complied with all externally imposed capital requirements during 2017. The Bank has
elected to use the standardised approach for credit risk. Under CRD 1V the bank must set aside 8% of
it's total risk weighted assets to cover its ‘Pillar 1' capital requirements. The Bank must also set aside
additional ‘Pillar 2' capital to provide for additional risks. This is calculated by multiplying the Pillar 1
capital by the Individual Capital Guidance (ICG) ratio. The ICG ratio is based on the various risks which
the Bank faces and is agreed by the PRA. The Bank's capital base was in excess of the minimum
required under the ICG. sows
