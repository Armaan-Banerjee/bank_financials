The Bank's income is derived principally from its interbank placements and small portfolio of commercial

Corporate governance statement

The Board is ultimately responsible for the Bank’s system of internal controls and discharges its duties
in this area by ensuring management implements effective systems of risk identification, assessment
and mitigation. These risk management systems are designed to manage, rather than eliminate, the
risk of failure to achieve business objectives and cannot provide absolute assurance against material
misstatement or loss. Management is responsible for establishing and maintaining adequate internal
controls over financial reporting. Internal controls over financial reporting is designed to provide
reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial
statements for external reporting purposes. A strategic planning and budgeting process is in place. One
of the directors’ responsibilities is regular monitoring of performance against plan on a monthly basis
and discussing reasons for variances. The UK Board receives relevant briefings from the Assets and
Liabilities Committee (ALCO) and the Credit Committee. The Board receives relevant briefings from the
Risk Monitoring Committee, Audit Committee and the Remuneration and Personnel Policy Committee.

The Risk Monitoring Committee's role is to identify and discuss the nature of the risks encountered and
ensure that management recognises the full range of risks to which the Bank is exposed, and their
relative scale, stress tested under various scenarios, in order that controls may be designed and
resources deployed to manage those risks effectively, and that the residual risk is contained within
parameters approved by the Board. The Audit Committee inter alia oversees the control environment,
ensuring that risks have been adequately recognised, that controls in place are appropriate to manage
those risks, and particularly that the controls are operating effectively.

ALCO is responsible for monitoring the management of the Bank's financial resources to ascertain that
the Bank has sufficient funding and liquidity to meet its business requirements within the prescribed
regulatory limits.

Operational risk

The Bank also faces operational risk, including the risk of non-compliance with regulatory and legal
requirements, which is managed by strict adherence to detailed and comprehensive procedures
together with high-level managerial oversight of the Bank's operations. There are policies in force for
prevention and management of cyber security and data protection, in the form of GDPR. A well-
documented policy on fraud prevention is also in place. The Bank has taken into consideration the
present geo-political climate in the middle east as an operational risk factor and have allocated
appropriate capital and the business strategy is accordingly planned. The Bank’s exposure to UK
economy is limited as it has a niche market in Iran and the Middle East and the business parameters
are accordingly factored. The operational risk in switching to the new bench mark reference based on
overnight rates in GBP and Euro currencies has already been factored in. Inter-bank deposits by the
Bank are on fixed rate basis negotiated with the counterparty banks, at the time of placements. The
loans portfolio is linked to the structured term rates based on the overnight risk-free rate ESTER.

The Bank has also considered the implementation of the new core banking system CSB2 as an
operational risk, involving Bank staff to get accustomed to the new software and manage operational
issues arising at the implementation stage. CSB2 is becoming operational now.

Climate Change

The Bank has continued to develop its approach towards the management of financial risks from climate
change. The Bank has substantially revised its procedures and reporting in order to ensure that its
governance and operations are in line with the specific requirements of $S3/19 which became effective
at the start of 2022. To this end the Bank has allocated responsibility for climate change issues to the
Chief Risk Officer and also engaged external consultants to review and advise on its procedures. The
Bank has set its standards according to the recommendations of the Task Force on Climate Related
Disclosures ‘TFCD’ and updates will be made as the quality of climate data and reporting methodologies
continue to evolve.

The risk types which the Bank has identified for more detailed analysis to evaluate the impact on the
Bank of potential climate change related financial risks are:

i. Physical risk — risks which arise from the increasing severity and frequency of climate and
weather related events, for instance rising sea water levels, floods, heat waves, hurricanes
and storms.

ii. Transition risk — risk which arise from the transition to net zero emissions which will require
structural changes to the economy and the resulting changes in policy, technology and
consumer demand which this entails.

i. Governance

The Board of Directors has direct supervision both over the strategy for climate change and the
management of associated financial risks arising from climate change. The Risk Management
Framework has been updated to incorporate Climate Risk, and the Credit Risk process has been
supplemented with a parallel assessment of the financial risks arising from Climate Change risk. Metrics
to evaluate climate change risk and across a range

of stressed scenarios have also been developed. Climate Risk is formally reviewed every six months
at the Risk Monitoring Committee which in turn reports to the Board of Directors. Staff training has been
conducted to create organisational awareness of the impact of climate change.

ii, Strategy

The Bank recognises the importance of Climate Change and is committed to manage both physical and
transitional financial risks due to climate change by controlling its portfolio risk and focussing
strategically on assets that will have a positive impact on climate change. The small size of the loan
book and the restrictions on business activity limit the Bank’s ability to implement this yet in a significant
way, but the Bank is optimistic that its business will grow and will enable it to implement its strategy as
new assets are introduced to the Bank.

iii, Risk Management

The Bank has comprehensively updated its Climate Change policy and developed a set of metrics to
quantify the financial risk arising from climate change and to apply this across a range of scenarios.
This enables a scoring matrix to be developed for each underwriting risk, and is reviewed as part of the
credit risk assessment process. The Bank will continue to develop this as clients familiarise themselves
with these new procedures and as our understanding of underlying risks improves, technical capabilities

14. Company information and basis of preparation ©
(a) Company information
The Bank is registered in the United Kingdom and its principal activities are banking.

These Financial Statements have been prepared in accordance with applicable United Kingdom
accounting standards including FRS 102 - The Financial Reporting Standard Applicable in the United
Kingdom and Republic of Ireland, with the Companies Act 2006 and the provisions relating to banking
companies in the Large and Medium-Sized Companies and Groups (Accounts and Reports)
Regulations 2008 and in accordance with provisions of the Regulations related to Banking Companies
(S12008/410 Para 5(1)).

(b) Basis of preparation
(i) Going Concern

The Financial Statements are prepared on a ‘going concern’ basis as the directors, having taken into
account all relevant information, including annual budgets, considered that the Bank has adequate Tier
1 capital to sustain it with sufficient capital adequacy, calculated in accordance with the FCA/PRA Rules,
for the foreseeable future; and existing assets will provide a sufficient income stream to cover expenses
far in excess of one year from the date of approval of the Financial Statements. The directors’ have also
assessed and satisfied themselves of the Bank's ability to continue as a going concern in the context
of reasonably foreseeable business and financial risks, economic uncertainties and_ liquidity
assessments. Although the Bank continues to experience difficulties in securing suitable correspondent
banking relationships following a second round of OFAC sanction in 2018, directors have put in place
systems and procedure to mitigate these and ensure that the Bank remains a going concern in respect
of its liquidity and capital adequacy.

(ii) Functional and presentation currency

The functional currency of the Bank and the presentation currency of these Financial Statements are in
Euro,

(iii) Basis of measurement
The Financial Statements have been prepared on the historical cost basis
(c) Income recognition
(i) Interest receivable
Interest receivable from financial assets classified as loans and advances is calculated using the
effective interest rate, which is the rate that discounts estimated future cash receipts through the
expected life of the financial asset to that asset's net carrying amount. When a receivable is impaired
recognition of interest income is reduced by calculating it using the original effective interest rate method
on the carrying amount as reduced by any provisions for impairment. The element of contractual income
thereby not recagnised in the Income Statement is added to the provision for impairment.

(ii) Fee & Commission income

Fees earned for services provided are recognised as revenue at the time of the service provided.

(d) Tangible assets

Tangible assets are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses. Cost includes
the original purchase price, costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to working conditions for its
intended use, dismantling and restoration costs. Land and buildings include freehold properties stated
at cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses. Land is not depreciated.

Tangible assets are revalued and written off, if found impaired
(e) Depreciation of tangible fixed assets

Freehold land is not depreciated. Depreciation of tangible fixed assets is provided for on a straight-line
basis in order to write off the cost of the assets over their expected useful lives as follows:

Freehold property 2% pa

Computer equipment 25% pa

The directors of the Bank review the impairment of fixed assets of plant, property and equipment
annually.

intangible assets are stated at cost less accumulated amortisation and. impairment losses, Intangible
assets are depreciated over the useful life or the license period

(g) Depreciation of intangible assets

(i) Loans to customers

Loans to customers arise when the Bank provides money to a debtor, with no intention of trading the
receivable. They are initially recognised at the present value of cash receivable including interest
payments and repayments of principal and are subsequently measured at amortised cost using the
effective interest rate method.

(j) Financial liabilities

All financial liabilities are measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method (see (c)
(i) above).

(k) Impairment of financial assets

If there is objective evidence that an impairment loss on financial assets carried at amortised cost has
been incurred, the amount of the loss is measured as the difference between the asset's carrying
amount and the present value of estimated future cash flows (excluding future credit losses that have
not been incurred) discounted at the financial asset's originai effective interest rate (i.e., the effective
interest rate computed at initial recognition). The carrying amount of the asset is reduced through use
of an allowance account. The amount of the loss is recognised in the Income Statement.

Factors considered in determining whether or not a financial asset is impaired include the adequacy
and ease of realisation of any security held; continuing non-payment of interest and/or principal; trading

(m) Taxation

Current taxation is recognised for the amount of Corporation Tax payable in respect of the taxable profit
for the current or past reporting periods using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or
substantively enacted by the reporting date.

Deferred taxation is provided in full on timing differences that result in an obligation at the reporting date
to pay more tax, or a right to pay less tax, at a future date, at rates expected to apply when they
crystallise, based on rates enacted or substantially enacted at the reporting date. Timing differences
arise from the inclusion of items of income and expenditure in taxation computations in periods different
from those in which they are included in the Financial Statements.

A deferred tax asset is recognised to the extent that it is regarded as more likely than not that it will be
recovered. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are not discounted.

15 Financial risk management

Loans and advances to banks and customers are carried at amortised cost net of impairment provisions
where appropriate.

The activities of the Bank expose it to a variety of risks, the most significant are: market risk, liquidity
risk and credit risk.

Market risk is the potential adverse change in the Bank's net assets or income arising from movements
in interest rates, foreign exchange rates or other market prices. Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank is
unable to meet its obligations as they fall due owing to insufficient financial resources. Credit risk is the
risk of financial loss as counterparties and borrowers are unable to meet their obligations as they fall
due.

15.1. Financial risk management framework

The Board has overall responsibility for the establishment and oversight of the Bank's risk management
framework. Sub-committees of the Board have been established to manage the various risks including
the UK Board, the Audit Committee, the Risk Monitoring Committee, the Assets and Liabilities
Committee and the Credit Committee. The UK board and Risk monitoring committee includes non-
executive directors also. The Audit Committee comprises the three non-executive directors and is
responsible for monitoring compliance with the Bank's risk management policies and procedures, and
for reviewing the adequacy of the risk management framework in relation to the risks faced by the Bank.
The Audit Committee is assisted in these functions by Internal Audit. Internal Audit undertakes both
regular and

15.3 Credit risk

15.6 Theinternal assessment criteria for business lending purposes

The Bank has developed a rating system, which is used in conducting reviews of borrowers to assess
their on-going credit standing and to consider whether a loan or advance should be placed on a "watch
list" due to a deteriorating assessment of the borrower. The system is based upon a point scoring
method with points awarded against various criteria from an analysis of financial statements provided
by borrowers. The sum of the points awarded determines the classification of each borrower from A
(the highest rating) to D (the lowest). It is likely that any D rated borrowers would be in default, which
may result in the raising of a provision against the debt. The Bank is careful to detect early on, any
deterioration of the financial conditions of the obligors as well as the providers of different support forms.
Early problem recognition is key to successful risk management. Early warning indicators provide signs
of credit deterioration and include the following:

- Deterioration in financial ratios, covenants, breaches and waiver requests.
- Macroeconomic environment deteriorates.

- Industry comes under financial, economic and political pressure.

- Decline in share / bond / CDS prices.

- Rating agency downgrades and changes to negative outlook.

- Adverse comments in the media.

A default shall be considered to have occurred when an obligor is unlikely to pay its credit obligations
or is past due on any material credit obligation. A period of 90 days past due is the regulatory definition
and should be used as a benchmark. However, consideration must be given to the circumstances of
each obligor and a past due period of less than 90 days may indicate payment is unlikely. Once a debt
is 90 days past due it will be referred monthly to the UK Board or to the full board for review.

15.7 Impaired loans and advances .

principal and interest due according to the contractual terms of the loan agreement through the default
of the borrower and the inability to realise adequate collateral, for whatever reason. When a loan or
advance is deemed to be impaired, the Bank raises a provision for the amount which is determined to
be unrecoverable and reduces the amount of the loan or advance on the Statement of Financial Position
by the amount of the provision. Under FRS 102, the Bank uses the incurred loss model.

15.11 Capital management

The Bank seeks to maximise its capital base within the requirement to provide its shareholder with a
return by way of dividend that compensates the shareholder for the cost of funding its investment in
the share capital of at a level that is commensurate with its peers in the market and adequately
reflects the risk to the shareholder. The Bank's regulator, the Prudential! Regulation Authority also
sets and monitors, a minimum capital requirement based upon a prescribed ratio of capital to total
risk exposure. The Bank's capital for regulatory purposes is analysed into two tiers. Tier 1 capital
which includes ordinary share capital, retained earnings and the general banking reserve; Tier 2
capital which includes qualifying subordinated debt. Bank Saderat does not have Tier 2 capital. The
Bank's capital for regulatory purposes is all tier 1. Tier 1 capital which includes ordinary share capital,
retained earnings and the general banking reserve.
