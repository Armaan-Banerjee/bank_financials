Strategic Report

The directors present the strategic report of Belmont Green Finance Limited (the “Company”) for the year ended
31 December 2018.

Business Model and Strategy

The Company’s lending is secured on residential properties only, covering both Buy to Let borrowers and
Owner occupiers. Customers typically have some element of complexity in their personal circumstances,
or in the case of BTL, their investment strategies. The Company distributes its products solely through
mortgage intermediaries, using the Vida brand, and based on its distribution model, does not provide advice
to customers.

The Group has established Vida as a strong brand in the specialist mortgage market, evidenced by £1.2bn of
completions in just 2 years. The Company’s proposition is criteria driven, using a combination of technology
and peopie to derive an understanding of the customer and their mortgage needs. All applications are
reviewed by experienced and skilled underwriters who ultimately make the lending decisions. The goal is to
provide a high level of service to the intermediary for the entire customer application journey through the
Company’s unique combination of criteria, people and technology. That service aspiration extends
throughout the life of the mortgage with its post-completion customer service operations, via its
outsourced service provider, Computershare Loan Services.

Financial Review

The financial year ended 31 December 2018 saw the Company's loss before tax reduce to £4.22m (31
December 2017: £17.6m) as detailed in the statement of comprehensive income on page 11.

The financial results and balance sheet reflect this year-on-year growth. Net interest income increased
to £20.2m million from £3.4 million in 2027. Administrative expenses increased to £24.4m (2017: £20.9m).
This reflected an increase in the number of employees by 30% to 178, along with a rise in other operating
costs to support the growth of the business.

Regulation

The Company adheres to all Acts of Law and regulations in the course of its operational activities. The
principie regulator for the Company’s mortgage lending business is the Financial Conduct Authority for
which alt appropriate regulatory permissions are in place and are active. Additionally, there are further
requirements which must be met under EU and UK laws and regulations which include obligations with
regards to financial crime, the combating of terrorist financing and data protection.

To offer assurance to key stakeholders that activities operate in adherence with our legal and regulatory
obligations, three lines of defence framework has been fully established. This framework offers oversight
via a systems and controls network that governs the day to day application of regulatory adherence and
through which upcoming regulatory change is identified and implemented in a timely manner. A top-down
compliance culture has been established that starts with the Board of Directors and Executive Committee
which oversee the compliance of business activities with legal and regulatory requirements. They are
supported by an experienced mortgage sector compliance team that ensures that obligations are met
consistently.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The directors have identified a number of potential risks and uncertainties to which the Company is
exposed, and which could impact its ability to conduct its business successfully. These are summarised
below.

Strategic risk

Strategic risk is the risk that can adversely affect the Company’s ability to achieve its business plan and
strategic objectives. These risks can reflect political and economic uncertainties which may materially
impact the ability to meet strategic corporate objectives.

The Board is vigilant within the current market climate and manages risks accordingly. It gives due
consideration to the potential market impacts on the Company’s operations, the affordability of new loans
for customers and any other impacts on the existing loan book.

Legal and regulatory risk

Legal and regulatory risk is the risk of losses or reputational damage arising from legal, regulatory or
financial crime compliance failures, or from regulatory and legislative changes that significantly impact the
Company's business model. The Board accepts the possibility that infrequent minor breaches and risk
events may occur, and this is considered within appetite limit. However, the Board has no appetite for any
systemic or material failure to comply with laws and regulations relevant to the Company and ensures
there are relevant controls in place to restrict the likelihood of this.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or a failure in internal processes, people and
systems, or from external events. {t also includes business continuity, outsourcing risks and the risk of theft
or misuse of confidential data or breaches of physical, personnel or technology security protocols. The
Company has robust systems and controls in place to manage these risks, including periodic testing of the
business continuity plan, alternative site arrangements, daily system back-ups to alternative site network
servers, and regular staff training.

Conduct risk

Conduct risk is the risk of customer detriment or poor outcomes through unsuitable products, poor service
or process failures. The Company takes measures not to offer products or services to its customers which
are unsuited to their needs, or which are likely to cause customer detriment. It also has controls in place
for preventing systemic conduct risk and poor outcomes for its customers and seeks to minimise these risks
as far as possible.

Market risk

Market risk is the risk that changes in market prices will affect the Company’s income or the value of its
holdings of financial instruments. The Company does not engage in any trading operations and does not
have any foreign currency exposure. The principal market risk that the Company is exposed to is interest
rate risk,

Interest rate risk is the potential adverse impact on the Company's future cash flows from changes in
interest rates and arises from the differing interest rate risk characteristics of the Company’s assets and

reduction in income as a result of variable rate interest increases. The Company manages interest rate
exposure by hedging 100% of its fixed rate mortgage loan exposure through balance guaranteed swaps to
match its floating rate funding.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Company will not be able to meet its financial obligations as they fall due.
The Company’s principal obligations arise fram funding mortgage originations, repaying amounts due on
secured notes and settling administration expenses as they fall due.

Whilst the Company is growing, but still loss making, the Company is dependent upon regular equity
injections from its principal shareholder, Pine Brock, to meet its ongoing financial obligations. Pine Brook
has provided a line of equity to the Company and remains committed to supporting the Company in its
growth.

Capital risk management

The Company manages its capital to ensure that entities in the Company have sufficient capita! resources
to continue as going concerns whilst optimising shareholder returns. The capital of the Company consists
of share capital, reserves and retained earnings as disclosed in these financial statements.

The Company is supervised by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and must comply with the MIPRU

these requirements on a monthly basis. Regulatory capital requirements were complied with at all times
during the year.

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of financial toss, arising from default on mortgage lending or from the default of a
moaney-market counterparty. Credit risk is managed through reviewing regular detailed credit reports
during the year, identifying large exposures, loan to values and arrears within the products. Forward
looking credit risk data including debt service coverage ratios for BTL and external bureau data covering
credit quality and affordability levels. The reports also highlight early warning indicators, which allow the
risk function to address potential credit issues before they develop into significant risk areas.

All credit risk polices undergo annual review and updates to policies including the Company lending policy,
the arrears, repossession and forbearance policies and the loan loss provisioning policy have been
approved by Board and the Assets and Liabilities Committee (ALCO).

2, Accounting policies

{a) Basis of preparation

The company’s financial statements have been prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting
Standards (IFRSs) as adopted by the European Union (EU). The financial statements have been prepared on the
historic cost basis. The principal accounting policies are set out below.

On 1 January 2018, the company adopted IFRS 9. IFRS 9 changes the classification and measurement of certain
financial instruments. Financial assets are classified as held at amortised cost, at fair value through other
comprehensive income (FVOCI), or fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL). The measurement of impairment
losses are based on expected credit loss model. The new standard requires an entity to recognise expected
credit losses at all times and to update the amount of expected credit losses recognised at each reporting date
to reflect changes in the credit risk of financial instruments. The IFRS 9 model is forward-looking and it
eliminates the threshold for the recognition of expected credit losses, so that it is no longer necessary for a
trigger event to have occurred before credit losses are recognised. Consequently, more timely information is
required to be provided about expected credit losses.

Impairment: Impairment provisions made in the 2017 financial statements were assessed in accordance with
IAS 39. This assessment used an incurred loss model for estimating the impairment of financial assets. With the
adoption of !FRS 9, provisions are. being recognised on either a 12-month or a lifetime expected loss basis, This
has resulted in the earlier recognition of potential future losses. However, whilst IFRS 9 differs significantly from
the previous impairment standard (IAS 39}, the amount of impairment losses recorded against an individual
loan over the life of that loan will not change as IFRS 9 only alters the timing of recognition of potential losses.

IFRS 15: Revenue from contracts with customers. IFRS 15 replaces the standards currently governing the
recognition of the income and sets the principles to be applied in connection with revenue from contracts with
customers. IFRS 15 applies to all contracts with customers but does not apply to lease contracts, insurance
contracts, financial instruments and certain non-monetary exchanges. Based on the financial instruments
exclusion, the introduction of the new standard will not have an effect on the 2018 financial statements.

(d) Interest income and expense

interest income and expense are recognised in the statement of comprehensive income for all financial
instruments measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate (”EIR”) method under tAS39 (Financial
Instruments: recognition and measurement). The EIR is the rate which exactly discounts the expected future
cash flows, over the expected life of the financial instrument, to the net carrying value of the financial asset or
liability. In calculating the EIR the Company estimates the cash flows considering all contractual terms but not
future credit losses. Origination fees received and paid on foans to customers are amortised over the expected
life of those assets using the EIR method.

In the 2018 accounts the fee income is included in the interest income and similar items and the fee expense in
the fee expense and similar charges. Fees that are an integral part of the effective interest rate of a financial
instrument are recognised using the EIR method, Fees that are not considered integra! to the effective interest
rate are generally recognised on an accruals basis when the service has been provided.

(e) Taxation

Tax on the profit or loss for the period comprises current tax and deferred tax. Tax is recognised in the income
statement except to the extent that it relates to items recognised directly in equity, in which case it is
recognised in other comprehensive income.

Current tax is the expected tax payable on the taxable profit for the year. Taxable profit differs from profit as
reported in the consolidated income statement because it excludes items of income and expense that are
taxable or deductible in future years and it further excludes items of income and expense that are never taxable
or deductible. The Company's liability for current tax is calculated using tax rates that have been enacted or
substantively enacted at the balance sheet date.

Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable on differences between the carrying amounts of
the assets and liabilities in the financial statements and the corresponding amounts used for taxation purposes
and is accounted for using the balance sheet liability method. Deferred tax liabilities are generally recognised
for all taxable temporary differences. Deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent that it is probable that
taxabte profits will be available against which deductible temporary differences can be utilised.

Deferred tax is calculated at the tax rates that are expected to apply in the year when the liability is settled or
the asset is realised based on tax laws and rates that have been enacted or substantively enacted at the balance
sheet date.

Defersyed tax assets and liabilities are offset when there is a legally enforceable right to set off current tax assets
against current tax liabilities and the Company intends to settle its current tax assets and liabilities on a net
basis.

(k) Impairment of financial assets

The group has adopted IFRS 9, the new accountancy standard covering the calculation of impairment
allowances. Impairment provisions on all financial assets are recognised based on either lifetime expected loss
for non-impairment assets that are deemed to have significantly deteriorated, or 12-month loss for ail other
non-impaired assets. The Group’s first loan completed in December 2016, hence there was insufficient internal
data to use in the development of IFRS 9 impairment models. As a result, the Group has developed bespoke
probability of default (PD), loss given default (LGD) and exposure at default (EAD) models using a combination
of external data and expert judgement.

e PD models: The PD of an exposure is the probability (%) that an account will move from a non-default
status into a default status. External data was used to develop PD curves using survival analysis
methodology to predict the probability of an account defaulting or prepaying. PD curves were then
recalibrated to the Graup’s actual 12 months PD.

« {GD model: LGD was developed based on management judgement and experience and has been
independently validated. LGD includes the probability of an account moving from default to possession,
the estimated recovery in the event of possession, costs incurred in the possession and sale of property
and discounting of recoveries to present value based on the time to sale.

® EAD model: EAD refers to the estimated exposure (£ balance) at the time of default. The derivation of
EAD was mechanical using the contractual amortisation of the loan using the assumption that the EAD
will be equivalent to the account balance with 3 months of non-payment and the associated accrued
interest.

The impairment charge in the income statement includes the change in expected credit losses. Expected credit
fosses {ECis) are based on an assessment of the probability of default (PD), toss given default (LGD) and
exposure at default (EAD) discounted to give a net present value. ECLs are calculated in a way that reflects an
unbiased and probability-weighted amount that is determined by evaluating a range of possible outcomes
considering all reasonable and supportable information including forecast economic assumptions.

To reduce the delay in recognising credit losses under IFRS 9, accounts are assigned to one of three stages
which are intended to reflect the deterioration in credit quality.

Stage 1 comprises of accounts that have shown no significant deterioration in credit quality since origination.
They are low risk accounts and carry an expected credit loss (ECL) allowance equivalent to the ECL that results
from those default events that are possible within 12 months of the reporting date (12-month ECL}.

Stage 2 comprises of accounts that have shown significant deterioration in credit quality since origination.
These are accounts showing an increase in risk but are not yet credit impaired and they carry an ECL equivalent
to the ECL over the contractual lifetime of asset (lifetime ECL).

Stage 3 comprises of accounts that either defaulted or are considered to be credit impaired. These accounts
carry a lifetime ECL. Under IFRS 9, provisions are based on the loans carrying value and the present value of the
estimated future cashflows.

The calculation of expected credit losses under IFRS 9 requires the use of forward-looking information. BGFL has
built a macroeconomic model based on the relationship between historical economic variables to identify key
drivers which impact credit risk. This has been applied to the Probability of Default (PD) model in the form of an
exogenous factor.

(t) Fair value of financial assets and liabilities

For financial instruments that are measured at fair value subsequent to initia! recognition, the Company uses a
fair value hierarchy with the following levels:

Level 1; valuations based upon quoted market prices (unadjusted) in an actively traded market for identical
assets and liabilities that the Company has the ability to access. Since valuations are based on readily available
observable market prices, this makes them most reliable, reduces the need for management judgement and
estimation and also reduces the uncertainty associated with determining fair values.

Level 2: valuation techniques based on one or more quoted prices in markets that are not active or for which all
significant inputs are taken from directly or indirectly observable market data. These include valuation models
used to calculate the present value of expected future cash flows and may be employed either when no active
market exists or when there are quoted prices available for similar instruments in active markets.

Level 3: valuation techniques for which any one or more significant input is not based on abservable market
data and the unobservable inputs have a significant effect on the instrument’s fair value. Valuation models that
employ significant unobservable inputs require a higher degree of management judgement and estimation in
determining the fair value. Management judgement and estimation are usually required for the selection of the
appropriate valuation model to be used, determination of expected future cash flows on the financial
instruments being valued, determination of the probability of counterparty default and prepayments,
determination of expected volatilities and correlations and selection of appropriate discount rates.

Financial instruments with fair value measured at level 3 include loans to customers and debt securities in issue.
Neither loans to customers nor debt securities in issue are measured at fair value in the statement of financial
position.

Loans to customers belong to this level because their valuation uses unobservable inputs on collectability rates
and redemption profiles. Their fair value is calculated using modelled receipts of interest and principal which
are discounted at market rates.

{ii} Effective interest rate

Interest income is recognised in the statement of comprehensive income at amortised cost using the effective
interest rate (“EIR”) method under IFRS 9 Financial instruments.

A number of assumptions were made when calculating the EIR for newly originated mortgage loans. These
include their expected lives and likely redemption profiles. Judgement is used in assessing the expected rate of
prepayment and hence the expected life of those mortgages that prepay. This applies to revenue and charges
related to mortgage asséts as the expected life of a mortgage will typically differ from the contractual term.
The expected life assumption determines the profile of revenue that is booked over the period of the loan.
These assumptions are monitored to ensure their ongoing appropriateness. Significant judgment is applied in
determining the EIR due to uncertainty in the timing and amounts of future cash flows.
