Strategic Report
For the year ended 31 March 2023

The Directors are pleased to present their strategic report for the Persia International Bank PLC
for the year ended 31 March 2023.

Introduction

Persia International Bank PLC (“PIB” or “the Bank”) is an authorised institution under the
Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. The Bank’s principal activity is the provision of
commercial banking services to its customers. Although financial sanctions imposed in 2010
were lifted on 16 January 2016. However, in November 2018 the US decision to unilaterally
withdraw from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) agreement and subsequent
re-imposition of OFAC sanctions means the normal banking activities have not yet been fully
resumed owing to continuing difficulties in arranging UK clearing facilities and in establishing
correspondent relationships with international banks.

Business Review

Historically, the Bank has offered a comprehensive service in trade finance and credit facilities
to its customers. Under trade finance, the Bank can receive and advice letters of credit, add
confirmation and provide short term credit facilities by discounting the bills drawn under them.
PIB also extended credit facilities to its corporate and institutional customers either on bilateral
terms or as part of syndication. Iranian corporate customers constituted a significant portion
of the Bank’s customer base.

On 23 July 2021 the Bank received licence from the Central bank of Italy for its Milan branch
as a branch of third country bank (TCB) outside the EU. The branch has implemented its core
banking systems and is operationally ready. This will enable the Bank to expand its business
mainly in trade finance and credit facilities.

The Statement of Financial Position shoes total assets of the Bank have reduced slightly due to
the scheduled amortisation of loan assets. Historically and before the sanctions the trade
finance had been an important portfolio in PIB’s business lines. Although it has secured a
€19.8Mn forfaiting business last year but, in this year the Bank did not have any opportunity
in this line of business. Further resumption of trade finance activities are possible only when
the issues surrounding correspondent banking relationships are resolved. However, the Bank
will consider all viable options in the future.

The outlook for the year 2023-24 will, however be challenging as economic and geo-political
uncertainties, ongoing regulatory changes and difficulties in securing suitable correspondent
banking relationships have created uncertainties. Looking ahead the Bank sees significant
opportunities in its key market. The Bank has skilled and motivated team and solid shareholders
support which will assist in handling the uncertainties. Its continued focus has been on projects
in the public sectors such as the petrochemical firms and those corporates which generate
revenue in foreign currencies. The Bank will continue to strike a balance between growing its
balance sheet and prudently managing the associated risks.

Financial Performance during the year 2022-23 and key performance indicators

The Bank’s financial performance for the year is better than the expectations set out at the
beginning of the year and made a profit €803k. The Bank's income is currently derived mainly
from interest earned on money-market placements, syndicated loan, forfaiting deals and loans

The total administrative expenses for the financial year are €6,332k (FY2022: €5,261k). The

biggest asset class is loans and advances to the banks which includes money market placements
€40.3Mn (FY2022: €30.0Mn) and forfaiting €19.8Mn (FY2022: €19.8Mn). The money market
placements are kept for varying periods of maturity. The management envisage replacing
money market lending with corporate loans when the opportunity arises for new lending
facilities. The collateral secured against some loans continued to be above 150% on average.

and currency risk. The Directors have also identified the lack of GBP and other currencies
clearing facilities, regulatory compliance, IT concentration due to reliance on few suppliers,
geo-political and succession risks as also the major risks faced by the Bank.

The Board has defined responsibilities for the overall direction, supervision and control of the
Bank. This includes assessment of the Bank’s competitive position, approval of strategic and
financial plans taking into consideration the likely consequences of any decisions in the long
run and review of the performance and financial status. It reviews and approves significant

OFAC sanctions

Following the decision of the US Government to unilaterally withdraw from the JCPOA, the
OFAC sanctions were imposed in November 2018. These sanctions prohibit people from the
US engaging in any trade, financial and other dealings with Iran. This has prevented the Bank
from engaging in business with the US based or linked counterparties and has made dealings
with other countries also more difficult. The management is of the view that the impact of
OFAC sanctions although visible but will not hamper the present business pattern of the Bank.
Its primary business dealings are in euro and exposures to Iranian entities. The non-availability
of SWIFT impacted business communications and transfer of funds. This has been managed
through book transfer mechanism with the involvement of fraternal banks in the EU.

Risk weighting of assets

The EU notification on the equivalence of the supervisory and regulatory requirements of non-
EU countries has excluded Iran from the list of those countries, and therefore exposure to Iran
will continue to be risk weighted at 150%. Considering our business activities are mainly
focused on Iranian corporate and financial institutions, these risk weighting measures will
impact the business level that the Bank can achieve and therefore its profitability.

Reputation

The Bank is authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and regulated by the
both, Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and PRA and; is structured in accordance with the
principles of good governance. This includes employing two Independent Non-Executive
Directors, one of whom chairs the Board & the Audit Committee and the other chairs the Risk
Committee and Remuneration Committees. They also have oversight of other management
level committees to ensure ‘four eyes’ are involved in all major policy and operational
decisions. The Bank take seriously all aspects of conduct risk and fully subscribe to the ©
principles of ‘treating customers fairly’ to ensure that the trust customers put on as bankers is
well founded.

or financial liabilities, as appropriate, on initial recognition.

Transaction costs directly attributable to the acquisition of financial assets or financial
liabilities at FVTPL are recognised immediately in profit or loss. If the transaction price differs
from fair value at initial recognition, the Bank will account for such difference as follows:

- if fair value is evidenced by a quote price in an active market for an incidental asset or

then the difference is recognised in profit and loss on initial recognition (1.e., day 1 in
profit and loss);

- In all other cases, the fair value will be adjusted to bring in line with the transaction
price. The Bank recognises the difference between the transaction price and fair value
in net trading income.

After initial recognition, the deferred gain or loss will be released to profit or loss on a rational
basis, only to the extent that it arises from a change in a factor (including time) the market

2.8 Impairment

The Bank recognises loss allowances for ECLs (expected credit losses) on the following
financial instruments that are not measured at FVTPL:

loans and advances to banks;
loans and advances to customers;
debt instrument securities;

loan commitments issued.

ECL is determined by using a staging model and forward-looking information. Impairment
provision on all financial assets is recognised either on 12 months losses or lifetime expected
losses and measured through a loss allowance at an amount equal to:

- 12-month ECL, ie. lifetime ECL that result from those default events on the financial
instrument that are possible within 12 months after the reporting date, (referred to as
Stage 1); or

- Full lifetime ECL, i.e. lifetime ECL that result from all possible default events over the
lifetime of the financial instrument, (referred as to Stage 2 and Stage 3).

A loss allowance for full lifetime ECL is required for a financial instrument if the credit risk
on that financial instrument has increased significantly since initial recognition. For all other
financial instruments, ECLs are measured at an amount equal to the 12-month ECL.

ECLs are probability-weighted estimate of the present value of credit losses. These are
measured as the present value of the difference between the cash flows due to the Bank under
the contract and the cash flows that the Bank expect to receive arising from the weighting of
multiple future economic scenarios, discounted at the asset’s EIR.

- For undrawn loan commitments, the ECL is the difference between the present value
of the difference between the contractual cash flows that are due to the Bank if the
holder of the commitment draws down the loan and the cash flows that the Bank expect
to receive if the loan is drawn down; and

- For financial guarantee contract, the ECL is the difference between the expected
payments to reimburse the holder of the guaranteed debt instrument less any amount
that the Bank expect to receive from the holder, the debtor or any other party.

The Bank measures its ECL on an individual basis. The measurement of the loss allowance is
based on the present value of the asset’s expected cash flows using the asset’s original EIR,
regardless of whether it is measured on an individual basis.

The Bank’s ECL calculations are outputs of model with a number of underlying assumptions
regarding the choice of variable inputs and their interdependencies. A number of significant

2.9 _Credit-impaired financial assets

A financial asset is ‘credit-impaired’ when one or more events that have a detrimental impact
on the estimated future cash flows of the financial asset have occurred. Credit-impaired
financial assets are referred to as Stage 3 assets. Evidence of credit- -impairment includes
observable data about the following events:

- significant financial difficulty or the borrower or issuer;

- A breach of contract such as a default or past due event;

- The lender of the borrower, for economic or contractual reasons relating to the
borrower’s financial difficulty, having granted to the borrower a concession that the

- lender would not otherwise consider

- The disappearance of an active market for a security because of financial difficulties;
or

- The purchase of a financial asset at a deep discount that reflects the incurred credit
losses.

It may not be possible to identify a single discrete event — instead, the combined effect of
several events may have caused financial asset to become credit-impaired. The Bank assess
whether debt instruments that are financial assets measured at amortised cost or FVTOCI are
credit-impaired at each reporting date. To assess if sovereign or corporate debt instruments are
credit impaired, the bank considers factors such as bond yields, credit ratings and the ability of
the borrower to raise funding.

A loan is considered credit-impaired when a concession is granted to the borrower due to a
deterioration in the borrower’s financial condition, unless there is evidence that as a result of
granting the concession the risk is not receiving the contractual cash flows has reduced
significantly and there are no other indicators of the impairment. For financial assets where
concessions are contemplated but not granted the asset is deemed credit impaired when there
is observable evidence of credit-impairment including meeting the definition of default.

2.11 Modification and derecognition of financial assets

A modification of a financial asset occurs when the contractual terms governing the cash flows
of a financial asset are renegotiated or otherwise modified between initial recognition and of
the financial asset. A modification affects the amount and/or timing of the contractual cash
flows either immediately or at a future date. In addition, the introduction or adjustment of
existing covenants of an existing loan would constitute a modification even if these new or
adjusted covenants do not yet affect the cash flows immediately but affect the cash flows
depending on whether the covenant is or is not met (e.g. a change to the increase in the interest
rate that arises when covenant is breached).

The Bank negotiate loans to customers in financial difficulty to maximise collection and to
minimise the risk of default. The revised terms in most of the cases include an extension of the
maturity of the loan, changes to the timing of cash flows of the loan (principal and interest
repayment), reduction in the amount of cash flows due (principal and interest forgiveness) and
amendments to covenants.

assess whether an impairment loss should be recorded in the profit or loss. In particular,
management’s judgement is required in the estimation of the amount and timing of future cash
flows when determining the impairment loss. These estimates are based on assumptions about
a number of factors and actual results may differ, resulting in future changes to the allowance.

Loans and advances that have been assessed individually and found not to be impaired are
assessed together with all individually insignificant loans and advances in groups of assets with
similar risk characteristics. This is to determine whether provision should be made due to
incurred loss events for which there is objective evidence, but the effects of which are not yet
evident. The collective assessment takes account of data from the loan portfolio (such as levels
of arrears, credit utilisation, loan-to-collateral ratios, etc.) and judgements on the effect of
concentrations of risks and economic data. See note 28 for details on judgements and estimates.
