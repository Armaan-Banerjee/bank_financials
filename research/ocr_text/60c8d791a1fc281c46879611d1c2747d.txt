Introduction

Redwood Bank's purpose is to meet the funding needs of both small and medium sized enterprises (SME's)
and individual property investors. Redwood Bank prides itself on being customer focussed and offering a true
partnership based on understanding and meeting the individual needs of every customer. The Bank offers
individually assessed commercial, residential and refurbishment secured mortgages, as well as Best Buy
savings accounts, which are available to charities, clubs, associations and businesses.

In this, the Bank’s first full year of trading, Redwood Bank has begun to develop brand loyalty and trust within
the UK SME market based on offering simple, transparent loans and savings accounts, backed up with tailored
service, efficient systems and with the knowledge that money is being invested back into British business.

i
The principal activities of the Bank continue to be secured property lending to businesses, along with business
deposits.

Business review

The Bank has consistently grown its business throughout 2018 while achieving positive customer ratings both.in
its lending and deposit business. It is supported by a growing team of dedicated and experienced professionals.

Lending increased significantly throughout the year. The credit approved loan pipeline remains strong as at 31
December 2018 at £27.0m (2017: £6.6m). We-have seen continued demand and the expectation is that this
will continue throughout 2019.

At this early stage, the Bank remains in a growth phase where the key objectives are to continue to build a
robust and sustainable platform from which to move from current expected losses and enter the next profit-
making phase of the business.

Redwood Bank has consistently featured within Business Deposit Best Buy tables throughout 2018, growing its
savings.customer base to over 1,600 accounts. Redwood’s 35-day and 95-day Notice Savings Accounts, and 1-
year Business Savings Bond have all proved very popular with customers and have been recognised with
industry awards, including fram Moneyfacts and Savings Champion.

The Bank has invested in its Business Development teams and the increased headcount enabled the Bank to
fully support customer demand. The Banking and Credit Operation teams also increased in line with the growth
in deposit and lending activities. The quick and efficient processing of both deposit and loan requests
contributed to high levels of customer satisfaction, and enabled the Bank to effectively operate within a highly
secure platform and a complex regulatory environment.

Investors illustrated their confidence in Redwood Bank by investing an additional £9.8m of share capital during
the year, providing sufficient capital buffers for the Bank to deliver its growth targets. Further capital investment
of £12.0m has been received in March 2019.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of suffering financial loss should borrowers or counterparties default on their contractual
obligations to the Bank. These risks are managed by the Board Risk Committee, along with the Credit
Committee, and are mainly mitigated by way of security in the form of property. Significant attention has been
paid to the impact of Brexit on the property market, to establish the Bank’s exposure should there be a
significant decline in property prices in the event of a disorderly Brexit. The Bank’s sensitivity testing has shown
that although a disorganised Brexit will impact short term profits it will not affect the long-term viability of the
Bank. ;

Credit risk on Treasury instruments is limited through a counterparty risk policy to manage exposures, and as at
31 December 2018 the exposure is predominately the UK government.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank will encounter difficulty in meeting its obligations to third parties as they
become payable. The Bank mitigates this risk by holding an adequate portion of its. assets in the form of cash
balances, Treasury Bills and UK Government Gilts, and by carefully managing the maturity of its funding. The
Bank manages its balance sheet to self-imposed liquidity ratios, in addition to regulatory metrics, that provides
sufficient liquidity given a number of adverse scenarios.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, whether people and/or
systems, or from external events such as cyber attacks. The Bank has a low tolerance for operational losses
and seeks to run its business in such a way that operational losses are avoided at all times. In order to reduce
the chance of a cybersecurity breach, the Bank partners with leading technology companies, and in addition
pioritises staff training, multi factor authentication, encryption and complex passwords. The Bank recognises
that some operational losses are inevitable and therefore ensures that any significant risk events are logged,
investigated and subject to root-cause analysis.

Concentration risk |

The target market and heartland lending strategy creates the potential for concentration risk, however this risk is
monitored, and limits are imposed to prevent over exposure in the event of a sector or region-specific financial
stress.

Competitive risks

Ensuring healthy competition and fair customer outcomes in the UK banking market has been a sharp focus for
both the regulator and the Competition and Markets Authority.

In the aftermath of the banking crisis, small and medium sized businesses suffered as many banks tightened
lending requirements. This provided an opportunity for Redwood Bank to enter into the market and deliver on
the needs of such businesses.

While there is a degree of competition from other challenger banks, Redwood Bank is able to take advantage of
the opportunities in the market and ensure future growth while concentrating upon its heartland regions and
operating within its risk appetite.

Customer behavioural risk

Customers are increasingly demanding online or digital servicing and greater flexibility in lending and saving
products. Time is also of the essence, as customers expect more frequent communication throughout the
lending application process as well as quicker approval decisions. The Bank employs experienced, specialist
underwriters, enabling the processing of customer applications quickly.

By employing specialist underwriting expertise, Redwood Bank maintains agility and speed in operation,
allowing the Bank to process customer applications quickly, ensuring customer satisfaction while maintaining
our approved risk appetite.

The Bank’s infrastructure is based purely in the Cloud and is therefore extremely adaptable to changing
customer requirements and needs. Products are able to be launched and withdrawn quickly in line with
changing customer demands and requirements.

Key man risk

Due the Bank's size, there is significant reliance on the skills of individuals. The Bank has a comprehensive
succession pian to mitigate this risk and continues to build robust policies and procedures to limit reliance on
key individuals.

Legislative and regulatory risks

Banking is a highly regulated market and can be‘affected by frequent changes and updates in legislation. The
Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and the Firiancial Conduct Authority (FCA) are the primary bodies which
regulate the banking industry in the UK. The Bank has implemented internal processes of capturing any future
legislative and regulatory change well in advance of its impiementation date, thus ensuring that the Bank is able
to meet all regulatory requirements adequately.

In line with PRA capital requirements, the Bank holds significant shareholder funds to enable the Bank to
survive adverse market conditions and shocks. At all times, historically and in forecasts, the Bank's capital
available exceeds the requirements.

Changes to the UK tax regime and revisions to regulatory standards are being monitored by the Board’s Risk
Committee to ensure current and future compliance.

Economic and political environment

The UK's decision to withdraw from the European Union could potentially have an adverse impact on the UK
economy due to significant uncertainty and disruption. The Bank is unlikely to be directly affected by the UK
leaving the EU as it operates entirely in the UK. However, as part of its ICAAP and ILAAP, the Bank has
considered several stress scenarios that could: be triggered by an event such as a ‘No Deal’ Brexit and has
evaluated the impact on the Bank’s capital and liquidity position. The scenarios consider the possible impact on
the Bank of changes to base rate, house price inflation, property interest rates and cost inflation.

Whilst recognising a negative impact to the Bank’s performance and capital position, the Bank is considered to
be sufficiently capitalised to continue in business, even under stressful economic conditions. Although the full
impact of Brexit is yet to be seen, the Board is confident that the risks have been appropriately captured within
the Bank’s strategic plan.

Management continue to review developments and their impact on the potential risks to the Bank.

Financial key performance indicators
The Bank made a loss before tax for the year ended 31 December 2018 of £3.8m (2017: £3.3m).

Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital as at 31 December 2018 was £15.1m and exceeded the regulatory
minimum set by the PRA.

The Bank’s liquidity coverage ratio at 31 December 2018 exceeded the regulatory minimum set by the PRA and
the Bank had a total value of high-quality liquid assets of £58m.

The average lending rate for the year was 6.73% and the average interest rate paid on deposits was 1.47%.
The balance sheet reflects loans of £79.4m (2017: £8.4m) and deposits of £122.4m (2017: £18.7m) reflecting a
significant growth in the first full year of trading.

Other key performance indicators

Redwood bank has almost doubled its staff base from 36 at the beginning of the year, to 61 staff members at
the end of 2018.

All staff have completed general’ conduct training and have each passed the requisite tests, and customer
feedback has been monitored since the launch in August 2017, with only three regulatory complaints having
been received in 2018.

The majority of lending deals were completed within 12 weeks of approval during 2018.

Redwood Bank makes use of a Cloud based platform, which enables flexible, timely and efficient customer
service. This modern approach significantly reduced the amount of waste produced by the Bank, by limiting the
amount of paper required. Redwood Bank believes that success is not possible without sustainability, and in a
further attempt to limit the Bank’s carbon footprint it has implemented office-wide recycling initiatives.

Future developments and prospects

Redwood Bank will continue to build on the strong foundations that have been put in place, by continuing to
build a strong business development function, supported by experienced operations, credit, finance, risk and
management teams.

Redwood Bank is committed to deliver simple and transparent financial products in a timely fashion to British
businesses, and any new products launched will be designed to meet the financial needs of customers and
allow them more time to focus on their businesses.

Through continued investment in our strategy and people we aim to deliver the highest levels of customer
service for local and national businesses and their owners.

4 We have nothing to report on going concern

The Directors have prepared the financial statements on the going concern basis as they do not intend to
liquidate the Bank or to cease its operations, and as they have concluded that the Bank's financial position
means that this is realistic. They have also concluded that there are no material uncertainties that could have
cast significant doubt over its ability to continue as a going concern for at least a year from the date of approval
of the financial statements (“the going concern period”).

Our responsibility is to conclude on the appropriateness of the Directors’ conclusions and, had there been a
material uncertainty related to going concern, to make reference to that in this audit report. However, as we
cannot predict ail future events or conditions and as subsequent events may result in outcomes that are
inconsistent with judgements that were reasonable at the time they were made, the absence of reference to a
material uncertainty in this auditor's report is not a guarantee that the Bank will continue in operation.

In our evaluation of the Directors’ conclusions, we considered the inherent risks to the Bank's business model
. and analysed how those risks might affect the Bank’s financial resources or ability to continue operations over
the going concern period. The risk that we considered most likely to adversely affect the Bank's available
financial resources over this period was the impact of Brexit on the Bank's liquidity and capital resources.

As these were risks that could potentially cast significant doubt on the Bank's ability to continue as a going
concern, we considered sensitivities over the level of available financial resources indicated by the Bank’s
financial forecasts taking account of reasonably possible (but not unrealistic) adverse effects that could arise
from these risks individually and collectively and evaluated the achievability of the actions the Directors consider
they would take to improve the position should the risks materialise. We also considered less predictable but
realistic second order impacts, such as the impact on collateral values and availability and cost of funding from
a disorderly Brexit and the impact of Brexit on unemployment and the impact on borrowers ability to repay debt.

Based on this work, we are required to report to you if we have concluded that the use of the going concern
basis of accounting is inappropriate or there is an undisclosed material uncertainty that may cast significant
doubt over the use of that basis for a period of at least a year from the date of approval of the financial
statements.

We have nothing to report in these respects, and we did not identify going concern as a key audit matter.

2.3 Interest income and interest expense

Interest income and expense are recognised in comprehensive income using the effective interest rate
method. The ‘effective interest rate’ is the rate that exactly discounts the estimated future cash payments
and receipts through the expected life of the financial asset or financial liability (or, where appropriate, a
shorter period) to the carrying amount of the financial asset or financial liability. When calculating the
effective interest rate, the Bank estimates future cash flows considering all contractual terms of the
financial instrument, but not future credit losses.

The calculation of the effective interest rate includes transaction costs and fees paid or received that are
an integral part of the effective interest rate. Transaction costs include incremental costs that are directly
attributable to the acquisition or issue of a financial asset or financial liability.

Interest income and expense presented in comprehensive income include:
- interest on financial assets and financial liabilities measured at amortised cost calculated on an

effective interest basis; and
- interest on available-for-sale investment securities calculated on an effective interest rate basis.

2.6 Financial instruments

Recognition

The Bank initially recognises loans and advances, deposits, and debt securities on the date on which they
are originated. A financial asset or financial liability is measured initially at fair value plus, for an item not
at fair value through profit or loss, transaction costs that are directly attributable to its acquisition or issue.
Classification

Financial assets

The Bank classifies its financial assets into one of the following categories:

Loans and advances to customers

‘Loans and advances to customers’ are non-derivative financial assets with fixed or determinable
payments that are not quoted in an active market and that the Bank does not intend to sell immediately or

in the near term.

Loans and advances are initially measured at fair value plus incremental direct transaction costs, and
subsequently measured at their amortised cost using the effective interest method (see Note 3).

Available-for-sale
Available-for-sale investments are non-derivative investments that are designated as available-for-sale or
are not classified as another category of financial assets. Available-for-sale investments comprise

Treasury Bills and Gilts and are measured at fair value after initial recognition.

Interest income is recognised in profit or loss using the effective interest method (see Note 3).
Impairment losses are recognised in comprehensive income.

Other fair value changes, other than impairment losses, are recognised in ‘Other Comprehensive Income’
and presented in the available for sale reserve within equity. When the investment is sold, the gain or loss
accumulated in equity is reclassified to comprehensive income.

Financial Liabilities

‘Trade creditors’ are initially measured at transaction price plus attributable transaction costs, and are
subsequently measured at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method.

Measurement

Amortised cost measurement

The amortised cost of a financial asset or financial liability is the amount at which the financial asset or
financial liability is measured at initial recognition, minus principal repayments, plus or minus the
cumulative amortisation using the effective interest method of any difference between the initial amount
recognised and the maturity amount, minus any reduction for impairment.

Fair value measurement

Fair vaiue is the amount for which an asset could be exchanged, a liability settled, or an equity instrument
granted could be exchanged, between knowledgeable, willing parties in an arm's length transaction.

When available, the Bank measures the fair value of an instrument using the quoted price in an active
market for that instrument. A market is regarded as active if transactions for the asset or liability take
place with sufficient frequency and volume to provide. pricing information on an ongoing basis.

If there is no quoted price in an active market, then the Bank uses valuation techniques that maximise the
use of relevant observable inputs and minimise the use of unobservable inputs. The chosen valuation
technique incorporates all of the factors that market participants would take into account in pricing a
transaction.

The best evidence of the fair value of a financial instrument at initial recognition is normally the
transaction price — i.e. the fair value of the consideration given or received. If the Bank determines that
the fair value at initial recognition differs from the transaction price and the fair value is evidenced neither
by a quoted price in an active market for an identical asset or liability or based on a valuation technique
that uses only data from observable markets, then the financial instrument is initially measured at fair
value, adjusted to defer the difference between the fair value at initial recognition and the transaction
price. Subsequently, that difference is recognised in profit or loss on an appropriate basis over the life of
the instrument, but no later than when the valuation is wholly supported by observable market data or the
transaction is closed out.

Identification and measurement of impairment

At each reporting date, the Bank assesses whether there is objective evidence that financial assets not
carried at fair value through profit or loss are impaired. A financial asset or a group of financial assets is
‘impaired’ when objective evidence demonstrates that a loss event has occurred after the initial
recognition of the asset(s) and that the loss event has an impact on the future cash flows of the asset(s)
that can be estimated reliably.

Objective evidence that financial assets are impaired includes:

- significant financial difficulty of the borrower or issuer;

- default or delinquency by a borrower; |

- the restructuring of a loan or advance by the Bank on terms that the Bank would not consider
otherwise; ,

- indications that a borrower or issuer will enter bankruptcy;

- the disappearance of an active market for a security; or

- observable data relating to a group of assets such as adverse changes in the payment status of
borrowers or issuers in the group, or economic conditions that correlate with defaults in the group.

The Bank considers evidence of impairment for loans and advances at both a specific asset and a
collective level. All loans and advances are assessed for specific impairment. Those found not to be
specifically impaired are then collectively assessed for any impairment that has been incurred but not yet
identified. Impairment losses on assets measured at amortised cost are calculated as the difference
between the carrying amount and the present value of estimated future cash flows discounted at the
asset's original effective interest rate. ,

Accounting estimates and judgements

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with FRS 102 requires management, from time to
time, to make judgements and estimates that affect the application of policies and reported amounts of
assets, liabilities, income and expenses.

These estimates are based on experience and various other factors that are believed to be reasonable
under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The estimates and underlying
assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting estimates are recognised in the
period in which the estimates are revised and in any future periods affected.

Effective interest rate

IAS 39 requires interest. earned from loans and advances to be measured under the Effective Interest
Rate Method (EIRM). Management must therefore use judgement to estimate the expected life of each
instrument and hence the expected cash flows relating to it. Management reviews the expected lives on a
segmental basis, whereby products of a similar nature are grouped into cohorts that exhibit homogenous
behavioural attributes.

The key assumptions applied by management in the EIR methodology are regarding the behavioural life
of the assets and the quantum of future fees and costs directly related to the instrument. The expected
life behaviours are subjected to changes in internal and external factors and may result in adjustments to
the carrying value of loans which must be recognised in the Statement of Profit and Loss and Other
Comprehensive Income.

Management has limited historical experience of the Bank's customer behaviours due to the relative
immaturity of the portfolios and therefore models expected behaviour based on market trends and
experience. The actual behaviour of the portfolios are compared to the modelled behaviour on a quarterly
basis and the modelled behaviours are adjusted if the modelled behaviour materially deviates from actual
behaviour, with adjustments recognised in the Statement of Profit and Loss and Other Comprehensive
Income. |

Market risk

Market risk is the risk that the value of, or income arising from, the Bank’s assets and liabilities change as
a result of changes in market prices, the principal element being interest rate risk. All of the Bank’s
exposure to market risk relates to non- trading portfolios.

- Interest rate risk

Interest rate risk is the risk of loss arising from adverse movements in market interest rates. Interest rate
risk arises from the loan and savings products that we offer. This risk is managed through the use of
appropriate financial instruments with established risk limits, reporting lines, mandates and other control
procedures. ;

~ Basis risk

Basis risk is the risk of loss arising from changes in the relationship between interest rates which have
similar, but not identical characteristics (for example, LIBOR and the Bank of England base rate). This is
monitored closely and regularly reported to the Assets and Liabilities Committee (ALCO). This risk is
managed by matching, and where appropriate and necessary, with established risk limits and other
contral procedures.

The Bank’s forecasts and plans take account of the risk of interest rate changes and are prepared and
stressed accordingly, in line with PRA guidance.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of direct or indirect loss arising from a wide variety of causes associated with
the Bank’s processes, personnel, technology and infrastructure, and from external factors other than
credit, market and liquidity risks, such as those arising from legal and regulatory requirements and
generally accepted standards of corporate behaviour. Operational risk arises from all of the Bank's
operations.

The Bank's objective is to manage operational risk so as to balance the avoidance of financial losses and
damage to the Bank's reputation with overall cost effectiveness and innovation. Bank policy requires
compliance with all applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

The Board of Directors has delegated responsibility for operational risk to its Risk Committee, which is
responsible for the development and management of controls to address operational risk.

Compliance with standards is supported ‘by a programme of periodic reviews undertaken by Internal
Audit. ,

The results of Internal Audit reviews are discussed in the Audit & Compliance Committee.
Capital risk management

The Bank's objective in. managing its capital is to maintain appropriate levels of capital to support its
business strategy and meet regulatory requirements, including holding capital for undrawn credit lines.

The Bank’s capital resources are comprised of 100% CET1 regulatory capital. The total capital ratio sits
at 23.18% (unaudited) (2017: 59.78%) and the Bank's regulatory capital requirements for CET1, Tier 1
and total capital are comfortably met. The Bank also holds capital for undrawn credit lines.

The Bank manages its capital under Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and Capital Requirements
Directive (together referred to as CRDIV). The framework is enforced by the Prudential Regulatory
Authority (PRA). The PRA sets and monitors the Bank's capital requirements.

The PRA's approach to the measurement of capital adequacy is primarily based on monitoring the
relationship of the capital resources requirement to available capital resources. The regulator sets
Individual Capital Guidance (ICG) for each bank in excess of the minimum resources requirement of 8%.
A key input to the ICG setting process is the Bank's Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process
(ICAAP).

The Bank's policy is to maintain a strong capital base to maintain investor and market confidence and to
sustain the future development of the business.
