The Directors present their Strategic Report for the year ended 31 December 2022.

Principal Activity
The principal activity of the Bank is the provision of finance in respect of instalment credit agreements. The Bank
accepts retail and SME deposits, with loans focused on small-to-medium sized enterprises.

Business Review
The focus of the Bank during 2022 has been on securing new investment to support its long-term viability.

An investor was identified during the year and their commitment to the business was demonstrated by an initial
investment of £2 million in August 2022.

The Bank has undertaken a detailed strategic review of its activities with the benefit of anticipated future investment.
This updated strategic review resulted in.an updated 5 year Regulatory Business Plan setting out a profitable and
sustainable growth trajectory for the Bank underpinned by ongoing support from its new investor.

This process has required a ‘Change in Control’ submission to the regulatory authorities (PRA and FCA). This was
approved by the PRA in March 2023 with legal completion on 1 April 2023. The Change in Control has resulted in
a £20m capital inflow into the Bank. This capital inflow will enable the Bank to restart lending activities on a much
larger scale than historically.

Bank lending during 2022 has been restricted as Board and management have focused on both capital
conservation and securing incremental capital supply through the new investment.

In addition to securing new investment there has been significant focus on developing the infrastructure of the Bank.
There has been ongoing investment in technology across both Bank operations and financial systems. This
investment will position the Bank well to grow following incoming capital.

Climate Change

The Bank and its Board are fundamentally committed to understanding and minimising the Bank’s carbon footprint
and helping its customers to do the same, supporting the transition to a low-carbon economy. The Bank has
embedded Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) considerations into its business processes. The Board
takes its ESG responsibilities very seriously providing sustainable social infrastructure, achieving a sustainability
ambition of new lending to be green/transition lending and in understanding its exposure to ESG risk.

The Bank continues to embed climate change risk across all its operations, especially credit risk management.
Climate change offers many opportunities for the Bank to differentiate its product offering, as a result there will be
significant demand for our joan products to upgrade many assets within the property sector which currently do not
meet minimum EPC gradings.

Given the relatively smail size of the Bank’s balance sheet, together with its small physical footprint (both in terms
of office space occupied and number of staff) the Bank presently has minimal exposure to climate-related financial
risks. The climate-based strategy is still evolving, with an analysis of the qualitative and quantitative climate risk of
the balance sheet.

The Bank will continue to develop its climate change strategy following the conclusion of the change in control
process in 2023 and subsequent recommencement of lending.

The Bank will endeavour to promote and fund energy efficient properties, with the development of products which
support owners/landlords to improve the energy efficiency of their properties. In addition, the Bank will assess the
potential to develop carbon offsetting savings products.

From an operational perspective the Bank will continue to identify opportunities to reduce its environmental impact
by moving to use e-documentation for its customers. The transition to nCino/Mambu systems has helped the Bank
to significantly reduce the amount of paper and physical documents which it generates.

Lending policies have been developed to give due consideration to climate risk to assess each applicant's appetite
for, and awareness of, climate risk.

Risk management within the Bank is ultimately owned by the Board of Directors although, on a day-to-day basis, it
is delegated to the Executive Management team. The Bank takes a conservative approach to risk management,
having a low-risk appetite for all risk types, apart from Credit Risk, for which its appetite is moderate, operating

exposure (e.g., fraud), but that instances can and do occur. The Bank therefore takes active steps to minimise the
possibilities of such incidents by strong controls to address the risk. The Bank monitors its overall risk profile closely
via its governance structure to ensure that the Bank always remains within Risk Appetite, in alignment with its

strategy.

During 2022 further enhancements have been made to the Risk Management Framework, which covers the key
risk categories and details the approach towards: identification, controls, reporting, appetite, governance etc., of
the risks the Bank faces. The Bank’s management and oversight of risk were further strengthened by the

Operational Risk

The Bank maintains a low appetite for operational risk with limited appetite for unexpected losses, disruption or
customer detriment caused by operational risk events. It aims to minimise incidents and losses arising from
operational risk issues by maintaining resilient infrastructure, including robust systems, employing and training the
competent people with a good cultural fit, minimising the impact of external events and having a framework in place
to ensure operational risks are captured, monitored and mitigated, with lessons learned from mistakes.

There is clear first line ownership of operational risks, review and challenge by the second line and assurance from
the third line. Focus is maintained on key risks, including outsourcing, operational resilience, people, cyber and
technology risks. Key risk indicators are in place to escalate issues to senior management and the Board, regular
reviews are undertaken and operational risk events are captured, recorded and reviewed ensuring sufficient
controls are in place to address the risks. The framework has been strengthened and ongoing enhancements are
being undertaken to ensure that the Bank’s Operational Risk Framework is in line with all regulatory requirements
and leading practices.

IT Risk

The migration of the underlying data to new systems was completed in 2022. The Bank maintains a low appetite
for operational resilience risk. The Bank will ensure that systems and operational capabilities are stable and resilient
and it has sufficient preventative measures in place to reduce the risk of a service failure as well as having the
‘capabilities, in terms of people, processes and organisational culture, to adapt and recover if required. This includes
having effective business continuity and disaster recovery plans in place and ensuring rapid and effective
communications with the people most affected by a business disruption, especially its customer base.

Liquidity Risk

The Bank has an established liquidity policy which provides a framework to minimise the risk to liquidity. The
objectives of the policy are to maintain adequate liquidity to meet known and anticipated withdrawal, loan advances
and operating costs, as well as maintaining adequate liquidity to cover unexpected cash flow imbalances. The Bank
maintains a low-risk appetite for liquidity and funding risk. The Bank has maintained high levels of liquidity during
the year. The Bank monitors liquidity daily to ensure it has sufficient liquid assets available to meet maturing
liabilities and the Bank maintains a highly liquid balance sheet.

The Bank holds sufficient liquid assets to meet liabilities as they fall due ensuring that satisfactory liquidity coverage
and loan to deposit ratios are maintained.

During 2022 the Board actively focused on maintaining surplus liquidity and holding High Quality Liquid Assets
(HQLA) in Treasury Bills to address liquidity risk. The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) is well above the regulatory
minimum and assesses. 30-day net cash outflows as a proportion of HQLA.

Credit Risk

Given the nature of its business, Credit Risk is one of the principal risks facing the Bank. The lending portfolio is
closely monitored via several detailed metrics, including concentration, breaches and exceptions, asset quality, and
credit risk indicators. Stress testing is employed to ensure that sufficient capital is maintained.

Birmingham Bank's primary exposure to credit risk arises through its loans and advances to customers. Birmingham
Bank deals with counterparties of good credit standing. Its policy in relation to its lending activities is to require,
where appropriate, suitable security to be provided prior to the disbursement of loans.

The Bank conducts annual reviews to ensure monitoring throughout the facility lifecycle. The Bank closely monitors
nonperforming loans, with detailed provisioning requirements and procedures.

Regulatory Risk

The Bank maintains a low appetite for legal, compliance and regulatory risk. Whilst it recognises that operational
errors can occur, the Bank maintains zero tolerance for reportable breaches of regulations, regulatory policy
breaches, breaches of applicable laws, late responses to regulatory requests or gifts/hospitality policy breaches.
Whilst accepting the possibility that minor infrequent risk events may occur, the Bank strives to ensure that it always
remains within the law and regulation. Processes are in place to ensure material breaches would be investigated,
identified and reported to the Risk Committee in a timely manner, rule changes are implemented within the
applicable regulatory timelines and staff operate within the Bank’s documented policies and controls and, where
applicable, industry guidelines. Whilst the Bank recognises that operational errors can occur, it has no appetite for
regulatory risk.

Ensuring compliance with all applicable regulatory requirements in a fast-changing landscape is a challenge to
which the Bank devotes considerable resources, and the Compliance Framework is continually under review to
ensure that it meets all requirements and is in line with leading industry practices.

The Bank has a proactive relationship with the UK regulators, and the Board continues to ensure regulatory
compliance is always maintained.

Capital Risk

The Bank maintains a low appetite for Capital Adequacy Risk. Its priority is to maintain a capital surplus above total
capital requirements sufficient to absorb any unexpected losses and costs without using regulatory buffers and
ensure that its capital base can support a growing and maturing book across the economic cycle, allowing for
". potential downturns, maintaining a capita! surplus buffer over minimum regulatory requirements.

Part of Birmingham Bank's capital management activity is the consideration of capital adequacy which is reported
to the Board and its Committees at every meeting. This review includes an assessment of the Bank's capital for

Taxation Risk

The Bank maintains a low-risk appetite for tax risk. The Bank has zero appetite for breaches of tax laws or the
HMRC Code of Practice for Banks. The Bank undertakes tax planning and assumes tax risk only where it is
consistent with the aims and purposes of its business operations. The Bank is transparent and open in its dealings
with all tax authorities and considers any tax position or arrangement with full’regard to its corporate social
responsibility. To mitigate tax risk the Bank uses expert external advisors to minimise tax risks and provide ad hoc
advice where needed. The Bank has a limited exposure to tax risk.

Interest Rate Risk

The Bank generally provides loans and advances to customers on a fixed interest rate, usually over a three-year
term and has variable interest rates on customer deposit accounts, with some fixed rates for longer-term
commitments. Some exposure to interest rate risk is mitigated by interest rate cap agreements in place with its
bankers.

Market Risk

Market risk is generally comprised of interest rate risk, currency risk and other price risk. Birmingham Bank conducts
its transactions in Sterling and does not deal in derivatives or commodity markets, except for two basic interest rate
hedging contracts as detailed in note 18. In the Bank’s case, market risk is limited to Interest Rate Risk in the
Banking Book which is monitored via Key Risk Indicators.

Internal Control
The Board of Directors are responsible for the Bank’s risk management systems and internal controls.

Corporate Governance

The Bank has a well-established corporate governance structure. The Board supports the principles of good
corporate governance as set out in the UK Corporate Governance Code. Whilst the Bank is exempt from several
provisions due to it not being a listed entity, and its overall size in terms of employee numbers, it has reviewed the
requirements and will ensure that its governance processes continue to follow best practice.

The Board believes its existing governance processes are appropriate for the current size and structure of the Bank.

Structure of Board and Board Committees

The Board has overall responsibility for the operations of the Bank and is comprised of four independent non-
executive Directors (INEDs). The non-executives were complemented by two executive Directors during 2022: the
Chief Executive Officer and the Chief Financial Officer.

The Board has its own Terms of Reference and has specific committees appointed by it for the purposes of
Nominations & Remuneration, Audit, Risk, and Credit. Each of these has its own Terms of Reference.

To ensure independence, and reduce the potential for conflict of interest, the sub-committees are each comprised
entirely of INEDs, although individual executive Directors and others attend either as a matter of course or when
requested to provide advice and guidance. An INED chairs each of the Board Committees.

Primary Responsibilities of the Board

While the day-to-day operation of the Bank is delegated to specific individual executives as managers, the Board,
appointed by the shareholders to monitor and govern the Bank's operations, is legally responsible for safeguarding
the interests of depositors and shareholders’ investments. Although the Board does not manage the Bank, one of
its foremost duties is to recruit and retain suitable management. The Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer,
Chief Risk Officer, Chief Operating Officer and Chief Governance Officer are the key roles and form the Executive
Committee. The Board appraises the Chief Executive Officer and provides oversight and agreement to the
appointment of other senior managers. In conjunction with the Bank's senior management team, the Board is
responsible for formulating broad priorities, goals, and strategies for the Bank. The formulation of clear objectives
and policies supplies a framework for the Chief Executive Officer to work within.

The Board both lays out the Bank's goals and monitors the progress against these. One of its main duties in this
capacity is to limit the Bank's exposure to excessive risk of all kinds, including legal, reputational and financial. By
managing risk judiciously, the Board tries to maintain a balance between enterprise and caution. The Board also
ensures that the Bank complies with all applicable statutes and regulations.

This is achieved by monitoring compliance with regulatory and policy requirements via the Bank’s compliance and
audit functions which inform the Board of how the Bank is being run. The audit operations are not limited to financial
matters and encompass the Bank’s structure and operations. The Board is also responsible for commissioning
audits to provide independent assessment and assurance of the Bank.

Responsibilities and Requirements of Independent Non-Executive Directors

The essential role of the INEDs is to provide independent assurance to the Bank’s shareholders that the business
is being conducted in such a manner as to protect the interests of the Bank's depositors and to comply with the
Principles for Business of the Regulator. This responsibility is discharged via oversight of, and appropriate challenge
to, the Bank’s senior management via the structure of the Bank’s Board and subcommittees. Part of the process
for selection is to ensure the INEDs are familiar with the regulatory principles and practices and to maintain their
knowledge of them.

Responsibilities and Requirements of Executive Directors

The executive Directors are responsible for the day-to-day operation of the Bank, supported by the senior
management team. Together, they form the Executive Committee. This is in part effected via policies and
procedures developed with the approval of the Board (directly or indirectly through committees and sub-
committees), partly through the Executive Committee and partly by the discharge of duties as specified within
individual job descriptions.

Chair and Chief Executive

The offices of the Chair and Chief Executive Officer are distinct and held by different people. The main role of the
Chair is to lead the Board and to ensure that it operates effectively. The Chief Executive's role is to put into effect
the strategies agreed by the Board and the general operational management of the Bank.

Compliance with the Senior Managers Regime
The PRA define a set of Prescribed Responsibilities and Overall Responsibilities, which must be allocated to a
senior manager performing a Senior Management Function.

Tax

Current tax represents the amount of tax payable or receivable in respect of the taxable profit (or loss) for the current
or past reporting periods. It is measured at the amount expected to be paid or recovered using the tax.rates and
laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end date. The financial year 2022 experienced
tax losses, so no tax charge for 2022 is presented in the financia! statements. There is a prior year over provision
arising for the carry back of prior year losses.

Deferred tax represents the future tax consequences of transactions and events recognised in the financial
statements of current and previous periods. It is recognised in respect of all timing differences, with certain
exceptions. Timing differences are differences between taxable profits and total comprehensive income as stated
in the financial statements that arise from the inclusion of income and expense in tax assessments in periods
different from those in which they are recognised in the financial statements. Unrelieved tax losses and other
deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they will be recovered against the
reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.

Deferred tax is measured using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year
end date that are expected to apply to the reversal of timing differences.

An assessment of the deferred tax asset in respect of losses was completed. A deferred tax asset has not been
included in the financial statements.

Deferred income - in respect of hire purchase and leasing contracts

The provision for unearned finance charges included in the Statement of Financial Position has been calculated by
attributing, under the actuarial method, a proportion of the total finance charges to hire purchase instalments and
finance lease rentals receivable after the year end date. Early settlement rebates are calculated on an actuarial
basis.
