Chairman's Statement
The 2025/26 financial year marks an important personal milestone for me, as this has been

my first year serving as Chairman of StreamBank, following my appointment in November
2025 when I succeeded John Reed. I feel privileged to take on this responsibility at such a
pivotal stage in the Bank’s development and excited about the future direction of the
organisation.

This year has been one of consolidation and strengthening for StreamBank. Against a
challenging and uneven market backdrop, the business has continued to mature, transitioning
further from early-stage growth into a more established, resilient specialist property and
savings bank. While wider market conditions have influenced the overall pace of growth, the
focus has remained firmly on building a sustainable institution underpinned by strong
governance, discipline and long-term thinking rather than short-term volume.

A particularly important development during the year has been the strengthening of the
Board. I am delighted to welcome Mike Hudson, who assumes the role of Chair of the Risk
Committee, and Simon Lindley as Independent Non-Executive Directors. Both bring a wealth
of experience from banking and closely aligned financial services organisations, and their
appointments significantly enhance the depth of expertise, challenge and governance
capability at Board level. I would like to formally welcome them to the Board and thank them
for the contribution they have already begun to make.

From an oversight and assurance perspective, the business has delivered strongly. During the
year, StreamBank has successfully completed a number of key audits, including Culture,
Operational Resilience, Complaints and Lending. These outcomes provide important
independent assurance over the quality of the Bank’s control environment, governance
framework and organisational maturity, and reflect the strength of the systems, processes
and leadership in place.

During the year, the Bank operated against a backdrop of continued economic uncertainty
and geopolitical volatility, which had a pronounced impact on the UK housing market.
Transaction volumes slowed, with properties taking longer to sell, and certain regions
experienced year-on-year house price declines. In response, the Bank deliberately tightened
its credit risk appetite to protect asset quality, which, by design, constrained new lending and
resulted in more modest loan book growth. At the same time, the existing back book
continued to be affected by these market conditions, leading to increased provisioning. The
higher provisions resulted in a loss of £3.8m before tax for the year. While this outcome is
clearly disappointing, the actions taken have strengthened the Bank’s risk position and leave
it better placed to deliver improved financial performance in the year ahead.

Although the rate of growth during the year has been influenced by market conditions, the
quality of delivery across the organisation has remained exceptionally high. Service standards
and customer experience continue to be a defining strength of StreamBank, reflected in
consistently excellent customer feedback and a Trustpilot rating of 4.7 out of 5. This is a
powerful indicator that the business is delivering not only commercially, but operationally and
culturally.

Business Model Review

StreamBank is a specialist property finance bank focused on delivering pragmatic lending
solutions supported by experienced underwriting and strong intermediary relationships. Our
model combines specialist credit expertise with efficient processes, enabling us to provide
specialist finance to property professionals across the UK.

Relationships remain central to our approach. We work closely with brokers and customers to
understand individual circumstances and provide solutions that support their objectives.
While technology supports efficient processing and customer access, decision-making
remains grounded in experienced human underwriting.

StreamBank provides short-term property lending alongside retail savings products that fund
our loan book. Our aim is to operate as a trusted specialist lender, a responsible employer
and a valued partner to the broker community.

Collaboration across the organisation is key to delivering consistent outcomes for customers
and maintaining disciplined growth.

Short-Term Lending
StreamBank focuses on bridging, supporting property investors, landlords, developers and

homeowners undertaking property purchases, refurbishment projects and planning activity.

Our lending supports improvements to the UK housing stock by enabling refurbishment and
redevelopment projects that bring properties back into productive use or improve their quality
and energy performance.

Loans are introduced exclusively through professional brokers and structured as short-term
facilities with interest typically rolled up to maturity. This structure provides borrowers with
flexibility while they complete sales, refinancing or asset repositioning strategies, including in
the context of chain breaks for homeowners.

Engagement with brokers remains a key driver of product development. Feedback from the
intermediary market continues to inform enhancements to our lending proposition.

Savings Account, providing customers with a longer-term savings option while further
supporting the stability of the Bank’s funding profile.

Chief Executive Officer’s Statement

2025 has been a year of progress and building momentum for StreamBank. The wider market
has been mixed, shaped by uncertainty around interest rates, transaction volumes and
valuations alongside the political backdrop for much of the year. Yet within specialist lending,
activity has remained resilient, driven by the continued importance of bridging transactions
to the UK property market. Against that backdrop, StreamBank has performed well.

Since our launch in 2023, our strategy has been clear: to build a sustainable specialist bank,
not to chase volume for volume’s sake. During 2025/26, that disciplined approach has
delivered tangible results. We increased our new lending volume by 24.7% year on year
(£21.9m), our transaction volumes increased by 50.9% year on year reflecting a lower
average transaction size, and we continued to grow our gross loan book by 3.2% year on
year (£5.1m). Importantly, this growth has been accompanied by improving asset quality,
demonstrating the strength of our credit discipline and risk framework. We have taken
significant one-off provisions against legacy loans during the year; we are not expecting this
to continue in financial year 2027. The higher provisions resulted in a loss of £3.8m before
tax for the year.

One of the most encouraging indicators of progress has been the continued expansion and
engagement of our intermediary base. We are seeing broader participation, stronger
relationships, and a growing level of repeat business — a clear signal that our proposition is
resonating in the market. Trust is built through consistency, delivery and reliability, and I am
proud of the reputation the business continues to build.

Alongside commercial growth, we have invested heavily in our foundations. Over the past
year, we have strengthened our operation through process refinement, technology
investment and cultural development. A key milestone has been the transformation
programme within our banking platform in partnership with Mutual Vision. This programme
is delivering a significantly improved user experience, more efficient internal processes, and
the introduction of APIs that reduce friction across the lending and savings journey. These
changes are not simply about technology — they are about scalability, resilience and service
quality as the business continues to grow.

Throughout 2025, market sentiment has been cautiously optimistic. While fragility remains,
particularly around transaction volumes, confidence is gradually returning as rates ease and
expectations stabilise. As we moved into 2026, we are closely monitoring transaction velocity
in residential and semi-commercial assets, the pace of recovery, and evolving borrower
behaviour as confidence improves. For specialist lenders, opportunities remain, but discipline
will continue to matter more than ever.

Our priorities for 2026/27 are clear and consistent: to grow in a controlled and disciplined
way, deepen and strengthen intermediary relationships, and maintain the high standards of
risk, service, governance and delivery that underpin our proposition. Success will not be
measured purely in volumes. It will be reflected in the quality of our loan book, the strength
of our partnerships, and our reputation as a dependable, specialist bank that performs
through different market cycles.

StreamBank is entering its next phase from a position of strength; commercially,
operationally and culturally. With a strong platform, a committed team, and a clear strategic
focus, we are well positioned to continue building a sustainable, high-quality specialist bank
for the long term.

11 June 2026

Chief Financial Officer’s Report

It has been a challenging year for the Bank, with the uncertain economic environment and
elevated impairment charges from our legacy loan book adversely affecting our results.
This led to the Bank posting a loss for the 2026 financial year. Profits before expected
credit losses have remained resilient, with stable margins and disciplined cost control.
Impairment charges for the year totalled £6.4m (2025:£1.7m), predominantly relating to
legacy loans originated pre financial year 2024, under credit criteria that are no longer in
place. The increase reflects a deterioration in underlying asset value in relation to the
default loan and prudent reassessment of recovery expectations considering the current
macroeconomic environment.

Importantly, loans originated under our current credit framework have performed in line
with expectations, with arrears and early warning indicators remaining stable. New
origination volumes are characterised by stronger borrower quality and tighter risk
controls.

Despite the continued uncertain world and UK economic landscape and the highly
competitive Bridging loan market, we will aim to return to profitability in financial year
2027 by growing our loan book, whilst keeping a tight control of operating costs,
maintaining strict credit underwriting criteria, and optimising our funding costs. The Bank
has invested in its sales team to drive the growth in the lending book over the coming
year, meaning we are confident and well-placed to return to monthly profitability by the
end of financial year 2027.

Risk Management

As a financial services provider in the UK managing risk effectively is fundamental to our
strategy, long term resilience and to operating successfully day to day. This is supported
by a business model with a medium to low risk profile.

Risk Management Framework
The Bank’s Risk Management Framework is an integral part of the StreamBank business
model to support:

e Delivering against its strategy within an appropriate risk culture.

e Building greater resilience to organisational threats.

e Protecting its customers from unfair outcomes.

The Bank’s strategy and business model is underpinned by clearly defined risk governance,
ensuring alignment with the Board’s appetite for risk. StreamBank operates entirely within
the UK financial services market and only takes on risks, which it has the capability to
understand and manage effectively. A Risk Management Framework, supported by three
lines of defence governance model, ensures strong risk awareness, assessment,
monitoring and management across all principal and emerging risks. Risks are managed
within the risk appetite set by the Board and stress testing is undertaken to ensure that
the capital and liquidity of the Bank would enable it to survive severe but plausible market-
wide and firm specific stresses.

Risk Culture

StreamBank has a strong culture of risk awareness and control and actively monitors and

manages the risks of its business, as well as emerging industry risks which may have an

impact on those activities, through its Risk Management Framework.

This culture is embedded through clear expectations of roles and responsibilities and

supported by defined risk appetite and risk management policies, together with effective -
training for all levels of staff. The Board supports a culture of openness and transparency,

encouraging issues to be raised as identified and providing the necessary resources and

support to remediate. This is underpinned by an emphasis on good customer outcomes

and sustainability of the business.

Three Lines of Defence Model

The Bank operates a three lines of defence model which clearly sets out responsibilities
for the management and oversight of risk. The Board retains ultimate responsibility for
risk management. The three lines of defence model is summarised below:

First Line of Defence — Day-to-Day Risk Management

The first line of defence has responsibility for implementation of the Bank’s strategy and
for the management of risk across the organisation and comprises executive committees,
management and staff.

Second Line of Defence — Risk Oversight

The second line of defence is responsible for providing independent oversight and
challenge of activities undertaken by the first line and provides guidance on risks relevant
to the strategy. This is provided through the Risk & Compliance function, which is led by
the Chief Risk Officer (CRO) who reports to the CEO and has an independent reporting line
to the Chairman of the Board Risk Committee. The Risk function is not customer facing
and has no responsibility for business targets or performance.

Credit risk is the probability of financial loss resulting from a borrower failing to meet its
contractual obligations in accordance with agreed terms. The risk of default on the Bank’s
loan book is the principal source of financial risk, as lending represents the Bank’s primary
source of income.

Probability of Default

PD estimates represent the likelihood of a borrower defaulting on its financial obligation. The
Z-model framework is employed to estimate PDs on our loan portfolio. The model includes
an economic response model to quantify the impact of macroeconomic forecasts and the
internal scorecard to quantify obligor level likelihood of default. Risk characteristics that feed
into the PD model framework include current and past information related to borrowers, and
future economic forecasts.

Exposure at Default
EAD represents the amounts expected to be owed at the time of default, given the nature of
bridging lending, these amounts are primarily focused in the following 12-month period.

Loss Given Default
LGD represents an expectation of the extent of loss on a defaulted exposure after taking into
account cash recoveries, including the value of collateral held and other credit risk mitigants.

The calculation of LGD across Stages 1 and 2 is based on the difference between the contractual
cash flows due and those that the lender would expect to receive, including from any collateral
as usually expressed as a % of the EAD.

The aim of the LGD model is to forecast the LGD for the lifetime of the account. The “workout
framework methodology” will be used with the following key LGD input components to be

e Percentage of cure: 20% with loss given cure of 0%

e Time to sell: 12/ 24 months

e Forced Sale Discount: 20%

« LGD floor: 5%

21 ~=Financial risk management objectives and policies

Risk management

The risk management approach encompasses the requirements for identifying, assessing,
managing, monitoring and reporting on risk.

The evaluation of the various risks and the setting of policy is carried out through the Bank’s
ERC which reports to the Board Risk Committee, which ensures adherence to the Bank’s Risk
Management Policy and Framework.

Risk management objectives

Risk is inherent in all aspects of the Bank’s business. The Risk Management Framework is in
place to ensure that all material risks faced by the Bank have been identified and measured,
and that appropriate controls are in place to ensure that each risk is mitigated to an
acceptable degree.

In the normal course of its business, the Bank is exposed to credit risk, liquidity risk, interest
rate risk, conduct risk and operational risk. These are discussed in more detail in sections a)
to e) below.

(a) Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk that borrowers or a counterparty will be unable or unwilling to meet a
commitment that they have entered with the Bank.

The maximum credit risk as at 315' March 2026 is the carrying value recognised on the
Balance Sheet as disclosed in the table in note 12.

Credit risk within the lending portfolio is defined as a borrower's inability to repay or service
their debt obligations.

The Bank’s accounting policy in terms of determining significant increase in credit risk is
disclosed in note 1.

For all lending, the Bank takes security in the form of a legal charge over the property against
which loans are advanced. The Bank’s low risk approach to new business lending is reflected
in the loan to value profile of the property book. The Bank manages its credit risk through its
Credit Committee. Lending Committee and Assets and Liabilities Committee. Regular credit
exposure reports are produced which include information on credit and property underwriting,
large exposures, asset concentration and levels of bad debt provisioning.

The primary driver of credit risk within the treasury assets portfolio, which comprises deposits
with other banks, government bonds and debt securities is counterparty default, meaning a
counterparty can no longer repay its obligations. Only instruments issued by counterparties
with a strong credit rating at the point of purchase are held. The Bank intends to hold its
treasury assets to maturity and is therefore not directly affected by market risk.

ECL Provisioning
ECLs are the discounted product of PD, EAD, and LGD, defined as follows:

- PD is the likelihood of a borrower defaulting on its financial obligation either in the next 12-
months or over the remaining lifetime of the obligation.

In order to generate Lifetime PD, StreamBank uses the “Z-Risk Framework” methodology

which translate through the cycle (TTC) PDs into point in time (PIT) PDs. These are then
adjusted for the future based on a forecast of an appropriate credit cycle index (CCI).

b) Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Bank will encounter difficulty in realising assets or otherwise
raising funds to meet commitments as they fall due. The Bank manages its liquidity risk
through its Assets and Liabilities Committee and monitors its liquidity position daily and has
adopted a policy to ensure that it has adequate resources to enable it to conduct its normal
business activities without interruption. The maturity analysis of assets and liabilities is
disclosed in note 20 to the financial staternents of the Bank.

The customer deposit base represents a stable source of funding due to the number and
range of depositors.

The Board has approved a Liquidity Risk Management policy that sets out the liquidity
requirements with which the Bank must comply. The principal liquidity risk mitigants used by
management are:
e A buffer of highly liquid assets (comprising high quality UK government bonds) which can
meet cash requirements;
e Cash reserves with the BoE.
e Cash resources held at other financial institutions.

c) Interest rate risk

Interest rate risk is the risk that arises when there is an imbalance between the maturity
dates of rate-sensitive assets, liabilities and commitments. The Bank manages its interest
rate risk through its Assets and Liabilities Committee. The Bank’s policy is to maintain
interest rate risk at a controlled level within limits set by the Board. Due to the nature of
our assets and liabilities the interest rate re-pricing profile of assets and liabilities are
materially naturally hedged.

d) Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of economic loss from systemic failure, human error and fraud
(control failures) or external events, which result in unexpected or indirect loss to the Bank.
When controls fail to perform, operational risks can cause damage to reputation, have legal

or regulatory implications or can lead to financial loss. The Bank cannot expect to eliminate
all operational risks but by initiating a rigorous control framework and by monitoring and
responding to potential risks, the Bank is able to manage the risks. Controls include effective
segregation of duties, access controls, authorisation and reconciliation procedures, staff
education and assessment processes, including the use of internal audit.

e) Conduct risk

Conduct risk is the risk that the Bank’s behaviour results in poor outcomes for customers.
The Bank is exposed to this risk by virtue of the markets in which it chooses to operate. The
ERC has overall responsibility for implementing and monitoring principles, frameworks,
policies and limits. The Committee is responsible for managing risk decisions and monitoring
risk levels which it reports to the Board Risk Committee.

f) Macroeconomic Economic risk

Macroeconomic Economic risk is the risk of economic loss because of a change in the economic
conditions that the Bank and its customers face. Unfavourable movements in the economic
conditions could heighten the risks that the Bank is exposed to, with credit risk being the
largest risk given the nature of the Bank’s lending portfolios. The ERC is responsible for
managing risk decisions and monitoring risk levels which it reports to the Board Risk
Committee,

g) Foreign Exchange Risk
The Bank does not hold any financial assets or liabilities denominated in foreign currencies.

22 #£=Capital risk management

StreamBank is supervised by the PRA, as a UK authorised bank. This requires it to satisfy the
liquidity and capital requirements as prescribed by the PRA.

StreamBank uses the standardised approach for credit risk. It is required to demonstrate to
the PRA that it can withstand liquidity, and capital stresses and carries out regular reviews of
the adequacy of its capital to support its current and future activities, including during periods
of stress. The Bank conducts an ICAAP, at least annually, which is approved by the Board.
This is used to assess the Bank's capital adequacy and to determine the level of capital
required to support the future development of the business as set out in the strategic plan.

The ICAAP considers all the Bank's material risks and includes board-approved stress
scenarios which are intended, as a minimum, to meet regulatory requirements. The ICAAP is
used by the PRA to set the Bank’s Total Capital Requirements (TCR).

The Bank’s capital resource requirements are calculated based on the Capital Requirements

Directive IV (CRD IV) Capital Requirements Regulation regulatory framework as implemented

by the PRA, namely:

- Pillar 1-based on a Standardised Approach for credit risk, operational risk and market
risk;

- Pillar 2-set by the PRA via the TCR to address those risks not covered under Pillar 1.

The Board is ultimately responsible for capital management and monitors the capital position
of the Bank at each board meeting through the receipt of MI which sets out the Bank’s current
and forecast capital position, based on the methodology adopted within its ICAAP. This means
that the Bank will:

i) Maintain a level of capital at least equal to the minimum amount set by the PRA in the TCR,
and;
it) Hold all its capital in the form of CET1 and CET2 capital.
