Strategic Report

The Directors present their strategic report for Persia International Bank pic for the year ended 31 March 2018.
Introduction

Persia International Bank PLC (“PIB” or “the Bank”) is an authorised institution under the Financial Services and Markets
Act 2000. Under normal operating conditions the principal activity of the Bank is the provision of commercial banking
services to its customers. The EU/UK financial sanctions imposed on the Bank on 27 July 2010 were lifted on 16 January
2016. However, normal banking activities have not yet been resumed owing to continuing difficulties in arranging UK
clearing facilities and in establishing correspondent relationships with international banks. In June 2016, the Bank joined
the Target 2 payments system through the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Business Review

Historically, the Bank offered a comprehensive service in trade finance and credit facilities to its individual and corporate
customers. Under trade finance, the Bank used to receive and advise letters of credit, add confirmation and provide short
term credit facilities by discounting the bills drawn under them. PIB also extended credit facilities to its corporate
customers either on bilateral terms or as part of a syndicate. Iranian corporate customers constituted a major portion of
the customer base of the Bank. The Bank had been performing well since its inception, meeting all its short and medium
term goals until 2010. A strong customer base was built up with a robust trade finance and corporate credit portfolio.

The first overseas branch was opened in August 2005 at Dubai international Financial Centre (DIFC), which further
expanded the Bank’s trade finance capabilities. However, the Dubai branch operation was suspended by the Dubai
regulator (DFSA) in March 2010 and the branch has remained inactive since then.

The balance sheet total assets of the Bank is €184,318k, lower than in the previous year owing mainly to scheduled
repayments of customer loans, and the return of customer and bank deposits. Trade finance activities ceased as of July
2011, but partly resumed during the year 2017-18. Full scale resumption of trade finance activities is possible only when

the issues surrounding correspondent banking relationships are resolved.

in the year 2018-19, the Bank will continue to increase its money market placements with Iranian banks, and also to
extend trade finance re-financing facilities to selected Iranian banks, as part of an exercise to diversify its business.
Money market placements with banks in Iran offer some returns, and will continue until credit expansion takes place.
Returns from re-financing facilities are significantly less but undertaken with the expectations that they will facilitate new

businesses in course of time and establish relationships with banks and corporate customers in Iran.

Financial Performance during the year 2017-18

The interest income as at 31 March 2018 decreased by 36.48% from €4,245k in the previous year to €2,696k in the
current year. The Bank has reported a Loss of €5,549k. The reason for the loss is that there has been no new major
business in the recent years (and most of the loans have been repaid).The Bank's income is presently derived mainly
from interest earned on money-market placements, on loans arranged prior to the EU sanctions, some rental income
from the property sub-let and a small fee income from trade finance activities. Bank Markazi, the Central bank of tran,
has stopped paying interest on the nostro balances maintained with them since October 2016.

Fee & commission income receivable was €54k and fee & commission payable was €219k. The latter was entirely due
to commission paid on a transfer of euro funds, caused by the Bank’s lack of access to normal GBP and euro
correspondent banking relationships. Other income (which mostly consisted of foreign exchange income) was €245k.

replace money market deposits with corporate loans when the opportunity arises for new lending. Corporate loan
repayments have caused a reduction to €15,512k in loans and advances to customers and the loan portfolio will continue
to wind down until normal banking activities resume and new loans are made.

Risk Assessment and Management

The Bank is exposed to liquidity risk, credit risk, operational risk, market risk, interest rate risk and currency risk (see
Note 19 for further details on the management of these risks). The Directors have also identified the lack of GBP and
euro clearing facilities, regulatory compliance, IT, political and succession risks as the major risks faced by the Bank in
its present situation. Direct membership of Target 2 has to some extent facilitated euro payments within the euro zone.
During the year 2018-19 the use of Target 2 to make euro payments will be increased as much as possible.

The Board and the management believe that they have taken adequate measures to mitigate these primary risks. Steps
have been initiated to diversify as far as possible GBP payment arrangements and advance payments have been made
to secure essential services and supplies, until participation in the local GBP clearing system is established. The Bank's
overall liquidity position is strong with major liabilities settled and the remaining liabilities manageable. The mismatch in
the maturities of the balance sheet assets and liabilities is not a cause for concern. Though a significant portion of its
short term funds are kept with Bank Markazi and some banks in Iran, the Bank has access to sufficient liquid funds within
the EU to meet its obligations and operating costs for the foreseeable future. The Directors are satisfied that the Bank
will remain a going concern for the foreseeable future and they remain confident regarding its longer term prospects.

The Chief Risk Officer has responsibility for the risk management process.

Financial reporting risks are managed through control activities performed at all levels of the organisation. These activities
include guidelines and instructions, approvals, authorisations, verifications, reconciliations, analytical reviews, and
segregation of duties. The Board has also established a Risk Committee chaired by an Independent Non-executive
Director. This committee (see Note 19 for details) monitors, reviews and updates all risks faced by the Bank internally
and externally. In terms of internal controls and financial reporting, external and internal information is obtained
systematically, and the Bank’s management is provided with relevant information on the Bank's activities. Timely, current
and accessible information relevant for financial reporting purposes is provided to the appropriate functionaries, such as
the Board, the senior management group and the relevant Board committees.

The Board has the overall responsibility for the proper arrangement of internal control and risk management over financial
reporting. The Board has appointed the Audit Committee (members are two Independent Non-executive Directors) to
undertake more specific tasks in relation to the financial reporting process control, and monitoring the efficiency of the
Bank's internal controls and management of issues arising out of IFRS 9 implementation in the Bank. The Audit
Committee also reviews regularly the main features of the internal contro! and risk management systems pertaining to

the financial reporting process.

Brexit

Following the Brexit, the direct membership of Target 2 system to transfer funds within euro and also access to
Bundesbank to maintain High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) for LCR purposes may not be available after 31 March 2019.
However, Management of the Bank is confident of using the services of EU based banks to transfer funds within the euro

zone.

Maintenance of HQLA is essential to conform to the requirements of Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR). At present part of
the funds with Bundesbank under Target 2 is earmarked as HQLA funds. PIB management envisages to open an
investment account for investing in Government Securities in EU and have identified one bank in Switzerland as a
possible custodian for these securities. The investment in the Government Securities in euro zone will constitute HQLA
for the Bank.

Funds in euro to corporate customers in Iran will be made available through book entry from our shareholder banks and
Bank Markazi. Money market placements with banks in Iran will be intermediated by shareholder banks and Bank
Markazi. Similarly repayments from corporate customers and banks in Iran will be through intermediation of shareholder

banks and Bank Markazi.

Purchases and supplies can also be paid in euro with an arrangement with the parties concerned and the correspondent

banks in euro zone,
OFAC sanctions

Following the decision of the US Government to withdraw from the JCPOA, the likelihood of OFAC sanctions to be re-
imposed on PIB is now a certainty. The management is of the view that the impact of OFAC sanctions will be visible but
may not hamper the present business pattern of the Bank. Business wise the primary dealings are in euro and exposure
are to Iranian entities. The non-availability of SWIFT will impact business communications and transfer of funds. This will
be managed through book transfer mechanism with the involvement of fraternal banks in the EU. As regards repayment
of the loan instalments and interest, the management has been advised by the Central Bank of Iran, that such
transactions will be given priority for availability of Foreign Exchange.

America (USA) from the JCPOA, may have a setback on our expectations of resuming normal banking business
operations. -The Bank had managed the full force of the EU, UN and OFAC sanctions in the past and now poised to

Accounting convention

Currency and basis of preparation

The accounts are prepared in Euros, as this is the currency of the primary economic environment in which the

Going concern

The EU sanctions imposed on the Bank were lifted on 16 January 2016 and the HMT licensing regime covering
the sanctions period has ended. The normal banking operations are expected to resume in course of time as
some of the issues surrounding correspondent banking relationships and payment systems are yet to be
resolved. The SWIFT system is in place and operational with banks who have exchanged SWIFT keys with
us. Reuters have not yet resumed operations with the Bank. The Bank expects to function normally once these
critical-functional issues are resolved.

The loan book is of value €15.51m, fully collateralised and also guaranteed by the shareholder banks.

The management has reviewed the situation arising out of the US Government withdrawing from the joint
Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) on 8 May 2018 and is of the view that the impact of OFAC sanctions
re-imposed will be visible but may not hamper the business pattern of the Bank.

The total liquid funds in GBP available in London is about £8.75m as at 31 March 2018 and about £15.226m
as at 31 May 2018. The average administration expenses for one year is about £8m and therefore with the
GBP funds available in UK, it is manageable for the next two financial years.

The euro funds available in euro zone as at 31 May 2018 is about €194.43m. Euro funds can be transferred
from the euro zone to UK through book transfer process.

Brexit as at 31 March 2019 may impact the transfer of funds within the euro zone due to non-availability of
direct membership of Target2. The management is of the view that the risk arising out of Brexit is not material
and can be managed _ with the assistance of fraternal banks in the euro zone.

Having considered the potential impact of the above points, the directors have, at the time of approving the
financial statements, a reasonable expectation that the Bank has adequate resources to continue in
operational existence for the foreseeable future. Thus they continue to adopt the going concern basis of
accounting in preparing the financial statements. Further detail is contained in the Strategic Report on page

Operating segments

An operating segment is a component of the Bank that engages in business activities from which it may earn
revenues and incur expenses. Its operating results are reviewed regularly by the Bank's Management Committee
(which comprises the CEO, CFO and COO and it meets at least once a month) and the Asset Liability Committee
(see Note 25 for details). Decisions about resource allocation are made and the performance can be assessed
using the available discreet financial information. The Chief Executive Officer is the chief operating decision maker
of the Bank.

Interest income and expense

Interest income and expense on financial assets and liabilities measured at amortised cost is recognised in the
statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method, which allocates the income or
expense over the relevant period. The effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash
flows to the carrying value on initial recognition, through the expected life of the financial asset or liability. In respect
of impaired loans where it is probable that interest on the loan will not flow to the entity, interest income is not
recognised in the statement of comprehensive income.

Fee and commission income
Fees receivables which represent a return for services provided are credited to income when the related service is
performed. Fees receivable, which represent a return for risk borne and are in the nature of interest, are taken to
the statement of comprehensive income using the effective interest rate method over the expected life of the
transaction to which they relate.

Provisions
A provision is recognised if, as a result of a past event, the Bank has a present legal or constructive obligation that
can be estimated reliably, and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the

obligation. Provisions are determined by discounting the expected future cash flows at a pre-tax rate that reflects
current market assessments of the time value of money, and where appropriate, the risks specific to the liability.

Financial assets:
Financial assets are classified into the following categories:

e financial assets at fair value through profit or loss: a financial asset is classified as such if it is held
principally for the purpose of selling in the short term. In the current and prior year, the Bank did not
hold any financial assets at fair value through profit or loss;

¢ loans and receivables: are non-derivative financial assets with fixed or determinable payments not
quoted in an active market; and

¢ available for sale: are financial assets not classified in one of the above categories. The Bank did not
hold any financial assets that are available for sale in the current year.

Loans and receivables are initially recognised at fair value, plus directly attributable transaction costs, and
subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest rate method.

Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss are initially and subsequently measured at fair value with
changes in fair value taken to the statement of comprehensive income.

Available for sale investments are non-derivative investments that are designated as available for sale or are not
classified as another category of financial assets. They are carried at fair value. Available for sale financial assets
are initially and subsequently measured at fair value, with changes in fair value recognised directly in equity until
disposal, when the cumulative gain or loss is taken to profit or loss and recognised in the statement of
comprehensive income. Foreign exchange gains and losses, interest income under the effective interest rate
method and impairment losses arising on available-for-sale securities are taken to profit or loss and recognised in
the statement of comprehensive income.

A non-derivative financial asset may be reclassified from the available-for-sale category to the loans and
receivables category if it otherwise would have met the definition of loans and receivables and if the Bank has the
intention and ability to hold that financial asset for the foreseeable future or until maturity.

Financial liabilities and equity

Financial liabilities and equity instruments are initially recognised at fair value; this occurs when the Bank becomes
a party to the contractual provisions of the corresponding instruments. The Bank derecognises financial liabilities
when its contractual obligations are discharged or cancelled or expire. Financial liabilities and equity instruments
are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is
any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the Bank after deducting all of its liabilities. Financial
liabilities comprise amounts due to other banks, customer accounts and subordinated debt liabilities. They are
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. Equity instruments issued by the Bank are
recorded at the proceeds received, net of direct issue costs.

Impairment of financial assets

A financial asset is impaired and impairment losses are incurred if there is objective evidence of impairment, as a
result of events that occurred after initial recognition of the financial asset. The amount of the loss is measured, for
financial assets held at amortised cost and as available-for-sale, as the difference between the carrying amount of
the asset and the value of the revised estimated future cash flows, discounted at the asset's original effective
interest rate. Financial assets are written off only when it is reasonably certain that any future cash flows are
irrecoverable.

Operating lease rentals
Rentals payable under operating leases are accounted for on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

Valuation of financial instruments
The Bank measures fair values using a fair value hierarchy that reflects the significance of the inputs used in
making the measurements.

Level 1: Quoted market price (unadjusted) in an active market for an identical instrument is taken as the fair value.
Level 2: Valuation techniques based on observable inputs, either directly (i.e. as prices) or indirectly (i.e. derived
from prices). This category includes instruments valued using: quoted market prices in active markets for similar
instruments; quoted prices for identical or similar instruments in markets that are considered less than active; or
other valuation techniques where all significant inputs are directly or indirectly observable from market data.
Level 3: Under this category Bank's assumptions about the market and market conditions are used in pricing the
asset and based on the best information available in the circumstances and not merely based on observable market
data (unobservable inputs).

impairment of property, plant and equipment

The carrying amount of the Bank's property, plant and equipment are reviewed at each reporting date to determine

Impairment loss is recognised, if the carrying amount of an asset exceeds its estimated recoverable amount.
Impairment losses are recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income. 4

Taxation
(a) Current income tax

Income tax payable (receivable) is calculated on the basis of the applicable tax law in the respective jurisdiction
and is recognised as an expense (income) for the period except to the extent that current tax related to items that
are charged or credited in other comprehensive income or directly to equity. In these circumstances, current tax is
charged or credited to other comprehensive income or to equity.

(b) Deferred income tax

Deferred income tax is normally provided in full, using the liability method, on temporary differences arising between
the tax bases of assets and liabilities and their carrying amounts in the financial statements. Deferred income tax
is determined using tax rates (and laws) that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the date of the
statement of financial position and are expected to apply when the related deferred income tax asset is realised or
the deferred income tax liability is settled. The tax effects of carry-forwards of unused losses or unused tax credits
are recognised as an asset when it is probable that future taxable profits will be available against which these
losses can be utilised.

Pensions and other post-retirement benefits
The Bank operates a defined contribution scheme, charging contributions to profit or loss within the statement of
comprehensive income as they become payable in accordance with the rules of the scheme.

Foreign currencies

Transactions in foreign currencies are recorded at the rate of exchange at the date of the transaction. A monetary
item is a right to receive or an obligation (contractual or constructive) to deliver a fixed or determinable number of
units of currency. Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies at the reporting date are
reported at the rates of exchange prevailing at that date. Translation differences on monetary items are recognised
in profit or loss within the statement of comprehensive income. Non-monetary assets and liabilities that are
measured at historical cost in a foreign currency are translated using the spot exchange rate as at the date of
recognition.

RISK MANAGEMENT
The following note on Risk Management states the Bank's policy in normal circumstances.

In transacting its normal business the Bank is exposed to the following principal risks: liquidity, credit, operational and market risk.

It Is the responsibility of the Risk Committee ("RC’), a Board Committee, to review and to monitor the Bank's risk appetite and profile, risk management policies and
procedures, and its risk management in practice, and to make recommendations to the Board on these matters. The RC comprises 6 members, an Independent Non-
executive Director as Chairman, the CEO, CFO, COO and one other Independent Non-executive Director and the Chief Risk Officer (CRO). The RC meets at least four
times a year, and from time to time as circumstances may require.

Overall responsibility for monitoring and managing the balance sheet risk is delegated by the Board to its Asset and Liability Committee ("ALCO"). It concerns itself with the
structure of the balance sheet, in particular its capital adequacy, liquidity, funding requirements, and tax capacity and planning, and also with credit, operational and market
tisks. The Committee is responsible for ensuring that the Bank does not exceed its agreed risk appetite, and to that end it receives regular reports of the Bank's
exposures. .

Membership of the ALCO comprises the CEO as Chairman, the CFO, COO, one Independent Non-executive Director, the Financial Controller, Operations Manager,
Treasury Manager, the Chief Risk Officer and Credit Manager as Secretary. ALCO is required to meet once a month and is chaired by the CEO. it can meet more regularly
if required, eg. to consider the impact of significant changes in market situations.The monthly meeting follows a fixed agenda but It also reviews additional material as
necessary, eg. qualitative risks such as reputation or political risk. The agenda includes reports on the four prime risks above, the bank's monthly management accounts
and comparison to budget, the regulatory (PRA) requirements and’ new business, If any. The directors are required to review the ALCO and Risk Committee minutes at
the quarterly board meetings.

Liquidity risk

Being adequately liquid at all times is one of the most significant risks for the Bank. However, due to continuing impediments in establishing correspondent banking
relationships and participating in clearing arrangements, the Bank is unable to function normally and expand its business activities. This has resulted in the Bank holding
net liquid assets of €70.1m in cash and central bank balances including balances with Bundesbank and also about €5.6m in nostro balances with banks primarily in the
euro zone, posing no significant liquidity risk in the near future. Liquidity risk, in normal circumstances is that the bank is unable to meet its financial obligations in a timely
manner as and when they fall due. Liquidity is monitored on a maturity mismatch basis and various stress scenarios are normally tested regularly. The Bank is currently

holding €48.97m as part of its High Quality Liquid Assets with Bundesbank to manage the liquidity risk. The Bank has no access to the wholesale money markets and no
bank will intermediate monay transfers.

Management of liquidity risk

The Bank's liquidity is managed by its treasury function. The Treasurer must ensure that the Bank can meet its obligations to its customers and also fully comply with the
PRA's requirements. The CFO (executive director) whe has full knowledge of potential naw assets and can influence their timing or negotiate additional charcholdor
funding accordingly, The asset and liability maturity profile is set in Notes 23.

Credit risk

Credit risk arises where the possibility exists of a counterparty defaulting on its obligations. Credit risk exposures are required to be controlled through close monitoring of
positions and credit ratings. Day-to-day management of credit ask is carried out by the credit department. The credit committee meets as and when required. It considers
all naw credit applications. It can approve those up to € 4,000k and recommends those above € 4,000k for board approval. The credit exposure reports and credit
committee minutes are discussed at the ALCO meetings. The Bank normally takes legally enforceable security for facilities which it grants; this may be security over
customer's assets, or in some instances cash and in yet others, securities listed on the Tehran Stock Exchange, which are charged to the Bank either by the borrower itself

or by a third party.

Market risk

Non-traging market risk

The Bank does not trade either interest rate or exchange rate risk. Interest rate and currency risk arise in the Bank's banking book through holding of financial instruments,

and managed on a daily basis by the Bank's Treasury function. Details of interest rate risk exposure are presented in note 20. The Bank similarly does not take (trading)
open positions in foreign currencies and so Is exposed to very litte foreign exchange risk, see note 21.

Interest rate risk

The Bank does not have interest rate gaps in excess of one year. Non-interest bearing assets and liabilities are included with capital funds which are also undated. The
potential effect of a 1% adverse mo ini rates all currencies and all dates at 31 March 2018 would increase this year’s loss by €187k (2017 €124k).
This calculation is determined by closing ail interest rate gap through compensating market deals at rates 1% above or below the existing gap rates.

Currency risk

market as soon as it is practical to do so. See note 21 for the position analaysis and geographical concentrations.

Derivative instruments / hedging

The Bank does not hold derivative instruments for trading purposes. Exposures to interest rate risk, eg. fixed rate security or oan, are limited through the use of hedging
instruments; an interest rate swap may be used to convert the interest flows from a fixed rate asset to floating rates so matching the associated funding. However, the
Bank has not adopted hedge accounting.
