OVERVIEW

Recognise Financial Services Limited is a subsidiary of the AIM-listed City of London Group PLC, an
established provider of financial services with a strategy to build a fully licenced bank lending to the UK
SME market and accepting deposits from business and retail savers. This strategy is progressing well
and, over the past year, we have further strengthened our experienced team with a flow of new
colleagues taking up key roles, including the appointment of a new Chief Risk Officer in late 2019 and
Chief Financial Officer in January 2020. Progress has also been achieved through the prudent
employment of the investment raised during the first quarter of 2019, particularly in the build of the
banking platforms. Working with our partners, Mambu and nCino, we are now in the final stages of the
launch of the lending platform and, in parallel, we are making similar progress with our savings operation
which is being outsourced to the market leaders, Newcastle Strategic Solutions Ltd. We expect to be
ready to lend as soon as we receive authorisation and to accept deposits immediately after we receive
full licence.

On the governance side, a high quality and very experienced board led by Philip Jenks, a veteran of
challenger bank delivery, has been in place since mid-2019, with all Board Committees operational.
This expertise and industry insight has added to the pace and quality of our plans with consistent
challenge along the way. Although the financial results inevitably reflect a business in the investment
period, expenditure has been closely controlled at each stage of this complex journey and our
expectations of profitability in the early years following granting of our full licence, remain unchanged.

Overall, we have made excellent progress towards achieving a full banking licence by the end of our
next financial year, in March 2021, with Authorisation with Restrictions expected by latest November
2020. This follows submission of our formal application in November 2019 and receipt of the Total
Capital Requirement (TCR) letter from the PRA in July 2020. To support the next stage of our plans,
the Group announced a new investment round in September 2020 which will underpin our lending
ambitions going forward and position Recognise for a live trading environment.

With the impact of the COVID-19 crisis still unwinding, UK SMEs will continue to find new credit difficult
to source particularly from existing lenders focused on resolving problem portfolios. The business plan
we have developed over recent years remains robust and, potentially, even more relevant given the
economic issues we are facing into. With no legacy book, Recagnise is well placed to help business
owners with viable business plans recover and re-build their confidence over the coming years.

STRATEGIC REPORT

Current and future strategy

In 2017, the City of London Group set out a new strategy to create a broadly-based jending business
supporting the UK’s vital small and medium-sized business market, and which would be majority funded
by personal and business customer savings. Integral to the strategy was the creation of a new
subsidiary, Recognise Financial Services Ltd led by its founding directors, Jason Oakley, Bryce Glover
and Adrian Golumbina. At the beginning of 2018, the Recognise management team commenced the
journey to become a fully authorised UK Bank able to accept deposits from customers to support its
lending ambitions. The joint Regulators, the PRA and FCA, rightly lay down stringent requirements to
become fully authorised and a process of continuous dialogue commences from the moment an
applicant joins the licence programme. The experienced Recognise management team has worked
assiduously over the past two years to meet and exceed these requirements and believe they are now
within a short distance of becoming authorised.

The strategy for Recognise remains robust and, potentially, even more relevant given the
unprecedented impact of COVID-19. It is to create a new UK SME Bank, using versatile cloud-based
technology to support a relationship-led lending operation which will focus on delivering service
excellence, speed of decision-making and execution, flexibility of structuring and access to senior
executives. With one of the most experienced management teams amongst the new bank applicants,
it will deliver a fresh face to customers and brokers, as well as building a regional presence in the North
West, Yorkshire, Midlands and the South. Recognise does not have the potential distraction of a legacy
book and is very familiar with the needs of busy entrepreneurs and business owners, particularly in less
familiar and more challenging times.

Deploying the new investment monies raised through City of London Group in Q1 2019, Recognise
has achieved the following:

* Formal application submitted to the Regulator in the final quarter of 2019 and receiving Total
Capital Requirement letter (TCR) in July 2020 with ongoing dialogue as we work towards
achieving Authorisation with Restrictions (AWR), hopefully by Q3 2020 and with a full licence
granted by Q1 2021;

¢ The lending platform, built with our partners Mambu and nCino, has been constructed and is
now in the final stages of testing ready for the first customers after we obtain authorisation;

* The savings platform, which we are outsourcing to Newcastle Strategic Solutions, the market
leaders, is on track to deliver personal savings by the end of 2020 and business savings in
Q1 2021, fully in line with our plans;

« Governance is now well established with a fully constituted independent Board since mid-
2019 and all Board Committees fully operational. The quality and relevant expertise of the
independent non-executive appointments, including the Chair, Philip Jenks, is very strong
and has contributed to the pace at which the operational model has been developed and
executed over the past year;

e Recognise now has 32 FTE (July 2020) and the Executive team, which has over 200 years
banking experience, has only one unfilled role with the remainder of this hand-picked team
having been in situ for at least 6 months and, in some cases, the full journey so far;

e The Risk and Compliance function is operational and the voluminous processes, policies and
procedures necessary for a Bank are very well advanced, including the lending and security
documentation we will require as we commence lending in the summer,

. @ The Finance function continues to build and prepare for detailed financial and regulatory
reporting whilst the responsibility for carrying out the Internal Audit function has been placed
with Deloitte to ensure that Recognise meets the highest standards and benefits from best
practice across the Financial Services market.

The Executive team has continued to exercise robust cost control, achieving this advanced stage of
progress at a cost well below other participants taking the same licence journey. At the same time,

The SME market opportunity for a new entrant like Recognise is as attractive now as when the strategy
was first put in place. Whilst we do not underestimate the serious economic and sector impact of COVID
-19, Recognise is well placed to help businesses with underlying strength and experienced
management to trade out the other side and also to be able to assist entrepreneurs who see
opportunities at this point in the cycle.

Going concern

The Company is in the process of applying for a UK banking licence and is expected to go into
mobilisation in Q4 of 2020. At the Group level, £15.2m was raised in March 2019 and in October 2020
City of London Group raised £26.8m capital to finance Recognise. This will enable the Company to
meet its regulatory capital adequacy requirements.

Recognise hopes to receive its Authorisation with Restrictions as a Bank in Q4 this year. On the basis
of the additional funding that has taken place, the Group has sufficient funding to capitalise the
Company for at least the next 12 months from the date of signing of these accounts.

The Directors have reviewed in detail the cash flow forecast for at least 12 months from the date of
approval of these accounts and, given the availability of further capital via the Group, it will have enough
regulatory capital and liquidity to meet its requirements. They have also considered the following risks
that could affect the business and the steps that the Company would take to mitigate these risks, which
include the following:

- Inherent uncertainties in market conditions including Brexit and COVID 19;
- Meeting the PRA milestones (legal & regulatory); and
- Capital and liquidity management.

The Board assesses its future capital and liquidity requirements regularly and, as part of its overall
strategy, has developed plans to access new funding as and when required. The business has prepared
annual budgets that include budgeted cash forecasts and funding requirements which have been
reviewed and approved both by the Company and the Group Board and form part of the submission to
the PRA.

Based on the above risk factors and the projected cash balances, the Directors are satisfied that the
Company has and will maintain sufficient financial resources to enable it to continue operating for the
foreseeable future and therefore continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the annual
report and accounts.

Principal Risks and Uncertainties

The objective of the Board is to set policies to manage and mitigate risk within acceptable levels whilst
maintaining the Company's ability to achieve its strategic objectives.

During the year the Company has developed further its risk appetite statements and risk tolerances,
enhanced its governance and risk reporting, including monitoring the Board’s top risks, and the
maintenance of a risk radar. It has also developed detailed plans on how it will implement and manage
its business activities should its application for a UK banking licence be successful.

The principal risks and uncertainties are currently capital raising, to fund lending and obtaining a full
banking licence. Subsequently, the principal risks to which Recognise will be exposed are:

e credit risk - the risk that as Recognise lends borrowers do not meet their repayment obligations;

e liquidity and funding risk - the risk that Recognise is unable to fund its lending activities and meet
obligations as they fall due;

¢ operational risks including
RECOGNISE FINANCIAL SERVICES LIMITED

- third party risk (the risk that a partner of Recognise is unable to provide services to
Recognise);

- cyber risk (the risk that Recognise is subject to a successful cyber attack); and

- compliance risk (the risk that Recognise does not meet its regulatory and legal obligations).

The principal mitigations of these risks are the recruitment of a team of people with extensive relevant
UK banking experience and the appointment of external advisors with relevant experience; the
establishment of appropriate policies and procedures to enable Recognise to deliver a first class
customer service whilst meeting its regulatory obligations in full {including Governance); and the
establishment of a fit for purpose risk management framework.

The economic background is currently challenging in the light of the recent COVID 19 pandemic, and
there is also a risk of a no-deal Brexit. However, in the absence of a legacy lending book Recognise is
well placed to support UK SMEs in what we expect to be a period of recovery. Our lending targets and
underwriting will reflect the economic background, and our expectations for sectors and businesses that
we think will thrive and those we expect to continue to be challenged.

People/succession

As Recognise successfully approaches AWR we have identified a number of key person dependencies
which have primarily arisen due to the minimal number of employees within the business. To ensure
that this risk is managed appropriately robust succession plans are currently being developed by the

Nominations Committee in close alignment with a robust remuneration strategy which has been
specifically designed by the Remuneration Committee to motivate and retain key personnel.

DIRECTORS’ REPORT

The Directors present their report and the financial statements of the Company for the year ended 31
March 2020.

Principal activities and business review

The Company was incorporated on 6 February 2017 with the name Biz Financial Services Limited. The
Company changed its name to Echo Financial Services Limited on 2 June 2017 and subsequently to
Recognise Financial Services Limited on 13 November 2018.

In the second half of 2017, the Directors explored the possibility of the Company applying for a UK
banking licence. On 31 January 2018, City of London Group Plc acquired a 73% interest in the Company
and the Company commenced the process of applying for a UK banking licence in February 2018. On
20 July 2018 the Group percentage holding was reduced from 73% to 72% as a result of a share

reorganisation.

The Company did not generate any revenue during the year to 31 March 2020. The loss for the year
comprises the costs incurred in preparing the licence application during the year.

Results and dividends

The loss for the year ended 31 March 2020 was £3,351,427 (Previous period to 31 March 2019 was
£1,591,254). The Directors do not recommend payment of a dividend.

3.3 Corporation tax expense

Corporation tax expense comprises current and deferred tax. Corporation tax expense is recognised in
the income statement except to the extent that it relates to items recognised in equity, in which case it is
recognised in equity.

Current tax is the expected tax payable on. the taxable income for the year, using tax rates enacted or
substantively enacted at the balance sheet date, and any adjustment to tax payable in respect of previous
years, ,

Deferred tax is provided for temporary differences between the carrying amounts of assets and liabilities
for financial reporting purposes and the amounts used for taxation purposes. Deferred tax is measured
at the tax rates that are expected to be applied to the temporary differences when they reverse, based
on the laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date.

A deferred tax asset is recognised only to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profits will be
available against which the asset can be utilised. Deferred tax assets are reviewed at each reporting date
and are reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that the related tax benefit will be realised.
Unrecognised deferred tax assets are reassessed at each balance sheet date and are recognised to the
extent that it has become probable that future taxable profits will allow the deferred tax asset to be
recovered.

current tax assets against current tax liabilities and the deferred taxes relate to the same taxable entity

from the initial recognition of an asset or liability in a transaction that is not a business combination and,

Classification and measurement categories

The Company applies IFRS 9 and classifies its financial assets in the following measurement categories:
- Fair value through profit or loss (FVPL)

~ Fair value through other comprehensive | income (FVOCI)

- Amortised cost

Classification and subsequent measurement depend on:

1) The Company’s business model for managing the asset; and
2) The cash flow characteristics of the asset.

Business model
The business model reflects how the Company manages the assets in order to generate cash flows. That

is, whether the Company’s objective is:

20
RECOGNISE FINANCIAL SERVICES LIMITED

- solely to collect the contractual cash flows from the assets (Hold to collect); or

- to collect both the contractual cash flows and cash flows arising from sale of the assets (Hold to collect
and sell); or

- neither of these (Other).

Factors considered by the Company in determining the business model for a group of assets include past
experience of how the cash flows for these assets were collected, how the assets’ performance is
evaluated and reported to key management personnel, how risks are assessed and managed and how
managers are compensated.

SPPI test

Where the business model is “Hold to collect” or “Hold to collect and sell’, the Company assesses whether
the financial instruments’ contractual cash flows represent solely payment of principal and interest on that
principal (SPPI). In making this assessment, the Company considers whether those cash flows are
consistent with a basic lending arrangement (i.e. interest includes only consideration for the time value of
money, credit risk, other basic lending risks and a profit margin that is consistent with a basic lending
arrangement) or reflect exposure to risk or volatility that are inconsistent with a basic lending arrangement.

Based on these factors, the Company classifies its financial instruments into one of the following
measurement categories:

Amortised cost
Financial assets within a “Hold to collect” business model whose contractual cash flows are SPPI are

measured at amortised cost.

The carrying amount of these assets is adjusted by any expected credit loss allowance. Interest income
from these financial assets is included in ‘Interest and similar income’ using the effective interest rate
method.

Fair value through other comprehensive income (FVOCI)
Financial assets with a “Hold to collect and sell” business model whose contractual cash flows are SPPI
are measured at FVOCI.

Movements. in the carrying amount are taken through OCI, except for the recognition of impairment gains
or losses, interest income and foreign exchange gains and losses on the instrument's amortised cost
which are recognised in profit or loss. When the financial asset is derecognised, the cumulative gain or
loss previously recognised in OCI is reclassified from equity to profit or loss and recognised in ‘Net
investment income’.

Interest income from these financial assets is included in ‘Interest and similar income’ using the effective
interest rate method.

Reclassification

The Company reclassifies debt investments when and only when its business model for managing those
assets changes. The reclassification takes place from the start of the first reporting period following the
change.

20 Ultimate Parent Company

The Company’s ultimate parent Company is City of London Group plc, a Company registered in England
and Wales which currently holds 72% of the ordinary share capital and 100% of the deferred shares. City
of London Group Plc is the largest and smallest group for which publicly available consolidated accounts
including the entity's position and results are included. Copies of City of London Group plc's accounts can
be obtained from 6th Floor, 60 Gracechurch Street, London, United Kingdom, EC3V OHR.

21 Events after the Balance Sheet

On 21 July 2020, Recognise received its TCR ietter from the PRA. The receipt of this letter, which sets
out the PRA’s capital and liquidity requirements for Recognise, is an important milestone in its application
for a UK banking licence.

On 4th September 2020, following the receipt of the Total Capital Requirement Letter announced on 23
July 2020, the minority equity interest in its subsidiary Recognise Financial Services Limited
("Recognise"), held by the Recognise management team, has been converted into ordinary shares in the
Company. As announced on 31 January 2018, the Company entered into an agreement with the
Recognise management team to form the subsidiary. The management held a minority interest in the
subsidiary and there were put and call arrangements in place for COLG to acquire the equity interest held
by the team. COLG has issued 5,600,000 new ordinary shares to the Recognise management team and
now owns 100% of Recognise.

In October 2020 City of London Group raised £26.8m capital to finance Recognise. Within this there is a
new cornerstone investor who is contributing £25m. Recognise Financial Services Limited is progressing
with its application to the PRA for a banking licence. Recognise hopes to receive its Authorisation with
Restrictions as a Bank in Q4 this year. Recognise will be a new bank with the specific aim to serve UK
businesses — a group that is underserved, both from a lending and savings perspective.
