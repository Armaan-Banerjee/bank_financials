Introduction

Persia Intemational Bank PLC (“PIB” or “the Bank”) is an authorised institution under the
Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. The Bank’s principal activity is the provision of
commercial banking services to its customers. Although financial sanctions imposed in 2010
were lifted on 16 January 2016, the US decision to unilaterally withdraw from the Joint

a Gomprehensive Plan-of Action (JCPOA).in November. 2018 and. the.subsequent.re-impasition
of OFAC sanctions means that normal banking activities have not yet been fully resumed due
to continuing difficulties in arranging UK cleaning facilities and establishing correspondent
relationships with intemationa] banks. The current geopolitical situation in the Middle East
region, coupled with the Ukraine and Russia conflict, has nevertheless added to uncertainties.

Business Review

Historically, the Bank has offered a comprehensive service in trade finance and credit facilities
to its customers. Under trade finance, the Bank receives and advises letters of credit, adds
confirmation, and provides short-term credit facilities by discounting the bills drawn under
them. PIB also extended credit facilities to its corporate and institutional customers either on
bilateral terms or as part of syndication. Iranian corporate customers constituted a significant
portion of the Bank’s customer base.

In July 2021, the Bank received a license from the Central Bank of Italy for its ¢ Milan branch
as a branch of a third-country bank (TCB) outside the EU. The branch has implemented its core
banking systems and is operational. This should enable the Bank to expand its business mainly
in trade finance and credit facilities; however, it is facing similar issues in establishing any
correspondent banking relationship due to US sanctions.

The Statement of Financial Position shows total assets of the Bank have reduced slightly due
to an FI drawing on its current account as well as the scheduled amortisation of loan assets.
Historically, and before the sanctions, trade finance had been an important portfolio in PIB’s
business lines. Although it secured a €19.8Mn forfaiting business in FY22, this year the Bank
did not have any opportunity in this line of business. Further resumption of trade finance
activities is possible only when the issues surrounding correspondent banking relationships are
resolved. However, the Bank will consider all viable options in the future.

The outlook for the year 2024-25 will be challenging as economic and geopolitical
uncertainties, ongoing regulatory changes, and difficulties in securing suitable correspondent
banking relationships have created uncertainties. Looking ahead, the Bank sees significant
opportunities in its key market. The Bank has a skilled and motivated team and solid
shareholder support, which will assist in handling the uncertainties. Its continued focus has
been on projects in the public sector, firms, and corporates that generate revenue in foreign
currencies.

OFAC Sanctions

Following the decision of the US Government to unilaterally withdraw from the JCPOA, the
OFAC sanctions were imposed in November 2018. These sanctions prohibit people from the
US from engaging in any trade, financial and other dealings with Iran. This has prevented the
Bank from engaging in business with the US-based or linked counterparties and has made
dealings with other countnes also more difficult. The management is of the view that the impact
of OFAC sanctions although visible will not hamper the present business pattern of the Bank.
Its primary business dealings are in euros and exposures to Iranian entities. The non-availability
of SWIFT impacted business communications and transfer of funds. This has been managed
through a book transfer mechanism with the involvement of fraternal banks in the EU.

Risk weighting of assets

The UK & EU notification on the equivalence of the supervisory and regulatory requirements
of non-EU countries has excluded Iran from the list of those countries, and therefore exposure
to [ran will continue to be risk-weighted at 150%. Considering our business activities are
mainly focused on Iranian corporates and financial institutions, these risk weighting measures
will impact the business level that the Bank can achieve and therefore its profitability.

ECL is determined by using a staging model and forward-looking information. Impairment
provision on all financial assets is recognised either on 12 months losses or lifetime expected
losses and measured through a loss allowance at an amount equal to:

- 12-month ECL, i.e. lifetime ECL that result from those default events on the financial
instrument that are possible within 12 months after the reporting date, (referred to as
Stage 1); or

- Full lifetime ECL, i.e. lifetime ECL that result from all possible default events over the
lifetime of the financial instrument, (referred to as Stage 2 and Stage 3).

A loss allowance for full lifetime ECL is required for a financial instrument if the credit risk
on that financial instrument has increased significantly since initial recognition or has become
credit impaired. For all other financial instruments, ECLs are measured at an amount equal to
the 12-month ECL.

ECLs are probability-weighted estimates of the present value of credit losses. These are
measured as the present value of the difference between the cash flows due to the Bank under
the contract and the cash flows that the Bank expects to receive arising from the weighting of
multiple future economic scenarios, discounted at the asset’s EIR.

- For undrawn loan commitments, the ECL is the difference between the present value
of the difference between the contractual cash flows that are due to the Bank if the
holder of the commitment draws down the loan and the cash flows that the Bank expects
to receive if the loan is drawn down; and

- For financial guarantee contracts, the ECL is the difference between the expected
payments to reimburse the holder of the guaranteed debt instrument less any amount
that the Bank expects to receive from the holder, the debtor or any other party.

The Bank measures its ECL on an individual basis. The measurement of the loss allowance is

2.9  Credit-impaired financial assets

A financial asset is ‘credit-impaired’ when one or more events that have a detrimental impact
on the estimated future cash flows of the financial asset have occurred. Credit-impaired
financial assets are referred to as Stage 3 assets. Evidence of credit-impairment includes
observable data about the following events:

- significant financial difficulty of the borrower or issuer;

- A breach of contract such as a default or past due event;

- The lender of the borrower, for economic or contractual reasons relating to the
borrower’s financial difficulty, having granted to the borrower a concession that the
lender would not otherwise consider

- The disappearance of an active market for a security because of financial difficulties;
or

- The purchase of a financial asset at a deep discount that reflects the incurred credit
losses.

It may not be possible to identify a single discrete event — instead, the combined effect of
several events may have caused the financial asset to become credit-impaired. The Bank
assesses whether debt instruments that are financial assets measured at amortised cost or

instruments are credit impaired, the Bank considers factors such as bond yields, credit ratings
and the ability of the borrower to raise funding.

Lease in which the Bank is a lessee

Leases are recognised as a right of use asset with a correspondingly liability at the date at which
the leased asset is available for use by the Bank. Each lease payment is allocated between the

at the end of the lease, the right of use asset is depreciated over the shorter of useful life of the
underlying asset or the lease term on a straight-line basis.

Right-of-use assets

The Bank recognises right-of-use assets at the commencement date of the lease (i.e., the date
the underlying asset is available for use). Right-of-use assets are measured at cost, less any
accumulated depreciation and impairment losses, and adjusted for any remeasurement of lease
liabilities. The cost of nght-of-use assets includes the amount of lease liabilities recognised,
initial direct costs incurred, and lease payments made at or before the commencement date less
any lease incentives received. Right-of-use assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis over
the lease term.

2.24 Provisions

Provisions are recognised when the Bank has a present legal or constructive obligation as a
result of a past event, it is probable that the Bank will be required to settle that obligation and
a reliable estimate can be made of the amount of the obligation. The amount recognised as a
provision is the best estimate of the consideration required to settle the present obligation at
the reporting date, taking into account the risks and uncertainties surrounding the obligation.
Where a provision is measured using cash flows estimated to settle the present obligation, its
carrying amount is the present value of those cash flows. Where some or all of the economic
benefits required to settle a provision are expected to be recovered from a third party, a
receivable is recognised as an asset if it is virtually certain that reimbursement will be received

and the amount of the receivable can be measured reliably.
